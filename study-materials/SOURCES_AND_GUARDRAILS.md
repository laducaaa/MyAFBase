### Access-copy rule

A mirrored copy can be used to overcome temporary access problems only after its title, publication date, revision, and relevant text are matched to the official listing. A mirror never outranks the official study portal or e-Publishing source.

## Facts established by the current baseline

1. AFH 1, 15 February 2025, states that it has no releasability restrictions.
2. The 26E5 and 26E6 catalogs identify that AFH edition as the PFE source under revision 76.
3. The 26E5 catalog scopes AFPT 00035 to Chapters 1, 5, 7–9, 11–12, 14–15, 17–20, 22, and 24. The 26E6 catalog scopes AFPT 00036 to Chapters 1, 5, 7–9, 11–20, 22, and 24. An ADTC rating alone does not place a chapter within a given catalog's scope.
4. AFH 1 contains the ADTC used to identify relevance and required comprehension by promotion grade.
5. The ADTC defines:
   - **A — Remembering:** recognize or recall knowledge;
   - **B — Understanding:** interpret, classify, summarize, infer, compare, or explain;
   - **C — Applying:** use learned material in a situation or procedure; and
   - **D — Analyzing:** break concepts into parts and determine their relationships.
6. A December 2021 announcement for 22E5/22E6 described an 80-item PFE composed of 60 knowledge items and 20 SJT items with a 100-point WAPS contribution. The still-linked February 2023 SJT guide uses four options and separate most-effective and least-effective questions. Neither source independently confirms the 2027 format.
7. The official SJT guide says retained operational items are keyed through subject-matter-expert and behavioral-science consensus. MyPromotion cannot recreate or represent that official validation process.
8. AFH 1, paragraphs 9.10–9.11, broadly summarizes individual-study and sharing restrictions. Current DAFMAN 36-2664, §4.12, is controlling and applies different provisions to potential examinees, examinees, and members who have accessed the relevant test category.

### Status-specific test-security summary

- **Potential examinee:** An enlisted Airman who may become eligible for a promotion test. Section 4.12.4 applies to potential examinees and examinees and restricts actual/suspected content, shared marked material or pretests, and use of another person's notes.
- **Examinee:** A person currently taking or who has previously taken the specific personnel test. Section 4.12.5 is headed “Examinees” and contains the manual's group-study and government-computer provisions. Because one subparagraph separately mentions potential examinees, obtain an official interpretation rather than silently applying the heading to every status.
- **Member with test access:** Section 4.12.6 bars participation in developing a guide or pretest for the accessed test category and bars sharing commercial guides. It states that individual use of a legally developed commercial guide, though not DAF-recommended or endorsed, is not itself a compromise.

These are working summaries, not substitutes for the full current manual or official interpretation.

## Copyright and originality policy

17 U.S.C. § 105 generally withholds U.S. copyright protection from works prepared by federal officers or employees as part of official duties. That does **not** establish that every element embedded in a federal publication is unrestricted. Contractor-created material, third-party quotations, photographs, illustrations, privacy interests, publicity rights, and trademarks can remain protected.

MyPromotion therefore uses the following conservative rules:

1. Write original explanations rather than reproducing AFH prose.
2. Quote only short language whose exact wording is educationally necessary, such as official titles, core-value names, oaths, or list labels.
3. Cite every source quotation and substantive factual claim.
4. Do not reproduce AFH photographs, illustrations, seals, insignia, layouts, or typography without a documented rights determination.
5. Do not reproduce competitor text, questions, screenshots, audio, databases, taxonomies, or proprietary teaching devices.
6. Use competitor listings only to identify commonly offered functionality.
7. Maintain a source-and-change record for every production release.
8. Obtain qualified legal review before commercial distribution in the United States or other countries.

If a published work consists predominantly of U.S. Government material and carries a copyright notice, 17 U.S.C. §403 requires the notice to identify the portions in which copyright is claimed. MyPromotion must not use a blanket notice that falsely suggests ownership of the underlying government text.

## Trademark and affiliation policy

The Air Force Seal is for official government use and must not appear in a public retail product. The Air Force symbol, Hap Arnold wings, emblems, unit insignia, and other protected service marks must not appear unless the Air Force Trademark Licensing Program gives written permission or a license.

Descriptive references such as “Air Force,” “WAPS,” “PFE,” “SSgt,” and “TSgt” must:

- be used only to explain product compatibility or subject matter;
- not be styled as a product endorsement;
- not dominate MyPromotion branding;
- not be combined into a logo that resembles an official mark; and
- appear with a prominent non-affiliation disclaimer reviewed by counsel.

A disclaimer does not authorize otherwise unauthorized trademark use.

## Security and policy review

DAFI 35-101 and the cited DoD public-release instructions require information concerning DoD or U.S. Government plans, policies, programs, or operations to pass through the appropriate Public Affairs security-and-policy review, whether prepared officially or as a personal enterprise. WAPS is a DAF program, so MyPromotion must obtain review before public release and retain the written result.

Private-capacity material should carry:

> The views expressed are those of the author and do not necessarily reflect the official policy or position of the Department of the Air Force, the Department of Defense, or the U.S. Government.

This disclaimer does not replace review, ethics compliance, or trademark permission.

## RegAF author safeguards

The project owner is a current Regular Air Force member who has not taken WAPS. Under the current DAFMAN 36-2664 definition, an enlisted Airman who may become eligible for a promotion test is already a **potential examinee** for that test. The owner is not yet a PFE **examinee** solely on that basis; the manual defines an examinee as someone currently taking or who has previously taken the specific test.

DAFMAN 36-2664, §4.12.6.1, creates a separate access trigger. A member who has accessed a category of personnel test may not participate in any way in developing a commercial guide or pretest for that category, whether or not it captures actual test material. The app's practice questions also fall close to the manual's broad “pretest” definition.

### Commercial-release blocker

No sales, subscriptions, advertising, affiliate income, royalties, or other compensation may begin until an ethics counselor gives a written determination addressing 5 C.F.R. §2635.807. The product concerns the ongoing DAF WAPS program, creating a substantial “related to official duties” issue that this project cannot decide for itself. DAF Form 3902 approval alone would not resolve that separate federal ethics question.

Before commercial activity, the owner should provide the local ethics or legal office a complete written description and ask:

1. Does 5 C.F.R. §2635.807 prohibit compensation for this writing and educational product, and what activities count as compensation?
2. Does 5 C.F.R. §3601.106 or local policy require DAF Form 3902 or other advance approval?
3. May the owner develop, own, review, market, or profit from the product before relevant PFE access?
4. What exact event constitutes “access” for §4.12.6.1, and what activities must stop at that event?
5. After access, may the owner retain ownership or receive passive revenue, or perform support, moderation, finance, or other non-content administration?
6. What restrictions apply to advertising to Airmen, especially junior members, coworkers, or subordinates?
7. May the owner identify as a current Airman or mention rank or experience without monetizing military status under JER §2-508?
8. Does the one-user, non-collaborative design satisfy the rules applicable to a mixed-status audience?
9. How must unsolicited suspected test material be handled without unauthorized retention, copying, analysis, or dissemination?
10. What trademark permission and DAFI 35-101 security-and-policy review are required before public release?

Until written guidance is received:

- use only personal time, devices, accounts, and funds;
- do not use government email, networks, computers, facilities, or personnel;
- do not perform project work during duty hours, as part of official duties, or with government personnel or resources;
- do not imply that military rank, position, or access supports the product;
- do not solicit subordinates, coworkers, or junior personnel;
- do not request information about operational tests;
- do not collect post-test “topic sightings,” wording, answers, or frequency reports; and
- do not use future personal test experience to alter the product.

Before the owner receives relevant PFE access, establish a documented handoff and recusal process. At access, the owner must stop content development and review unless the responsible testing-policy authority and JAG provide written contrary guidance. Do not assume passive or administrative activities are permitted after access without a written determination.

## Test-security controls for the app

### Prohibited

- Public, unit, or shared PFE decks
- Group-study rooms or collaborative PFE sessions
- Shared annotations or highlights
- Free-text test-experience, question, answer, or item-upload features
- “What I saw on my test” reports
- Crowdsourced estimates of operational item wording or frequency
- Government-device installation instructions
- Claims of official questions, official scoring keys, guaranteed promotion, or exact exam prediction

### Required

- Individual-user terms and account controls
- A ban on credential and content sharing
- A visible “never submit actual or recalled test content” warning
- Content provenance and review records for publisher-authored material
- A procedure that stops access and dissemination if suspected test material is received unsolicited and immediately requests handling instructions from the servicing TCO/JAG
- A prohibition on independently circulating, analyzing, deleting, or retaining copies of suspected test material except as directed by the responsible authority
- A non-affiliation disclaimer
- The DAFI 35-101 personal-capacity disclaimer
- A cycle/version notice
- An “exam source versus current duty policy” notice

## Human-review policy

No reviewer should be asked what appeared on an operational promotion test. Reviewers evaluate only:

- fidelity to public cited sources;
- clarity and educational usefulness;
- realistic but wholly invented examples;
- rank-appropriate application;
- internal consistency;
- accessibility and bias;
- item-writing quality; and
- whether a scenario has a defensible educational rationale.

If reviewer feedback references remembered operational test content, stop access and dissemination and immediately obtain handling instructions from the servicing TCO/JAG. Do not incorporate, circulate, analyze, delete, or retain the material except as directed.
