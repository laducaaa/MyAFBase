## Section 10A — Eligibility and Restrictions

### C10-FC-001
- **Prompt:** Why does the USAF classify and assign Airmen worldwide according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** To ensure a high state of readiness to accomplish the mission, using a coherent classification system to identify valid manpower requirements, describe each occupational specialty, set minimum prerequisites, and place qualified Airmen into each specialty—while recognizing special assignment considerations for Airmen with exceptional needs.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-002
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 frame voluntary versus involuntary PCS assignments?
- **Answer:** To the maximum extent possible, the USAF assigns a PCS on a voluntary basis. When required, involuntary assignments are distributed among similarly qualified Airmen to minimize family separation or allow Airmen to tend to essential military and personal responsibilities.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-003
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 characterize Air Force Reserve assignments?
- **Answer:** Reserve assignments may be full-time or part-time and are all voluntary; thus new assignments must be applied for, particularly when pursuing promotion opportunities.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-004
- **Prompt:** What PCS policy roles does AFH 1 assign to the Department of Defense?
- **Answer:** DoD allocates funds, delegates authority, and directs policies for PCS assignments of Airmen to satisfy national security requirements.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-005
- **Prompt:** Who may initiate assignments to fill valid vacant manpower authorizations?
- **Answer:** The director of assignments (or equivalent), in coordination with major commands, field operating agencies, and direct reporting units.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-006
- **Prompt:** What are the final approval authorities for SMSgt-and-below versus CMSgt/CMSgt-select assignments?
- **Answer:** AFPC is final approval for Senior Master Sergeant and below. The Air Force Executive Talent Management Office is final approval for Chief Master Sergeant and Chief Master Sergeant-select assignments.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-007
- **Prompt:** What instruction governs operational, training, and force-structure assignments in AFH 1?
- **Answer:** DAFI 36-2110, Total Force Assignments—covering operational (including rotational), training (including formal education and PME), and force structure assignments.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-008
- **Prompt:** When are published PCS assignments not normally cancelled?
- **Answer:** Within 60 days of the projected departure date, unless the Airman cannot be effectively used at the projected location.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-009
- **Prompt:** What happens if an Airman claims hardship from an assignment cancellation?
- **Answer:** The Airman prepares a written statement coordinated through the unit commander to the MPS; the MPS then advises the assignment OPR to consider reinstatement, provide an alternate assignment, or confirm cancellation with reasons the Airman must remain.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-010
- **Prompt:** What is the primary factor in selecting Airmen for a PCS?
- **Answer:** The Airman’s qualifications to fill a valid manpower requirement and perform productively in the position.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-011
- **Prompt:** What is the Special Experience Identifier system used for?
- **Answer:** It complements assignments and, with grade, AFSC, and AFSC prefixes/suffixes, matches uniquely qualified Airmen to jobs with special requirements when specific experience or training is critical and no other means is appropriate or available.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-012
- **Prompt:** When may security-access urgency change how Airmen are selected for a position?
- **Answer:** When urgency to fill does not allow selection using PCS eligibility criteria and subsequent processing or investigation for the specified access level; selection may then be from Airmen who currently have access or can be granted access immediately.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-013
- **Prompt:** How are SMSgt-and-below normally selected for assignment relative to AFSC and skill level?
- **Answer:** Normally in their control AFSC based on grade and skill level. If grade and control AFSC skill level are incompatible due to retraining or reclassification, they are selected and allocated against requirements commensurate with their grade regardless of control AFSC skill level.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-014
- **Prompt:** Which skill-level positions do SMSgt, MSgt/TSgt, SSgt/SrA, and Amn grades primarily fill?
- **Answer:** SMSgt fill 9-skill level; MSgt and TSgt fill 7-skill level; SSgt and SrA fill 5-skill level; A1C/Amn/AB fill 3-skill level. CMSgts primarily fill CEM code positions and may be assigned in any AFSC or CEM code they possess or are qualified to be awarded.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-015
- **Prompt:** How does volunteer status interact with time-on-station requirements?
- **Answer:** Qualified volunteers should be considered for all assignments. Volunteers who have not met TOS may be considered before qualified non-volunteers who have met TOS, but require a TOS waiver. A qualified volunteer who meets minimum TOS is considered first based on longest on station.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-016
- **Prompt:** What is the first-term Airman two-assignment limitation during the first four years?
- **Answer:** First-term Airmen serving an initial enlistment of four or more years may not be given more than two assignments in different locations following initial basic and skill training during their first four years, regardless of tour length. After two PCS moves, an additional PCS is permitted with approved humanitarian, join spouse, as a volunteer, or as a mandatory move. Low-cost moves are excluded from the two-move count.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-017
- **Prompt:** What base-of-preference options and limits does AFH 1 state for enlisted Airmen?
- **Answer:** FTA and career Airmen may request BOP as an incentive for continued service. FTA with reenlistment/retraining may request CONUS–CONUS or overseas–CONUS PCS; CONUS FTA may request in-place BOP. PCS BOP is not authorized CONUS–overseas or overseas–overseas; in-place BOP is not authorized for Airmen assigned overseas.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-018
- **Prompt:** What join-spouse caution does AFH 1 give military couples?
- **Answer:** Each serves in his or her own right and must fill valid manning requirements with trained skills; joint residence may be considered when those criteria are met, but couples should not base future service, career development, or family planning on an assumption they will always colocate or that join spouse is guaranteed.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-019
- **Prompt:** What is the purpose of home-basing and follow-on assignment programs, and who must be briefed?
- **Answer:** They reduce PCS costs and turbulence and increase stability by providing advance assignment consideration. The servicing MPS must brief all eligible Airmen selected for or electing an unaccompanied overseas tour of 15 months or less; Airmen must apply or decline in writing.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-020
- **Prompt:** When is assignment of family members to the same location prohibited?
- **Answer:** Family members will not be assigned where one will or may hold a command or supervisory position over another, because of preferential treatment or loss of impartiality perceptions that compromise command/supervisory integrity. Same location/unit is otherwise not prohibited.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-021
- **Prompt:** What does the Voluntary Stabilized Base Assignment Program provide?
- **Answer:** A stabilized tour in exchange for volunteering for an assignment to a historically hard-to-fill location (enlisted only).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-022
- **Prompt:** What is an extended long overseas tour volunteer commitment?
- **Answer:** For a long-tour location (accompanied ≥24 months; unaccompanied >15 months), volunteers agree to the standard tour plus an additional 12 months and are considered ahead of standard overseas tour volunteers per USAF priorities.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-023
- **Prompt:** What are the TDY maximum and involuntary-overseas RNLTD protections in AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Maximum TDY at any one location in a 12-month period is 180 days unless SecAF waives. If selected for involuntary overseas while performing certain TDY, the RNLTD will not be within 120 days of TDY completion.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-024
- **Prompt:** Why is service retainability required upon PCS selection, and what happens if a career Airman declines it?
- **Answer:** Retainability ensures enough remaining RegAF status to offset PCS costs. Airmen without it may decline or, if eligible, retire instead of accepting PCS. Declining retainability makes a career Airman ineligible for promotion and reenlistment.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-025
- **Prompt:** What retainability rules apply to OCONUS→CONUS and CONUS→CONUS PCS?
- **Answer:** Overseas to CONUS usually requires at least 12 months obligated service retainability; failure keeps the Airman overseas until DOS. CONUS to CONUS requires 24 months regardless of career status.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-026
- **Prompt:** What retainability implications attach to accompanied overseas tours?
- **Answer:** Eligible Airmen desiring dependents at government expense must serve the longer accompanied tour and obtain retainability for it. Ineligible or declining Airmen will not receive approval for dependent travel at government expense or command sponsorship.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-027
- **Prompt:** What time-on-station requirements apply to CONUS PCS and overseas PCS?
- **Answer:** Most CONUS PCS moves require at least 48 months TOS for FTA and career Airmen (except eligible FTA BOP). FTA need at least 12 months TOS for CONUS→overseas; career Airmen need 24 months TOS before an overseas PCS.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-028
- **Prompt:** What do EQUAL and EQUAL-Plus provide, and what is each Airman’s preference responsibility?
- **Answer:** EQUAL lists upcoming assignment requirements by AFSC/grade/location so Airmen can align preferences to USAF needs. EQUAL-Plus advertises special duty, joint/departmental, short-notice overseas, and all CMSgt assignments with qualifications, locations, reporting instructions, and POCs. Each Airman is responsible for currency and accuracy of preferences and should update them when preference or status changes.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-029
- **Prompt:** How are Chief Master Sergeant assignments managed for development?
- **Answer:** The Chiefs’ Group uses CMSgt assignment policies to support continued Chief development; policies are comparable with other senior-leader methods but managed differently than other enlisted grades. Additional info is on the USAF Portal, Senior Leader Career Management System Guide, and CMSgt Information Handbook.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-030
- **Prompt:** What are the core eligibility conditions for humanitarian reassignment or deferment?
- **Answer:** Helps resolve severe short-term family-member problems resolvable in a reasonable time (normally 12 months); the Airman’s presence must be absolutely essential; and the Airman must be effectively utilized in the control AFSC at the new assignment. Covered family members are spouse, children, parents, parents-in-law, and in loco parentis (5-year parental role before age 21 or RegAF entry, whichever earlier). Sibling terminal illness may be considered as an exception.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-031
- **Prompt:** How does EFMP differ from a base-of-choice program?
- **Answer:** EFMP addresses long-term/permanent special medical or educational needs for a spouse or child; it is not a base-of-choice program. Assignments follow USAF manning needs at locations where those needs can be met; enrollment/identification is mandatory. Reassignment or usually-12-month deferment may apply when care cannot be met or a new condition requires establishing care.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-032
- **Prompt:** What deferment options cover education, high-school seniors, and adoption?
- **Answer:** Educational deferment may be requested before PCS selection when nearly completing a vocational program or degree. High School Senior Assignment Deferment allows SMSgt and below (and officers through Lt Col) a one-year deferment for dependent high-school graduation, case-by-case with USAF needs first. Adoption may authorize deferment for six months after official placement and up to 21 days permissive TDY with ordinary leave.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-033
- **Prompt:** Who may receive expedited transfers under AFH 1, and what offender-reassignment consideration applies?
- **Answer:** Eligible Airmen filing unrestricted reports for sexual assault, stalking, or other listed sexual misconduct; Airmen whose adult military dependent files unrestricted non-domestic-abuse sexual assault under stated military-nexus conditions; and eligible victims of physical domestic violence by spouse/intimate partner—via SAPR, FAP, VWAP/Legal, or commander. Potential reassignment of the alleged offender shall be considered by the vice wing commander (or equivalent), balancing victim and alleged-offender interests.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10A; B—Understanding

## Section 10B — Occupational Codes

### C10-FC-034
- **Prompt:** What is the military personnel classification system designed to do?
- **Answer:** Match personnel qualifications with job/career field requirements.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10B; A—Remembering

### C10-FC-035
- **Prompt:** Which publication does AFH 1 cite for officer and enlisted AFSC examples and notes?
- **Answer:** AFMAN 36-2100.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10B; A—Remembering

### C10-FC-036
- **Prompt:** How many digits/characters do officer and enlisted AFSCs use?
- **Answer:** Officer AFSCs use four; enlisted AFSCs use five.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.11; Table 10.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10B; A—Remembering

### C10-FC-037
- **Prompt:** What do the first-digit career groups 1 through 9 represent?
- **Answer:** 1 Operations; 2 Logistics; 3 Support; 4 Medical or Dental; 5 Legal or Religious Affairs; 6 Acquisition or Finance; 7 Special Investigations; 8 Special Duty Identifier; 9 Reporting Identifier.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.11; Table 10.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10B; A—Remembering

### C10-FC-038
- **Prompt:** What do the second and third enlisted AFSC characters represent?
- **Answer:** Second = career field (example 2T Transportation and Vehicle Management); third = career field subdivision (example 2T3 Vehicle Management).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.11; Table 10.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10B; A—Remembering

### C10-FC-039
- **Prompt:** What are the enlisted skill-level codes and meanings?
- **Answer:** 1 Helper; 3 Apprentice; 5 Journeyman; 7 Craftsman; 9 Superintendent; 0 Chief Enlisted Manager (CEM).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.11; Table 10.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10B; A—Remembering

### C10-FC-040
- **Prompt:** What are the officer AFSC qualification levels?
- **Answer:** 1 Entry (any AFSC); 2 Intermediate (only for AFSCs so designated in the AFOCD); 3 Qualified (any AFSC); 4 Staff (functional responsibility in positions above wing level).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.11; Table 10.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10B; A—Remembering

### C10-FC-041
- **Prompt:** What does the fifth enlisted AFSC character identify?
- **Answer:** The specific AFSC (example: 2T372 Special Vehicle Maintenance within Vehicle Management Craftsman).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.11; Table 10.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10B; A—Remembering

### C10-FC-042
- **Prompt:** What is an AFSC alpha prefix?
- **Answer:** An ability, skill, special qualification, or system designator not restricted to a single AFSC (example: Instructor).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.11; Table 10.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10B; A—Remembering

### C10-FC-043
- **Prompt:** What is an AFSC alpha suffix (shred out)?
- **Answer:** Positions associated with particular equipment or functions within a single specialty.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.11; Table 10.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10B; A—Remembering

### C10-FC-044
- **Prompt:** What is the AFECD?
- **Answer:** The Air Force Enlisted Classification Directory—the official guide to USAF enlisted classification codes. It establishes the enlisted occupational structure primarily for personnel officials/agencies engaged in procurement, classification, and training so enlisted members can specialize while the USAF meets changing mission requirements.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10B; A—Remembering

### C10-FC-045
- **Prompt:** What is the AFOCD?
- **Answer:** The Air Force Officer Classification Directory—the official guide to USAF officer classification codes; it serves a purpose very similar to the AFECD.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10B; A—Remembering

### C10-FC-046
- **Prompt:** How are career fields classified in the AFECD numerical/alphabetical structure?
- **Answer:** Operations (1X), Logistics (2X), Support (3X), Medical (4X), Paralegal and Religious Affairs (5X), Contracting and Financial (6X), Special Investigations (7X), Special Duty Identifiers (8X), and Reporting Identifiers (9X).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10B; A—Remembering

### C10-FC-047
- **Prompt:** What do Reporting Identifiers (9X) identify?
- **Answer:** Authorizations and individual enlisted Airmen who are not identifiable in the classification structure and for whom specialty descriptions are not written, as provided in the AFECD.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10B; A—Remembering

### C10-FC-048
- **Prompt:** What does the Aircrew Operations career field (1A) encompass at a high level?
- **Answer:** Pre-flight, in-flight, and post-flight duties of inspecting, training, directing, and performing combat, mobility, and special operations pertinent to enlisted primary aircrew activities.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10B; A—Remembering

### C10-FC-049
- **Prompt:** What does the Cyber Warfare career field (1B) encompass?
- **Answer:** Functions to develop, sustain, and enhance cyberspace capabilities to defend national interests from attack and to create effects in the cyberspace domain to achieve national objectives, using established TTPs for service, CCMD, and national objectives.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10B; A—Remembering

### C10-FC-050
- **Prompt:** What does the Intelligence career field (1N) encompass, and what is excluded?
- **Answer:** Collecting, producing, and distributing data of strategic, tactical, or technical intelligence value, including information security and language translation/interpretation. Excluded are collecting, collating, interpreting, and distributing general information of primary concern to other career fields.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10B; A—Remembering

### C10-FC-051
- **Prompt:** What distinctive role does the Special Warfare career field (1Z) play?
- **Answer:** It forms the Air Force’s premier ground maneuver element—employed across the conflict spectrum to extend airpower and enhance lethality—including global access, precision strike, and personnel recovery functions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10B; A—Remembering

### C10-FC-052
- **Prompt:** What does the Aerospace Maintenance career field (2A) broadly cover?
- **Answer:** Installing, maintaining, calibrating, and repairing avionics and related systems; aircrew egress, fuel, pneudraulics, AGE, aerial delivery parachutes, aerospace materials/parts, pressurized systems; multiple aircraft types; fabrication and corrosion control—excluding CE corrosion/sheet metal and certain cryogenic container functions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10B; A—Remembering

### C10-FC-053
- **Prompt:** What does the Force Support career field (3F) sustain and build?
- **Answer:** Ready and resilient Airmen through installation support in personnel, military equal opportunity, education and training, manpower, and services functions and activities (including MWR/sustainment services such as dining, lodging, fitness, mortuary, and related programs).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10B; A—Remembering

### C10-FC-054
- **Prompt:** What force-protection duties define the Security Forces career field (3P)?
- **Answer:** Force protection duties requiring use of force up to deadly force—installation security; nuclear/conventional weapons and resources security; air base defense; law enforcement; information security; military working dogs; combat arms training and maintenance—performing the military police function within the USAF.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10B; A—Remembering

### C10-FC-055
- **Prompt:** What does the Special Investigations career field (7S) encompass at a high level?
- **Answer:** Criminal, economic crime, environmental crime, computer crime, counterintelligence, counter threat, force protection, and personnel suitability investigations, plus technical services and special inquiries—excluding air intelligence functions belonging to the intelligence career field.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10B; A—Remembering

### C10-FC-056
- **Prompt:** To whom does AFH 1 say ANG strength-management responsibilities in ¶10.13 apply?
- **Answer:** They are not applicable to the RegAF or Air Force Reserve; they organize/conduct ANG recruiting and retention programs.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10B; A—Remembering

### C10-FC-057
- **Prompt:** What is the Production Recruiter Retainer (PRR) role in ANG strength management?
- **Answer:** Conducts ANG recruiting and retention programs under the strength-management team; organizes/executes enlist/appoint/retain programs; interviews, screens, tests, and evaluates applicants; develops zone information sources; and executes related marketing, presentations, and retention efforts.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10B; A—Remembering

### C10-FC-058
- **Prompt:** How do RRM and RRS roles differ in ANG strength management?
- **Answer:** The Recruiting Retention Manager develops annual unit recruiting plans, trains/supervises/coaches PRRs, and coordinates resources/advertising/production standards. The Recruiting Retention Superintendent provides managerial oversight of all state ANG recruiting and retention strength-management programs and advises state command staff.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10B; A—Remembering

### C10-FC-059
- **Prompt:** Why does AFH 1 structure AFSCs with multiple digit positions?
- **Answer:** To indicate specific identifiers within the military classification structure so personnel qualifications can be matched to job/career field requirements—moving from career group through field/subdivision to qualification or skill level and, for enlisted, specific AFSC.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.11; Table 10.1
- **Tags:** TSgt; 10B; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-060
- **Prompt:** How do officer fourth-character qualification levels differ in purpose from enlisted fourth-character skill levels?
- **Answer:** Officer fourth characters mark entry/intermediate/qualified/staff functional responsibility (staff = above wing level). Enlisted fourth characters mark Helper→Apprentice→Journeyman→Craftsman→Superintendent→CEM skill progression. Same digit position, different classification meaning.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.11; Table 10.1
- **Tags:** TSgt; 10B; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-061
- **Prompt:** How do alpha prefixes and alpha suffixes relate differently to AFSCs?
- **Answer:** A prefix marks an ability/skill/special qualification/system designator not restricted to a single AFSC. A suffix (shred out) marks positions tied to particular equipment or functions within a single specialty.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.11; Table 10.1
- **Tags:** TSgt; 10B; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-062
- **Prompt:** How do the AFECD and AFOCD relate in purpose while remaining distinct guides?
- **Answer:** Both are official classification-code guides serving very similar purposes; AFECD governs enlisted occupational structure for procurement/classification/training, while AFOCD is the officer counterpart.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.12
- **Tags:** TSgt; 10B; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-063
- **Prompt:** How do Special Duty Identifiers (8X) differ from Reporting Identifiers (9X)?
- **Answer:** 8X are Special Duty Identifiers within the career-field classification. 9X Reporting Identifiers identify authorizations and enlisted Airmen not identifiable in the classification structure and for whom specialty descriptions are not written.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.12
- **Tags:** TSgt; 10B; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-064
- **Prompt:** How do Cyber Warfare (1B), Cyber Defense Operations (1D), and Cyberspace Support (3D) differ as AFH 1 frames them?
- **Answer:** 1B develops/sustains/enhances cyberspace capabilities and creates effects for national objectives. 1D manages/performs Defensive Cyber Operations and DoDIN operations. 3D is Support-group cyberspace support (3D0 knowledge/systems/surety/programming; 3D1 client systems/transport/RF/spectrum/cable-antenna).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.12
- **Tags:** TSgt; 10B; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-065
- **Prompt:** How do Intelligence (1N) and Special Investigations (7S) differ in mission lane?

- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.12
- **Tags:** TSgt; 10B; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-067

## Section 10C — Special Duties

### C10-FC-069
- **Prompt:** Which ranks may serve developmental special duty positions, and why are those duties labeled developmental?
- **Answer:** Staff Sergeant through Master Sergeant. They are identified as developmental due to unique leadership roles and the Airman’s responsibility to mentor and mold future leaders.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10C; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-070
- **Prompt:** What nomination standard does AFH 1 set for developmental special duty positions?
- **Answer:** Commanders, through their MAJCOM, nominate their best Airmen; nominees must be the best in the USAF and ambassadors/role models for USAF core values and discipline, providing a developmental career path into critical positions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10C; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-071
- **Prompt:** To what do special duty identifiers apply?
- **Answer:** Authorizations for enlisted Airmen assigned to and performing an actual group of tasks on a semi-permanent or permanent duty basis.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10C; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-072
- **Prompt:** What does a Career Assistance Advisor (8A100) do?
- **Answer:** Serves as principal advisor to commanders and supervisors on force management and professional enhancement; assists career counseling; advises on career progression/planning; monitors mandatory pay and benefits briefings; conducts advertising/publicity; and briefs at informed decision seminars, professional enhancement courses, individual counseling, and FTACs.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10C; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-073
- **Prompt:** What is the propriety standard for Enlisted Aide (8A200) duties?
- **Answer:** Duties relate to supporting the General or Flag Officer’s military and official responsibilities, including DoD social responsibilities; propriety is governed by the official purpose served rather than the nature of the duties, and specifics rest on the officer’s needs tailored to household support.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10C; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-074
- **Prompt:** What is the primary purpose of Protocol (8A300)?
- **Answer:** Function as a protocol specialist providing expertise and support for all protocol matters—performing/managing/directing protocol at installation through HAF levels; DV support; escort; itineraries/ceremonies/special events; and advising leaders on acceptable protocol practices.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10C; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-075
- **Prompt:** How do MTI (8B000), MTL (8B100), and Academy Military Training NCO (8B200) differ?
- **Answer:** MTI conducts BMT for non-prior-service Airmen (including Air Reserve Forces) and initial military training for cadets. MTL supervises Airmen during technical training (including ANG/AFR liaisons) and evaluates bearing/discipline while scheduling military training. Academy MTNCO leads/mentors/develops/supervises USAFA cadets and advises cadet squadron leadership.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10C; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-076
- **Prompt:** What readiness services does the M&FRC Readiness NCO (8C000) develop and provide?
- **Answer:** Personal and family readiness services related to pre-deployment, deployment/sustainment, redeployment/reintegration, and post-deployment education and consultation to Total Force Airmen, Guardians, and their families—plus disaster response support plans—supporting the M&FRC mission for service members, DoD civilians, and families.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10C; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-077
- **Prompt:** What does a Language and Culture Advisor (8D100) do?
- **Answer:** Serves as key advisor and consultant to commanders and supervisors on foreign language and regional culture issues and functions as interpreter or translator as required.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10C; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-078
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 describe the First Sergeant (8F000) role?
- **Answer:** First sergeants serve as the commander’s advisor and critical link for matters concerning Airmen; they support the mission through interaction, support, and management of Airmen and families.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10C; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-079
- **Prompt:** How do USAF Honor Guard (8G000) and Installation Honor Guard Program Manager (8G100) differ?
- **Answer:** 8G000 covers the USAF Honor Guard at JB Anacostia-Bolling, D.C., representing the USAF at ceremonies requiring that degree of honor guard/escort. 8G100 covers the installation program manager at any USAF installation with a local honor guard requirement.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10C; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-080
- **Prompt:** What responsibilities define an Airmen Dorm Leader (8H000)?
- **Answer:** Full-time manager of USAF unaccompanied housing—daily UH operations; mentoring residents and adjustment to military life; compliance with directives/living standards; good order and discipline; facility/campus management; budget/program/project execution; BAH transactions; supplies and furnishings for quality facilities.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10C; B—Understanding

### C10-FC-081
- **Prompt:** How do IG Superintendent (8I000), Inspections Coordinator (8I100), and Complaints & Resolutions Superintendent (8I200) differ?
- **Answer:** 8I000 advises the IG on AFIS, Complaints Resolution, and CCIP and controls methods/procedures for IG policy. 8I100 coordinates inspection actions for AFIS—CCIP/Exercise & Evaluation at wing-level equivalents; UEIs/readiness assessments/on-site visits at MAJCOM. 8I200 advises the IG and commander on the Complaints Resolution Program and assists the IG.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10C; B—Understanding

Defense attachés manage DAO budget/fiscal data and information files; coordinate U.S. Naval ship visits and military aircraft overflight/landing clearances; coordinate office support with embassy officials; and perform admin/support per DIA standards.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶10.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 10C; B—Understanding
