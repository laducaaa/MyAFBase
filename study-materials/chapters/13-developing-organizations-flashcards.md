## Section 13A — Strategic Thinking and Results Focus

### C13-FC-001
- **Prompt:** What is organizational management, and what ability does it give leaders?
- **Answer:** It is organizing, planning, leading, and controlling resources within an entity to achieve established goals. It gives leaders the ability to make decisions and resolve issues effectively for the benefit of the organization and its employees.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13A; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-002
- **Prompt:** Why is strategic thinking important for enlisted members at unit or work-center level?
- **Answer:** Although some may think it is above their pay grade, strategic thinking matters when establishing goals and planning how to achieve them in any unit or work center, and it can be applied at any level across an enterprise or on a specific project.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13A; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-003
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define Strategic Thinking?
- **Answer:** Thinking on both a large and small scale, long- and short-term, to identify and achieve desired goals.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13A; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-004
- **Prompt:** What scenario and stakeholder behaviors did retired USAF general officers associate with Airmen who excelled at Strategic Thinking?
- **Answer:** Identifying best- and worst-case scenarios for how a situation might be resolved, and considering how other stakeholders would be affected by proposed courses of action.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13A; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-005
- **Prompt:** What senior-perspective and time-horizon behaviors marked excellent Strategic Thinking according to retired USAF general officers?
- **Answer:** Viewing issues from more senior leadership’s perspective (“work your boss’s problems”), identifying a realistic time horizon for goals, and building on small successes (“Thought BIG. Started small. Scaled fast.”).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13A; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-006
- **Prompt:** What does strategy set, and how does it align Path and Target?
- **Answer:** Strategy sets priorities, goals, objectives, and key performance indicators (metrics/measures) aligned from the lowest echelon to the highest so all Airmen march down the same Path toward the same Target.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13A; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-007
- **Prompt:** When current performance is not reaching the target, which continuous improvement methods does AFH 1 cite?
- **Answer:** Lean, Six Sigma, or Theory of Constraints, with reference to DoDI 5010.42 and DoDI 5010.43 for DoD-wide CPI/LSS program guidance.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13A; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-008
- **Prompt:** What are the three fundamental components of a strategic thinking mindset?
- **Answer:** Intellectual Flexibility, Intellectual Inclusiveness, and Intellectual Humility.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13A; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-009
- **Prompt:** What is Intellectual Flexibility in the strategic thinking mindset?
- **Answer:** A willingness and proclivity to adjust one’s understanding, opinions, or approach when conditions change or new information is presented; flexibility bends without abandoning long-term strategy.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13A; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-010
- **Prompt:** In the denied-training example, what does Intellectual Flexibility look like?
- **Answer:** Rather than ignoring the need or only resubmitting the original request, consider alternatives such as sending one Airman who then teaches the unit, or using nearby units whose members already have the training.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13A; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-011
- **Prompt:** What is Intellectual Inclusiveness, and how should Airmen practice it?
- **Answer:** Welcoming information and opinion from a broad range of sources. Look beyond the immediate organization; use formal and informal networks; reach peers from past assignments, other Services, or former instructors/classmates with relevant expertise.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13A; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-012
- **Prompt:** What is Intellectual Humility in the strategic thinking mindset?
- **Answer:** Comfort with being wrong or having an incomplete understanding, accompanied by checking oneself and examining issues as if one’s understanding is wrong in some way.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13A; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-013
- **Prompt:** What four main activities constitute strategic thinking in practice?
- **Answer:** Scanning, questioning, conceptualizing, and testing.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13A; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-014
- **Prompt:** What is scanning, and what example techniques does AFH 1 give?
- **Answer:** Identification of emerging patterns in the environment by taking in, deconstructing, and synthesizing information from different sources to apply it to the future. Examples include seeking expert-panel input and analyzing historical data to identify trends.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13A; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-015
- **Prompt:** What does questioning accomplish in strategic thinking?
- **Answer:** It more fully understands an issue from different perspectives by framing issues broadly, exploring problems rather than aiming to solve them immediately, and considering input from all stakeholders—asking questions of others and oneself.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13A; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-016
- **Prompt:** What does conceptualizing involve when identifying future direction?
- **Answer:** Identifying a broad range of options (brainstorming), using analytic tools and techniques to explore potential solutions, and rejecting options only after exploration.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13A; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-017
- **Prompt:** How does testing support informed speculation about a proposed action?
- **Answer:** It anticipates impact on organizational performance—for example by role-playing stakeholder communication and likely responses—and pilot-tests the proposed solution on a small scale to evaluate initial impact and address early problems.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13A; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-018
- **Prompt:** What does SMART stand for in goal setting?
- **Answer:** Specific, Measurable, Attainable, Relevant, and Time-bound.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13A; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-019
- **Prompt:** What should Specific objectives/targets answer?
- **Answer:** Who is involved; what is to be accomplished; where; when; which requirements and constraints exist; and why (purpose) the objective is being accomplished.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13A; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-020
- **Prompt:** What does Measurable require for goals?
- **Answer:** Criteria for measuring progress toward and attainment of each objective/target/milestone until the desired objective is met.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13A; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-021
- **Prompt:** What does Attainable require, and what does it not imply?
- **Answer:** Ensure applicable resources are available and objectives/tasks are possible within acceptable risk; action-oriented statements help. It does not imply goals should be easy—difficult or challenging goals improve performance more than easy or only moderately difficult goals.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13A; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-022
- **Prompt:** What makes a goal Relevant and Time-bound?
- **Answer:** Relevant goals link to mission, vision, and goals and are meaningful to the user—obtainable yet purposeful. Time-bound goals provide a completion date for periodic and overall accountability.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13A; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-023
- **Prompt:** How do ego-centric goals differ from group-centric goals in interdependent work?
- **Answer:** Ego-centric goals maximize individual results and may backfire when collaboration is needed. Group-centric goals focus on how individuals contribute to the group and are better when work is interdependent.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13A; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-024
- **Prompt:** Why may individual help-desk ticket quotas harm team performance?
- **Answer:** Egocentric goals for each employee to resolve a certain number of tickets may disincentivize training and coaching new teammates, while group-centric unit goals incentivize both individual processing and peer support.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13A; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-025
- **Prompt:** How does a strategic thinking mindset connect continuous improvement to Path and Target?
- **Answer:** When current performance is not reaching the target level, continuous improvement methods such as Lean, Six Sigma, or Theory of Constraints may be used to address identified issues so Airmen stay aligned on the same Path toward the same Target.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13A; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-026
- **Prompt:** Why should brainstorming reject options only after exploration?
- **Answer:** Conceptualizing requires identifying a broad range of options and using analytic tools to explore potential solutions; options should be rejected only after that exploration.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13A; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-027
- **Prompt:** How do Specific and Measurable elements work together in SMART goals?
- **Answer:** Specific objectives answer who, what, where, when, constraints, and purpose; Measurable criteria then track progress and attainment of each objective, target, or milestone until the desired objective is met.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13A; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-028
- **Prompt:** What organizational-management cycle connects resources to established goals?
- **Answer:** Organizing, planning, leading, and controlling resources within an entity with the overall aim of achieving established goals.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13A; B—Understanding


## Section 13B — Resources and Organizational Structure

### C13-FC-029
- **Prompt:** What five interrelated fronts must planning and execution generally redesign or coordinate for effective organizational change?
- **Answer:** Organization and People; Technology; Policies, Legislation, and Regulations; Physical Infrastructure; and Process.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13B; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-030
- **Prompt:** Why remain focused on Organization and People even when processes change?
- **Answer:** Human resources are key to future viability and organizational growth in a continuous learning environment; focus should remain on providing workers appropriate knowledge, skills, experiences, and tools.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13B; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-031
- **Prompt:** What enabling effects does Technology provide as a redesign front?
- **Answer:** It allows compression of cycles, lead time, and distance; broader access to information and knowledge assets; and elimination of barriers between customers and suppliers.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13B; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-032
- **Prompt:** Why might Policies, Legislation, and Regulations need change during redesign?
- **Answer:** Changing existing policies, regulations, and legislation may be required for new processes.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13B; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-033
- **Prompt:** What should Physical Infrastructure be designed to support?
- **Answer:** Physical facilities, equipment, and tools should support and maximize changes in workflow, information technology, and human resources.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13B; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-034
- **Prompt:** How must Process be redesigned according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** The flow of work and information into and throughout the organization must be redesigned using standard continuous process improvement methodologies, with reference to DoDD 5010.42 and DoDI 5010.43.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13B; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-035
- **Prompt:** What is DOTMLPF, and what does the process consider?
- **Answer:** A DoD thought process/mnemonic—Doctrine, Organization, Training, Materiel, Leadership and Education, Personnel, and Facilities—that considers solutions involving any combination of these elements for problems, future requirements, strategic direction, and performance improvement.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13B; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-036
- **Prompt:** Why is DOTMLPF valuable for staff planners and combatant commanders?
- **Answer:** It is a mnemonic to consider certain issues before new efforts. Combatant commanders, defining requirements with OSD, can consider gaps in strategic direction for the U.S. Armed Forces and influence requirements earlier in acquisition.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13B; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-037
- **Prompt:** In the military context, what do Doctrine, Organization, and Training mean in DOTMLPF?
- **Answer:** Doctrine is the way we fight; Organization is how we organize the fight; Training is how we prepare to fight tactically (BMT, advanced individual training, unit training, joint exercises).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13B; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-038
- **Prompt:** In DOTMLPF, what do Materiel, Leadership and Education, Personnel, and Facilities cover?
- **Answer:** Materiel is the stuff needed to equip forces (weapons, spares); Leadership and Education prepares leaders from squad leaders to four stars; Personnel is availability of qualified people for peacetime, wartime, and contingencies; Facilities are real property, installations, and industrial facilities that support forces.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13B; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-039
- **Prompt:** What factors should determine organizational design, and what two needs must be balanced?
- **Answer:** Design should follow overall mission and strategy, considering environment, size, and pace of technology, while balancing precision (order, reduced ambiguity, followed direction) with flexibility (quick adaptation and innovation).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13B; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-040
- **Prompt:** How is a mechanistic (bureaucratic) structure arranged, and when does it fit?
- **Answer:** It is vertically arranged with top-down communication and decision-making, little low-level decision involvement, specialty-based teams, and heavy reliance on rules. It fits efficiency-focused strategies, quick accurate tasks, stable environments, and often intermediate-to-large organizations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13B; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-041
- **Prompt:** What is a key disadvantage of a mechanistic structure?
- **Answer:** Job satisfaction may suffer because subordinates basically do as they are told with little to no contribution to how things are done.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13B; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-042
- **Prompt:** How does an organic structure communicate and decide?
- **Answer:** It has horizontal and vertical communication across departments and among co-workers without bureaucratic lines; cross-talk and opinions are encouraged for more involvement in decision-making and how work is done.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13B; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-043
- **Prompt:** What strategy, environment, and size typically suit an organic structure?
- **Answer:** Strategy geared toward innovativeness and creativity; unstable environment where change is the norm; most often small to moderate size, adapting technology rather than complying, with creative R&D.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13B; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-044
- **Prompt:** What disadvantages may accompany an organic structure?
- **Answer:** It may slow implementation, lead to low efficiency, and reduce standards—even though information sharing and participation can increase satisfaction and produce well-rounded decisions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13B; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-045
- **Prompt:** When is a diverse organizational design used?
- **Answer:** When the organization needs mechanistic rigidity in some areas and organic flexibility in others—for example, mechanistic admin processing of reports/decorations/orders and organic training delivery innovation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13B; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-046
- **Prompt:** What is a matrix design, and when is it usually best?
- **Answer:** An organizational design or team within a mechanistic, organic, or diverse organization, usually best for a temporary need; short-lived while the overall structure remains intact, pooling workers from different sections for a particular function.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13B; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-047
- **Prompt:** Who are the two bosses in a matrix design, and what are the design’s strength and weakness?
- **Answer:** The functional boss writes the performance report and schedules normal duty hours; the project boss/team leader ensures the task is accomplished. Strength is pooling expertise and resources; weakness is confusion about who is in charge.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13B; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-048
- **Prompt:** How do the five redesign fronts relate to continuous process improvement?
- **Answer:** Process—the flow of work and information—must be redesigned using standard CPI methodologies, while Organization and People, Technology, Policies/Legislation/Regulations, and Physical Infrastructure are coordinated as interrelated fronts.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13B; B—Understanding


## Section 13C — Change and Problem Solving

### C13-FC-049
- **Prompt:** When is change appropriate, and what is organizational change?
- **Answer:** Change is appropriate when there is a perceived gap between what norms are and what they should be. Organizational change is the deliberate adoption of a new idea or behavior and establishment of new norms involving technology, tasks, structure, and resources including people.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-050
- **Prompt:** What three leadership actions does AFH 1 describe for creating and implementing change?
- **Answer:** Create a climate conducive to change by explaining present shortfalls and proposed benefits; facilitate change by walking Airmen through details and answering questions; show appreciation for contributors and help refocus those who do not contribute.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-051
- **Prompt:** What mindset and education path should Airmen pursue regarding change and CPI?
- **Answer:** Critical thinking and problem solving as a first duty; as Airmen mature, basic, fundamental, intermediate, and Master-level education for change management and continuous process improvement. Tough-minded, realistic optimism is the best quality when coping with change.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-052
- **Prompt:** Who is the change agent, and how does planning help overcome resistance?
- **Answer:** The change agent advocates for or leads organizational change. A well thought-out plan builds confidence, anticipates questions, develops courses of action, and addresses opposing perceptions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-053
- **Prompt:** How do Distrust and Uncertainty differ as responses to change?
- **Answer:** Distrust involves tough questions about purpose/intent and doubt when leaders cannot clarify. Uncertainty involves fear of the unknown or threats to stability/job security and questions about retaining or doing the new job or learning new processes.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-054
- **Prompt:** What are Self-interest, Different Perception/No Felt Need, and Over-Determination as responses to change?
- **Answer:** Self-interest questions post-change power and one’s role; Different Perception means people may not feel the need, especially if change is dropped on them; Over-Determination means rigid structure (for example mechanistic) may inhibit change and damage relationships.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-055
- **Prompt:** How do Education and Communication and Participation and Involvement reduce resistance?
- **Answer:** Education/Communication uses open communication and education about need and expected results to reduce uncertainty. Participation/Involvement actively involves those affected in designing and implementing change to generate ownership and commitment.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-056
- **Prompt:** How do Facilitation and Support and Negotiation and Agreement help implement change?
- **Answer:** Facilitation/Support introduces change gradually, provides training if needed, and reinforces high expectations. Negotiation/Agreement may offer incentives to continuing resistors and use negotiated agreements to focus employees on agreed changes.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-057
- **Prompt:** Why is Coercion not recommended for change acceptance?
- **Answer:** It is a forcing technique that can negatively affect attitudes and cause long-term negative consequences; coerced compliance requires constant leadership oversight to keep the change in effect.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-058
- **Prompt:** What three stages did Kurt Lewin recommend for the change process?
- **Answer:** Unfreezing, changing, and refreezing. Leaders analyze restraining forces to reduce them and strengthen supporting forces pushing toward change.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-059
- **Prompt:** What is Unfreezing, and what is its most neglected essential part?
- **Answer:** A deliberate management activity preparing people for change by knowing and going where issues may exist. The most neglected yet essential part is creating an environment where people feel the need for change by making them knowledgeable about importance and job/structure effects.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-060
- **Prompt:** What happens during the Changing stage?
- **Answer:** Technology, tasks, structure, or distribution of people are modified; the organization moves from old norms to the new state. The change agent is essential; monitor affected people, respond to feedback, and readdress unfreezing rather than force a harmful change.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-061
- **Prompt:** What is Refreezing, and why is positive reinforcement crucial?
- **Answer:** Locking in desired outcomes and new norms so they become permanent; evaluate results and provide support/training as needed. Rewarding alignment emphasizes new behaviors’ value and helps freeze them; highlighting success reduces return to old ways.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-062
- **Prompt:** What is Continuous Improvement, and what three concepts does its philosophy center on?
- **Answer:** CI increases operational capabilities while reducing associated costs by applying proven methodologies to mission processes. It centers on always finding ways to improve for mission/customer requirements, constantly improving based on metrics aligned to strategic objectives, and replicating efficiencies to the extent practical.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-063
- **Prompt:** Where does AFH 1 place CI among commander, inspection, and standards responsibilities?
- **Answer:** CI is an Airmen Leadership Quality, a commander’s responsibility (AFI 1-2), a major graded area in the Air Force Inspection System (AFI 90-201), and a focal point within Air Force Standards under “Excellence in all we do” (AFI 1-1).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-064
- **Prompt:** How do Lean and Six Sigma differ as CI methodologies?
- **Answer:** Lean focuses on workflow, customer value, and eliminating process waste—especially non-value-added activities. Six Sigma is a rigorous, data-driven methodology focused on minimizing waste by identifying, controlling, and reducing process variation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-065
- **Prompt:** What are Business Process Reengineering and Theory of Constraints?
- **Answer:** BPR comprehensively changes the fundamental way processes are performed, eliminating unnecessary activities and empowering innovative redesign. Theory of Constraints systematically optimizes resource utilization by identifying, exploiting, subordinating, elevating, and reassessing bottlenecks.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-066
- **Prompt:** What is the practical problem-solving method, and what does it accomplish?
- **Answer:** An eight-step standardized, structured approach at the core of USAF CI, used by global industry leaders and adopted by DoD, to clarify problems, identify root causes, and develop appropriate countermeasures to achieve change.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-067
- **Prompt:** What must Step 1 of PPSM do, and what must a problem statement not assume?
- **Answer:** Clarify and validate the problem with a data-based problem statement covering where it occurs, impact, and performance versus a standard with scope and direction. It must not assume a root cause, solution, or countermeasure; pause if needed data does not exist.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-068
- **Prompt:** What do Steps 2 and 3 of PPSM accomplish?
- **Answer:** Step 2 breaks down the problem and identifies the performance gap between current state and expected end state. Step 3 sets challenging but achievable improvement targets based on expected outcomes and strategic goals/objectives toward the ideal state.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-069
- **Prompt:** What guidance does Step 4 give for determining root cause?
- **Answer:** Avoid repeatedly solving symptoms; trade off digging as deeply as possible versus the deepest point within the team’s sphere of influence; validate the root cause with the same data used in Step 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-070
- **Prompt:** What should Step 5 countermeasures achieve, and what formula describes solution impact?
- **Answer:** Practical, efficient, effective countermeasures that close gaps, move toward the ideal state, and are sustainable and repeatable, with a strategic vector check. Impact is a combination of solution quality and acceptance by people who implement it.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-071
- **Prompt:** What do Steps 6 and 7 require after countermeasures are developed?
- **Answer:** Step 6 sees countermeasures through with detailed implementation plans and regular progress reviews. Step 7 confirms results against gaps, targets, and expected outcome; incorrect root-cause determination is the most common CI mistake—if targets are unmet, return to Step 4.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-072
- **Prompt:** Why is Step 8 important, and what three questions should be answered?
- **Answer:** It is the most commonly neglected step and codifies results: What is needed to standardize improvements? How should improvements and lessons learned be communicated? Were other opportunities or problems identified?
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-073
- **Prompt:** What are the three levels of Practical Problem Solving Method effort?
- **Answer:** Just Do It (point improvement: one person/small team, less than a day); Rapid Improvement Event (small SME team, less than a week); Improvement Project (large team, longer period).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-074
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define project management, and how does a project differ from a program?
- **Answer:** Leading, coordinating, planning, and controlling a diverse and complex set of processes and people to achieve an established objective. Programs are ongoing; a project is temporary with an established objective and generalized time frame.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-075
- **Prompt:** What are the six project management steps in AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Define the Project Objective; Develop Solution Options; Plan the Project; Execute the Plan; Monitor and Control Progress; Close the Project (document lessons learned).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-076
- **Prompt:** What are the main project management constraints, and what is a driving constraint?
- **Answer:** Quality (accordance with specifications), Time (time available), and Cost (resources including money, manpower, machinery, materials). One constraint will drive the project and impact the other two.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-077
- **Prompt:** What does planning a project answer, and which techniques are especially important?
- **Answer:** Who, what, when, where, and how. Especially important: gathering important information, creating a work breakdown structure, and conducting a task analysis.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-078
- **Prompt:** What is a work breakdown structure, and why use it?
- **Answer:** A technique dividing a project into sub-units or work packages, typically with two or three levels of detail (more if complex), so all required elements are identified and essential steps are less likely to be overlooked.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-079
- **Prompt:** What information does task analysis add beyond a work breakdown structure?
- **Answer:** Task milestones, how milestones can be measured, and resources or requirements. It shows how tasks interrelate and supports proper sequencing before the project begins.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 13C; B—Understanding

### C13-FC-080
- **Prompt:** What Osborn-Parnes aspects does AFH 1 link to PPSM Steps 1 and 5?
- **Answer:** Step 1 incorporates Mess Finding, Data Finding, and Problem Finding. Step 5 incorporates Idea Finding, Solution Finding, and Acceptance Finding; Developing Ideas techniques can be particularly useful when developing countermeasures.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶13.13
