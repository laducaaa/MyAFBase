## Section 18A — Integrated Defense

### C18-FC-001
- **Prompt:** What does AFH 1 say is the purpose of security?
- **Answer:** To never permit the enemy to acquire an unexpected advantage.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18A; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-002
- **Prompt:** Why does AFH 1 say security of friendly forces is a paramount concern?
- **Answer:** Because of the lethal consequences of enemy attack.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18A; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-003
- **Prompt:** To whom does security apply according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** All members of the USAF at all times, given the multiplicity of security with regard to the USAF mission and the protection of all resources.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18A; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-004
- **Prompt:** How do planners create an effective security program beyond integrated defense alone?
- **Answer:** By coordinating with other Department of Defense and Department of the USAF programs; air base protection and defense also require coordinated emergency management, antiterrorism, and other mission support forces under the mission assurance umbrella.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18A; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-005
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define the USAF Integrated Defense Program?
- **Answer:** The integration of multidisciplinary active and passive, offensive, and defensive capabilities employed to mitigate potential risks and defeat adversary threats to USAF operations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18A; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-006
- **Prompt:** What is the goal of integrated defense?
- **Answer:** To neutralize security threats within the base boundary and the base security zone to ensure unhindered USAF operations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18A; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-007
- **Prompt:** What threat actors does AFH 1 list for integrated defense (non-exhaustive)?
- **Answer:** Terrorists, insiders, criminals, foreign intelligence, and security services.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18A; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-008
- **Prompt:** What does AFH 1 mean by saying integrated defense is an “All Airmen” program?
- **Answer:** Teaming creates a united, seamless defense stronger than individual or unit efforts alone, ensuring all Airmen are trained to defend themselves and integrate into defense operations in garrison or deployed.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18A; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-009
- **Prompt:** What is the base perimeter?
- **Answer:** The physical boundary of the installation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18A; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-010
- **Prompt:** How does JP 3-10 (as cited in AFH 1) define the base boundary?
- **Answer:** A line that delineates the surface area of a base for facilitating coordination and deconfliction of operations between adjacent units, formations, or areas.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18A; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-011
- **Prompt:** Must the base boundary coincide with the fenced perimeter, property lines, or legal boundaries?
- **Answer:** No. Boundaries may not necessarily coincide with the fenced perimeter, property lines, or legal boundaries.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18A; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-012
- **Prompt:** What is the base security zone, and what standoff threats does AFH 1 name?
- **Answer:** A USAF unique intra-service planning term for the area of concern around an air base outside the base perimeter from which the base may be vulnerable from standoff weapons (mortars, rockets, and man portable air defense systems) and waterborne threats.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18A; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-013
- **Prompt:** If the base boundary does not include all terrain of the base security zone, what is the installation commander responsible for?
- **Answer:** Either mitigating (through coordination with local, state, federal agencies, or the host nation or area commander) or accepting the risks of enemy attack from terrain outside the base boundary.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18A; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-014
- **Prompt:** List the nine integrated defense desired effects named in AFH 1.
- **Answer:** Anticipate, Deter, Detect, Assess, Warn, Defeat, Delay, Defend, and Recover.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18A; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-015
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 describe Delay as an integrated defense effect?
- **Answer:** Delay adversaries using a layered application of barriers, obstacles, technology, physical security measures, and forces—also described as applying defense-in-depth procedures.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18A; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-016
- **Prompt:** What recovery tool does AFH 1 name under the Recover effect?
- **Answer:** Developing and exercising Comprehensive Emergency Management Plan 10-2, with effective command and control.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18A; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-017
- **Prompt:** In AFH 1’s application example, what preferred progression follows a failed deter attempt?
- **Answer:** Detect the threat; assess; warn friendly forces; attempt to defeat or eliminate; if not defeated, delay; if delay is not possible, take defensive measures to mitigate effects; then implement recovery to consolidate, reorganize, and restore operations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18A; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-018
- **Prompt:** What is the base defense operations center?
- **Answer:** The command and control center for integrated defense operations during routine and emergency operations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18A; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-019
- **Prompt:** Who establishes the BDOC, and what operates under its control?
- **Answer:** The defense force commander establishes it to coordinate and direct, via the operational chain of command, tactical control of integrated defense forces and supporting capabilities. Central security control, the law enforcement desk, and other security forces control centers operate under BDOC control at home station and deployed.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18A; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-020
- **Prompt:** What does the integrated defense risk management process enable leaders to produce?
- **Answer:** Effects-based, integrated defense plans using a standardized model to identify risks and develop risk management strategies that leverage finite resources against adaptive threats.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18A; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-021
- **Prompt:** What assessment products does the integrated defense risk management process help generate?
- **Answer:** Criticality assessment and risk assessment products, developed through a logical process involving asset criticality, threat, and vulnerability assessments.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18A; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-022
- **Prompt:** What consequence standard defines Protection Level 1, and what deterrence intent does AFH 1 assign?
- **Answer:** Loss, theft, destruction, damage, misuse, or compromise would result in unacceptable mission degradation to the strategic capability of the United States or catastrophic consequences for the nation. PL1 security must result in the greatest possible deterrence against hostile acts and provide maximum means to detect and defeat a hostile force before it can seize, damage, or destroy resources.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18A; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-023
- **Prompt:** What PL2 examples does AFH 1 list?
- **Answer:** Nonnuclear alert forces; designated space and launch systems; expensive, few in number, or one-of-a-kind systems or facilities; and intelligence-gathering systems.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18A; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-024
- **Prompt:** How are PL4 resources secured according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** By containing them in controlled areas with owners or users responsible for security; security forces provide response to threats. This level must reduce the opportunity for theft of or damage to resources. Examples include facilities storing Category I, II, or III sensitive conventional arms, ammunition, and explosives; fuels and liquid oxygen storage areas; and USAF accounting and finance vault areas.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18A; B—Understanding

## Section 18B — Operations Security

### C18-FC-025
- **Prompt:** What is the purpose of operations security?
- **Answer:** To reduce the vulnerability of USAF missions by eliminating or reducing successful adversary collection and exploitation of critical information.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18B; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-026
- **Prompt:** What cycle does OPSEC use, and to what activities does it apply?
- **Answer:** A cycle to identify, analyze, and control critical information that applies to all activities used to prepare, sustain, or employ forces during all phases of operations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18B; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-027
- **Prompt:** What do OPSEC program managers and signature managers do with the signature management methodology?
- **Answer:** Apply measures or countermeasures to hide, control, or simulate indicators; signature managers also recommend modifying day-to-day activities to create variations in the status quo.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18B; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-028
- **Prompt:** What three attentiveness actions does AFH 1 list for operations security?
- **Answer:** Identify actions observable by adversary intelligence systems; determine what indications could be collected, analyzed, and interpreted to derive critical information in time to be useful; select and execute measures that eliminate or reduce vulnerabilities to an acceptable level.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18B; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-029
- **Prompt:** When is operational effectiveness enhanced regarding OPSEC?
- **Answer:** When commanders and other decision-makers apply operations security from the earliest stages of planning.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18B; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-030
- **Prompt:** List the five distinct actions of the operations security cycle.
- **Answer:** Identify critical information; analyze threats; analyze vulnerabilities; assess risk; apply appropriate operations security countermeasures.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18B; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-031
- **Prompt:** Into what planning and activities must OPSEC principles be integrated?
- **Answer:** Operational, support, exercise, acquisition planning, and day-to-day activities to ensure a seamless transition to contingency operations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18B; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-032
- **Prompt:** What are operations security indicators?
- **Answer:** Friendly, detectable actions and open-source information that can be interpreted or pieced together by an adversary to derive critical information.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18B; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-033
- **Prompt:** What are signatures in OPSEC terms?
- **Answer:** Observable activities and operational trends that reveal critical information to adversary intelligence collection.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18B; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-034
- **Prompt:** What is signature management?
- **Answer:** A systematic approach to identify, prioritize, and manage physical, technical, and administrative indicators of friendly forces’ operational profiles that, if ignored, will be exploited by an adversary to achieve an operational advantage.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18B; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-035
- **Prompt:** What is an association as an OPSEC indicator characteristic?
- **Answer:** The relationship of an indicator to other information or activities; the characteristics of the relationship make it identifiable or cause it to stand out.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18B; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-036
- **Prompt:** What is a profile in OPSEC terms?
- **Answer:** The sum of an activity’s more-or-less unique signatures and associations; profiling maps the local operating environment and captures process points with critical information value.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18B; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-037
- **Prompt:** What is a contrast as an OPSEC indicator characteristic?
- **Answer:** Any difference observed between an activity’s standard profile and most recent or current actions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18B; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-038
- **Prompt:** What is exposure as an OPSEC indicator characteristic?
- **Answer:** When and for how long an indicator is observed; the longer it is observed, the better chance an adversary can form associations and update the profile of operational activities.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18B; B—Understanding

## Section 18C — Information Protection

### C18-FC-039
- **Prompt:** What is information protection, and which three core security disciplines does it consist of?
- **Answer:** A subset of the USAF security enterprise consisting of personnel, industrial, and information security used to determine eligibility for access to classified information, protect classified information released to industry under classified contracts, and protect classified information and CUI from unauthorized disclosure that could reasonably be expected to cause damage to national security.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18C; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-040
- **Prompt:** Who is responsible for protecting classified information and CUI under their custody and control?
- **Answer:** All personnel in the USAF.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18C; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-041
- **Prompt:** What are the three levels of classification named in AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Top Secret, Secret, and Confidential.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18C; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-042
- **Prompt:** What unauthorized-disclosure damage standard defines Top Secret?
- **Answer:** Exceptionally grave damage to the national security that the original classification authority is able to identify or describe.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18C; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-043
- **Prompt:** What unauthorized-disclosure damage standard defines Secret?
- **Answer:** Serious damage to the national security that the original classification authority is able to identify or describe.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18C; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-044
- **Prompt:** What unauthorized-disclosure damage standard defines Confidential?
- **Answer:** Damage to the national security that the original classification authority is able to identify or describe.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18C; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-045
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI)?
- **Answer:** Information the government creates or possesses, or that an entity creates or possesses for or on behalf of the government, that a law, regulation, or Government-wide policy requires or permits an agency to handle using safeguarding or dissemination controls.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18C; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-046
- **Prompt:** What is original classification, and who may make those decisions?
- **Answer:** The initial decision by an original classification authority that an item of information could reasonably be expected to cause identifiable or describable damage to the national security if subjected to unauthorized disclosure and requires protection in the interest of national security. Only officials designated in writing may make original classification decisions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18C; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-047
- **Prompt:** What two training conditions does AFH 1 state for DoD cleared personnel authorized to derivatively classify information?
- **Answer:** They have received initial training before making derivative classification decisions, and they have received refresher training every year.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18C; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-048
- **Prompt:** What is derivative classification?
- **Answer:** Incorporating, paraphrasing, restating, or generating classified information in a new form or document.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18C; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-049
- **Prompt:** What is one of the most important responsibilities of the derivative classifier?
- **Answer:** To observe and respect the classification determinations made by an original classification authority.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18C; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-050
- **Prompt:** What purposes does marking classified information serve according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Alert holders to classified information; identify information needing protection; indicate classification level; provide downgrading/declassification guidance; give sources/reasons for classification; notify of special access/control/safeguarding requirements; and promote information sharing, judicious resource use, and simplified management through uniform processes.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18C; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-051
- **Prompt:** What three markings does AFH 1 call the most common on a classified document?
- **Answer:** Banner lines, portion markings, and the classification authority block.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18C; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-052
- **Prompt:** What three prerequisites must a person have before being granted access to classified information?
- **Answer:** (1) security clearance eligibility, (2) a signed SF 312, Classified Information Non-Disclosure Agreement, and (3) a need-to-know.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18C; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-053
- **Prompt:** Which cover sheets does AFH 1 name for Top Secret, Secret, and Confidential documents not in secure storage?
- **Answer:** SF 703 (Top Secret), SF 704 (Secret), and SF 705 (Confidential).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18C; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-054
- **Prompt:** What form records end-of-day security checks, and where is it required?
- **Answer:** SF 701, Activity Security Checklist, required for any area where classified information is used or stored.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18C; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-055
- **Prompt:** What must anyone finding classified material out of proper control do?
- **Answer:** Take custody of and safeguard the material and immediately notify their commander, supervisor, or security manager.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18C; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-056
- **Prompt:** What is a security infraction according to AFH 1’s brief description?
- **Answer:** A security incident involving failure to comply with requirements that cannot reasonably be expected to, and does not, result in the loss of classified records or in the suspected or actual compromise of classified information; requires an inquiry for immediate corrective action but not an in-depth investigation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18C; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-057
- **Prompt:** What is a compromise?
- **Answer:** A security incident (more specifically, a violation) in which there is an unauthorized disclosure of classified information to a person(s) who does not have a valid clearance, authorized access, or a need to know.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18C; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-058
- **Prompt:** When does a loss occur?
- **Answer:** When records containing classified information cannot be physically located or accounted for, including classified records/equipment discovered missing during an audit and which cannot be immediately located.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18C; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-059
- **Prompt:** What is a classified data spill, and how is it handled initially?
- **Answer:** Classified data introduced onto an unclassified system, a lower-classification system, or a system not accredited for that restrictive category; handled as a possible compromise involving information systems until inquiry determines whether unauthorized disclosure occurred.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18C; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-060
- **Prompt:** If classified information appears in public media, what must DoD personnel be careful not to do?
- **Answer:** Make any statement or comment that confirms the accuracy of or verifies the information requiring protection; report as instructed and do not discuss with anyone lacking appropriate clearance and need to know.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18C; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-061
- **Prompt:** What is USAF industrial security policy for classified contracts?
- **Answer:** Identify specific information and sensitive resources that must be protected against compromise or loss while entrusted to industry.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18C; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-062
- **Prompt:** What does the Personnel Security Program entail at a high level?
- **Answer:** Policies and procedures ensuring military, civilian, and contractor personnel who access classified information or occupy a sensitive position are consistent with interests of national security, including investigation, adjudication for eligibility, and continuous evaluation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18C; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-063
- **Prompt:** What must commanders and supervisors do regarding subordinates’ trustworthiness for sensitive positions or classified eligibility?
- **Answer:** Continually observe and evaluate subordinates and immediately report any unfavorable conduct or conditions that might bear on trustworthiness and eligibility.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18C; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-064
- **Prompt:** Who is the designated authority to grant, deny, and revoke security clearance eligibility, and what concept do they apply?
- **Answer:** The Department of Defense Central Adjudication Facility, using the 13 adjudicative guidelines while applying the whole person concept and mitigating factors.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18C; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-065
- **Prompt:** Name the 13 adjudicative guidelines listed in AFH 1.
- **Answer:** Allegiance to the United States; Foreign Influence; Foreign Preference; Sexual Behavior; Personal Conduct; Financial Considerations; Alcohol Consumption; Drug Involvement; Psychological Conditions; Criminal Conduct; Handling Protected Information; Outside Activities; Use of Information Technology.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18C; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-066
- **Prompt:** What adverse-inference cautions does AFH 1 note for Sexual Behavior and Psychological Conditions?
- **Answer:** No adverse inference concerning Sexual Behavior standards may be raised solely on the basis of sexual orientation; no negative inference concerning Psychological Conditions standards may be raised solely on the basis of seeking mental health counseling.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18C; B—Understanding

## Section 18D — Information Access, Cyber Security, and Mobility

### C18-FC-067
- **Prompt:** What does the Privacy Act of 1974 (as amended) establish according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** A code of fair information practices that govern the collection, maintenance, use, and dissemination of personal information about individuals maintained in a system of records by federal agencies; it also provides means to seek access and amendment and sets agency record-keeping requirements.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18D; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-068
- **Prompt:** Can Privacy Act rights be asserted derivatively by others?
- **Answer:** No. Privacy Act rights are personal to the individual who is the subject of the record and cannot be asserted derivatively by others (except a parent of a minor or legal guardian of an incompetent may act on behalf of that individual).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18D; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-069
- **Prompt:** What must be published when collecting information for a system of records, and what comment period does AFH 1 state?
- **Answer:** System of Records Notices (SORNs) must be published in the federal register allowing the public a 30-day comment period; a Privacy Act statement must be given when individuals are asked to provide personal information about themselves for collection in a system of records.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18D; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-070
- **Prompt:** What is a Privacy Act system of records?
- **Answer:** A group of any records under the control of any agency from which information is retrieved by the individual’s name, number, or unique identifier.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18D; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-071
- **Prompt:** How does OMB define a personally identifiable information breach as quoted in AFH 1?
- **Answer:** A loss of control, compromise, unauthorized disclosure, unauthorized acquisition, unauthorized access, or any similar term referring to situations where persons other than authorized users and for an other than authorized purpose have access or potential access to personally identifiable information, whether physical or electronic.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18D; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-072
- **Prompt:** What does the Freedom of Information Act provide?
- **Answer:** Access to federal agency records (or parts of these records) except those protected from release by specific exemptions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18D; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-073
- **Prompt:** What mandatory FOIA time limit and extension does AFH 1 state?
- **Answer:** 20 workdays to either deny the request or release the requested records, with an additional 10-workday extension if specific unusual circumstances exist. Denials require notification of appeal rights.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18D; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-074
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define cybersecurity?
- **Answer:** The prevention of damage to, protection of, and restoration of computers, electronic communications systems, wire communication, and electronic communication, including information contained therein, to ensure availability, integrity, authentication, confidentiality, and nonrepudiation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18D; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-075
- **Prompt:** What cybersecurity disciplines does AFH 1 list?
- **Answer:** Air Force Risk Management Framework, IT controls/countermeasures, communications security, TEMPEST (formerly emissions security), AF Assessment and Authorization (formerly Certification and Accreditation Program), and Cybersecurity Workforce Improvement Program.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18D; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-076
- **Prompt:** What must the USAF Cybersecurity Program risk management strategy ensure?
- **Answer:** That the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of all information owned or held in trust by the USAF is protected, and that the strategy is integrated into all key mission and business processes.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18D; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-077
- **Prompt:** What Information Assurance Awareness training requirement does AFH 1 state?
- **Answer:** All USAF personnel are required to complete Information Assurance Awareness training prior to system access and annually thereafter.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18D; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-078
- **Prompt:** List the five functions of the USAF Cybersecurity Program.
- **Answer:** Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and Recover.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18D; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-079
- **Prompt:** What does computer security consist of?
- **Answer:** Measures and controls that ensure confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information systems assets including hardware, software, firmware, and information being processed, stored, and communicated.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.18
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18D; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-080
- **Prompt:** What limits does AFH 1 place on limited authorized personal use of government hardware and software?
- **Answer:** Reasonable duration and frequency approved by the supervisor or office policy that does not adversely affect performance of official duties, overburden systems, or reflect adversely on the USAF or DoD; official use and limited authorized personal use only.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.18
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18D; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-081
- **Prompt:** When accessing internet-based capabilities with federal government resources in an authorized personal or unofficial capacity, what guidance must individuals comply with?
- **Answer:** Operations security guidance in AFI 10-701 and the requirements of the Joint Ethics Regulation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.18
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18D; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-082
- **Prompt:** What is an information system according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** A discrete set of information resources organized for the collection, processing, maintenance, use, sharing, dissemination, or disposition of information, also including specialized systems such as industrial/process controls, telephone switching and private branch systems, and environmental controls.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18D; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-083
- **Prompt:** What is a countermeasure?
- **Answer:** Any action, device, procedure, or technique that meets or opposes (counters) a threat, vulnerability, or attack by eliminating, preventing, or minimizing damage, or by discovering and reporting the event so corrective action can be taken.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18D; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-084
- **Prompt:** What three steps does AFH 1 name for protecting information systems from viruses and malicious logic?
- **Answer:** Infection, Detection, and Reaction—combining human and technological countermeasures throughout the lifecycle; reaction means immediately notifying the information system security officer and following local procedures.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18D; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-085
- **Prompt:** What approval rule applies to wireless systems before processing DoD information?
- **Answer:** All wireless systems (including associated peripheral devices, operating systems, applications, network connection methods, and services) must be approved prior to processing Department of Defense information; the ISSO maintains documented approval authority and inventory information on approved devices.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18D; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-086
- **Prompt:** May Airmen use hotel business centers to process government-owned unclassified, sensitive, or classified information?
- **Answer:** No. Do not use public computing facilities or services, such as hotel business centers, to process government-owned unclassified, sensitive, or classified information.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18D; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-087
- **Prompt:** What happens if e-mail applications, messaging software, or web applications are used on public computing to access web-based government services?
- **Answer:** It constitutes a compromise of login credentials and must be reported as a security incident.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18D; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-088
- **Prompt:** What is communications security?
- **Answer:** Measures and controls taken to deny unauthorized persons information derived from information systems of the U.S. Government related to national security and to ensure the authenticity of such information systems.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18D; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-089
- **Prompt:** Name the three COMSEC components AFH 1 describes.
- **Answer:** Cryptosecurity, Transmission Security, and Physical Security.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18D; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-090
- **Prompt:** What is TEMPEST, and what is its objective?
- **Answer:** Formerly known as emissions security, TEMPEST is protection from measures denying unauthorized persons information of value derived from interception and analysis of compromising emanations from cryptographic equipment, information systems, and telecommunications systems. The objective is to deny access to classified, and in some instances unclassified, information that contains compromising emanations within an inspectable space.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.23
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18D; B—Understanding

## Section 18E — Antiterrorism

### C18-FC-091
- **Prompt:** How does the USAF seek to deter or limit the effects of terrorist acts according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** By giving guidance on collecting and disseminating timely threat information, providing training to all USAF members, developing comprehensive plans to deter and counter terrorist incidents, allocating funds and personnel, and implementing antiterrorism measures.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.24
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18E; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-092
- **Prompt:** What lead-agency role does AFH 1 assign to AFOSI for antiterrorism-related threats?
- **Answer:** Lead USAF agency for collection, investigation, analysis, and response for threats arising from terrorists, criminal activity, foreign intelligence, and security services; primarily focused on countering adversary intelligence collection against U.S. Armed Forces; USAF single point of contact with federal, state, local, and foreign nation law enforcement, counterintelligence, and security agencies.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.24
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18E; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-093
- **Prompt:** What should commanders encourage personnel to report regarding terrorism threats?
- **Answer:** Information on individuals, events, or situations that could pose a threat to the security of DoD personnel, families, facilities, and resources.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.24
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18E; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-094
- **Prompt:** How often must commanders conduct comprehensive field and staff antiterrorism training, and what FPCON baseline-through-Charlie rule does AFH 1 state?
- **Answer:** At least annually; additionally, the current baseline through force protection condition ‘Charlie’ measures shall be exercised annually at installations and self-supported separate facilities.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.24
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18E; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-095
- **Prompt:** What is the intent of the Random Antiterrorism Measures Program?
- **Answer:** Provide random, multiple security measures that consistently change the look of an installation’s antiterrorism program, introducing uncertainty to defeat surveillance attempts and make actions difficult for a terrorist to accurately predict.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.24
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18E; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-096
- **Prompt:** According to AFH 1, when do criminal and terrorist acts against individuals usually occur, and what is the most predictable habit?
- **Answer:** Usually outside the home and after the victim’s habits have been established; the most predictable habit is the route traveled on a regular basis.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.25
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18E; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-097
- **Prompt:** What should you do if you detect something out of the ordinary during a vehicle check?
- **Answer:** Do not touch anything; immediately contact the local authorities.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.25
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18E; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-098
- **Prompt:** Within what period before overseas travel should you get the required area-of-responsibility threat briefing?
- **Answer:** Within three months prior to traveling overseas.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.26
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18E; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-099
- **Prompt:** What ticket and affiliation practices does AFH 1 advise for commercial air travel overseas?
- **Answer:** Do not use rank or military address on tickets; do not discuss military affiliation; use office symbols on travel documents if the word description denotes a sensitive position; use military contracted flag carriers; avoid high-risk areas; do not carry classified documents unless absolutely mission essential; dress conservatively; cover military/U.S.-affiliated tattoos; carry plain civilian luggage; do not wear or carry distinct military items.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.26
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18E; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-100
- **Prompt:** Name several suspicious package or mail indicators listed in AFH 1.
- **Answer:** Unusual/unknown origin; no return address; excessive postage; abnormal size/shape; protruding strings, foil, or wires; misspelled words; differing return address and postmark; handwritten labels; unusual odor; unusual/unbalanced weight; springiness; inflexibility; crease marks; discoloration/oily stains; incorrect titles or title with no name; excessive security material; ticking/beeping/sounds; special instruction markings such as “personal, rush, do not delay, or confidential”; powder or contaminants.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.27
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18E; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-101
- **Prompt:** What should you never do to wrappings on a suspect package, and what if the package has been moved?
- **Answer:** Never cut tape, strings, or other wrappings; if moved, place the package in a plastic bag to prevent leakage of contents; report immediately and list personnel who were in the room when it was identified.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.27
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18E; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-102
- **Prompt:** List several general antiterrorism personal protection actions AFH 1 provides.
- **Answer:** Dress/behave without drawing attention; be inconspicuous; travel in small groups; avoid spontaneous gatherings/demonstrations; be unpredictable; vary daily routines; be alert; avoid unnecessary personal details; refuse meetings with strangers outside the workplace; advise associates/family of destination and ETA; report unsolicited contacts; do not open doors to strangers; memorize key phone numbers; be cautious about family travel/security information; overseas, learn key local phrases.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.28
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18E; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-103
- **Prompt:** What home and family security actions does AFH 1 list regarding keys, locks, mail, and vehicles?
- **Answer:** Restrict house-key possession; lock all entrances at night including the garage; keep the house locked even if home; destroy items showing name/rank/personal information; remove names and rank from mailboxes; watch for unfamiliar vehicles cruising or parked frequently with occupants remaining for extended periods.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.29
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18E; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-104
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define human intelligence and counterintelligence?
- **Answer:** HUMINT is intelligence derived from information collected and provided by human sources and collectors where the human being is the primary collection instrument. Counterintelligence is information gathered and activities conducted to protect against such threats.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.30
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18E; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-105
- **Prompt:** What four primary HUMINT collection efforts does AFH 1 briefly describe?
- **Answer:** Interrogation, Source Operations, Debriefing, and Document and Media Exploitation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.30
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18E; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-106
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define espionage?
- **Answer:** The act of obtaining, delivering, transmitting, communicating, or receiving information about national defense with intent or reason to believe the information may be used to the injury of the United States or to the advantage of any foreign nation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.30
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18E; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-107
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define terrorism as a HUMINT threat area?
- **Answer:** The calculated use of unlawful violence or threat of unlawful violence to inculcate fear intended to coerce or intimidate governments or societies in the pursuit of goals that are generally political, religious, or ideological.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.30
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18E; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-108
- **Prompt:** Within what timeframe must reportable contacts or information be reported to AFOSI?
- **Answer:** Immediately (within 30 days of the contact), either verbally or in writing.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.31
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18E; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-109
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define contact for reporting purposes, with examples?
- **Answer:** Any exchange of information directed to an individual, including solicited or unsolicited telephone calls, e-mail, radio contact, and face-to-face meetings. Examples include contact with a foreign diplomatic establishment; requests for illegal/unauthorized access to classified or unclassified controlled information; personal contact suggesting foreign intelligence or terrorist targeting; or information indicating deliberate compromise or unauthorized release of classified or unclassified controlled information.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.31
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18E; B—Understanding

### C18-FC-110
- **Prompt:** What special reporting obligation do Armed Forces-affiliated individuals have regarding U.S. Secret Service protectees?
- **Answer:** Report information regarding the safety and protection of the U.S. President or anyone else under U.S. Secret Service protection, including the Vice President, President- and Vice President-elect, visiting heads of foreign states or governments, and in most cases former Presidents and their spouses (lifetime protection).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶18.32
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 18E; B—Understanding
