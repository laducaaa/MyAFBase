# Chapter 23 Supplemental Situational Judgment Practice

> **Status:** Draft and provisional for 27E5/27E6 preparation; the official 2027 format is unconfirmed.
>
> **Reserve-chapter status:** Chapter 23 was outside the historical 26E5 and 26E6 catalog scopes and remains reserve pending the 2027 WAPS catalogs. Prior exclusion does not prove future inclusion, weighting, or scenario testability.
>
> **Two-track source rule:** For exam-source study, preserve the facts in the AFH edition assigned by the controlling catalog. For duty use, separately verify current DAFI 36-2903, DAFI 52-201, local supplements, and commander-directed wear policy; duty-current information does not overwrite a catalog-controlled answer.
>
> These ten supplemental scenarios are wholly original. They are not actual, recalled, reconstructed, or predicted PFE items. They are supplemental SJT practice only, not a claim that dress-and-appearance content is scenario-tested.
>
> AFH 1 assigns Chapter 23 sections **23A and 23B as B—Understanding for both SSgt and TSgt**. Those designations apply to recognizing and explaining dress/appearance facts; they do not make these scenarios official behavioral items. Chapter 23 supplies context only and **does not establish behavioral scoring keys**. The most-effective and least-effective selections below are MyPromotion educational judgments based on Chapter 1 professionalism, core values, and ethical reasoning.
>
> The official Air Force uses subject-matter experts and behavioral scientists to establish operational SJT scoring keys. These supplemental judgments are not official keys or substitutes for current supervisory, command, legal, religious-accommodation, or dress-regulation procedures. Any process facts needed to rank an option are supplied within the scenario as invented instructional constraints.
>
> **Uniform-policy volatility:** Scenarios treat AFH 1 facts as a dated snapshot. Real corrections require current DAFI 36-2903 and the chain of command.

## How to use this set

Each scenario produces two selections:

1. Select the **most effective** response.
2. Select the **least effective** response.

Read every option before choosing. Judge the learner's present-day conduct. Consider whether the response:

- represents dress and appearance standards honestly;
- corrects or clarifies within authority without inventing rules;
- protects safety, good order, dignity, and mission;
- routes accommodation/ETP questions to the proper process;
- avoids public humiliation, favoritism, or willful noncompliance; and
- reflects integrity, respect, accountability, and disciplined stewardship.

Ten scenarios produce 20 response selections, mirroring only the historical public structure announced for the [22E5/22E6 cycles in December 2021](https://www.afpc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/2874484/waps-adjusts-for-22e6-22e5-testing-cycles/). The separate [public SJT informational guide](https://www.studyguides.af.mil/Portals/15/documents/Notices/Situational-Judgment-Test-Informational-Guide.pdf) explains the four-option most/least format. Neither source confirms the 2027 structure.

## C23-SJT-001 — Walking in uniform with food

You are walking between buildings in service dress with a junior Airman. The Airman starts eating a sandwich while walking and says, “Everyone does it between meetings.” AFH 1 states members do not consume food while walking in uniform, though beverage consumption is authorized and commanders may authorize food during special functions. Customs and courtesies take precedence.

### Response options

A. Join in eating while walking so the Airman does not feel singled out.  
B. Quietly correct the standard: food is not consumed while walking in uniform unless a commander has authorized it for a special function; suggest finishing before walking or waiting until seated.  
C. Publicly yell across the sidewalk that the Airman is a disgrace.  
D. Tell the Airman the rule never applies to NCOs.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 23.1 supplies the food-while-walking standard. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it corrects accurately and respectfully within ordinary supervisory influence. Chapter 1 integrity and respect provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A models willful noncompliance. Chapter 1 integrity and accountability provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** Normalizes a clear violation.
- **B — Most effective:** Accurate, calm correction tied to the stated rule.
- **C — Ineffective:** Public humiliation fails respect and good order.
- **D — Ineffective:** Invents an NCO exemption AFH 1 does not provide.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶23.1 (food/walking context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Professional image; standards enforcement; respect  
**ADTC:** 23A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of corrective-counseling tone; local commander special-function authorizations control exceptions

### Response options

C. Post the tattoo on social media to “warn the squadron.”  
D. Ignore it because tattoos are “personal business only.”

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 23.3 supplies unauthorized-content and no-cover-up rules. B is most effective because it refuses a false compliance path and points the member to lawful correction. Chapter 1 integrity and courage provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A assists concealment of unauthorized content. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** Actively assists noncompliance.
- **B — Most effective:** Honest standard + proper next-step posture.
- **C — Ineffective:** Public shaming/risk without fixing the compliance duty.
- **D — Ineffective:** Unauthorized content is not merely private under AFH 1.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶23.3 (tattoo content/cover-up context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Tattoo policy; integrity; accountability  
**ADTC:** 23A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Sensitive; real cases need command/legal/medical processes under current DAFI 36-2903

## C23-SJT-003 — OCP off-base restaurant choice

Your team plans a quick lunch off base in OCP. Someone suggests a steakhouse where most diners wear business attire, then a second option—a casual restaurant where people wear comparable civilian attire. AFH 1 allows OCP for short convenience stops and comparable-attire restaurants, but not business-attire restaurants or alcohol-primary establishments.

### Response options

A. Choose the business-attire steakhouse because “OCP is fine everywhere.”  
B. Select the comparable-attire casual restaurant (or change into authorized attire) and briefly explain the AFH off-base OCP limit.  
C. Go to a bar that operates primarily to serve alcohol while in OCP.  
D. Remove nametapes so the OCP “looks less military” at the steakhouse.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 23.10 supplies off-base OCP limits. B complies and teaches the distinction. Chapter 1 discipline and stewardship provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: C

Alcohol-primary establishments are expressly out of bounds for OCP off-base wear. Chapter 1 discipline provides the least-effective basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** Business-attire restaurants are prohibited for OCP.
- **B — Most effective:** Matches authorized off-base wear.
- **C — Least effective:** Directly conflicts with the alcohol-primary prohibition.
- **D — Ineffective:** Stripping tapes does not authorize a prohibited venue.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶23.10 (OCP off-base context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** OCP wear; judgment; standards  
**ADTC:** 23B, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Revalidate current DAFI 36-2903 off-base rules for duty use

## C23-SJT-006 — Religious beard bulk question

An Airman with an approved religious beard accommodation asks whether they may grow the beard to four inches “for a holiday,” then trim later. AFH 1 states an accommodated beard’s bulk may not exceed 2 inches from neck/chin/cheeks and excess bulk must be rolled/tied to meet the 2-inch bulk. The scenario states the accommodation approval is already documented.

### Response options

A. Tell the Airman any length is fine because “accommodation means no limits.”  
B. Explain the 2-inch bulk limit and neat/conservative grooming requirement; if needed, help the Airman use authorized rolling/tying methods to stay within the approved standard.  
C. Order the Airman to shave immediately despite the approved accommodation.  
D. Post the Airman’s faith practice on the unit social page for “transparency.”

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 23.25 supplies the accommodated-beard bulk rule. B honors the approval while enforcing the printed limit. Chapter 1 respect and integrity provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: C

C disregards an approved accommodation without authority in the scenario. Chapter 1 respect and justice provide the least-effective basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** Accommodation is not limitless under AFH 1.
- **B — Most effective:** Accurate bulk standard + supportive compliance.
- **C — Least effective:** Unlawful/unauthorized negation of an approved accommodation in this scenario.
- **D — Ineffective:** Privacy/dignity failure.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶23.25 (religious beard context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Religious accommodation; dignity; standards  
**ADTC:** 23B, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Real accommodation conditions are controlled by the approval memo and DAFI 52-201/DAFI 36-2903

## C23-SJT-010 — Volatile uniform tip sheet

A shop tip sheet claims, without citation, that FIRE duty-identifier patches are authorized on OCP. The designated AFH 1 baseline states the FIRE identifier (black border) is worn only on the 2PFDU and that wear of that duty identifier on the OCP is not authorized. You may correct one sentence before the tip sheet is emailed.

### Response options

A. Leave the false OCP-FIRE claim because “policy changes all the time.”  
B. Replace the sentence with the AFH 1 rule that FIRE (black border) is authorized on the 2PFDU only, not on OCP, and add a note to revalidate current DAFI 36-2903 before duty use.  
C. Delete all patch guidance and tell Airmen to copy random internet photos.  
D. Expand the false claim to say all duty identifiers are banned on every uniform.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 23.10 supplies the FIRE-on-2PFDU-only rule and supports a two-track revalidation note. B corrects the exam-source fact without pretending duty policy never changes. Chapter 1 integrity and disciplined communication provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A knowingly ships a false standard. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** Preserves a clear misstatement.
- **B — Most effective:** Corrects to AFH 1 and flags duty revalidation.
- **C — Ineffective:** Replaces standards with unverified images.
- **D — Ineffective:** Overcorrects into a new falsehood.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶23.10 (duty-identifier/FIRE context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Volatile uniform policy; honest communication; OCP vs 2PFDU  
**ADTC:** 23B, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** **2025 source snapshot—revalidate** against current DAFI 36-2903 before real tip sheets
