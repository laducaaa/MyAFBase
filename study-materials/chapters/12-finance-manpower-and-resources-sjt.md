# Chapter 12 Situational Judgment Practice

> **Status:** Draft and provisional for 27E5/27E6. Chapter 12 is shared core in the historical 26E5/26E6 catalog scopes.
>
> These scenarios are wholly original. They are not actual, recalled, reconstructed, or predicted PFE items. The official Air Force uses subject-matter experts and behavioral scientists to establish operational SJT scoring keys. Chapter 12 describes finance, travel, manpower, resource, and environmental facts, but those facts alone do not establish an official behavioral scoring key. All most-effective and least-effective keys below are MyPromotion educational judgments grounded in Chapter 1 professionalism, core values, and ethical reasoning, with Chapter 12 material supplying context only. They are not official scoring keys or substitutes for current command, finance, travel, legal, ethics, manpower, logistics, environmental, or reporting procedures.
>
> **Not finance advice:** Scenarios use AFH 1 finance/travel facts as context only. They are not personal financial counseling, tax advice, entitlement authorization, or claim-approval authority.
>
> **Two-track source rule:** For exam-source study, preserve the facts in the AFH edition assigned by the controlling catalog. For duty use, separately verify current pay, travel, manpower, resource, and environmental rules; duty-current information does not overwrite a catalog-controlled answer.

## How to use this set

Each scenario produces two items:

1. Select the **most effective** response.
2. Select the **least effective** response.

Read every option before choosing. Evaluate whether the response:

- represents pay, travel, manpower, resource, and environmental facts honestly;
- protects LES, voucher, manpower-request, and compliance-reporting integrity;
- refuses misuse of GTCC, GPC, or other resources;
- stays within stated role and delegated authority;
- uses the two-track caution when source snapshots may differ from current rules; and
- reflects integrity, accountability, loyalty, respect, and mission stewardship.

Chapter 12 supplies finance and resource context; it does not prescribe the behavioral sequence in every response. When a rationale depends on general professional judgment—such as honesty, courage, accountability, loyalty, respect, mission focus, or teamwork—it is identified as an application of the cited foundational Chapter 1 paragraphs.

Ten scenarios produce 20 response selections, mirroring the historical public structure announced for the [22E5/22E6 cycles in December 2021](https://www.afpc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/2874484/waps-adjusts-for-22e6-22e5-testing-cycles/). The separate [public SJT informational guide](https://www.studyguides.af.mil/Portals/15/documents/Notices/Situational-Judgment-Test-Informational-Guide.pdf) explains the four-option most/least format. The 2027 structure is unconfirmed. This set covers only selected Chapter 12 concepts and is not a full-spectrum SJT simulation.

## C12-SJT-001 — Financial responsibility honesty

You supervise a Staff Sergeant who received a formal complaint alleging unpaid private debt. AFH 1 states members will pay financial obligations in a proper and timely manner and that commanders forward complaints to the immediate commander, attempt to respond within 15 days, and monitor until resolved. The Airman asks you to tell the complainant the unit “never received” the complaint so the issue disappears before a pending award package. The complaint package is in your inbox today.

### Response options

A. Tell the complainant the complaint was never received and destroy the package.  
B. Acknowledge receipt through the proper channel, notify the Airman of the requirement to address the obligation, document actions, and help the commander meet the response and monitoring expectations without falsifying status.  
C. Promise the Airman the complaint will be ignored if the award package is withdrawn.  
D. Post the complainant’s personal financial details on the unit social-media page to “clear the air.”

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 12.14 supplies financial-obligation and complaint-handling context; it does not score the integrity choice. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it keeps the complaint process honest and supports timely resolution without concealing facts. Chapter 1 honesty, accountability, and respect provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A falsifies receipt and destroys official correspondence to protect an award package. Chapter 12’s complaint process assumes actual handling; Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It knowingly falsifies and destroys complaint information.
- **B — Most effective:** It processes the complaint honestly and supports required follow-through.
- **C — Ineffective:** It bargains with concealment.
- **D — Ineffective:** It violates privacy and professionalism.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶12.14 (financial obligations/complaints context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Financial responsibility honesty; integrity; accountability  
**ADTC:** 12A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of complaint realism; DAFI 36-2906 and command channels control real cases

## C12-SJT-002 — LES accuracy

During a PCS in-processing checklist, an Airman shows you an LES that still reflects the old duty location’s BAH. AFH 1 says PCS is a time to apply increased attention to the LES because entitlements such as BAH and COLA often change, and that significant pay variances should be raised with the servicing finance office. The Airman asks you to “just leave it” because the higher old BAH helps cover deposits this month.

### Response options

A. Agree to ignore the discrepancy and coach the Airman to spend the difference quietly.  
B. Tell the Airman entitlement errors are the government’s problem only after separation.  
C. Help the Airman identify the BAH mismatch, document what the LES shows versus the new duty location, and escort the Airman to the servicing finance office the same day for correction.  
D. Alter a printed LES photocopy to show the new BAH so the sponsor package looks complete.

### Most effective: C

Paragraph 12.3 supplies LES/PCS attention context; it does not score the counseling behavior. C is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it treats the LES as a accuracy obligation and uses the proper finance channel promptly. Chapter 1 honesty and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A knowingly preserves a likely overpayment for personal benefit. Chapter 12 highlights LES vigilance at PCS; Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It endorses keeping a known entitlement mismatch.
- **B — Ineffective:** It delays required correction and misstates responsibility.
- **C — Most effective:** It verifies and routes the issue to finance immediately.
- **D — Ineffective:** It fabricates a record.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶12.3–12.4 (LES/BAH context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** LES accuracy; honesty; accountability  
**ADTC:** 12A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Finance office controls real entitlement corrections

## C12-SJT-003 — Travel claim integrity

An Airman returns from TDY and asks you to sign as reviewer on a DTS voucher that includes two dinner receipts from a night the traveler was not at the TDY location. AFH 1 requires filing within five business days and states the traveler’s signature certifies the claim is accurate and that the traveler is aware of liability for a false claim. The true itinerary is visible in the orders and lodging record.

### Response options

A. Approve the claim as written to “keep the team moving” and shred the conflicting lodging printout.  
B. Refuse to endorse the inaccurate expenses, require the Airman to remove unsupported meal claims, and ensure the corrected voucher is filed within the five-business-day window.  
C. Tell the Airman to add three more unsupported meals so the falsehood is “consistent.”  
D. Ignore the voucher completely and hope finance never notices.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 12.17 supplies voucher timing and false-claim liability context; it does not create an official scoring key. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it refuses false certification while still enabling a timely, accurate claim. Chapter 1 honesty and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A knowingly advances a false claim and destroys conflicting evidence. Chapter 12 identifies false-claim liability; Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It certifies and conceals a false claim.
- **B — Most effective:** It corrects inaccuracies and preserves timely filing.
- **C — Ineffective:** It expands the falsehood.
- **D — Ineffective:** Avoidance leaves both integrity and timeliness failures unaddressed.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶12.17 (travel claim context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Travel claim integrity; honesty; accountability  
**ADTC:** 12B, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Current DTS/JTR and AO/reviewer authorities control real vouchers

## C12-SJT-004 — Manpower request honesty

Your section wants two additional funded authorizations on the UMD. AFH 1 says a manpower change request with justification goes to the servicing manpower organization and that manpower must be programmed against validated requirements. A peer drafts a request that invents a continuous wartime workload the section does not perform so the request “looks stronger.” You are the NCOIC reviewing the package before submission.

### Response options

A. Submit the inflated wartime narrative because “everyone pads manpower requests.”  
B. Rewrite the justification to match actual mission workload and validated needs, then submit a truthful change request through the servicing manpower organization.  
C. Bypass manpower entirely and tell the commander the UMD already changed.  
D. Destroy the current UMD printout so no one can compare authorizations to reality.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 12.20–12.22 supply manpower validation and UMD-change context; they do not score the honesty choice. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it preserves justification integrity while still using the proper request channel. Chapter 1 honesty and mission stewardship provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A knowingly falsifies workload to obtain authorizations. Chapter 12 requires validated requirements and justified change requests; Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It institutionalizes a false manpower justification.
- **B — Most effective:** It aligns the request with actual validated need and process.
- **C — Ineffective:** It fabricates approval and bypasses manpower.
- **D — Ineffective:** It conceals the authorization baseline.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶12.20–12.22 (manpower/UMD context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Manpower request honesty; integrity; stewardship  
**ADTC:** 12C, A—Remembering / TSgt B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Servicing manpower and HAF validation rules control real changes

## C12-SJT-005 — Resource stewardship

You discover a work center routinely orders excess consumable supplies “just in case,” then discards unopened packages each quarter to “zero out” the locker before inspection. AFH 1 defines waste as extravagant, careless, or needless expenditure or consumption from deficient practices and states every member must ensure cost-effective resource use. The shop chief asks you to ignore it because the budget “use-or-lose” culture rewards spending.

### Response options

A. Join the discard practice and order still more supplies before fiscal year-end.  
B. Document what you observed, stop the discard practice in your lane, and elevate the pattern to the supervisor/resource advisor so consumption can be aligned with actual need.  
C. Take the unopened supplies home for personal use so they are not “wasted.”  
D. Threaten to accuse the shop chief of fraud publicly without gathering facts or using command channels.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 12.27 and 12.31 supply stewardship and waste context; they do not score the reporting sequence. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it stops needless consumption and uses proper channels with facts. Chapter 1 accountability and mission stewardship provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: C

C converts government property to personal use—crossing from waste into unauthorized diversion. Chapter 12’s fraud/abuse concepts and stewardship duties support MyPromotion’s least-effective judgment with Chapter 1 integrity.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It expands needless expenditure.
- **B — Most effective:** It stops the practice and routes facts to the stewardship chain.
- **C — Least effective:** It converts property to personal use.
- **D — Partially effective intent, ineffective execution:** Raising concern matters, but unfounded public accusation skips factual, lowest-level channels AFH 1 encourages for FWA issues.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶12.27, 12.31 (stewardship/FWA context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Resource stewardship; accountability; integrity  
**ADTC:** 12D, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Local resource advisors/commanders and FWA channels control real cases

## C12-SJT-006 — Environmental compliance reporting

A facility process is discharging a substance in a way that appears to violate a posted environmental handling requirement. AFH 1 requires commanders, supervisors, and process owners to integrate environmental considerations into daily work and decisions and describes EMS priorities of compliance, risk reduction, and continuous improvement. A teammate wants the team to wait until after a VIP tour next week before reporting anything.

### Response options

A. Delay all reporting until after the VIP tour and clean only the visible area.  
B. Stop or contain the unsafe/noncompliant activity as your authority allows, notify the chain and environmental/civil-engineer points of contact promptly with factual details, and document what was observed.  
C. Tell junior Airmen the requirement is optional during high-visibility weeks.  
D. Alter logbooks to show continuous compliance for the past month.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 12.34 and 12.36 supply EMS responsibility and compliance-support context; they do not score the exact local notification roster. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it prioritizes compliance and truthful reporting over appearance. Chapter 1 honesty, courage, and mission stewardship provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D falsifies compliance records. Chapter 12’s compliance priority makes accurate reporting essential; Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It delays compliance for optics.
- **B — Most effective:** It acts within role, reports facts, and protects compliance.
- **C — Ineffective:** It teaches juniors to treat environmental rules as optional.
- **D — Least effective:** It fabricates compliance history.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶12.34, 12.36 (EMS/compliance context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Environmental compliance reporting; honesty; courage  
**ADTC:** 12E, A—Remembering / TSgt B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Installation environmental/CE procedures control real reporting

## C12-SJT-007 — Two-track policy

You build a study sheet that quotes AFH 1’s January 2023 TSP contribution maxima ($22,500 / $66,000). A classmate finds a blog claiming “new unlimited TSP” and wants the sheet changed before tonight’s optional study group. The finance office can confirm current contribution limits tomorrow; no one is making a TSP election tonight.

### Response options

A. Replace the AFH figures with the blog’s “unlimited” claim and present it as settled current policy.  
B. Keep the AFH 1 figures as the exam-source snapshot, label them as the designated baseline, note that duty-current limits may differ, and tell the group finance will confirm current limits tomorrow before any election change.  
C. Delete all numbers and say contribution limits never matter for WAPS or duty.  
D. Tell Airmen to elect 100 percent of basic pay tonight because “FICA will sort itself out.”

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 12.13 supplies the TSP snapshot; it does not adjudicate later IRS/TSP changes. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it preserves two-track fidelity—exam-source accuracy versus duty verification—without acting on rumor. Chapter 1 honesty and disciplined uncertainty management provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D pushes a contribution action AFH 1 says is not permitted above 92 percent of basic pay and treats rumor as authority. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** Overwrites the study baseline with unverified media.
- **B — Most effective:** Separates exam-source and duty-current tracks.
- **C — Partially effective caution, ineffective extremity:** Removes useful sourced content and dismisses needed verification.
- **D — Least effective:** Encourages an unsupported pay-election action.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶12.13 (TSP context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Two-track policy; honesty; uncertainty management  
**ADTC:** 12A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Preserve January 2023 figures as source snapshot; finance/TSP rules control real elections

## C12-SJT-008 — Avoiding entitlement overclaim

An Airman on CONUS PCS asks whether to claim both Temporary Lodging Expense and Temporary Lodging Allowance for the same nights “to maximize money.” AFH 1 distinguishes TLE (CONUS; paid on a travel voucher) from TLA (OCONUS; paid in military pay/LES). The finance office travel section is open, and the orders show a CONUS location.

### Response options

A. Advise claiming both for the same CONUS nights because “finance will catch duplicates.”  
B. Explain the AFH 1 distinction—TLE for CONUS voucher payment versus TLA for OCONUS military-pay payment—tell the Airman not to claim an entitlement that does not apply, and walk the Airman to finance/travel for claim-specific confirmation.  
C. Invent a hybrid “CONUS TLA” rate to put on the voucher.  
D. Refuse to discuss entitlements at all and tell the Airman to guess.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 12.16 supplies the TLE/TLA distinction; it does not authorize a specific claim. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it prevents overclaim while routing the Airman to the confirming authority. Chapter 1 honesty and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A encourages knowingly claiming overlapping/inapplicable entitlements. Chapter 12’s TLE/TLA distinction and Chapter 1 integrity support the least-effective judgment.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It pushes entitlement overclaim.
- **B — Most effective:** It teaches the source distinction and verifies before claiming.
- **C — Ineffective:** It fabricates an entitlement.
- **D — Partially effective caution, ineffective extremity:** Avoids false advice but abandons available accurate help.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶12.16 (TLE/TLA context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Avoiding entitlement overclaim; honesty; accountability  
**ADTC:** 12B, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** JTR/finance control real entitlement determinations

## C12-SJT-009 — GPC/resource misuse refusal

A coworker asks you to use a government purchase card on a personal electronics accessory “and code it as shop tools,” promising to repay the account in cash later. AFH 1 defines abuse as intentionally wrongful or improper use of USAF resources and fraud as intentional deception to secure unauthorized benefits. You are not the cardholder; the cardholder is being pressured in front of you.

### Response options

A. Encourage the cardholder to make the purchase and offer to split the accessory.  
B. Tell both Airmen the purchase would be improper, refuse to participate, and ensure the issue is reported to the supervisor/card-approving official through the proper channel.  
C. Stay silent and later ask for a similar personal purchase.  
D. Take the card and complete the purchase yourself so the assigned cardholder has “deniability.”

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 12.31 supplies FWA definitions and reporting context; local GPC rules control card details. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it refuses misuse and elevates the issue factually. Chapter 1 integrity, courage, and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D actively commits the improper purchase and manufactures concealment. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It promotes misuse for personal gain.
- **B — Most effective:** It refuses and reports through proper channels.
- **C — Ineffective:** Silence enables misuse and seeks personal benefit later.
- **D — Least effective:** It executes and conceals the misuse.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶12.31 (FWA context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** GPC/resource misuse refusal; integrity; courage  
**ADTC:** 12D, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Current GPC program and command channels control real cases; scenario uses AFH FWA principles as context

## C12-SJT-010 — Teaching juniors accurate allowances

A Senior Airman you mentor is preparing a flight-room brief on allowances and plans to teach that “everyone in dorms gets full BAH” and that “FSA-temporary starts on day one of any TDY.” AFH 1 states BAH is payable when government quarters are not provided (with partial BAH rules for certain members in single-type quarters) and that FSA-temporary requires continuous TDY away from the PDS for more than 30 days with dependents not at or near the TDY station. You can correct one slide before the brief.

### Response options

A. Leave both inaccurate claims because the brief is “just awareness.”  
B. Replace the claims with AFH 1-accurate statements: BAH depends on quarters/dependency conditions (including partial BAH rules), and FSA-temporary requires continuous TDY of more than 30 days with dependents not at/near the TDY station; add a note to verify current entitlements with finance.  
C. Broaden the slide to promise every Airman OHA while in CONUS dorms.  
D. Tell the Airman to cite anonymous social media as the entitlement authority.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 12.4 and 12.6 supply BAH/FSA context; they do not score mentoring technique. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it corrects material inaccuracies and adds the duty-verification caution. Chapter 1 honesty, respect, and disciplined communication provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: C

C replaces one false entitlement claim with a broader false claim. Chapter 12’s allowance distinctions and Chapter 1 integrity support the least-effective judgment.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It knowingly teaches false entitlement rules.
- **B — Most effective:** It restores source-accurate teaching and verification discipline.
- **C — Least effective:** It expands inaccurate entitlement promises.
- **D — Ineffective:** It substitutes rumor for authoritative channels.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶12.4, 12.6 (BAH/FSA context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Teaching juniors accurate allowances; honesty; mentoring integrity  
**ADTC:** 12A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Finance/current entitlements control real eligibility; brief is educational only
