# Chapter 11 Situational Judgment Practice

> **Status:** Draft and provisional for 27E5/27E6. Chapter 11 is shared core in the historical 26E5/26E6 catalog scopes.
>
> These scenarios are wholly original. They are not actual, recalled, reconstructed, or predicted PFE items. The official Air Force uses subject-matter experts and behavioral scientists to establish operational SJT scoring keys. Chapter 11 describes personnel programs, benefits, leave, associations, and civilian programs, but those facts alone do not establish an official behavioral scoring key. All most-effective and least-effective keys below are MyPromotion educational judgments grounded in Chapter 1 professionalism, core values, and ethical reasoning, with Chapter 11 material supplying context only. They are not official scoring keys or substitutes for current command, supervisory, safety, legal, ethics, finance, personnel, medical, or reporting procedures.
>
> **Non-advice rule:** Scenarios may involve benefits, retirement, insurance, leave, or civilian programs. Rationales explain honest representation of AFH text and proper referral; they are not financial, legal, medical, or entitlements advice.
>
> **Two-track source rule:** For exam-source study, preserve the facts in the AFH edition assigned by the controlling catalog. For duty use, separately verify current benefits, leave, retirement, association-endorsement, and civilian-personnel rules; duty-current information does not overwrite a catalog-controlled answer.

## How to use this set

Each scenario produces two items:

1. Select the **most effective** response.
2. Select the **least effective** response.

Read every option before choosing. Evaluate whether the response:

- represents benefits, leave, association, and civilian-program facts honestly without overclaim;
- uses proper readiness, personnel, finance, legal, and counseling channels;
- protects leave and certification integrity;
- respects privacy, dignity, and next-of-kin stewardship;
- stays within stated role and delegated authority;
- applies two-track caution when policy may be volatile; and
- reflects integrity, accountability, loyalty, respect, and mission stewardship.

Chapter 11 supplies program context; it does not prescribe the behavioral sequence in every response. When a rationale depends on general professional judgment—such as honesty, courage, accountability, loyalty, respect, mission focus, or teamwork—it is identified as an application of the cited foundational Chapter 1 paragraphs.

Ten scenarios produce 20 response selections, mirroring the historical public structure announced for the [22E5/22E6 cycles in December 2021](https://www.afpc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/2874484/waps-adjusts-for-22e6-22e5-testing-cycles/). The separate [public SJT informational guide](https://www.studyguides.af.mil/Portals/15/documents/Notices/Situational-Judgment-Test-Informational-Guide.pdf) explains the four-option most/least format. The 2027 structure is unconfirmed. This set covers only selected Chapter 11 concepts and is not a full-spectrum SJT simulation.

## C11-SJT-001 — Family care plan honesty

You supervise a military couple with dependents. During the annual family care review, they admit their written caregiver is no longer available and they have not identified a nonmilitary alternate who can assume custody if both deploy. The DAF Form 357 on file still lists the unavailable caregiver. A peer suggests they “just resign the old form today” so the unit looks ready for an upcoming inspection, then fix the plan “after the inspection.”

### Response options

A. Tell them to resign the outdated form as-is so the inspection binder looks complete, and warn them not to mention the missing caregiver.  
B. Document that no plan is needed because inspections matter more than deployability.  
C. Stop the false certification, counsel that AFH 1 requires workable worldwide-deployable arrangements with a nonmilitary caregiver, help them engage the commander/first sergeant process the same day, and set a clear follow-up to complete a truthful DAF Form 357.  
D. Ignore the issue because family care plans are optional for military couples.

### Most effective: C

Paragraph 11.5 supplies family care plan context; it does not create a behavioral scoring key. C is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it refuses a false readiness picture, restores an honest workable plan through the proper counseling/certification channel, and protects deployability. Chapter 1 honesty, accountability, and mission stewardship provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A knowingly preserves a false certification for inspection appearance. Chapter 11 identifies the certification/workability expectation; Chapter 1 integrity and honesty provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It falsifies readiness documentation for appearance.
- **B — Ineffective:** It invents an exception AFH 1 does not grant and subordinates deployability to optics.
- **C — Most effective:** It corrects the truth gap through the proper process immediately.
- **D — Ineffective:** Military couples with dependents must have a family care plan per AFH 1.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶11.5 (family care context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Family care plan honesty; certification integrity; deployability; accountability  
**ADTC:** 11A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of counseling realism; current family-care directives control real cases

## C11-SJT-002 — Leave integrity

An Airman asks you how to “save leave days” by ending leave on Friday, taking the weekend off the books, and starting a new leave on Monday for the same trip. LeaveWeb is available, and the period clearly includes the weekend. A coworker says “everybody does it” and offers to approve the split if you look the other way.

### Response options

A. Approve the Friday/Monday split because local custom overrides AFH 1.  
B. Explain that nonduty days inside an authorized leave period are chargeable and that ending Friday to restart Monday is not permitted; help the Airman submit one accurate leave request for the full period.  
C. Tell the Airman to omit the weekend dates in LeaveWeb while still traveling those days.  
D. Say leave rules are only advisory and that special passes can secretly cover the weekend without command knowledge.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 11.13 supplies the weekend/chargeable-day rule; it does not score the counseling behavior. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it represents the leave rule honestly and steers the Airman to accurate accounting. Chapter 1 honesty and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: C

C coaches concealment of chargeable days. Chapter 11 identifies the charging rule; Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** Local custom does not authorize the prohibited split.
- **B — Most effective:** Accurate rule + correct LeaveWeb action.
- **C — Least effective:** Deliberate false leave accounting.
- **D — Ineffective:** Misstates leave/pass authorities and invites concealment.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶11.13–11.14 (leave integrity context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Leave integrity; honesty; accountability  
**ADTC:** 11B, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Preserve AFH leave rules; current DAFI 36-3003 controls real leave actions

## C11-SJT-003 — Benefit representation without overclaim

During a unit professional-development hour, a slide drafted by a peer claims, “Everyone in this room is already fully eligible for every VA disability, home-loan, and burial benefit listed in AFH 1, so no one needs to contact the VA.” AFH 1 lists major VA categories and directs members needing specific information to www.va.gov or the closest VA department for eligibility. You can correct one slide before delivery.

### Response options

A. Leave the “fully eligible for every benefit” claim because it motivates the audience.  
B. Replace the claim with AFH 1’s category list and a clear statement that individual eligibility must be checked through VA channels—not assumed from a briefing slide.  
C. Broaden the claim to include guaranteed TRICARE-for-life costs identical to VHA for all listeners.  
D. Delete all VA references and tell the audience benefits are secret.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 11.6 supplies VA-category context; it does not create a behavioral key for briefing edits. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it prevents overclaim and points to the source’s referral path. Chapter 1 honesty and disciplined communication provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A knowingly preserves an eligibility overclaim. Chapter 11 limits the handbook to categories plus VA referral; Chapter 1 accurate representation provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** Overclaims eligibility for motivation.
- **B — Most effective:** Accurate AFH representation + proper referral.
- **C — Ineffective:** Adds further false sameness claims about TRICARE/VHA.
- **D — Partially effective intent, ineffective execution:** Removes falsehood but withholds legitimate information.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶11.6–11.7 (benefits context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Benefit representation without overclaim; honesty; referral discipline  
**ADTC:** 11A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Non-advice; current VA/TRICARE rules control real eligibility

## C11-SJT-004 — Association purpose accuracy

A shop chief asks you to announce that “the Air Force officially endorses every veterans club and private organization that wants to fundraise at the gate this weekend,” citing Chapter 11’s discussion of military associations. AFH 1 lists limited JER Section 3-210 fundraising/membership-drive activities. An ethics counselor is available today.

### Response options

A. Make the announcement exactly as requested because associations exist for lobbying, fraternity, and support, which equals blanket endorsement.  
B. Refuse to discuss associations at all and criticize the shop chief publicly.  
C. Explain that AFH 1 separates community association purposes from a limited endorsement list; decline the blanket claim; and walk the issue to the ethics counselor/command channel before any endorsement language is used.  
D. Invent a new seventh endorsement category for “any gate fundraiser approved by a shift leader.”

### Most effective: C

Paragraph 11.18 supplies association/endorsement context; it does not score the counseling behavior. C is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it preserves purpose-versus-endorsement accuracy and uses the proper ethics channel. Chapter 1 honesty, loyalty, and respect for process provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A converts community purpose language into unauthorized blanket endorsement. Chapter 11’s limited list identifies the conflict; Chapter 1 accurate representation provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** Overclaims endorsement authority.
- **B — Ineffective:** Avoids the false claim but handles the correction unprofessionally.
- **C — Most effective:** Accurate distinction + proper ethics channel.
- **D — Ineffective:** Fabricates endorsement authority.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶11.18 (association context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Association purpose accuracy; honesty; ethics channels  
**ADTC:** 11C, A—Remembering / TSgt B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Current JER/ethics guidance controls real endorsement actions

## C11-SJT-005 — Civilian program distinction

A new NCO says civilian employees in your mixed workforce “wear invisible enlisted ranks,” accrue military leave at 2.5 days per month in LeaveWeb, and can be awarded three-day special passes like Airmen. A civilian personnel specialist is available to help clarify. The NCO wants you to brief the shop using those claims this afternoon.

### Response options

A. Deliver the brief as the NCO drafted it to avoid disagreement.  
B. Correct the distinctions using AFH 1: civilians do not have rank; positions are graded via CPDs; civilian leave/pay/hours follow civilian rules; military LeaveWeb/pass constructs are not interchangeable—and bring the specialist into the brief.  
C. Tell civilian employees they must enroll in SOES as if they were military members.  
D. Say civilian programs are identical to RAP and WEAR.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 11.20 and 11.28 supply civilian-system context; they do not create a behavioral key. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it prevents harmful system-merging and uses the servicing expert. Chapter 1 honesty, respect, and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A knowingly spreads false equivalencies that can mismanage civilian absences and status. Chapter 11 distinctions identify the conflict; Chapter 1 accurate leadership communication provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** Propagates false military/civilian equivalency.
- **B — Most effective:** Accurate distinction + proper specialist support.
- **C — Ineffective:** Misapplies military emergency-data/insurance systems.
- **D — Ineffective:** Confuses unrelated recruiting programs with civilian personnel systems.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶11.20, 11.27–11.28 (civilian distinction context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Civilian program distinction; honesty; respect for workforce systems  
**ADTC:** 11D, A—Remembering / TSgt B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Servicing civilian personnel section controls real civilian rules

## C11-SJT-006 — Two-track BRS/policy

An Airman asks whether to enroll in BRS during a break-in-service reentry window and wants you to “just pick the richest option” based on AFH 1 Table 11.1 Note 11 and Note 12 contribution figures. Your study baseline is the 15 February 2025 AFH snapshot. Finance and M&FRC counseling are available the same day.

### Response options

A. Order the Airman to enroll immediately based only on the handbook dollar figures and promise a specific lifetime outcome.  
B. Quote AFH 1’s dated BRS/TSP snapshot accurately, state that personal elections require current finance/M&FRC counseling, and escort the Airman to those counselors without recommending an election yourself.  
C. Invent a hybrid plan combining REDUX COLA with BRS matching that AFH 1 does not describe.  
D. Refuse to acknowledge Table 11.1 exists because numbers are volatile.

### Most effective: B

Table 11.1 Notes 11–13 supply BRS context; they do not authorize personal financial advice. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it preserves two-track honesty: exam-source accuracy plus duty-use referral. Chapter 1 honesty, humility about limits, and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A converts a study snapshot into coercive personal financial direction. Chapter 11’s non-advice posture and counseling pointers identify the boundary; Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** Overclaims authority and outcome certainty.
- **B — Most effective:** Accurate snapshot + proper counselors; no personal election advice.
- **C — Ineffective:** Fabricates a plan.
- **D — Partially effective caution, ineffective execution:** Volatility caution is good, but erasing the source is dishonest for study/context.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, Table 11.1 Notes 11–13 (BRS context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Two-track BRS/policy; non-advice; honesty  
**ADTC:** 11A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Not financial advice; current finance/M&FRC rules control elections

## C11-SJT-007 — Survivor benefit stewardship

You help an Airman update emergency data before deployment. The Airman wants to name a PADD who has never been told and is unaware of funeral wishes, and also wants to skip the annual SOES/RED review “until after deployment.” AFH 1 stresses annual/life-event updates and that the PADD should understand disposition decisions. A CAR briefing resource is available.

### Response options

A. Allow the skip and silent PADD designation because deployment paperwork is already heavy.  
B. Complete the forms with placeholder beneficiaries “to be determined” without telling anyone.  
C. Explain why current RED/SOES data and an informed PADD matter for dignified, timely notification and disposition; help the Airman complete truthful updates now; and offer CAR/M&FRC pointers for questions beyond your lane.  
D. Post the Airman’s beneficiary choices on the unit social media page for “transparency.”

### Most effective: C

Paragraphs 11.3 and 11.9 supply RED/SOES/SBP-related stewardship context; they do not create a behavioral key. C is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it protects people through timely, informed designations while respecting counseling limits. Chapter 1 loyalty, respect, and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D violates privacy and dignity around sensitive beneficiary/disposition choices. Chapter 1 respect and loyalty provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** Defers critical readiness/stewardship updates without cause.
- **B — Ineffective:** Creates incomplete/unusable designations.
- **C — Most effective:** Timely truthful updates + informed PADD + proper referral.
- **D — Least effective:** Publicizes private beneficiary/disposition information.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶11.3, 11.9 (survivor/emergency-data context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Survivor benefit stewardship; privacy; dignity; accountability  
**ADTC:** 11A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** CAR/SBP counselors control real elections and notifications

## C11-SJT-009 — Readiness center use

An Airman shows clear work/life strain before a 45-day deployment tasking and asks whether “anyone actually has to do those M&FRC briefings.” Pre-deployment briefings are mandatory for firm taskings of 30 or more days. The Airman also needs help updating vRED and finding free financial readiness counseling. You can walk them to the M&FRC today.

### Response options

A. Tell the Airman M&FRC support is only for spouses and that pre-deployment briefings are optional under 45 days.  
B. Joke that only weak Airmen use readiness centers, then change the subject.  
C. Confirm the mandatory pre-deployment briefing trigger, walk the Airman to the M&FRC for briefing/vRED/financial readiness help, and follow up that appointments were made.  
D. Order the Airman to skip M&FRC and instead post personal financial details on a group chat for peer advice.

### Most effective: C

Paragraph 11.3 supplies M&FRC/deployment-briefing context; it does not create a behavioral key. C is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it uses the readiness system as designed and follows through. Chapter 1 loyalty to people, mission readiness, and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D both skips mandatory readiness support and exposes personal financial information improperly. Chapter 1 respect, loyalty, and judgment provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** Misstates audience and the 30-day mandatory trigger.
- **B — Ineffective:** Stigmatizes help-seeking and abandons the Airman.
- **C — Most effective:** Accurate requirement + same-day M&FRC use + follow-up.
- **D — Least effective:** Avoids M&FRC and compromises privacy.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶11.3 (M&FRC context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Readiness center use; mission; loyalty; privacy  
**ADTC:** 11A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Local M&FRC schedules and current directives control real appointments

## C11-SJT-010 — Privacy and dignity

A casualty notification exercise discussion turns casual. Someone suggests practicing by role-playing with a real member’s actual RED names and phone numbers pulled from a shared drive, then texting those details to a group chat “for realism.” AFH 1 emphasizes dignified, professional, timely notification and the sensitivity of emergency data.

### Response options

A. Share the real RED extract to the group chat because training realism matters most.  
B. Stop the proposed misuse immediately, remind the team that emergency data and notification practices require dignity and privacy, use only sanitized training scenarios, and report the exposed real data through the proper privacy/security channel.  
C. Stay silent to avoid conflict, hoping no one actually sends the text.  
D. Post the real PNOK information on a whiteboard in a public hallway for the “exercise inject.”

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 11.3 supplies casualty/RED dignity context; it does not create a behavioral scoring key. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it protects people, stops the privacy breach, and uses proper reporting channels. Chapter 1 respect, loyalty, courage, and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D maximizes public exposure of sensitive next-of-kin data. Chapter 1 respect and dignity provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** Misuses real emergency data for convenience.
- **B — Most effective:** Stops harm, preserves dignity, reports exposure.
- **C — Ineffective:** Avoids conflict while leaving the breach likely.
- **D — Least effective:** Publicizes protected personal/casualty-related information.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶11.3 (casualty/RED dignity context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Privacy; dignity; courage; accountability  
**ADTC:** 11A, B—Understanding
