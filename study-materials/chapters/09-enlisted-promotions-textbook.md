## Why this chapter matters

Chapter 9 answers how Regular Air Force enlisted promotions are designed to work:

1. **What limits and structures promotions?** DoD/service quotas, equal-pay principles, and grade ceilings.
2. **What opportunities exist beyond ordinary timelines?** Below-the-zone, STEP, WAPS, SNCO boards, and supplemental processes.
3. **Who is eligible, and when?** Cycles, PECD, time-in-grade/service, skill level, PME, and commander recommendation.
4. **What must the Airman own?** Study plan, official references, record accuracy, testing readiness, and test-security duties.
5. **How are tests and boards used?** PFE/SKT development and scoring for NCO grades; evaluation boards and weighted factors for SNCO grades; score notices with strict access limits.

A useful chapter-wide sequence is:

> Career-progression purpose → quotas → promotion programs → eligibility cycles → individual preparation and integrity → official study references → testing → points/boards → score feedback

That sequence is a **MyPromotion memory aid**, not an official promotion flowchart. Real promotion actions depend on AFI 36-2502, the current cycle catalog, personnel systems, and the chain of command. (AFH 1, ¶¶9.1–9.21)

# Section 9A — Promotion Systems and Programs

## Learning objectives

At ADTC level B, an Airman should be able to:

1. Explain the enlisted promotion system’s purpose in supporting career progression, retention/motivation, and equal pay for equal work.
2. Explain why promotion quotas exist and what constrains E-8/E-9 averages.
3. Compare SrA below-the-zone, STEP, WAPS, SNCO board selection, and supplemental processes.
4. Explain that WAPS selections are made within each AFSC (or applicable identifier), not across all AFSCs.
5. Distinguish in-system supplemental consideration from SNCO supplemental board consideration. (AFH 1, ¶¶9.1–9.3)

## 1. The enlisted promotion system supports visible career progression (¶9.1)

AFH 1 states that the enlisted promotion system supports DoD Directive 1304.20, *Enlisted Personnel Management System (EPMS)*, by providing visible, relatively stable career progression opportunities; attracting, retaining, and motivating the people the military needs; and ensuring uniform application of the principle of equal pay for equal work among the services. (AFH 1, ¶9.1)

Despite significant changes over time, the handbook emphasizes standardized, consistent aspects that Airmen recognize and rely on for fair and accurate promotion consideration. For detailed program rules, AFH 1 points to AFI 36-2502, *Enlisted Airman Promotion and Demotion Programs*. (AFH 1, ¶9.1)

**Source scope:** This chapter applies to **RegAF** enlisted promotions. (AFH 1, ¶9.1)

> **MyPromotion memory aid (unofficial):**  
> **Progress → retain/motivate → equal pay for equal work.**  
> Compresses ¶9.1 purposes; not official wording.

## 2. Quotas are limited by law, funding, and vacancies (¶9.2)

The Department of Defense limits how many Airmen the RegAF may have in the top five enlisted grades. Promotion quotas for Staff Sergeant through Chief Master Sergeant are tied to fiscal year-end strength and are affected by funding limits, regulatory limits, and the number of projected vacancies in specific grades. (AFH 1, ¶9.2)

Public law, as outlined in Title 10, U.S.C., Armed Forces, Chapter 36, limits the number who may serve in the RegAF in the top two enlisted grades. **2025 source snapshot—revalidate:** The authorized average of enlisted members on RegAF status (other than for training) in pay grades E-8 and E-9 in a fiscal year may not be more than **2.5 percent** and **1.25 percent**, respectively. (AFH 1, ¶9.2)

| Constraint theme | Source idea |
|---|---|
| DoD grade limits | Top five enlisted grades are numerically limited |
| Quota drivers | FY-end strength, funding, regulation, projected vacancies |
| E-8 / E-9 statutory averages | Not more than 2.5% (E-8) and 1.25% (E-9), excluding training status as printed |

Quota existence explains why “eligible and recommended” does not automatically mean “promoted” at NCO and SNCO grades: opportunity is constrained. (AFH 1, ¶¶9.2–9.3)

## 3. Promotion opportunities combine noncompetitive and competitive paths (¶9.3)

AFH 1 states that the USAF promotion system is designed to promote eligible Airmen recommended by their commander on a noncompetitive basis, while also describing additional opportunities and factors Airmen should understand. (AFH 1, ¶9.3)

### Senior Airman Below-the-Zone

Under the Senior Airman Below-the-Zone Program, Airmen in the grade of Airman First Class may be considered **one time** for early advancement to Senior Airman if they meet minimum eligibility criteria. If promoted BTZ, the promotion effective date is **six months** before their fully qualified date. Consideration occurs in the month (December, March, June, or September) before the quarter in which they are eligible (January–March, April–June, July–September, or October–December). (AFH 1, ¶9.3)

### Stripes for Exceptional Performers (STEP)

STEP is designed for unique circumstances that, in a commander’s judgment, clearly warrant promotion—compelling, though perhaps not quantifiable, reasons. Isolated acts or specific achievements should **not** be the sole basis for a STEP promotion. (AFH 1, ¶9.3)

Commanders at various organizational levels may promote a limited number of exemplary performers with exceptional potential to **Staff Sergeant** and **Technical Sergeant**. An individual may not receive more than one promotion under any combination of promotion programs within a **12-month** period, with one exception: Senior Airmen must serve **six months** of time in grade before promotion to Staff Sergeant. Commanders must ensure promoted personnel meet eligibility requirements. (AFH 1, ¶9.3)

### Weighted Airman Promotion System (WAPS)

NCOs and SNCOs compete for promotion and test under WAPS in the **control AFSC (CAFSC)** held on the promotion eligibility cutoff date. Contributing factors are “weighted,” or assigned points based on relative importance to promotion. (AFH 1, ¶9.3)

| WAPS test element | Source focus |
|---|---|
| Promotion Fitness Examination (PFE) | Wide range of USAF knowledge |
| Specialty Knowledge Test (SKT) | AFSC broad technical knowledge |

Selections under WAPS are made **within each AFSC**, not across all AFSCs. Eligibles compete with those currently working in their AFSC. Selectees are those with the highest scores in each AFSC within quota limitations. If more than one individual has the same total score at the cutoff point, the USAF promotes **everyone with that score**. (AFH 1, ¶9.3)

### Senior Noncommissioned Officer promotions

Airmen compete and are selected for promotion:

- to **MSgt** in their control AFSC;
- to **SMSgt** in the superintendent level of their control AFSC; and
- to **CMSgt** in the chief enlisted manager code of their control AFSC  

as of the PECD. Individuals with a reporting identifier or special duty indicator designated as their CAFSC on the PECD compete within that identifier. SNCO promotions are accomplished via a **central board**. (AFH 1, ¶9.3)

### Supplemental processes

| Process | Source rhythm | Who / what |
|---|---|---|
| In-system supplemental | Typically monthly | Eligible SSgt through SMSgt whose weighable data changes; also those who test after initial selects (for example, deployed Airmen or anyone unable to test in the normal window) |
| SNCO supplemental board | Semiannual | Promotion-eligible to MSgt, SMSgt, or CMSgt; members may request if they believe they have a valid request |

With the exception of a missing static closeout date evaluation, there are **no automatic approvals** for supplemental board consideration when a record did not meet a previous board for which the member was eligible. Supplemental consideration may **not** be granted if an error or omission appeared on the data verification record or in the SNCO selection record in the personnel records display application, and the individual did not take necessary steps to correct it before promotion selection or the evaluation board. Fully documented requests—including proof of corrective or follow-up actions—are submitted in writing to the MPF with the unit commander’s recommendation; the MPF forwards the request to AFPC for final approval. (AFH 1, ¶9.3)

### Distinctions that matter at level B

| Do not confuse | Correct distinction |
|---|---|
| Noncompetitive design language with unlimited promotion | ¶9.3 pairs commander-recommended design with competitive WAPS/board realities and quota limits |
| BTZ with STEP | BTZ is early SrA (one-time); STEP is limited SSgt/TSgt promotion for exceptional circumstances |
| WAPS competition across the Air Force | Competition is within AFSC (or applicable RI/SDI) |
| Tie at the cutoff | Everyone with that identical total score is promoted |
| In-system supplemental with SNCO supplemental board | Monthly weighable-data/testing catch-up vs semiannual board request process |
| MyPromotion materials with EPRRC references | Official study references are those listed in the EPRRC / official portals |

## Common misconceptions

1. **“If my commander recommends me, promotion is automatic.”**  
   Quotas, WAPS scores, AFSC competition, and (for SNCOs) board results constrain selection. (AFH 1, ¶¶9.2–9.3)

2. **“WAPS pits every AFSC against every other AFSC.”**  
   Selections are within each AFSC (or applicable identifier). (AFH 1, ¶9.3)

3. **“A single outstanding act is enough for STEP.”**  
   Isolated acts or specific achievements should not be the sole basis. (AFH 1, ¶9.3)

4. **“Supplemental board consideration is automatic if something was wrong.”**  
   Except for a missing static closeout date evaluation, automatic approval is not the rule; uncorrected DVR/selection-record errors can block consideration. (AFH 1, ¶9.3)

## Section 9A summary

- The enlisted promotion system supports stable career progression, force attraction/retention/motivation, and equal pay for equal work.
- Quotas for SSgt–CMSgt reflect strength, funding, regulation, vacancies, and statutory E-8/E-9 averages.
- Key opportunities include SrA BTZ, STEP, WAPS (PFE/SKT), SNCO central boards, and supplemental processes.
- WAPS competition is within AFSC; identical cutoff scores promote everyone tied. (AFH 1, ¶¶9.1–9.3)

# Section 9B — Promotion Cycles

## Learning objectives

At ADTC level B, an Airman should be able to:

1. Explain why the USAF uses promotion cycles and what factors can affect eligibility.
2. Summarize basic TIG/TIS/skill-level guidelines from Airman through Chief Master Sergeant as printed in AFH 1.
3. Identify the PECD and test-cycle snapshots printed for SSgt and TSgt.
4. Explain Promotion Statement of Understanding / retainability rules for certain SNCO selects.
5. Explain how promotion sequence numbers are assigned and how declination and ineligibility work. (AFH 1, ¶¶9.4–9.9)

## 1. Cycles create forecasting, workload balance, and PECD (¶9.4)

The USAF establishes promotion cycles to ensure timely periodic promotions and to permit accurate forecasting of vacancies. Cycles also balance administrative workload and provide promotion eligibility cutoff dates (PECD). (AFH 1, ¶9.4)

Factors for promotion eligibility may include:

- proper skill level;
- sufficient time in grade;
- sufficient time in service;
- commander recommendation;
- completion of enlisted PME;
- completion of a college degree;
- cumulative years of enlisted service; and
- high year of tenure. (AFH 1, ¶9.4)

**Contrast note (not the chapter’s RegAF system):** Air Reserve Component promotions are based on a combination of position vacancy, time-in-grade, time-in-service, fitness, and completion of appropriate PME. (AFH 1, ¶9.4)

## 2. Basic promotion guidelines (¶9.5)

AFH 1 provides the following basic guidelines. Treat calendars and skill-level gates as **2025 source snapshots—revalidate** against the controlling catalog and AFI 36-2502 for duty use.

| To grade | Source eligibility snapshot | Other source cues |
|---|---|---|
| Airman | AB may be eligible at **6 months** TIG | — |
| Airman First Class | Amn may be eligible at **10 months** TIG | Six-year enlistees: AB → A1C upon completion of technical training or **20 weeks** of technical training after BMT (whichever first); DOR adjusted to BMT certificate signatory date, without back pay and allowances |
| Senior Airman | **36 months** TIS and **20 months** TIG, or **28 months** TIG (whichever first) | Required skill level in primary AFSC: **3-level**; BTZ is one-time consideration six months earlier than basic SrA timelines |
| Staff Sergeant | SrA: **3 years** TIS and **6 months** TIG | PECD **31 March**; test cycle **May–June**; primary AFSC **5-level** |
| Technical Sergeant | SSgt: **5 years** TIS and **23 months** TIG | PECD **31 January**; test cycle **February–March**; primary AFSC **7-level** |
| Master Sergeant | TSgt: **8 years** TIS and **24 months** TIG | PECD **30 November**; primary AFSC **7-level** |
| Senior Master Sergeant | MSgt: **11 years** TIS and **20 months** TIG | PECD **30 September**; primary AFSC **7-level** |
| Chief Master Sergeant | SMSgt: **14 years** TIS and **21 months** TIG | PECD **31 July**; primary AFSC **9-level** |

(AFH 1, ¶9.5)

> **MyPromotion SSgt/TSgt calendar cue (unofficial):**  
> **SSgt: PECD 31 Mar → test May–Jun (5-level).**  
> **TSgt: PECD 31 Jan → test Feb–Mar (7-level).**  
> Snapshot recall aid only; revalidate for duty and for any future catalog-controlled edition.

## 3. Accepting promotion can create retainability obligations (¶9.6)

Selects to **Master Sergeant** and **Senior Master Sergeant** with more than **18 years** Total Active Federal Military Service (TAFMS) by the effective date of promotion will sign a Promotion Statement of Understanding within **10 duty days** after selections are confirmed. In addition, **all Chief Master Sergeant-selects**, regardless of TAFMS, will sign a Promotion Statement of Understanding within **10 duty days** after selections are confirmed. (AFH 1, ¶9.6)

| Select | Retainability / ADSC acknowledgment in AFH 1 |
|---|---|
| MSgt-select and SMSgt-select | Obtain **two years** service retainability and incur a **two-year** ADSC from the effective date of promotion |
| CMSgt-select | Obtain **three years** service retainability and incur a **three-year** ADSC from the effective date of promotion |

Failure to withdraw an existing retirement application (approved or pending) within **10 duty days** from presentation of the Promotion Statement of Understanding results in removal from the selection list. (AFH 1, ¶9.6)

## 4. Promotion sequence numbers order projected promotions (¶9.7)

AFPC assigns promotion sequence numbers to Airmen selected for promotion to Staff Sergeant through Chief Master Sergeant based on **date of rank**, **TAFMS**, and **date of birth**. Supplemental selectees are assigned promotion sequence numbers of **.9** (increment previously announced) or **.5** (unannounced future increment). (AFH 1, ¶9.7)

## 5. Declining promotion is a written, non-reinstating act (¶9.8)

Airmen may decline a promotion in writing by submitting a letter to the MPF any time prior to the promotion effective date. The letter must include:

- name;
- social security number;
- promotion cycle;
- promotion sequence number if already selected; and
- a statement of understanding that **reinstatement will not be authorized**. (AFH 1, ¶9.8)

The MPF updates the Military Personnel Data System (MilPDS) and forwards a copy to AFPC for filing in the member’s Automated Records Management System (ARMS). (AFH 1, ¶9.8)

## 6. Ineligibility blocks testing, consideration, and projected promotion (¶9.9)

AFH 1 lists many reasons an Airman may be ineligible for promotion, such as:

- approved retirement;
- declination for extension or reenlistment;
- court-martial conviction;
- control roster action;
- no commander recommendation;
- failure to appear for scheduled testing without a valid reason; and
- absent without leave. (AFH 1, ¶9.9)

When individuals are ineligible, they **cannot test**, **cannot be considered if already tested**, and a projected promotion, if already selected, **will be canceled**. (AFH 1, ¶9.9)

### Distinctions that matter at level B

| Do not confuse | Correct distinction |
|---|---|
| Eligibility factors list with a complete checklist for every case | ¶9.4 says factors “may include”; AFI 36-2502 carries detailed rules |
| SSgt PECD with TSgt PECD | 31 March vs 31 January in the printed snapshot |
| Declining promotion with later reinstatement | Declination includes understanding that reinstatement is not authorized |
| Ineligibility with a mere delay | Ineligibility cancels testing/consideration/projected promotion as printed |
| RegAF cycle rules with ARC vacancy rules | ARC note is contrast; chapter applies to RegAF |

## Common misconceptions

1. **“PECD is only a testing administrative date.”**  
   PECD determines eligibility consideration and what must be updated for selection folders and promotion evaluation briefs. (AFH 1, ¶¶9.4, 9.13)

2. **“I can decline now and ask to be put back later.”**  
   The declination letter must acknowledge that reinstatement will not be authorized. (AFH 1, ¶9.8)

3. **“If I already tested, ineligibility no longer matters.”**  
   Ineligible members cannot be considered even if already tested, and projected promotions are canceled. (AFH 1, ¶9.9)

4. **“Sequence numbers are random.”**  
   AFPC assigns them based on DOR, TAFMS, and date of birth. (AFH 1, ¶9.7)

## Section 9B summary

- Cycles support timely promotions, vacancy forecasting, workload balance, and PECD.
- Basic TIG/TIS/skill-level guidelines run from Amn through CMSgt; SSgt/TSgt snapshots include PECD and test windows.
- Certain SNCO selects must acknowledge retainability/ADSC via a Promotion Statement of Understanding.
- Sequence numbers, written declination, and ineligibility rules control projection, refusal, and cancellation. (AFH 1, ¶¶9.4–9.9)

# Section 9C — Preparation and Responsibilities

## Learning objectives

At ADTC level B, an Airman should be able to:

1. Explain individual responsibilities for promotion preparation and testing readiness.
2. Explain AFH 1’s enlisted promotion test-compromise rules as examinee responsibilities, including the group-study prohibition and stated exceptions.
3. Distinguish official study-reference distribution from commercial guides and from MyPromotion materials.
4. Explain PECD’s role for eligibility, CAFSC competition lanes, and record completeness.
5. Explain why the Data Verification Record must be reviewed and how it differs from DVB/SURF. (AFH 1, ¶¶9.10–9.14)

## 1. Preparing for promotion testing is an individual responsibility (¶9.10)

AFH 1 states that preparing for promotion testing is an **individual responsibility**. All promotion-eligible individuals must:

- know their eligibility status;
- maintain specialty and military qualifications;
- initiate a self-study plan;
- obtain all applicable study references as outlined in the Enlisted Promotions References and Requirements Catalog (EPRRC);
- ensure their selection folder is accurate if it will be reviewed by the central evaluation board; and
- be prepared to test on the beginning of the testing window. (AFH 1, ¶9.10)

The importance of individual responsibility cannot be overemphasized. Members who will be unavailable during the entire testing cycle must be prepared to test **prior to departure**, even if that is before the first day of the testing cycle. Airmen may opt to test early provided the correct test booklets are available. (AFH 1, ¶9.10)

## 2. Enlisted promotion test compromise — source rules for integrity (¶9.11)

**Educational purpose of this section:** Explain what AFH 1 requires of Airmen so scores reflect individual knowledge. This is **not** instruction on how to compromise a test, recreate items, or evade security rules.

Airmen must use a **self-initiated program of individual study and effort** under WAPS. **Group study (two or more people) for the purposes of enlisted promotion testing is strictly prohibited.** The prohibition protects promotion-testing integrity by ensuring scores reflect each member’s individual knowledge. (AFH 1, ¶9.11)

AFH 1 states that enlisted personnel who violate these prohibitions are subject to prosecution under **Article 92** of the Uniform Code of Military Justice for violating a lawful general regulation. Conviction can result in a dishonorable discharge, forfeiture of all pay and allowances, and confinement for up to **two years**. Information concerning enlisted promotion test compromise is contained in **DAFMAN 36-2664**. (AFH 1, ¶9.11)

### What AFH 1 expressly does *not* treat as group study

- Training designed to improve **general military knowledge** does not constitute group study as long as the intent is **not** to study for promotion tests.
- Training to improve **general study habits** or **test-taking skills** is permissible if the training does **not** focus on preparing for promotion tests. (AFH 1, ¶9.11)

### Restrictions AFH 1 briefly covers

| Prohibited activity (as summarized in AFH 1) | Source point |
|---|---|
| Discussing promotion test content with anyone other than the test control officer or test examiner | Written inquiries/complaints about a test are handled by the test control officer |
| Sharing pretests or lists of test questions recalled from a current or previous promotion test; personal study materials; underlined or highlighted study reference material; and commercial study guides with other individuals | Integrity and individual-effort rules |
| Placing commercial study guide software on government computers | USAF does not recommend, endorse, or support commercial study guides; Airmen may still use commercial study materials in preparation |
| Creating, storing, or transferring personal study notes on government computers | Government computers may only be used to view electronic versions of **official** study references |

(AFH 1, ¶9.11)

### How to read these rules without overclaiming

- AFH 1 is a **handbook summary**. Current **DAFMAN 36-2664** is the controlling personnel-assessment/security publication and may distinguish potential examinees, examinees, and members who have accessed a test category.
- This lesson does **not** invent operational WAPS questions, “recalled” items, or sharing techniques.
- **MyPromotion** content is an independent educational explanation. It is **not** an official EPRRC study reference, not USAF-endorsed, and not a substitute for AFH 1, the WAPS Catalog, or official electronic references.
- Individual use of a legally developed commercial guide is addressed in broader DAFMAN context outside this AFH paragraph; AFH 1 itself stresses non-endorsement plus sharing and government-computer limits. Do not treat “may use commercial materials” as permission to share them or to place them on government systems. (AFH 1, ¶9.11; SOURCES_AND_GUARDRAILS status summary)

> **MyPromotion integrity cue (unofficial):**  
> **Study alone for WAPS; use official references; do not share marked materials, recalled items, or guides.**  
> Compresses ¶9.11 duties; not a complete DAFMAN restatement.

## 3. Official study references are distributed through official channels (¶9.12)

The Barnes Center for Enlisted Education, Air Force Career Development Academy, is responsible for providing promotion-eligible members access to WAPS Career Development Courses. They are available online at the USAF Enlisted Promotion Study Guides website (`https://www.studyguides.af.mil/`). The site is updated to coincide with release of the EPRRC. (AFH 1, ¶9.12)

Study materials such as instructions, manuals, or technical orders are made available online at the Air Force Departmental Publishing Office website (`https://www.e-publishing.af.mil/`) or other approved repositories. Individuals may ask unit WAPS monitors to order study reference material listed in the catalog that is not locally available. (AFH 1, ¶9.12)

According to DAFMAN 36-2664, promotion-eligible Airmen must have access to reference materials at least **60 days** before the scheduled test date. If not, the Airman may request a delay in testing. (AFH 1, ¶9.12)

| Source | Role for examinees |
|---|---|
| EPRRC / studyguides.af.mil | Official catalog of authorized tests and study references; WAPS CDCs |
| e-Publishing / approved repositories | Official publications used as study references |
| Unit WAPS monitor | Helps obtain catalog-listed material not locally available |
| MyPromotion (this product) | Unofficial explanation only — **not** an EPRRC study reference |

## 4. The PECD freezes the competition picture (¶9.13)

The promotion eligibility cutoff date (PECD) is used to determine Airman promotion eligibility and is the cutoff for when contents of the selection folder and information on promotion evaluation briefs must be updated and complete. The PECD is the date that promotion criteria are considered for promotion—such as data on the Data Verification Brief used for promotion board evaluation consideration. For SNCOs, that information is used to post the SNCO evaluation brief filed in the SNCO selection folder. (AFH 1, ¶9.13)

### Promotion criteria and career-broadening identifiers

Individuals with a reporting identifier or special duty identifier designated as their CAFSC on the PECD compete within that reporting identifier or special duty identifier. Otherwise:

- Master Sergeants competing for SMSgt compete in the **superintendent level** of the control AFSC held as of the PECD.
- Senior Master Sergeants competing for CMSgt compete in the **chief enlisted manager code** of the control AFSC held as of the PECD. (AFH 1, ¶9.13)

## 5. The Data Verification Record is the critical accuracy tool (¶9.14)

The Data Verification Record (DVR) is the document used for promotion consideration and is the **most important tool** to review to ensure information on a promotion record is complete. All eligible Airmen must review their DVR in ARMS/PRDA. If an error or omission is noted, the Airman must immediately contact the MPF for assistance. # Section 9D — Promotion Testing

## Learning objectives

At ADTC level B, an Airman should be able to:

1. Explain who develops enlisted promotion tests and how PFE and SKT content roles differ.
2. Explain what the EPRRC is, when it is published, and what it lists.
3. Distinguish electronic test administration/scoring from exceptional paper administration.
4. Apply the minimum score rules printed for PFE, SKT, combined score, and PFE-only testing.
5. Explain that complete promotion-point charts live in AFI 36-2502, not as a full reprint in AFH 1. (AFH 1, ¶¶9.15–9.18)

## 1. Promotion tests are written by Airmen for Airmen (¶9.15)

Air Education and Training Command, Studies and Analysis Squadron, Airman Advancement Section, Joint Base San Antonio-Randolph, Texas, produces all USAF enlisted promotion tests, which are written by Airmen for Airmen. Teams of SNCOs travel to work in-house with test development specialists and apply their knowledge and expertise to develop current, relevant, and accurate test questions. (AFH 1, ¶9.15)

At the beginning of a test development project, the most current tests are administered to the test development teams. This gives writers the examinee’s point of view and helps them evaluate how test content relates to performance in their specialties. Teams carefully check each question’s references for currency and accuracy before developing new questions. Test development specialists provide psychometric and developmental expertise to help ensure tests are credible, valid, and fair. (AFH 1, ¶9.15)

### Promotion Fitness Examination

PFEs measure military and supervisory knowledge required of Staff Sergeants and Technical Sergeants. For the PFE, test development specialists select Chief Master Sergeants based on extensive experience and major command representation to develop and validate questions. (AFH 1, ¶9.15)

### Specialty Knowledge Tests

SKTs measure important job knowledge required in a particular specialty. Test development specialists work closely with USAF career field managers to stay abreast of career-field changes that may impact promotion test development. SNCOs, based on specialties and job experiences, are selected to develop and validate SKT questions. Resources used to develop SKTs include Career Field Education and Training Plans, occupational analysis data, and experiences that keep content related to important specialty tasks. (AFH 1, ¶9.15)

## 2. The EPRRC lists authorized tests and study references (¶9.16)

Published annually on **1 October**, the Enlisted Promotions References and Requirements Catalog (EPRRC) lists all enlisted promotion tests authorized for administration and the study references associated with those tests. Every question on a promotion test comes from one of the publications listed in the EPRRC, and only publications used to support questions on a given promotion test are listed. (AFH 1, ¶9.16)

| Test | Study-reference pattern in AFH 1 |
|---|---|
| PFE | Grade-specific Enlisted Promotion Study Guides derived from AFH 1 |
| SKT | Often a combination of Career Development Courses and technical references |

Career Development Courses used as study references may be **different** from those issued for upgrade training. The catalog also contains administrative and special instructions for test control officers. The EPRRC is available at the USAF Enlisted Promotion Study Guides website (`https://www.studyguides.af.mil/`). (AFH 1, ¶9.16)

## 3. Tests are administered and scored under standardized controls (¶9.17)

Promotion tests are administered **electronically** to all Airmen competing for promotion to the grades of Staff Sergeant through Technical Sergeant. Test administration procedures are standardized to ensure fairness. All promotion tests are electronically scored at HQ AETC SAS following thorough quality-control steps to ensure accurate results. (AFH 1, ¶9.17)

In some cases a paper copy is administered. Those instances are the exception, must be approved by AFPC, and use strict procedures for handling, storing, and transmitting paper booklets and answer sheets. # Section 9E — Evaluation Boards

## Learning objectives

At ADTC level B, an Airman should be able to:

1. Explain why the promotion evaluation board is important in the SNCO selection framework described by AFH 1.
2. Describe board-member selection, panel composition, and the trial-run purpose.
3. Summarize what boards may consider and how scoring, split-vote resolution, and post-board processing work.
4. Explain score-notice access limits and the commander’s notification/counseling role.
5. Distinguish board-centered SNCO selection from WAPS testing for SSgt/TSgt without inventing a hybrid formula AFH 1 does not print. (AFH 1, ¶¶9.19–9.21)

## 1. Boards account for a major share of SNCO promotion score points (¶9.19)

AFH 1 states that the promotion evaluation board is very important because it accounts for **over half** the total promotion score points. Understanding board-member selection, the evaluation board process, and areas board members consider can provide insight into what is required to get promoted. (AFH 1, ¶9.19)

**Level-B framing for SSgt/TSgt learners:** Section 9E describes the **evaluation board** process used in the SNCO promotion framework. SSgt/TSgt primary competition under ¶¶9.3 and 9.15–9.17 centers on WAPS testing (PFE/SKT) and weighted factors within AFSC quotas. Learn board mechanics as handbook knowledge; do not assume SSgt/TSgt selection is board-scored the same way.

### Selection of board members

The number of promotion-eligible personnel, identified by major commands and AFSCs, determines the career-field backgrounds of board members. Board members are divided into panels, each consisting of **one Colonel** and **two Chief Master Sergeants**. The board president is always a **General Officer**. Before evaluating records, board members are briefed and sworn to complete the board’s task impartially. They also participate in an extensive trial-run process to ensure scoring consistency before evaluating any “live” records. (AFH 1, ¶9.19)

## 2. The evaluation board process (¶9.20)

The evaluation board looks at several areas regarding an Airman’s career, which may include:

- performance;
- education;
- breadth of experience;
- job responsibility;
- professional competence;
- specific achievements; and
- leadership. (AFH 1, ¶9.20)

Board scores vary from year to year—new panel members, changed or improved records, and a new pool of eligibles. While scores vary between panels, all records within a Chief Enlisted Manager (CEM) Code or AFSC are evaluated under the **same standard**. The important aspect of a final board score is how one eligible compares to peers in the final **order of merit**. Board members do **not** have access to the weighted scores of individuals competing for promotion. When board members disband, they do **not** know who was selected. (AFH 1, ¶9.20)

### Trial run

Board members are given two selected sets of records to score as a practice exercise using secret ballots before actual board scoring begins. The trial run helps establish a scoring standard applied consistently across the board. Each board arrives at its own scoring standard. Everyone competing in a CEM Code or AFSC is looked at under the same standard. (AFH 1, ¶9.20)

### Scoring

After the trial run is completed and discussed, panels begin actual scoring of live records. The same panel evaluates all eligibles competing in a CEM Code or AFSC. Each panel member scores each record using a **6- to 10-point** scale in **half-point** increments. An individual’s record may receive a panel composite score (three members) from a minimum of **18** (6-6-6) to a maximum of **30** (10-10-10) points. The composite score is later multiplied by a factor of **15**, resulting in a total board score of **270 to 450**. (AFH 1, ¶9.20)

Using a secret ballot, panel members score the record individually with **no discussion**. Records are given to each panel member; after scoring, ballots go directly to a recorder. This ensures independent scoring. (AFH 1, ¶9.20)

### Scoring resolution (split votes)

A record scored with a difference of more than **one point** between any of the panel members (for example, 8.5, 8.0, and 7.0) is a **split vote** and is returned to the panel for resolution. Panel members may then discuss the record openly. Only panel members **directly involved in the split** may change their scores. If they cannot agree, they give the record to the **board president** for resolution. This ensures scoring consistency and prevents one panel member from having a major positive or negative impact on an individual’s board score. (AFH 1, ¶9.20)

### Post-board processing

After the board finishes, weighted factor scores are combined with board scores. This electronic operation builds an order of merit listing by total score within each CEM Code or AFSC, and the overall promotion quota is applied to each list. After selection results are approved, data is transmitted to the military personnel section. (AFH 1, ¶9.20)

| Board arithmetic snapshot | Value in AFH 1 |
|---|---|
| Individual scale | 6 to 10, half-point increments |
| Panel composite range | 18 to 30 |
| Multiplier | ×15 |
| Total board score range | 270 to 450 |
| Split-vote trigger | More than one point difference among panel members |

## 3. Promotion score notices have strict access limits (¶9.21)

Promotion score notices give Airmen a report of their relative standing in the promotion consideration process and should **never** be provided to or used by anyone other than the individual and his or her **commander**. An Airman’s scores cannot be disclosed without the Airman’s **written consent**. Commander’s support staffs, first sergeants, supervisors, and similar personnel are **not** authorized access to promotion scores. (AFH 1, ¶9.21)

The commander has the specific duty to notify Airmen of promotion selection or non-selection results and may need to review score notices to determine status. Commanders must restrict use of the scores to **notification and advisory counseling** on behalf of the Airmen and must not allow further dissemination. (AFH 1, ¶9.21)

Individuals may retrieve a copy of their score notice on the virtual military personnel flight after the initial promotion selection for the current cycle. Individuals can also compare their scores with promotion statistics available in the MPF or posted on vMPF through AFPC (`https://www.afpc.af.mil/Promotion/Enlisted-Promotions/`). (AFH 1, ¶9.21)

### Invented example: score privacy

**Invented, non-operational instructional example:** An NCO asks a supervisor to “pull my WAPS scores so we can study what I missed.” Under ¶9.21, supervisors are not authorized access to promotion scores, and scores are not a shared study worksheet. The Airman retrieves his or her own notice through authorized self-service channels; the commander’s role is notification and advisory counseling, not wide redistribution.

This example illustrates the access rule; it does not recreate any actual score notice or test content. (AFH 1, ¶9.21)

### Distinctions that matter at level B

| Do not confuse | Correct distinction |
|---|---|
| Board score with weighted factor scores | Board members do not see weighted scores while scoring records |
| Panel disagreement with automatic president action | Split votes return to the panel first; president resolves only if unresolved |
| Order of merit within CEM/AFSC with one Air Force-wide list | Quota is applied to each CEM Code or AFSC list |
| Commander access with first sergeant/supervisor access | Only the individual and commander (for notification/counseling); others need the Airman’s written consent |
| SSgt/TSgt WAPS testing with SNCO board scoring mechanics | Related promotion architecture; different primary selection mechanisms as described across ¶¶9.3 and 9.15–9.20 |

## Common misconceptions

1. **“Board members know who got selected before they leave.”**  
   When they disband, they do not know who was selected; they also lack access to weighted scores during scoring. (AFH 1, ¶9.20)

2. **“Any score difference among panel members is a split.”**  
   A split is a difference of **more than one point** between any panel members. (AFH 1, ¶9.20)

3. **“My first sergeant can look up everyone’s scores to mentor the shop.”**  
   CSS, first sergeants, supervisors, etc., are not authorized access; disclosure requires the Airman’s written consent. (AFH 1, ¶9.21)

4. **“The board alone is the entire score.”**  
   Post-board processing combines weighted factor scores with board scores before applying quotas to the order of merit. (AFH 1, ¶9.20)

## Section 9E summary

- Evaluation boards are described as accounting for over half of total promotion score points in the SNCO framework; panels are one Colonel and two CMSgts under a General Officer president.
- Boards may consider performance, education, experience breadth, responsibility, competence, achievements, and leadership; trial runs set a consistent standard.
- Scoring uses a 6–10 half-point scale, 18–30 composites, ×15 for 270–450 board points, with split-vote resolution rules.
- Score notices are for the Airman and commander only (notification/advisory counseling), unless the Airman consents in writing to disclosure. (AFH 1, ¶¶9.19–9.21)

# Chapter synthesis

## The integrated promotion model

AFH 1 Chapter 9 connects purpose, constraint, opportunity, eligibility, individual integrity, testing, and board evaluation:

> EPMS purposes → quota limits → BTZ/STEP/WAPS/SNCO board/supplementals → cycle eligibility & PECD → individual preparation & test integrity → official references & scores → (for SNCOs) board order of merit → controlled score feedback

That chain is a **MyPromotion explanation**, not an official process map. (AFH 1, ¶¶9.1–9.21)

## High-value relationships

### Quotas and fairness

Equal pay for equal work and visible career progression operate inside numerical and fiscal limits. Fairness in AFH 1’s telling includes standardized consideration rules, within-AFSC competition, tie-at-cutoff promotion, and controlled testing/board procedures—not unlimited vacancies. (AFH 1, ¶¶9.1–9.3, 9.17, 9.20)

### PECD as the freeze point

PECD decides whose data/picture counts for eligibility and which CAFSC/RI/SDI/superintendent/CEM lane a competitor is in. Record completeness obligations attach to that date. (AFH 1, ¶¶9.4, 9.13–9.14)

### Individual effort and test integrity

WAPS depends on individual knowledge. Group study for promotion testing, sharing recalled questions or marked materials, and misuse of government computers for personal notes or commercial guide software attack that premise. General military-knowledge or study-skills training remains distinguishable when it is not promotion-test preparation. (AFH 1, ¶¶9.10–9.11)

### Official references versus third-party materials

EPRRC-listed publications and official portals define the authorized study-reference set for testing. Commercial materials are not USAF-recommended or endorsed; MyPromotion is likewise unofficial and must not be treated as an EPRRC reference. (AFH 1, ¶¶9.11–9.12, 9.16)

### Testing versus boards

For SSgt/TSgt competition, AFH 1 emphasizes electronic PFE/SKT administration and minimum score rules. For SNCO promotion, AFH 1 emphasizes central evaluation boards, trial runs, independent scoring, split resolution, and combination with weighted factors into an order of merit. (AFH 1, ¶¶9.3, 9.15–9.20)

### Score privacy

Relative standing belongs to the Airman and, for notification/advisory counseling only, the commander—not the broader supervisory chain—unless the Airman consents in writing. (AFH 1, ¶9.21)
