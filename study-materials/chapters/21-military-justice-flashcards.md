## Section 21A — Military Law

### C21-FC-001
- **Prompt:** Why does AFH 1 say a special system of military laws and courts is required?
- **Answer:** Because military discipline enhances combat capability, the military environment and duties are unique, and military personnel serve throughout the world—so a special system is required to maintain good order and military discipline.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21A; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-002
- **Prompt:** Besides maintaining warfighting capability through good order and discipline, what does a fair military justice system safeguard according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** The morale of those who risk their lives in service to their nation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21A; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-003
- **Prompt:** What does AFH 1 identify as the most desirable means of maintaining standards?
- **Answer:** Effective leadership.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21A; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-004
- **Prompt:** What disposition options does AFH 1 list for a commander exercising military-justice discretion?
- **Answer:** Take no action; initiate administrative action; offer nonjudicial punishment; or prefer court-martial charges.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21A; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-005
- **Prompt:** Under Article 24a as described in AFH 1, which office has exclusive court-martial authority over covered offenses?
- **Answer:** The Office of Special Trial Counsel (Office of the Special Trial Counsel).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21A; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-006
- **Prompt:** What examples of covered offenses does AFH 1 name when describing OSTC exclusive court-martial authority?
- **Answer:** Sexual assault, murder, domestic violence, child pornography, and many other serious offenses.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21A; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-007
- **Prompt:** Who must personally decide to refer a case to trial by court-martial according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** The convening authority.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21A; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-008
- **Prompt:** What three types of court-martial may receive referred charges according to AFH 1 ¶21.2?
- **Answer:** Summary, special, or general.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21A; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-009
- **Prompt:** What general responsibilities does AFH 1 assign all Airmen regarding the military justice system?
- **Answer:** Be familiar with the system; correct marginal or substandard behavior or duty performance of subordinates; support the commander in applying the system for good order and discipline; become involved when breaches occur; and report violations to proper authorities.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21A; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-010
- **Prompt:** According to DAFPD 36-29 as printed in AFH 1, within how many days must RegAF or active-status Reserve members report a U.S. criminal-law conviction in writing?
- **Answer:** Within 45 days of the date of conviction, to the first-line military supervisor or appropriate designated official.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21A; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-011
- **Prompt:** According to AFI 1-1 as printed in AFH 1, within how many days must Airmen above E-6 report a civilian criminal-law conviction in writing?
- **Answer:** Within 15 days of the date of conviction, to their first-line supervisor (RegAF or active status in a Reserve Component).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21A; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-012
- **Prompt:** What are the four pillars of the military justice system in AFH 1?
- **Answer:** The U.S. Constitution; the Uniform Code of Military Justice (UCMJ); the Manual for Courts-Martial (MCM); and legal precedents.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21A; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-013
- **Prompt:** What is the first pillar and ultimate source of authority for military law according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** The U.S. Constitution.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21A; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-014
- **Prompt:** When was the UCMJ enacted, signed, and made effective according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Enacted by Congress in 1950, signed by President Harry S. Truman, and effective in 1951.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21A; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-015
- **Prompt:** Name several military-unique offenses AFH 1 lists under the UCMJ.
- **Answer:** Examples include desertion, AWOL, disrespect toward superiors, failure to obey orders, dereliction of duty, wrongful disposition of military property, drunk while on duty, malingering, and conduct unbecoming an officer.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21A; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-016
- **Prompt:** What do MCM Parts I through V cover according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Part I Preamble; Part II Rules for Courts-Martial; Part III Military Rules of Evidence; Part IV punitive articles; Part V Article 15 nonjudicial punishment rules and procedures.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21A; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-017
- **Prompt:** How do AFCCA and CAAF decisions normally function as precedent according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** A decision of the Air Force Court of Criminal Appeals, or its superior court the Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces, normally serves as binding precedent in future courts-martial or appeals where the same issue arises.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21A; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-018
- **Prompt:** Do court-martial panels have to reach a unanimous verdict to convict under AFH 1?
- **Answer:** No—panels are not required to reach a unanimous verdict to convict, except in capital cases (where the death penalty is an authorized punishment).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21A; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-019
- **Prompt:** What is Article 138, UCMJ, designed to address according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Complaints of wrongs—protecting members’ rights and addressing grievances when members believe they have been wronged by their commanding officer.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21A; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-020
- **Prompt:** Within what period must a member ordinarily apply for Article 138 redress according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Within 90 calendar days of discovery of the wrong complained of (absent unusual circumstances).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21A; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-021
- **Prompt:** Which adverse outcomes does AFH 1 say normally cannot be the subject of an Article 138 complaint because they have their own appeal processes?
- **Answer:** Examples include a conviction at court-martial, Article 15 nonjudicial punishment, or a poor enlisted evaluation report.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21A; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-022
- **Prompt:** Before interrogation of a suspect, what three advisements does Article 31 require according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Tell the person the nature of the accusation; advise that he or she need not make any statement regarding the offense; and advise that any statement made may be used as evidence against him or her in a trial by court-martial.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21A; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-023
- **Prompt:** What must happen if a suspect requests counsel before or during interrogation according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Questioning must cease until counsel is present.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21A; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-024
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 describe Area Defense Counsel independence from base leadership?
- **Answer:** ADCs work for a separate chain of command and report directly to senior defense attorneys; they do not report to anyone at base level, including the wing commander or base Staff Judge Advocate, ensuring undivided loyalty to the client.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21A; B—Understanding

## Section 21B — Legal Enforcement

### C21-FC-025
- **Prompt:** What is apprehension under AFH 1, and what civilian concept does it equal?
- **Answer:** Apprehension is the act of taking a person into custody and is the equivalent of a civilian arrest.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21B; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-026
- **Prompt:** What oral statement does AFH 1 say is usually sufficient notice of apprehension?
- **Answer:** “You are under apprehension.”
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21B; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-027
- **Prompt:** Absent exigent circumstances, who should apprehend civilians according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Law enforcement personnel.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21B; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-028
- **Prompt:** When may enlisted members not performing LE duties apprehend commissioned or warrant officers?
- **Answer:** Only on specific orders from a commissioned officer, or when such apprehension prevents disgrace to the service or prevents commission of a serious offense or escape of someone who has committed a serious offense.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21B; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-029
- **Prompt:** What two threshold conditions must exist before pretrial restraint may be ordered?
- **Answer:** A reasonable belief that the person committed an offense triable by court-martial, and circumstances that require restraint.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21B; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-030
- **Prompt:** Who may order pretrial restraint of an officer, and may that authority be delegated?
- **Answer:** Only an officer’s commander can order pretrial restraint of an officer; this authority cannot be delegated.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21B; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-031
- **Prompt:** Who may order pretrial restraint of an enlisted person according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Any commissioned officer may order pretrial restraint of any enlisted person; an enlisted person’s commander may also delegate such restraint authority to an enlisted member.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21B; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-032
- **Prompt:** What are conditions on liberty as a pretrial restraint?
- **Answer:** Orders directing a person to do or refrain from specified acts—such as reporting periodically, staying away from a place, or not associating with specified persons—and they must not hinder pretrial preparation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21B; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-033
- **Prompt:** How do restrictions in lieu of arrest differ from arrest in duty expectations according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** During restrictions in lieu of arrest, the alleged offender performs full military duties unless otherwise directed; a person in arrest is not expected to perform full military duties.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21B; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-034
- **Prompt:** What kind of restraint is “arrest” as a pretrial restraint in the Armed Forces?
- **Answer:** A moral restraint limiting liberty by directing a person to remain within specified limits; no physical restraint is exercised to prevent breaking arrest, and it is not imposed as punishment.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21B; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-035
- **Prompt:** What three beliefs constitute probable cause to order pretrial confinement?
- **Answer:** Reasonable belief that (1) an offense triable by court-martial has been committed, (2) the person confined committed it, and (3) confinement is required by the circumstances.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21B; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-036
- **Prompt:** What four things must a person ordered into pretrial confinement promptly be informed of?
- **Answer:** Nature of the offenses; right to remain silent and that statements may be used against the person; right to retain civilian counsel at no U.S. expense and to request military counsel; and procedures by which pretrial confinement will be reviewed.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21B; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-037
- **Prompt:** What is “authorization to search” in military practice according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** The military equivalent of a civilian search warrant—an express permission, written or oral, issued by a competent military authority to search a person or area for specified property or evidence, or for a specific person, and to seize such property, evidence, or person.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21B; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-038
- **Prompt:** When does probable cause to search exist according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** When there is a reasonable belief that the person, property, or evidence sought is located in the place or on the person to be searched.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21B; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-039
- **Prompt:** Who may authorize probable cause searches and seizures over persons subject to military law or places on an installation?
- **Answer:** Commanders, military judges, and magistrates (as authorized).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21B; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-040
- **Prompt:** What neutrality requirement applies to a commander who authorizes a search or seizure?
- **Answer:** The commander must be neutral and detached from the case and facts; gathering facts and maintaining discipline must remain separate from the legal decision to grant search authorization.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21B; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-041
- **Prompt:** What is the primary purpose of an inspection according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Determining and ensuring the security, military fitness, or good order and discipline of a unit, organization, or installation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21B; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-042
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 distinguish an inspection from a search?
- **Answer:** An inspection is not conducted for the primary purpose of obtaining evidence for trial or other disciplinary proceedings and does not focus on a particular suspect or individual.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21B; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-043
- **Prompt:** Is contraband seized during an inspection admissible in court according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Yes—contraband seized during an inspection (for example, vehicle entry checks and random drug testing) is admissible in court.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21B; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-044
- **Prompt:** May commanders conduct inspections of their units according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Yes. Commanders may conduct inspections of their units; inspections are not searches.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21B; B—Understanding

## Section 21C — Nonjudicial Punishment

### C21-FC-045
- **Prompt:** What is nonjudicial punishment under Article 15 designed to provide commanders?
- **Answer:** An essential and prompt means of maintaining good order and discipline that promotes positive behavior changes without the stigma of a court-martial conviction.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21C; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-046
- **Prompt:** Who can be punished by Article 15 according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Any USAF member.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21C; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-047
- **Prompt:** Ordinarily, what maximum-sentence cue marks a “minor offense” for Article 15 purposes?
- **Answer:** An offense for which the maximum sentence authorized would not include a dishonorable discharge or confinement for more than one year if tried by general court-martial.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21C; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-048
- **Prompt:** With whom must commanders confer before initiating NJP and before imposing punishment?
- **Answer:** The Staff Judge Advocate, or a designee.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21C; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-049
- **Prompt:** What standard of proof must commanders apply in determining guilt under Article 15?
- **Answer:** Preponderance of the evidence—more likely than not that the member committed the alleged offense.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21C; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-050
- **Prompt:** Which AF Forms document Record of Nonjudicial Punishment Proceedings for AB–SSgt, TSgt–CMSgt, and officers?
- **Answer:** AF Form 3070A (AB thru SSgt); AF Form 3070B (TSgt thru CMSgt); AF Form 3070C (Officer).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21C; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-051
- **Prompt:** How soon after Article 15 notification must a member accept or reject NJP?
- **Answer:** The member is not required to accept or reject sooner than 72 hours following notification (extensions may be granted for good cause upon written application).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21C; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-052
- **Prompt:** Does acceptance of nonjudicial punishment mean the member admits guilt?
- **Answer:** No. Acceptance is a choice of forum, not an acceptance of guilt.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21C; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-053
- **Prompt:** What forum right does an Airman have instead of accepting Article 15?
- **Answer:** The right to turn down NJP and demand trial by court-martial for the accused offense(s).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21C; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-054
- **Prompt:** What matters may a member present if accepting the NJP forum?
- **Answer:** Matters in defense, extenuation, or mitigation, and a personal appearance before the commander (except in extraordinary circumstances or when the commander is unavailable).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21C; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-055
- **Prompt:** What punishment types may Article 15 include according to AFH 1 ¶21.10?
- **Answer:** Reduction in grade, forfeiture of pay, restrictions, extra duties, and correctional custody (limited by imposing commander’s grade and offender’s grade).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21C; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-056
- **Prompt:** According to Table 21.1, may a lieutenant or captain impose NJP on a CMSgt or SMSgt?
- **Answer:** No. A lieutenant or captain may not impose nonjudicial punishment on CMSgt or SMSgt.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.10; Table 21.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21C; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-057
- **Prompt:** According to Table 21.1, what is the maximum correctional custody a major may impose?
- **Answer:** 30 days.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.10; Table 21.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21C; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-058
- **Prompt:** According to Table 21.1 notes, who may reduce a CMSgt or SMSgt one grade under NJP?
- **Answer:** Only MAJCOM commanders, commanders of unified or specified commands, or commanders to whom promotion authority to these grades has been delegated.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.10; Table 21.1 notes
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21C; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-059
- **Prompt:** Are bread and water or diminished rations authorized NJP punishments according to AFH 1 Table 21.1 notes?
- **Answer:** No. Bread and water and diminished rations punishments are not authorized.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.10; Table 21.1 notes
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21C; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-060
- **Prompt:** What NJP authority does a frocked commander exercise according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Only the authority associated with the actual pay grade; no authority is conferred by the frocked grade.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.10; Table 21.1 notes
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21C; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-061
- **Prompt:** Within what time and in what form must a member appeal Article 15?
- **Answer:** Within five calendar days, in a written appeal; an oral statement is not acceptable.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21C; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-062
- **Prompt:** If appeal action is not taken within five days and the member requests, what unexecuted punishments are delayed?
- **Answer:** Any unexecuted punishment involving restraint or extra duties will be delayed until after appeal.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21C; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-063
- **Prompt:** What is suspension of Article 15 punishment?
- **Answer:** Postponing application of all or part of a punishment for a probationary period, with automatic remission (cancellation) at the end if the member does not engage in further misconduct; the probationary period may not exceed six months.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21C; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-064
- **Prompt:** When may suspension occur relative to execution of punishment?
- **Answer:** When the commander imposes the punishment or within four months of executing the punishment.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21C; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-065
- **Prompt:** What is remission of Article 15 punishment?
- **Answer:** An action canceling any portion of the unexecuted punishment, normally used as a reward for good behavior or when the punishment imposed was too severe.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21C; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-066
- **Prompt:** When do commanders use set-aside of Article 15 punishment according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Only when they believe, under all the circumstances, the punishment has resulted in clear injustice; set-aside restores property, privilege, or rights affected by the portion set aside.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21C; B—Understanding

## Section 21D — Courts-Martial

### C21-FC-067
- **Prompt:** Which UCMJ articles are the punitive articles according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Articles 77 through 134.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21D; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-068
- **Prompt:** What kinds of unspecified offenses does Article 134 address according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** “Disorders and neglects” punishable because they prejudice good order and discipline or otherwise discredit the armed forces in the view of society, plus certain military-specific offenses not addressed elsewhere.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21D; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-069
- **Prompt:** What three levels of courts-martial did Congress establish?
- **Answer:** General (GCM), Special (SPCM), and Summary (SCM).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21D; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-070
- **Prompt:** Who may be tried by summary court-martial, and what objection right exists?
- **Answer:** Enlisted members subject to the UCMJ for noncapital UCMJ offenses; no person subject to SCM jurisdiction may be brought to trial before SCM if he or she objects.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21D; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-071
- **Prompt:** Does a guilty finding at summary court-martial constitute a criminal conviction?
- **Answer:** No. SCM is a non-criminal forum; a finding of guilty does not constitute a criminal conviction.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21D; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-072
- **Prompt:** What is the composition of a summary court-martial?
- **Answer:** One commissioned officer.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21D; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-073
- **Prompt:** What confinement and discharge ceilings apply to special court-martial (traditional) under AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Among other limits, SPCM may not adjudge death, dishonorable discharge, dismissal, or confinement for more than one year (plus listed hard-labor and forfeiture limits).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21D; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-074
- **Prompt:** How many members are on a special court-martial panel when members are used?
- **Answer:** A panel of four members (plus alternate members if authorized by the convening authority).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21D; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-075
- **Prompt:** What additional punishment limits apply if charges are referred to a judge-alone special court-martial?
- **Answer:** Confinement of no more than six months and forfeitures of pay of no more than six months; a punitive discharge may not be imposed.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21D; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-076
- **Prompt:** What panel sizes may a general court-martial use according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** A military judge with a panel of eight or more members; a military judge alone; or in capital cases, a military judge with a panel of twelve members.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21D; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-077
- **Prompt:** What two jurisdiction questions must both be answered “yes” for a court-martial to decide a case?
- **Answer:** Personal jurisdiction (Is the accused a person subject to the UCMJ?) and subject-matter jurisdiction (Is the conduct prohibited by the UCMJ?).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21D; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-078
- **Prompt:** Give examples of purely military offenses for which courts-martial have exclusive jurisdiction.
- **Answer:** Desertion, failure to obey orders, or disrespect toward superiors.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21D; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-079
- **Prompt:** Who alone may approve trying a member by court-martial after a state-court trial for the same act, and when?
- **Answer:** Only SecAF, and only in the most unusual cases when the ends of justice and discipline can be met in no other way.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21D; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-080
- **Prompt:** What is trial counsel’s objective according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Justice, not merely securing a conviction.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21D; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-081
- **Prompt:** What disclosure duty does trial counsel have to the defense?
- **Answer:** An affirmative duty to disclose evidence that negates the accused’s guilt, mitigates the degree of guilt, or reasonably tends to reduce the punishment of the accused.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21D; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-082
- **Prompt:** To whom does the Office of Special Trial Counsel report according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Directly to SecAF with no intervening authority.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21D; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-083
- **Prompt:** May a military judge vote with court members according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** No. The military judge does not vote with the members of the court and may not consult with members except in the presence of the accused, trial counsel, and defense counsel.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21D; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-084
- **Prompt:** When was the Victims’ Counsel pilot program created, and what worldwide personnel count does AFH 1 print?
- **Answer:** Created in 2013; expanded to 101 world-wide personnel with an independent chain of command.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21D; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-085
- **Prompt:** What burden of proof applies to findings of guilt at court-martial?
- **Answer:** Beyond a reasonable doubt.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21D; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-086
- **Prompt:** What member-vote threshold is required for a finding of guilty (non-capital)?
- **Answer:** At least three-fourths of the members must vote for a finding of guilty; voting is by secret written ballot.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21D; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-087
- **Prompt:** What vote is required for a death sentence according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** A unanimous vote by panel members on both the findings of guilt and the propriety of death as punishment.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21D; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-088
- **Prompt:** When does the Air Force Court of Criminal Appeals automatically review a case (unless waived)?
- **Answer:** Cases involving a sentence that includes death, dismissal of a commissioned officer, a punitive discharge, or confinement of two years or more (appellate review cannot be waived in death-penalty cases).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21D; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-089
- **Prompt:** How is the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces composed according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Five civilian judges appointed by the President; it is the highest appellate court in the military justice system.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21D; B—Understanding

### C21-FC-090
- **Prompt:** After CAAF review without relief, where may an appellant petition for further review?
- **Answer:** The U.S. Supreme Court (petition for a writ of certiorari).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶21.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 21D; B—Understanding
