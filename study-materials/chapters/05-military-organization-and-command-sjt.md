# Chapter 5 Situational Judgment Practice

> **Status:** Draft and provisional for 27E5/27E6.
>
> These scenarios are wholly original. They are not actual, recalled, reconstructed, or predicted PFE items. The official Air Force uses subject-matter experts and behavioral scientists to establish operational SJT scoring keys. Chapter 5 describes organizations, authorities, and responsibilities, but those organizational facts alone do not establish an official behavioral scoring key. All most-effective and least-effective keys below are MyPromotion educational judgments grounded in the cited public material; they are not official scoring keys or substitutes for current command, supervisory, safety, legal, ethics, fiscal, technical, or reporting procedures.

## How to use this set

Each scenario produces two items:

1. Select the **most effective** response.
2. Select the **least effective** response.

Read every option before choosing. Evaluate whether the response:

- uses the proper chain or channel without suppressing a lawful concern;
- distinguishes operational authority from administrative authority;
- coordinates across joint, service-component, and Total Force relationships;
- reports facts accurately and identifies uncertainty;
- protects readiness, training, equipment, and shared resources;
- stays within delegated authority and avoids unsupported commitments; and
- reflects integrity, respect, mission stewardship, and teamwork.

Chapter 5 supplies the organizational boundaries for these scenarios; it does not prescribe the behavioral sequence in every response. When a rationale depends on general professional judgment—such as honesty, courage, accountability, loyalty, respect, mission focus, or teamwork—it is identified as an application of the cited foundational Chapter 1 paragraphs.

Ten scenarios produce 20 response selections, mirroring the historical public structure announced for the [22E5/22E6 cycles in December 2021](https://www.afpc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/2874484/waps-adjusts-for-22e6-22e5-testing-cycles/). The separate [public SJT informational guide](https://www.studyguides.af.mil/Portals/15/documents/Notices/Situational-Judgment-Test-Informational-Guide.pdf) explains the four-option most/least format. The 2027 structure is unconfirmed. This set covers only selected Chapter 5 concepts and is not a full-spectrum SJT simulation.

## C05-SJT-001 — A concern omitted from an update

You consolidate and transmit a resource update that the flight chief certifies. Verified records show that a recurring scheduling conflict meets the published reporting criteria and materially changes the current figure. After you show the flight chief the criteria and records, the flight chief maintains the direction to use the original figure. The local process permits referral to a reporting-review official when a known material discrepancy remains after flight-level discussion, and that official is available before today's deadline.

### Response options

A. Send the original figure with the verified adjustment described in an attachment for the reporting office.  
B. Hold transmission briefly and refer the discrepancy to the reporting-review official for a same-day ruling.  
C. Submit the original figure and retain the supporting records in the unit reporting file.  
D. Return the update to the contributing Airman for a new submission under the published criteria.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 5.1 supplies the organizational context by describing levels of responsibility and accountability through a chain of command; it does not create the reporting-review process stated in the scenario. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because the discrepancy is verified and material, flight-level discussion has occurred, and the supplied referral trigger now applies. Chapter 1 honesty, courage, accountability, and loyalty support using that established review path while leaving certification with the responsible officials.

### Least effective: C

C transmits a figure that the scenario establishes is materially inconsistent with the reporting criteria even though a same-day review path is available. Paragraph 5.1 explains the chain-of-command setting, but the MyPromotion least-effective judgment comes from Chapter 1 accurate-reporting and accountability standards. Under these stated facts, preserving the records does not resolve responsibility for the current update.

### Option analysis

- **A — Partially effective:** It makes the adjustment visible but selects a reporting treatment that the certifying and review officials have not resolved.
- **B — Most effective:** It uses the stated trigger for an unresolved material discrepancy and preserves the assigned certification roles.
- **C — Least effective:** It knowingly carries the material error into the current decision product despite an available review path.
- **D — Ineffective:** It seeks another submission even though the records and reporting criterion are already established, shifting rather than resolving the dispute.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.1, 1.3.1–1.3.2  
**Primary concepts:** Chain of command; lawful concerns; honesty; courage; accountability; loyalty  
**ADTC:** 5A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of escalation realism; current command and protected-channel procedures control real cases

## C05-SJT-002 — Two kinds of authority

You lead an Air Force support team assigned to a joint force. The joint operations cell sends an authorized change to an approved mission priority, while an Air Force administrative office sets a same-day personnel-roster deadline. Under delegated team-lead authority, you must allocate the work and report the result through both channels. The mission change affects tomorrow's sortie schedule. One specialist can produce a limited change protecting the highest-priority sorties; both specialists can complete the change. The remaining members can produce the roster fields used at the initial personnel review, while completing every roster field today requires one specialist.

### Response options

A. Assign one specialist to each requirement and deliver the limited mission change with a complete roster.  
B. Complete every roster field and place the mission change in tomorrow's regular work queue.  
C. Assign both specialists to the mission change and deliver the roster when every field is resolved.  
D. Assign both specialists to the mission change and have remaining members produce the initial-review roster fields.

### Most effective: D

Paragraphs 5.7 and 5.9 supply the organizational distinction between operational authority and continuing service administrative authority; they do not establish a universal priority rule for local workload conflicts. D is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it assigns scarce expertise to the time-sensitive operational change while using the rest of the team for the information needed by the initial administrative review. Chapter 1 accountability, discipline, and teamwork provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: B

B maximizes administrative completeness at the stated cost of placing the authorized mission change beyond the planning period it affects. Chapter 5 explains why both requirements are legitimate but arise through different authority relationships. The scenario-specific mission effect, together with Chapter 1 mission focus and accountability, provides the behavioral basis for MyPromotion's least-effective judgment; an administrative requirement is not inherently unimportant.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It produces a complete administrative product and protects the highest-priority sorties, but it does not complete the authorized mission change.
- **B — Least effective:** It achieves a complete administrative product by accepting the known operational shortfall.
- **C — Partially effective:** It protects the mission change but gives the personnel review less timely support than the available team can produce.
- **D — Most effective:** It aligns specialized capability with the operational task and preserves useful administrative support from the remaining team.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.7, 5.9, 1.3.1, 1.3.3  
**Primary concepts:** Operational control; administrative control; work allocation; mission effects; accountability  
**ADTC:** 5C, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of authority nuance; actual tasking orders and command relationships control real cases

## C05-SJT-003 — Provisional joint-planning data

You are the designated Air Force support representative on a joint planning team. An Army planner requests updated airlift assumptions for a consolidated brief. A service-component validation meeting is at 1400, and the brief locks at 1500. The planner must run a draft feasibility check before the meeting. The last validated set no longer reflects documented aircraft changes, while the latest set incorporates those changes but still awaits component review. The team may use clearly identified working assumptions in its draft, but you remain responsible for version control and for identifying the set that enters the locked brief.

### Response options

A. Keep the airlift section blank until the component meeting produces a single set of figures.  
B. Build from the last validated figures and reserve a clearly dated replacement block for the update.  
C. Use the latest set for draft calculations and personally confirm or replace it at brief lock.  
D. Give the planner both versions and ask the planner to select one when the component message arrives.

### Most effective: C

Paragraphs 5.5 and 5.9 supply the organizational context for interservice cooperation and Air Force service-component input; they do not create the draft, validation, or version-control rules stated in the scenario. C is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it supports the required feasibility check with the set reflecting documented changes while preserving the designated representative's ownership of the locked version after component review. Chapter 1 honesty, accountability, teamwork, and management of uncertainty provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A leaves a material section empty even though the stated process permits identified working assumptions and a validation decision is scheduled before brief lock. The Chapter 5 paragraphs explain the joint and service-component setting, but Chapter 1 mission focus, teamwork, and disciplined management of uncertainty provide the behavioral basis for MyPromotion's least-effective designation.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It deprives the draft of usable airlift assumptions despite a controlled way to represent uncertainty.
- **B — Partially effective:** It provides a reliable baseline and space for revision, but it underuses material current information.
- **C — Most effective:** It supports current planning while retaining accurate status and version ownership through brief lock.
- **D — Ineffective:** It shares useful information but transfers the designated representative's version-control judgment to the planner.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.5, 5.9, 1.3.1, 1.3.3, 1.4.1  
**Primary concepts:** Joint coordination; service-component input; data validation; honesty; teamwork  
**ADTC:** 5C, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human joint-staff review; current release rules and validated planning procedures control real data

## C05-SJT-004 — One team, different statuses

You are the staffing adviser for a high-visibility improvement project at a Total Force organization. You are authorized to evaluate candidates and recommend both the slate and initial roles; the project lead approves the final selection. Published mission criteria require complementary technical skill, continuity with the affected process, and availability at three decision points. Duty-status, labor, and basic qualification limits have already been verified for Regular Air Force, Air National Guard, Air Force Reserve, and civilian candidates. No workforce category is uniformly stronger across the criteria, and status representation is not a selection requirement.

### Response options

A. Recommend role assignments pairing technical specialists with members providing process continuity at each decision point.  
B. Recommend the project lead's initial slate and use selected specialists as scheduled advisers for identified gaps.  
C. Recommend the highest technical scorers available at the decision points and use a project-history brief for continuity.  
D. Recommend candidates with the greatest schedule overlap and use team orientation to distribute specialized work.

### Most effective: A

Paragraph 5.10 supplies the organizational context by describing the Total Force as one team and identifying component and civilian contributions; it does not require a project to include every status group or prescribe individual assignments. A is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it applies every published mission criterion and integrates complementary contributions through defined roles. Chapter 1 professionalism, respect, mission focus, and teamwork provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D treats schedule overlap as the dominant staffing method and relies on orientation to distribute work that the scenario says requires complementary technical skill and process continuity. Paragraph 5.10 supports integrating relevant contributions, not representation quotas. Chapter 1 mission focus, accountability, and teamwork provide the behavioral basis for MyPromotion's least-effective judgment.

### Option analysis

- **A — Most effective:** It evaluates people against the stated work and creates a role-defined team whose capabilities complement one another.
- **B — Partially effective:** It preserves the lead's preferred core while adding access to missing expertise, but advisers provide less integration and continuity than core roles.
- **C — Ineffective:** It covers technical and timing needs but treats a shared briefing as a substitute for candidate-level process continuity.
- **D — Least effective:** It optimizes one criterion and assumes orientation can supply the two capability dimensions omitted from selection.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.10, 1.1, 1.3.2–1.3.3  
**Primary concepts:** Total Force integration; component contribution; civilian contribution; respect; teamwork  
**ADTC:** 5D, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human Total Force review; actual qualifications, status agreements, and scheduling requirements control

## C05-SJT-005 — An incomplete headquarters return

Your wing is consolidating readiness figures for a major command (MAJCOM) headquarters request used for a same-day allocation decision. Headquarters accepts clearly qualified submissions, but the decision deadline is fixed. One squadron cannot validate its current return until tomorrow, and a current maintenance event could materially change its figure. You are preparing a recommendation to the wing reporting official.

### Response options

A. Recommend the dated prior-period value and its historical variance range as context for headquarters.  
B. Recommend a subtotal of validated returns with the missing squadron identified in the decision table.  
C. Recommend a current estimate from maintenance records, identified as wing-calculated for the allocation decision.  
D. Recommend treating the wing return as one package tied to the squadron's validation time.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 5.1 and 5.15–5.16 supply organizational context by identifying command levels and accountability relationships; they do not establish a reporting treatment. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it provides the current decision with all validated information while making the missing unit visible in the same decision product. Chapter 1 honesty, accountability, mission focus, and stewardship provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D ties every validated squadron's information to a validation time that the scenario places beyond the fixed allocation decision. The command levels in Chapter 5 explain the reporting setting, but Chapter 1 mission focus and accountability provide the behavioral basis for MyPromotion's least-effective judgment. D protects completeness but supplies no wing information to the current decision even though qualified submissions are accepted.

### Option analysis

- **A — Partially effective:** It is transparent and numerically complete, but a materially uncertain prior value can distort the current allocation.
- **B — Most effective:** It preserves current-period validation and gives headquarters usable, visibly qualified information for its decision.
- **C — Ineffective:** It offers a current approximation and labels its origin, but the estimate lacks squadron validation.
- **D — Least effective:** It preserves one complete package at the cost of excluding the entire wing from the stated current decision.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.1, 5.15–5.16, 1.3.1, 1.3.3  
**Primary concepts:** Headquarters reporting; command levels; data integrity; transparency; accountability  
**ADTC:** 5E, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human reporting-process review; current instructions control estimates, validation, and deadlines

## C05-SJT-006 — Air Reserve Component training resources

At a Total Force association, a required Regular Air Force readiness event has an unexpected equipment shortfall while Air Reserve Component (ARC) equipment is scheduled for its own required training. The Regular Air Force participants deploy in three days; their normal recurring event falls beyond that deployment. Available substitute equipment meets every required objective at lower throughput if the event runs as two serial sessions. You advise the official responsible for the resource decision, and any equipment change will use the applicable approval channel.

### Response options

A. Shift ARC equipment to the Regular Air Force event and reserve the first makeup window.  
B. Return the Regular Air Force event to its normal recurring date and preserve existing equipment schedules.  
C. Divide the ARC equipment between both events and reduce each session's planned student load proportionally.  
D. Run two Regular Air Force sessions with substitute equipment and retain the ARC training schedule.

### Most effective: D

Paragraphs 5.10–5.11 supply the organizational context for integrated associations, ARC readiness, equal equipping priority, and limits on withdrawal, diversion, or reassignment. They do not rank the two local events. D is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it uses a stated viable substitute to complete the deployment-linked event while preserving the scheduled ARC requirement. Chapter 1 accountability, mission stewardship, discipline, and teamwork provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: B

B preserves the current equipment schedules but moves the Regular Air Force requirement to a date the scenario places beyond the participants' deployment. Chapter 5 protects ARC readiness without creating a universal priority rule for either event. The known readiness consequence and the available substitute, together with Chapter 1 mission focus and accountability, provide the behavioral basis for MyPromotion's least-effective judgment.

### Option analysis

- **A — Partially effective:** It meets the deployment-linked requirement but displaces the ARC event and creates another training demand.
- **B — Least effective:** It preserves equipment assignments while knowingly placing the readiness event beyond the members' deployment.
- **C — Ineffective:** It shares the immediate shortfall but reduces the ability of both events to train their full student loads.
- **D — Most effective:** It accepts lower throughput to preserve the required objectives and schedules of both groups.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.10–5.11, 1.3.1, 1.3.3  
**Primary concepts:** ARC readiness; equipment priority; training stewardship; coordination; accountability  
**ADTC:** 5D, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human ARC and resource review; actual ownership, orders, and approval requirements control

## C05-SJT-007 — A Civil Air Patrol offer

You advise the scheduler for a non-combat search exercise. A local Civil Air Patrol (CAP) squadron offers aerial observation support. Full-block observation is a required exercise objective, and the existing ground-observer plan remains feasible. The designated USAF channel—not the local squadron—matches and assigns CAP support; a requester may provide availability information but submits the capability requirement. The schedule remains open for one support decision.

### Response options

A. Route the full observation requirement and treat the squadron's offer as availability information.  
B. Route the offered sortie as the request and reshape the observation block around its details.  
C. Route the full requirement naming the local squadron and aircraft as the preferred support source.  
D. Route the full requirement while asking the local squadron to reserve its offered aircraft.

### Most effective: A

Paragraph 5.12 supplies the organizational context by identifying CAP's auxiliary relationship, non-combat role, and USAF approval and assignment boundary; it does not prescribe the local request content stated in the scenario. A is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it communicates the complete mission need while allowing the assignment channel to match resources and use the local offer as relevant availability information. Chapter 1 duty, accountability, and teamwork provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: B

B lets the locally offered sortie define the request and reshapes the required observation block around that resource. Paragraph 5.12 explains the organizational boundary between a local offer and a USAF assignment, while Chapter 1 duty, mission focus, and accountability provide the behavioral basis for MyPromotion's least-effective judgment. The assignment channel needs the mission requirement in order to match appropriate support.

### Option analysis

- **A — Most effective:** It gives the assigning channel the actual requirement and treats the offer as information rather than a resource decision.
- **B — Least effective:** It makes the available sortie the organizing requirement rather than asking the assignment channel to support the stated objective.
- **C — Partially effective:** It preserves the full requirement and offers useful sourcing information, but it can narrow the assigning channel's choices.
- **D — Ineffective:** It preserves the full requirement but asks the offering squadron to reserve a specific asset outside the assignment decision.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.12, 1.3.1–1.3.3  
**Primary concepts:** CAP; USAF Auxiliary; non-combat support; approval; delegated authority  
**ADTC:** 5D, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human CAP review; current approval, mission, risk, and coordination procedures control

## C05-SJT-008 — A sister service's available equipment

At a joint base, Army-owned classroom equipment meets your section's approved training requirement and has capacity for the current and forecast classes. The joint-base support office can evaluate recurring sharing before your pending purchase reaches its funding decision, and the purchase can remain at its current stage meanwhile. No shared-use arrangement exists, and the Army custodian's availability forecast is not a support commitment. You are preparing a recommendation.

### Response options

A. Withdraw the purchase request and plan future classes around the Army custodian's availability forecast.  
B. Continue the purchase for section ownership and use Army equipment as contingency capacity.  
C. Maintain the pending purchase at its current stage while the support office develops shared-use terms.  
D. Convert the purchase request into a joint purchase with shared support responsibilities.

### Most effective: C

Paragraph 5.5 supplies the organizational expectation to use another service's resources when economy and effectiveness increase, while paragraph 5.14 supplies joint-partnership and stewardship context. Neither paragraph establishes a local sharing process. C is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it preserves a viable purchase path while the designated support office develops the arrangement needed to judge recurring economy and effectiveness. Chapter 1 accountability, mission focus, and teamwork provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A makes the irreversible purchase recommendation depend on a custodian's availability forecast that the scenario distinguishes from a support commitment. Chapter 5 encourages economical and effective interservice use but does not turn forecast capacity into a durable arrangement. Chapter 1 accountability and stewardship provide the behavioral basis for MyPromotion's least-effective judgment.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It closes the owned-equipment path while relying on availability that has not become a support arrangement.
- **B — Partially effective:** It protects section control and retains extra capacity, but it may duplicate a capability available for recurring use.
- **C — Most effective:** It keeps both outcomes feasible while the responsible office converts forecast availability into decision-ready terms.
- **D — Ineffective:** It creates a formal shared responsibility model, but buying another asset is poorly matched to the available existing capacity.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.5, 5.14, 1.3.1, 1.3.3  
**Primary concepts:** Cross-service support; economy; effectiveness; stewardship; joint partnership  
**ADTC:** 5B, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human support-agreement and resource review; actual ownership and approval rules control

## C05-SJT-009 — An oversimplified component brief

You are the assigned reviewer for an orientation brief at a Component MAJCOM (C-MAJCOM). A draft slide uses one arrow from the combatant commander (CCDR) to assigned Air Force units with the caption, “The CCDR directs operations and administration through the Commander, Air Force Forces (COMAFFOR).” The briefer wants one simple diagram. The local process sends every proposed command-relationship revision through component approval, so your task is to choose the content revision to submit.

### Response options

A. Relabel the single arrow as operational direction and place service administration in a footnote.  
B. Replace the arrows with definitions of OPCON, ADCON, and COMAFFOR for verbal explanation.  
C. Use parallel arrows from the CCDR and service headquarters directly to assigned Air Force units.  
D. Use aligned operational and administrative lanes that meet at the COMAFFOR-led component.

### Most effective: D

Paragraphs 5.7, 5.9, and 5.15 supply the organizational facts distinguishing CCDR authority, service administrative authority, and the COMAFFOR-led component role; they do not prescribe the briefing review process stated in the scenario. D is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it presents both relationships in one durable diagram while showing the component connection central to each. Chapter 1 honesty and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: C

C depicts the CCDR and service headquarters as acting directly on assigned units and removes the COMAFFOR-led component from both relationships. Chapter 5 establishes why that simplification misstates the organizational model, while paragraph 1.3.1 supplies the accurate-reporting and accountability basis for MyPromotion's least-effective judgment.

### Option analysis

- **A — Partially effective:** It correctly narrows the arrow to operations and acknowledges service administration, but the footnote obscures how the relationships connect.
- **B — Ineffective:** It supplies accurate terms but leaves their organizational relationship dependent on a verbal explanation.
- **C — Least effective:** It creates two direct relationships and omits the component commander who connects Air Force forces to the joint structure.
- **D — Most effective:** It preserves a simple visual while accurately distinguishing and connecting operational and administrative authority.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.7, 5.9, 5.15, 1.3.1  
**Primary concepts:** CCDR; COMAFFOR; operational authority; service responsibilities; accurate instruction  
**ADTC:** 5C, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human component-command review; governing orders and actual command relationships control

## C05-SJT-010 — A requested multinational commitment

An allied liaison asks you to commit Air Force support to a proposed combined exercise event by tomorrow. You are the staff point of contact and may clarify requirements. An established coordination channel gives you direct same-day access to the official who can accept commitments for the command. You have no delegated commitment authority.

### Response options

A. Acknowledge the request and send its current wording to the decision official through the staff channel.  
B. Clarify the requested capability, timing, and support conditions and send the resulting package through the staff channel.  
C. Advise the liaison to reserve resources for probable support while the command considers the request.  
D. Place the request on today's meeting agenda for the liaison to clarify directly with the official.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 5.6 supplies the organizational context for combined missions and relevant multinational agreements, while paragraph 5.1 supplies the context of assigned authority and accountability; neither prescribes this staff response. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it uses the access and fact-development role stated in the scenario while leaving the commitment decision with the responsible official. Chapter 1 accountability, teamwork, and the paragraph 1.4 principle governing promises that purport to bind the government provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: C

C creates a favorable planning expectation that the stated decision authority has not accepted. Chapter 5 explains the multinational and command setting, but Chapter 1 honesty, accountability, and the paragraph 1.4 commitment principle provide the behavioral basis for MyPromotion's least-effective designation.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It uses the direct channel promptly but gives the official an undeveloped requirement.
- **B — Most effective:** It performs the assigned staff role and gives the responsible official a complete basis for a timely decision.
- **C — Least effective:** It can cause the partner to allocate resources around an outcome the command has not accepted.
- **D — Partially effective:** It puts the liaison and official together the same day, but it uses a scheduled meeting for clarification the staff point of contact can perform.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.1, 5.6, 1.3.1–1.3.3, 1.4  
**Primary concepts:** Combined command; multinational agreement; staff coordination; delegated authority; commitments  
**ADTC:** 5C, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human multinational-coordination review; actual agreements and delegated authorities control
