# Chapter 5 Situational Judgment Practice

> **Status:** Draft and provisional for 27E5/27E6.
>
> These scenarios are wholly original. They are not actual, recalled, reconstructed, or predicted PFE items. The official Air Force uses subject-matter experts and behavioral scientists to establish operational SJT scoring keys. Chapter 5 describes organizations, authorities, and responsibilities, but those organizational facts alone do not establish an official behavioral scoring key. All most-effective and least-effective keys below are MyPromotion educational judgments grounded in the cited public material; they are not official scoring keys or substitutes for current command, supervisory, safety, legal, ethics, fiscal, technical, or reporting procedures.

## How to use this set

Each scenario produces two items:

1. Select the **most effective** response.
2. Select the **least effective** response.

Read every option before choosing. Evaluate whether the response:

- uses the proper chain or channel without suppressing a lawful concern;
- distinguishes operational authority from administrative authority;
- coordinates across joint, service-component, and Total Force relationships;
- reports facts accurately and identifies uncertainty;
- protects readiness, training, equipment, and shared resources;
- stays within delegated authority and avoids unsupported commitments; and
- reflects integrity, respect, mission stewardship, and teamwork.

Chapter 5 supplies the organizational boundaries for these scenarios; it does not prescribe the behavioral sequence in every response. When a rationale depends on general professional judgment—such as honesty, courage, accountability, loyalty, respect, mission focus, or teamwork—it is identified as an application of the cited foundational Chapter 1 paragraphs.

Ten scenarios produce 20 response selections, mirroring the historical public structure announced for the [22E5/22E6 cycles in December 2021](https://www.afpc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/2874484/waps-adjusts-for-22e6-22e5-testing-cycles/). The separate [public SJT informational guide](https://www.studyguides.af.mil/Portals/15/documents/Notices/Situational-Judgment-Test-Informational-Guide.pdf) explains the four-option most/least format. The 2027 structure is unconfirmed. This set covers only selected Chapter 5 concepts and is not a full-spectrum SJT simulation.

## C05-SJT-001 — A concern omitted from an update

You are consolidating a resource update when an Airman provides records showing a recurring conflict was omitted. Your flight chief says to leave it out because higher headquarters may ask difficult questions. The update is due today, but there is no immediate emergency.

### Response options

A. Seek process advice from another supervisor, but postpone reporting until the next scheduled update.  
B. Verify the records, seek correction from the flight chief, and escalate appropriately if unresolved.  
C. Remove the concern and tell the Airman not to raise substantiated issues outside the flight.  
D. Add the concern without notifying the flight chief, assuming accurate data justifies bypassing coordination.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 5.1 establishes levels of responsibility and accountability through a chain of command, but it does not prescribe this exact response sequence. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it applies Chapter 1 honesty, courage, accountability, and loyal use of the chain: verify the facts, address the issue at the current level, and escalate appropriately if it remains unresolved.

### Least effective: C

C intentionally suppresses a substantiated concern and reinforces silence. Its MyPromotion least-effective designation comes from the Chapter 1 standards for honesty, courage, accountability, and loyalty—not from treating the organizational description in paragraph 5.1 as an official behavioral key.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** Advice may help, but delaying a current, substantiated issue leaves today's decision-makers uninformed.
- **B — Most effective:** It verifies the concern, gives the current leader an opportunity to act, and preserves appropriate escalation.
- **C — Least effective:** It conceals relevant facts and discourages a lawful concern from reaching an appropriate level.
- **D — Partially effective:** It restores the information but bypasses coordination without first addressing the leader's decision.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.1, 1.3.1–1.3.2  
**Primary concepts:** Chain of command; lawful concerns; honesty; courage; accountability; loyalty  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of escalation realism; current command and protected-channel procedures control real cases

## C05-SJT-002 — Two kinds of authority

You support an Air Force element assigned to a joint force. The joint operations cell sends a lawful change to an approved mission priority, while an Air Force administrative office sends a same-day personnel-roster deadline. Your team cannot meet both without direction, and an Airman says whichever message arrived first controls all work.

### Response options

A. Complete the operational change first and leave the administrative office unanswered until it follows up.  
B. Tell the joint cell that the administrative office canceled its mission change, then redirect the team.  
C. Try to meet both silently, then report the conflict only if one deadline slips.  
D. Sort each request by purpose, verify it through the respective chain, and elevate the resource conflict before committing.

### Most effective: D

Paragraphs 5.7 and 5.9 distinguish combatant-command operational authority from service administrative authority. They do not resolve every local workload conflict. D is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because Chapter 1 accountability and teamwork support verifying each request in its proper channel and giving authorized leaders the conflict before the team acts.

### Least effective: B

B treats an administrative request as authority to cancel an operational direction and communicates a decision the scenario never delegated to you. Chapter 5 supplies the authority distinction; Chapter 1 accountability and discipline support the MyPromotion judgment that inventing authority is least effective.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It recognizes operational urgency but ignores a valid administrative requirement and fails to communicate.
- **B — Least effective:** It falsely claims a cancellation and redirects work without component or joint authorization.
- **C — Partially effective:** It attempts both requirements, but concealed conflict prevents timely prioritization and support.
- **D — Most effective:** It recognizes both authorities, verifies the requests, and places the conflict before the proper decision-makers.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.7, 5.9, 1.3.1, 1.3.3  
**Primary concepts:** Operational control; administrative control; verification; escalation; accountability  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of authority nuance; actual tasking orders and command relationships control real cases

## C05-SJT-003 — Provisional joint-planning data

You represent an Air Force support element on a joint planning team. An Army planner requests updated airlift assumptions before a consolidated brief. Your service-component focal point has not validated the latest numbers, but the planner believes they are final.

### Response options

A. Mark the latest numbers final to meet the timeline, planning a correction if validation changes them.  
B. Provide the last validated figures with their date, but defer new validation until the normal cycle.  
C. Tell the planner the data are provisional, coordinate immediate component validation, and promptly update the joint product.  
D. Refuse to discuss any assumptions because the planner belongs to another military service.

### Most effective: C

Paragraphs 5.3, 5.5, and 5.9 describe joint integration, interservice cooperation, and the Air Force service-component relationship. They do not specify how this staff exchange must occur. C is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because Chapter 1 honesty, accountability, and teamwork favor transparent uncertainty, prompt component coordination, and useful joint follow-through.

### Least effective: A

A knowingly converts provisional information into a final representation. Its MyPromotion least-effective designation rests on Chapter 1's requirement to report accurately and take ownership; the Chapter 5 paragraphs explain why reliable service-component input matters to the joint process.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It knowingly mislabels unvalidated numbers and makes the joint product unreliable.
- **B — Partially effective:** It clearly dates reliable information, but avoidably delays validation of the requested update.
- **C — Most effective:** It corrects the planner's assumption, uses the component channel, and supports the joint deadline with validated data.
- **D — Ineffective:** It substitutes service parochialism for the coordination expected in a joint force.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.3, 5.5, 5.9, 1.3.1, 1.3.3  
**Primary concepts:** Joint coordination; service-component input; data validation; honesty; teamwork  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human joint-staff review; current release rules and validated planning procedures control real data

## C05-SJT-004 — One team, different statuses

At a Total Force organization, qualified Regular Air Force, Guard, Reserve, and civilian members are available for a high-visibility improvement project. A lead proposes using only Regular Air Force members because different statuses complicate scheduling. The other partners hold relevant operational experience and continuity.

### Response options

A. Assign roles by qualifications, availability, and mission need, incorporating component and civilian expertise from the start.  
B. Divide every task equally by status, even when qualifications and schedules differ.  
C. Ask each group for constraints, but develop separate plans before combining them near execution.  
D. Reserve all meaningful work for Regular Air Force members and use other partners only for overflow.

### Most effective: A

Paragraph 5.10 describes the Total Force as one team, identifies the value of every component and civilian Airmen, and explains how integrated organizations share resources. It does not prescribe project assignments. A is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because Chapter 1 professionalism, respect, mission focus, and teamwork support using relevant talent fairly rather than assigning work by status alone.

### Least effective: D

D imposes a categorical status-based exclusion despite the stated qualifications and mission value of the partners. Paragraph 5.10 establishes their integral role; Chapter 1 respect and teamwork provide the behavioral basis for MyPromotion's least-effective judgment.

### Option analysis

- **A — Most effective:** It integrates the team around mission-relevant qualifications, availability, and complementary experience.
- **B — Partially effective:** It appears equitable but ignores the qualifications and schedules needed for effective assignment.
- **C — Ineffective:** It seeks useful constraints but postpones integration until coordination problems are harder to solve.
- **D — Least effective:** It marginalizes capable Total Force partners and discards relevant expertise.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.10, 1.1, 1.3.2–1.3.3  
**Primary concepts:** Total Force integration; component contribution; civilian contribution; respect; teamwork  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human Total Force review; actual qualifications, status agreements, and scheduling requirements control

## C05-SJT-005 — An incomplete headquarters return

Your wing is consolidating readiness figures for a MAJCOM headquarters request. One squadron's validated return is missing. The draft silently substitutes last month's figure, making the wing total appear fully current, and the reporting deadline is approaching.

### Response options

A. Hold the report past the deadline without notification until every subordinate return is validated.  
B. Flag the missing return, label any approved estimate, notify the reporting chain, and replace it after validation.  
C. Exclude the squadron from the total, note the omission in a footnote, and await questions.  
D. Submit last month's value as current because a delay could reflect poorly on the wing.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 5.1 and 5.15–5.16 identify command levels and accountability relationships, but they do not establish a local reporting method or authorize estimates. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because Chapter 1 honesty and accountability require accurate reporting and transparent uncertainty while the actual reporting chain decides whether a labeled estimate is permitted.

### Least effective: D

D knowingly presents stale data as current to protect appearances. The MyPromotion least-effective judgment is grounded directly in paragraph 1.3.1's professional requirement to conscientiously and accurately report findings, not in an invented behavioral rule attributed to Chapter 5.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It protects validation but misses the deadline without giving headquarters notice or options.
- **B — Most effective:** It exposes the gap, avoids unlabeled assumptions, and lets the proper reporting authority direct the submission.
- **C — Partially effective:** It discloses an omission, but submits an incomplete total without first coordinating the reporting approach.
- **D — Least effective:** It knowingly misstates the status and can distort headquarters decisions.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.1, 5.15–5.16, 1.3.1, 1.3.3  
**Primary concepts:** Headquarters reporting; command levels; data integrity; transparency; accountability  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human reporting-process review; current instructions control estimates, validation, and deadlines

## C05-SJT-006 — Air Reserve Component training resources

At a Total Force association, a Regular Air Force event has an unexpected equipment shortfall. Air Reserve Component equipment is already scheduled for required training, and no approved reallocation is documented. You are asked to recommend a response.

### Response options

A. Recommend pausing both events while leadership decides, without developing sharing or substitution options.  
B. Move the scheduled ARC equipment without approval and conceal the change until training concludes.  
C. Ask ARC leaders to absorb the shortfall even if several scheduled training events are lost.  
D. Confirm authority, coordinate with ARC leaders, and present alternatives protecting both readiness requirements.

### Most effective: D

Paragraph 5.11 states that ARC forces meet the same readiness standards, receive equipment at equal priority, and are subject to limits on equipment withdrawal, diversion, or reassignment. It does not decide the status of a particular local asset. D is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because Chapter 1 accountability, mission stewardship, and teamwork favor verifying authority and presenting coordinated alternatives that protect both requirements.

### Least effective: B

B combines an unapproved equipment move with deliberate concealment. Paragraph 5.11 establishes why ARC equipment and training cannot be treated as a convenient lower priority; Chapter 1 honesty and accountability make concealment uniquely least effective in this MyPromotion judgment.

### Option analysis

- **A — Partially effective:** It preserves leadership's decision but offers no analysis to reduce the impact on either requirement.
- **B — Least effective:** It bypasses approval, disrupts scheduled training, and hides the action.
- **C — Ineffective:** It seeks coordination but treats ARC training as the default source of losses.
- **D — Most effective:** It verifies authority, includes ARC leadership, and develops mission-focused options before resources move.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.10–5.11, 1.3.1, 1.3.3  
**Primary concepts:** ARC readiness; equipment priority; training stewardship; coordination; accountability  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human ARC and resource review; actual ownership, orders, and approval requirements control

## C05-SJT-007 — A Civil Air Patrol offer

During planning for a non-combat search exercise, a local Civil Air Patrol squadron offers aerial observation support. Your unit has no approved or assigned CAP support yet. A planner wants to list the sortie as confirmed so the schedule can close.

### Response options

A. Define the non-combat need, seek USAF/CAP approval, and schedule support only after authorized assignment.  
B. Announce support as officially assigned and direct CAP to launch before approval.  
C. Mark support tentative and ask volunteers to reserve time before initiating authorized coordination.  
D. Reject the offer because a civilian auxiliary can never supplement Air Force resources.

### Most effective: A

Paragraph 5.12 identifies CAP as the USAF Auxiliary in the applicable federal-service context and states that CAP missions are approved and assigned by the USAF for non-combat programs and missions. A is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it stays within those stated boundaries; Chapter 1 duty and accountability support clear coordination before representing support as committed.

### Least effective: B

B falsely announces an assignment and directs activity before approval. Paragraph 5.12 supplies the approval, assignment, and non-combat boundaries, while Chapter 1 honesty and accountability support the MyPromotion least-effective judgment.

### Option analysis

- **A — Most effective:** It defines an eligible need, uses the approval relationship, and avoids treating an offer as an assignment.
- **B — Least effective:** It misrepresents approval and attempts to direct CAP support without an assigned mission.
- **C — Partially effective:** It preserves flexibility but creates an expectation before beginning the authorized coordination.
- **D — Ineffective:** It avoids overcommitment but incorrectly rejects CAP's stated ability to supplement USAF resources.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.12, 1.3.1–1.3.3  
**Primary concepts:** CAP; USAF Auxiliary; non-combat support; approval; delegated authority  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human CAP review; current approval, mission, risk, and coordination procedures control

## C05-SJT-008 — A sister service's available equipment

At a joint base, Army-owned classroom equipment meets your section's approved training requirement and is available during the needed period. Your section has a pending request to buy duplicate equipment, but no shared-use arrangement exists yet. You are preparing a recommendation.

### Response options

A. Move the Army equipment into your workspace now and arrange permission after use.  
B. Ask about availability, but compare only purchase price and omit support or scheduling effects.  
C. Verify compatibility, availability, and costs; coordinate authorized shared use; recommend the more effective solution.  
D. Continue the duplicate purchase because separate ownership avoids future interservice coordination.

### Most effective: C

Paragraph 5.5 directs services to use other services' personnel, equipment, and facilities when economy and effectiveness increase, and paragraph 5.14 emphasizes joint partnership and resource stewardship. Neither paragraph authorizes informal transfer. C is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because Chapter 1 accountability, mission focus, and teamwork support a complete comparison and authorized coordination.

### Least effective: A

A takes another service's equipment without permission and plans to regularize the action afterward. Chapter 5 supports efficient shared use, not unilateral possession; Chapter 1 accountability makes the unauthorized move MyPromotion's uniquely least-effective response.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It bypasses ownership and coordination while exposing both services to avoidable disruption.
- **B — Partially effective:** It explores shared use but omits factors needed to judge overall effectiveness.
- **C — Most effective:** It evaluates both economy and effectiveness and coordinates before recommending shared use.
- **D — Ineffective:** Separate ownership may be convenient, but rejecting comparison wastes a supported joint opportunity.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.5, 5.14, 1.3.1, 1.3.3  
**Primary concepts:** Cross-service support; economy; effectiveness; stewardship; joint partnership  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human support-agreement and resource review; actual ownership and approval rules control

## C05-SJT-009 — An oversimplified component brief

You are reviewing an orientation brief at a Component MAJCOM. One slide says a CCDR controls every operational and administrative matter for assigned Air Force units and that the COMAFFOR merely relays messages. The briefer wants to keep it because a single chain is easier to explain.

### Response options

A. Remove the authority slide, flag the gap, and provide corrected material after formal review.  
B. Seek peer review, revise obvious errors, and brief before the component validates the changes.  
C. Present the draft unchanged because a simple single-chain explanation is easier to remember.  
D. Correct the distinctions using governing references, obtain component validation, and brief only confirmed relationships.

### Most effective: D

Paragraphs 5.7, 5.9, and 5.15 distinguish CCDR operational authority, service administrative authority, and the COMAFFOR-led component role. Those facts show the slide is inaccurate. D is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because Chapter 1 honesty and accountability support correcting and validating the material instead of knowingly teaching a convenient falsehood.

### Least effective: C

C knowingly preserves both inaccuracies for simplicity. Chapter 5 establishes the command relationships; paragraph 1.3.1 provides the professional basis for MyPromotion's least-effective judgment.

### Option analysis

- **A — Partially effective:** It prevents immediate misinformation but leaves an important orientation gap until follow-up.
- **B — Ineffective:** Peer review may improve the slide, but briefing unvalidated authority changes retains avoidable risk.
- **C — Least effective:** It knowingly teaches false command relationships and invites later mistakes.
- **D — Most effective:** It corrects both authority errors, uses appropriate references, and obtains accountable component review.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.7, 5.9, 5.15, 1.3.1  
**Primary concepts:** CCDR; COMAFFOR; operational authority; service responsibilities; accurate instruction  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human component-command review; governing orders and actual command relationships control

## C05-SJT-010 — A requested multinational commitment

An allied liaison asks you to commit Air Force support to a proposed combined exercise event by tomorrow. You can gather the requirement, but you have no delegated authority to commit forces or accept responsibilities on behalf of the command.

### Response options

A. Explain that approval is pending, but wait until the next routine meeting to raise the request.  
B. Clarify the request and deadline, make no commitment, and promptly elevate complete facts through authorized channels.  
C. Promise the requested support so partner planning continues, then seek approval after arrangements begin.  
D. Acknowledge the request and forward the original message to your supervisor without context or recommendation.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 5.6 states that combined missions and command responsibilities must conform to relevant multinational agreements, while paragraph 5.1 describes established levels of authority and accountability. They do not prescribe this exact staff action. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because Chapter 1 accountability, loyalty, and teamwork support timely, complete escalation without exceeding delegated authority.

### Least effective: C

C makes an unsupported commitment and allows a partner to rely on it before authorized review. Chapter 5 establishes that combined responsibilities require proper authority and agreement; Chapter 1 honesty and accountability support MyPromotion's least-effective designation.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It avoids a false commitment but delays a time-sensitive request without reason.
- **B — Most effective:** It develops usable facts, protects the partner's timeline, and preserves the authorized decision.
- **C — Least effective:** It exceeds stated authority and creates reliance before the command agrees.
- **D — Partially effective:** It reaches a supervisor but omits the context needed for timely decision-making.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.1, 5.6, 1.3.1–1.3.3  
**Primary concepts:** Combined command; multinational agreement; escalation; delegated authority; commitments  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human multinational-coordination review; actual agreements and delegated authorities control
