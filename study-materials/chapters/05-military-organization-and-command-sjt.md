# Chapter 5 Situational Judgment Practice

> **Status:** Draft and provisional for 27E5/27E6.
>
> These scenarios are wholly original. They are not actual, recalled, reconstructed, or predicted PFE items. The official Air Force uses subject-matter experts and behavioral scientists to establish operational SJT scoring keys. Chapter 5 describes organizations, authorities, and responsibilities, but those organizational facts alone do not establish an official behavioral scoring key. All most-effective and least-effective keys below are MyPromotion educational judgments grounded in the cited public material; they are not official scoring keys or substitutes for current command, supervisory, safety, legal, ethics, fiscal, technical, or reporting procedures.

## How to use this set

Each scenario produces two items:

1. Select the **most effective** response.
2. Select the **least effective** response.

Read every option before choosing. Evaluate whether the response:

- uses the proper chain or channel without suppressing a lawful concern;
- distinguishes operational authority from administrative authority;
- coordinates across joint, service-component, and Total Force relationships;
- reports facts accurately and identifies uncertainty;
- protects readiness, training, equipment, and shared resources;
- stays within delegated authority and avoids unsupported commitments; and
- reflects integrity, respect, mission stewardship, and teamwork.

Chapter 5 supplies the organizational boundaries for these scenarios; it does not prescribe the behavioral sequence in every response. When a rationale depends on general professional judgment—such as honesty, courage, accountability, loyalty, respect, mission focus, or teamwork—it is identified as an application of the cited foundational Chapter 1 paragraphs.

Ten scenarios produce 20 response selections, mirroring the historical public structure announced for the [22E5/22E6 cycles in December 2021](https://www.afpc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/2874484/waps-adjusts-for-22e6-22e5-testing-cycles/). The separate [public SJT informational guide](https://www.studyguides.af.mil/Portals/15/documents/Notices/Situational-Judgment-Test-Informational-Guide.pdf) explains the four-option most/least format. The 2027 structure is unconfirmed. This set covers only selected Chapter 5 concepts and is not a full-spectrum SJT simulation.

## C05-SJT-001 — A concern omitted from an update

You are responsible for consolidating a resource update when an Airman provides records suggesting a recurring scheduling conflict was omitted. Your flight chief believes the issue falls outside the update's scope and directs you to submit without it. The update is due today, but there is no immediate emergency.

### Response options

A. Ask another supervisor how similar issues are reported, then submit today's update unchanged while awaiting advice.  
B. Verify the records and seek correction through the flight chief, using another authorized channel only if needed.  
C. Submit the update as directed and retain the records for discussion during the next reporting cycle.  
D. Ask the flight chief to note that the issue is under review, without first verifying the records.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 5.1 supplies the organizational context by describing levels of responsibility and accountability through a chain of command; it does not prescribe how this flight-level reporting disagreement must be handled. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because Chapter 1 honesty, courage, accountability, and loyalty support checking the evidence, seeking correction at the current level, and preserving authorized follow-up.

### Least effective: C

C accepts the omission without testing the new evidence and postpones reconsideration through another reporting cycle. Paragraph 5.1 explains the chain-of-command setting, but the MyPromotion least-effective judgment comes from the Chapter 1 standards for accurate reporting, accountable action, and appropriate use of the chain.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It seeks useful advice but lets the current update proceed before that advice can affect it.
- **B — Most effective:** It verifies the issue, addresses the scope decision directly, and preserves an authorized path if disagreement remains.
- **C — Least effective:** It takes no current step to assess or report potentially material evidence and defers the issue for a full cycle.
- **D — Partially effective:** It seeks a transparent qualification but proposes changing the report before establishing what the records support.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.1, 1.3.1–1.3.2  
**Primary concepts:** Chain of command; lawful concerns; honesty; courage; accountability; loyalty  
**ADTC:** 5A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of escalation realism; current command and protected-channel procedures control real cases

## C05-SJT-002 — Two kinds of authority

You lead an Air Force support team assigned to a joint force. An authorized change to an approved mission priority is transmitted through the joint operations cell, while an Air Force administrative office sends a same-day personnel-roster deadline. Your team cannot meet both, and neither message states which requirement takes precedence.

### Response options

A. Start the mission change and notify the administrative office that its roster may be late.  
B. Treat the administrative deadline as controlling and redirect the team before checking the operational channel.  
C. Split the team between both requests and seek priority guidance if either effort falls behind.  
D. Present the conflict to the operational and administrative decision-makers before reallocating the team's work.

### Most effective: D

Paragraphs 5.7 and 5.9 supply the organizational distinction between combatant-command operational authority and service administrative authority; they do not set the priority for this local workload conflict. D is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because Chapter 1 accountability and teamwork support identifying the proper decision-makers and presenting the conflict before changing the team's work.

### Least effective: B

B assumes that an administrative deadline displaces an authorized operational change and redirects the team before checking that assumption. Chapter 5 explains why the two requests can arise through different authority relationships; Chapter 1 accountability and discipline provide the behavioral basis for MyPromotion's least-effective judgment.

### Option analysis

- **A — Partially effective:** It protects the operational change and communicates the likely roster delay, but assumes the priority instead of resolving it.
- **B — Least effective:** It lets an administrative deadline displace operational work before the appropriate operational authority is consulted.
- **C — Ineffective:** It preserves progress on both requests but delays guidance until resources may already be committed ineffectively.
- **D — Most effective:** It distinguishes the authority relationships and gives the conflict to those empowered to resolve it.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.7, 5.9, 1.3.1, 1.3.3  
**Primary concepts:** Operational control; administrative control; verification; escalation; accountability  
**ADTC:** 5C, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of authority nuance; actual tasking orders and command relationships control real cases

## C05-SJT-003 — Provisional joint-planning data

You are the designated Air Force support representative on a joint planning team. An Army planner requests updated airlift assumptions before a consolidated brief. The team's stated process requires service-component validation before new figures are labeled final. The latest figures await validation, but the planner believes they are final.

### Response options

A. Send the latest figures as final so the joint brief can proceed, and request validation afterward.  
B. Send the last validated figures with their date and leave the next update for the normal cycle.  
C. Label the latest figures provisional and seek timely component validation before giving the planner a final status.  
D. Share the provisional figures with their limitations, but leave validation follow-up to the planner.

### Most effective: C

Paragraphs 5.5 and 5.9 supply the organizational context for interservice cooperation and Air Force service-component input; they do not create the validation procedure, which the scenario states directly. C is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because Chapter 1 honesty, accountability, teamwork, and management of uncertainty support accurate labeling and ownership of timely follow-through.

### Least effective: A

A represents figures as final before the stated validation step and leaves correction for later. The Chapter 5 paragraphs explain the joint and service-component setting, but Chapter 1 accurate-reporting and uncertainty principles provide the behavioral basis for MyPromotion's least-effective designation.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It gives the joint team an unsupported final status and postpones the required check until after use.
- **B — Ineffective:** It supplies reliable but stale information and does not pursue the update requested for the current brief.
- **C — Most effective:** It communicates the present status accurately and owns both validation and follow-up with the planner.
- **D — Partially effective:** It shares useful current information honestly but shifts validation follow-through away from the designated representative.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.5, 5.9, 1.3.1, 1.3.3, 1.4.1  
**Primary concepts:** Joint coordination; service-component input; data validation; honesty; teamwork  
**ADTC:** 5C, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human joint-staff review; current release rules and validated planning procedures control real data

## C05-SJT-004 — One team, different statuses

You are the authorized staffing adviser for a high-visibility improvement project at a Total Force organization. You may recommend members and roles within already-approved duty-status, civilian-work, qualification, and schedule limits. Qualified Regular Air Force, Air National Guard, Air Force Reserve, and civilian candidates are available. The project lead proposes a Regular Air Force slate to simplify scheduling, although other candidates offer relevant experience and continuity.

### Response options

A. Recommend a team by relevant skill and availability, using complementary experience in each proposed role.  
B. Recommend available members from each group, then refine individual roles after the project begins.  
C. Gather each group's constraints and draft the project plan before selecting the final team.  
D. Accept the proposed staffing slate to simplify scheduling, revisiting other candidates only if a vacancy appears.

### Most effective: A

Paragraph 5.10 supplies the organizational context by describing the Total Force as one team and identifying component and civilian contributions; it does not prescribe project assignments or override the limits stated in the scenario. A is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because Chapter 1 professionalism, respect, mission focus, and teamwork support matching available capability to mission roles.

### Least effective: D

D accepts a convenience-based slate without comparing the relevant qualifications and continuity already available from other candidates. Paragraph 5.10 explains why integrated contributions matter organizationally; Chapter 1 respect, mission focus, and teamwork provide the behavioral basis for MyPromotion's least-effective judgment.

### Option analysis

- **A — Most effective:** It works within the supplied constraints and matches complementary capability to the project's needs.
- **B — Partially effective:** It creates an available integrated team but delays matching individual experience to project roles.
- **C — Ineffective:** It gathers useful constraints but develops the work before selecting the people whose expertise will shape it.
- **D — Least effective:** It adopts the convenient slate without evaluating candidates whose stated experience could improve the project.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.10, 1.1, 1.3.2–1.3.3  
**Primary concepts:** Total Force integration; component contribution; civilian contribution; respect; teamwork  
**ADTC:** 5D, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human Total Force review; actual qualifications, status agreements, and scheduling requirements control

## C05-SJT-005 — An incomplete headquarters return

Your wing is consolidating readiness figures for a major command (MAJCOM) headquarters request. One squadron's current-period validated return has not been received. The draft carries forward last month's figure without a label, making the total appear fully current. The deadline is approaching, and you are preparing a recommendation.

### Response options

A. Ask headquarters for a short delay while the squadron validates its current return.  
B. Disclose the missing return and ask the reporting chain how to handle the total before submission.  
C. Submit the total without that squadron, identifying the omission in a note for headquarters.  
D. Retain the carried-forward figure as a placeholder and reconcile it in the next update.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 5.1 and 5.15–5.16 supply organizational context by identifying command levels and accountability relationships; they do not establish a reporting method or authorize an estimate. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because Chapter 1 honesty and accountability support disclosing the gap and obtaining a timely decision from the actual reporting authority.

### Least effective: D

D uses a known prior-period figure without identifying it as such, allowing the total to appear current until a later reconciliation. The command levels in Chapter 5 explain the reporting setting, but paragraph 1.3.1's accurate-reporting standard provides the behavioral basis for MyPromotion's least-effective judgment.

### Option analysis

- **A — Partially effective:** It protects validation and communicates the problem but assumes a delay is preferable to other approved treatments.
- **B — Most effective:** It makes the gap visible and lets the responsible reporting authority choose an approved treatment before submission.
- **C — Ineffective:** It is transparent about the omission but selects the reporting treatment without first coordinating it.
- **D — Least effective:** It preserves a complete-looking total by leaving a stale value unlabeled during the current decision cycle.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.1, 5.15–5.16, 1.3.1, 1.3.3  
**Primary concepts:** Headquarters reporting; command levels; data integrity; transparency; accountability  
**ADTC:** 5E, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human reporting-process review; current instructions control estimates, validation, and deadlines

## C05-SJT-006 — Air Reserve Component training resources

At a Total Force association, a required Regular Air Force readiness event has an unexpected equipment shortfall. Air Reserve Component (ARC) equipment is already scheduled for required training, and no reallocation decision is documented. You are advising the responsible leader before either event begins.

### Response options

A. Recommend pausing the Regular Air Force event while leadership reviews the shortfall, without proposing substitute resources.  
B. Treat the association as sufficient permission to shift the ARC equipment, then document the decision afterward.  
C. Ask ARC leaders whether they can release the equipment, accepting the resulting training loss if they agree.  
D. Confirm reallocation authority and present coordinated options that reduce effects on both required events.

### Most effective: D

Paragraphs 5.10–5.11 supply the organizational context for integrated associations, ARC readiness, equal equipping priority, and limits on withdrawal, diversion, or reassignment. They neither rank these local events nor identify the decision authority for this asset. D is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because Chapter 1 accountability, mission stewardship, and teamwork support confirming authority and comparing coordinated alternatives.

### Least effective: B

B assumes that the association itself conveys reallocation permission and places documentation after the resource move. Chapter 5 explains the organizational interests and limits involved, but Chapter 1 accountability and discipline provide the behavioral basis for MyPromotion's least-effective judgment; the source does not create a universal priority rule for every ARC event.

### Option analysis

- **A — Partially effective:** It avoids disturbing ARC training but stops one event without offering the leader alternatives.
- **B — Least effective:** It substitutes an assumption about the association for an authority determination and moves the resource first.
- **C — Ineffective:** It consults affected leaders but treats loss of scheduled ARC training as the only solution considered.
- **D — Most effective:** It confirms who can decide and gives that authority coordinated options addressing both requirements.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.10–5.11, 1.3.1, 1.3.3  
**Primary concepts:** ARC readiness; equipment priority; training stewardship; coordination; accountability  
**ADTC:** 5D, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human ARC and resource review; actual ownership, orders, and approval requirements control

## C05-SJT-007 — A Civil Air Patrol offer

You advise the scheduler for a non-combat search exercise. A local Civil Air Patrol (CAP) squadron offers aerial observation support. The exercise's stated process allows proposed support to remain tentative while the designated channel routes a request; only CAP support approved and assigned by the USAF may be marked confirmed. No request has been submitted, and the planner wants to close the schedule.

### Response options

A. Keep the sortie tentative and route a complete request for aerial support through the designated assignment channel.  
B. Mark the sortie confirmed from the squadron's offer and submit the assignment request after the schedule closes.  
C. Keep the sortie tentative and contact the squadron about availability, but wait to start the assignment request.  
D. Remove CAP from the current schedule and ask the planner to reconsider support during the next revision.

### Most effective: A

Paragraph 5.12 supplies the organizational context by identifying CAP's auxiliary relationship, non-combat role, and USAF approval and assignment boundary; it does not prescribe preliminary scheduling steps. The scenario supplies the applicable planning process. A is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because Chapter 1 duty, accountability, and teamwork support timely use of that process while representing the sortie accurately.

### Least effective: B

B converts a local offer into confirmed support before completing the stated assignment process. Paragraph 5.12 explains why an offer and a USAF assignment are organizationally distinct; Chapter 1 honesty and accountability provide the behavioral basis for MyPromotion's least-effective judgment.

### Option analysis

- **A — Most effective:** It preserves an accurate tentative status while promptly beginning the supplied assignment process.
- **B — Least effective:** It represents support as settled before the process that distinguishes an offer from an assignment.
- **C — Partially effective:** It preserves an accurate status and checks availability but delays the request needed to resolve it.
- **D — Ineffective:** It avoids premature confirmation but postpones a potentially useful eligible support request.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.12, 1.3.1–1.3.3  
**Primary concepts:** CAP; USAF Auxiliary; non-combat support; approval; delegated authority  
**ADTC:** 5D, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human CAP review; current approval, mission, risk, and coordination procedures control

## C05-SJT-008 — A sister service's available equipment

At a joint base, Army-owned classroom equipment meets your section's approved training requirement and is available during the needed period. Your section has a pending request to buy duplicate equipment, but no shared-use arrangement exists yet. You are preparing a recommendation.

### Response options

A. Recommend borrowing the equipment based on its availability, with ownership coordination completed after the training.  
B. Compare purchase and loan costs, but leave support and scheduling effects for later review.  
C. Compare compatibility, support, timing, and cost, then recommend an authorized sharing or purchase decision.  
D. Continue the purchase request now and explore shared use only if funding is not approved.

### Most effective: C

Paragraph 5.5 supplies the organizational expectation to use another service's resources when economy and effectiveness increase, while paragraph 5.14 supplies joint-partnership and stewardship context. Neither paragraph establishes a local loan process. C is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because Chapter 1 accountability, mission focus, and teamwork support a complete comparison followed by an authorized recommendation.

### Least effective: A

A places equipment use ahead of ownership coordination merely because the asset appears available. Chapter 5 supports economical and effective interservice use but does not confer local transfer authority; Chapter 1 accountability and stewardship provide the behavioral basis for MyPromotion's least-effective judgment.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It recommends use before resolving ownership coordination, exposing both sections to avoidable disruption.
- **B — Partially effective:** It compares direct cost but postpones factors needed to determine overall effectiveness.
- **C — Most effective:** It evaluates the relevant economy and effectiveness factors before recommending an authorized solution.
- **D — Ineffective:** It preserves the existing purchase path but treats shared use only as a funding fallback.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.5, 5.14, 1.3.1, 1.3.3  
**Primary concepts:** Cross-service support; economy; effectiveness; stewardship; joint partnership  
**ADTC:** 5B, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human support-agreement and resource review; actual ownership and approval rules control

## C05-SJT-009 — An oversimplified component brief

You are the assigned reviewer for an orientation brief at a Component MAJCOM (C-MAJCOM). The local process requires component approval before command-relationship material is presented. One unapproved slide says a combatant commander (CCDR) controls every operational and administrative matter for assigned Air Force units and that the Commander, Air Force Forces (COMAFFOR), merely relays messages. The briefer favors the single-chain explanation because it is easier to teach.

### Response options

A. Remove the slide for this session and submit a corrected version for the next approved briefing.  
B. Correct the CCDR statement and route the slide for approval, leaving the COMAFFOR description unchanged.  
C. Recommend keeping the simplified slide and relying on a verbal caveat that relationships vary by mission.  
D. Correct both relationships from governing references and route the revised slide through required component approval.

### Most effective: D

Paragraphs 5.7, 5.9, and 5.15 supply the organizational facts distinguishing CCDR authority, service administrative authority, and the COMAFFOR-led component role; they do not prescribe a briefing review process. The scenario supplies the approval requirement. D is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because Chapter 1 honesty and accountability support correcting both errors and completing the stated review before use.

### Least effective: C

C preserves both inaccurate descriptions and relies on an oral caveat rather than correcting the instructional product. Chapter 5 establishes why the descriptions are inaccurate, while paragraph 1.3.1 supplies the accurate-reporting and accountability basis for MyPromotion's least-effective judgment.

### Option analysis

- **A — Partially effective:** It prevents current misinformation but leaves a useful command-relationship explanation for later.
- **B — Ineffective:** It follows the review process but leaves one of the two identified authority errors in the slide.
- **C — Least effective:** It retains both errors in durable material and depends on a caveat to prevent misunderstanding.
- **D — Most effective:** It corrects both distinctions from appropriate references and completes the supplied approval process.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.7, 5.9, 5.15, 1.3.1  
**Primary concepts:** CCDR; COMAFFOR; operational authority; service responsibilities; accurate instruction  
**ADTC:** 5C, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human component-command review; governing orders and actual command relationships control

## C05-SJT-010 — A requested multinational commitment

An allied liaison asks you to commit Air Force support to a proposed combined exercise event by tomorrow. You can gather the requirement, but you have no delegated authority to commit forces or accept responsibilities on behalf of the command.

### Response options

A. Say the request is under review and raise it at the scheduled meeting later that day.  
B. Clarify the support and deadline, then promptly send complete facts to the authorized decision-maker without forecasting approval.  
C. Tell the liaison to plan on likely support while you seek confirmation from the command.  
D. Forward the request promptly to your supervisor and note that you lack commitment authority.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 5.6 supplies the organizational context for combined missions and relevant multinational agreements, while paragraph 5.1 supplies the context of assigned authority and accountability; neither prescribes this staff response. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because Chapter 1 accountability, teamwork, and the paragraph 1.4 rule against unauthorized commitments support timely fact development without encouraging partner reliance.

### Least effective: C

C gives the liaison a planning expectation that the stated decision authority has not approved. Chapter 5 explains the multinational and command setting, but Chapter 1 honesty, accountability, and the unauthorized-commitment principle in paragraph 1.4 provide the behavioral basis for MyPromotion's least-effective designation.

### Option analysis

- **A — Partially effective:** It manages the liaison's expectation and uses a scheduled channel but delays fact development until later that day.
- **B — Most effective:** It develops decision-ready facts promptly while leaving the commitment with the authorized official.
- **C — Least effective:** It creates foreseeable partner reliance through a favorable forecast before the command decides.
- **D — Ineffective:** It forwards the request promptly and identifies the authority limit but adds no clarified requirement.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.1, 5.6, 1.3.1–1.3.3, 1.4  
**Primary concepts:** Combined command; multinational agreement; escalation; delegated authority; commitments  
**ADTC:** 5C, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human multinational-coordination review; actual agreements and delegated authorities control
