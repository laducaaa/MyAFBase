## Section 6A — Doctrine

### C06-Q-001

- **ADTC:** 6A, A—Remembering
- **Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt
- **Difficulty:** Foundational

In what three forms does AFH 1 say doctrine exists?

A. Only Service doctrine and classified tactical manuals.
B. Only basic doctrine and campaign plans.
C. Only COCOM directives and EXORDs.
D. Joint doctrine, multinational doctrine, and Service doctrine.

**Correct answer: D**

**Rationale:** AFH 1 states that doctrine exists as joint doctrine, multinational doctrine, and Service doctrine.

**Why the other options are incorrect:**

- **A:** Tactical manuals are not presented as one of the three doctrine forms.
- **B:** Basic doctrine is a USAF doctrine level, not one of the three forms named here.
- **C:** COCOM directives and EXORDs are not the three doctrine forms listed.

**Source:** AFH 1, ¶6.1
**Review flag:** Standard citation verification

How do WARNORD, PLANORD, and ALERTORD sequence relative to COA approval?

A. ALERTORD comes first and authorizes execution; WARNORD never requests an estimate; PLANORD only transfers forces between CCMDs.
B. All three authorize execution of the approved COA.
C. They are issued only by the JFACC without CJCS or directing-authority roles.
D. WARNORD initiates COA development/evaluation and requests a commander’s estimate; PLANORD directs plan development before COA approval; ALERTORD directs plan development after COA approval but does not authorize execution.

**Correct answer: D**

**Rationale:** The three planning directives sequence around COA work and approval: WARNORD starts COA development/evaluation; PLANORD starts plan development before approval; ALERTORD starts plan development after approval without authorizing execution.

**Why the other options are incorrect:**

- **A:** It misstates sequence and assigns execution authority incorrectly.
- **B:** ALERTORD expressly does not authorize execution.
- **C:** WARNORD is issued by the CJCS; PLANORD/ALERTORD are planning directives of the directing authority construct in AFH 1.

**Source:** AFH 1, ¶6.12
**Review flag:** TSgt sequence planning orders vs COA approval

How do CONOPS, EXORD, and OPORD relate in moving from intent to coordinated execution?

A. CONOPS presents commander assumptions/intent for an overall picture; EXORD initiates military operations under President/SecDef authority (issued by CJCS at their direction) to implement the approved CONOPS; OPORD directs subordinates for coordinated execution of an operation.
B. OPORD always precedes any CONOPS; EXORD is identical to ADCON; CONOPS authorizes force transfers between CCMDs.
C. EXORD is issued by any flight commander; CONOPS replaces President/SecDef authority; OPORD only requests a commander’s estimate.
D. They are three names for the same non-executable staff note.

**Correct answer: A**

**Rationale:** CONOPS outlines assumptions/intent; EXORD initiates operations to implement the approved CONOPS under President/SecDef authority via CJCS; OPORD effects coordinated execution by subordinates.

**Why the other options are incorrect:**

- **B:** It scrambles functions: deployment/redeployment orders transfer forces; ADCON is not an EXORD.
- **C:** It misassigns issuance authorities and WARNORD-like functions.
- **D:** AFH 1 assigns distinct roles to each.

**Source:** AFH 1, ¶6.12
