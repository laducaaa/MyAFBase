## Section 22A — USAF Fitness

### C22-FC-001
- **Prompt:** What is the goal of the Physical Fitness Program (PFP) according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Motivate all Airmen to participate in a year-round physical conditioning program emphasizing total fitness, including proper aerobic conditioning, muscular fitness training, and healthy eating.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22A; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-002
- **Prompt:** Why must USAF members be physically fit, and what readiness benefits does AFH 1 associate with an active lifestyle?
- **Answer:** Members must be physically fit to support the USAF mission. An active lifestyle increases productivity, optimizes health, and decreases absenteeism, helping maintain higher readiness.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22A; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-003
- **Prompt:** To whom does DAFMAN 36-2905 fitness guidance apply as stated in AFH 1?
- **Answer:** The entire Department of the Air Force, including RegAF, Air Force Reserve, Air National Guard, and United States Space Force.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22A; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-004
- **Prompt:** What does the installation or ANG wing commander oversee regarding the PFP, and what course approvals are involved?
- **Answer:** Oversees the PFP and compliance with DAFMAN 36-2905; provides a healthy lifestyle environment; decides PTL/UFPM FAC augmentation plans; renders decision on 1.5-mile run, 2 km walk, and 20-meter HAMR courses after collaboration with CE, FSS, and Wing Safety.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22A; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-005
- **Prompt:** What does the Force Support Squadron oversee for the fitness program?
- **Answer:** The function and management of the Fitness and Sport Center and the Fitness Assessment Cell (FAC); plans/programs/budgets safe and effective fitness operations; reports monthly assessment statistics.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22A; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-006
- **Prompt:** What is the Unit Fitness Program Manager (UFPM) responsible for?
- **Answer:** Responsible to the commander for the unit PFP; liaison among unit commander, FAC, and Fitness Program Manager; may augment FAC if trained/appointed; reviews/approves PFA scheduling; validates FSQ and AF Form 469; enters exemptions; informs Airmen of FIP requirements; maintains case files and metrics.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22A; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-007
- **Prompt:** What supervision ratio applies to FAC augmentees conducting PFA components?
- **Answer:** No more than 12 Airmen for every one FAC augmentee.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22A; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-008
- **Prompt:** What score must Physical Training Leaders maintain, and what happens if it drops below that score?
- **Answer:** Must maintain a fitness assessment score of 75.0 or higher (2025 source snapshot—revalidate). If score drops below 75.0 or becomes noncurrent, PTL certification is voided; reinstatement requires a passing score and re-accomplishment of necessary training.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22A; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-009
- **Prompt:** What training must an individual complete to become a certified PTL?
- **Answer:** Hands-on Basic Life Support training (or equivalent), CPR/AED training, and fitness assessment procedures.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22A; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-010
- **Prompt:** Is use of DAF Form 4446 mandatory for documenting fitness assessment results?
- **Answer:** Yes. Use of DAF Form 4446 is mandatory; locally produced scorecards cannot be used.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22A; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-011
- **Prompt:** Does failure to complete the Fitness Screening Questionnaire invalidate the fitness assessment?
- **Answer:** No. Failure to complete FSQ does not invalidate the assessment; FAC documents incomplete FSQ cases and attaches them to the assessment.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22A; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-012
- **Prompt:** What Airman responsibilities does AFH 1 emphasize for fitness assessments?
- **Answer:** Maintain year-round fitness and nutrition; notify UFPM/FAC/chain in writing to schedule assessment; monitor exemptions; seek medical evaluation if a condition may impact the FA; complete FSQ; notify UFPM upon receiving AF Form 469 with fitness restrictions/exemptions and provide a copy before testing.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22A; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-013
- **Prompt:** What Body Composition Program measurement replaced abdominal circumference, and how is it calculated?
- **Answer:** Waist-to-Height Ratio (WHtR), calculated by dividing waist circumference by height. Annual BCA is completed within the birth month.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22A; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-014
- **Prompt:** When did BCP implementation start, and what adaptation-period limit on administrative actions does AFH 1 state?
- **Answer:** Implementation started 1 April 2023 with a one-year adaptation period. Administrative actions are not authorized based solely on BCA results during the adaptation period, which was extended 8 April 2024 and remains until 180 days after publication of AFMAN 36-2912. (2025 source snapshot—revalidate)
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22A; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-015
- **Prompt:** What WHtR thresholds define Meeting vs Not Meeting BCP standard in AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Meeting Standard (Low-Moderate Risk): WHtR < 0.55. Not Meeting Standard (High Risk): WHtR > 0.55. (2025 source snapshot—revalidate)
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22A; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-016
- **Prompt:** Who drives the Body Composition Program at unit level, and what BCM sex requirement exists?
- **Answer:** It is a unit commander–driven program. Units identify male and female Body Composition Managers; units must have at least one of each sex; there is no grade requirement.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22A; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-017
- **Prompt:** Is enrollment in the informal self-directed BCIP considered a failure to meet the BCP standard?
- **Answer:** No. Enrollment in the informal self-directed BCIP is not considered a failure to meet the BCP standard, and commanders will not take administrative actions during informal BCIP.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22A; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-018
- **Prompt:** When is enrollment in the formal self-directed BCIP considered the first failure to meet the BCP standard?
- **Answer:** After subsequent failures to achieve BCP standards following the informal program path; formal BCIP enrollment is considered the first failure to meet the BCP standard.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22A; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-019
- **Prompt:** May members accomplish their BCA before birth month, and what post-exemption timing applies?
- **Answer:** Members may opt to accomplish BCA one calendar month before birth month or reassessment date. After deployment/commander/ARC non-participating exemption expiration, accomplish BCA within two months.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22A; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-020
- **Prompt:** May BCMs assess members of the opposite sex?
- **Answer:** No. BCMs administer BCAs within the unit and only assess members of the same sex, using sex reflected in MilPDS.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22A; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-021
- **Prompt:** What must Airmen with WHtR > 0.55 complete under the informal BCIP path?
- **Answer:** Enroll in 12-month informal self-directed BCIP; schedule medical evaluation/risk-factor assessment (ARC: RMU/GMU records review); review HeRO Body Composition Guide; develop and submit a body composition improvement action plan to BCM and unit commander.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22A; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-022
- **Prompt:** What form is used for BCIP processing until DAF Form 113 is published?
- **Answer:** DAF Form 108, Department of the Air Force Fitness Education and Intervention Processing, until publication of DAF Form 113.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22A; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-023
- **Prompt:** Are BCAs taken when a member is on medical restriction?
- **Answer:** Yes, unless a profile (AF Form 469) indicates a BCA exemption.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22A; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-024
- **Prompt:** What IT platform do BCMs use for WHtR results and exemptions?
- **Answer:** myBodyComp.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22A; B—Understanding


## Section 22B — Official Fitness Assessment

### C22-FC-025
- **Prompt:** Within what time window must all fitness assessment components be completed?
- **Answer:** Within a three-hour window on the same day.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22B; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-026
- **Prompt:** What is the required order of fitness assessment components?
- **Answer:** Body composition (height/weight) first; then muscular strength (push-ups or HRPU); then core endurance (sit-ups, CLRC, or forearm plank); cardiorespiratory fitness last (1.5-mile run, 20 m HAMR, or 2 km walk if not medically cleared to run).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22B; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-027
- **Prompt:** What minimum rest period is required between FA components?
- **Answer:** A minimum five-minute rest period between components.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22B; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-028
- **Prompt:** May fitness assessments be videotaped for the appeal process?
- **Answer:** No. Videotaping of fitness assessments is not allowed, nor should it be used in the appeal process.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22B; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-029
- **Prompt:** What are the maximum component points for the composite fitness score?
- **Answer:** 60 points aerobic; 20 points muscular; 20 points sit-up/core component; composite scale 0 to 100. (2025 source snapshot—revalidate)
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22B; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-030
- **Prompt:** Do meeting minimum scores in each FA component guarantee a passing composite score?
- **Answer:** No. Meeting component minimums does not constitute the points needed to pass; scoring minimums in all components will not generate enough points for a composite of 75 or greater. (2025 source snapshot—revalidate)
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22B; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-031
- **Prompt:** What composite score achieves Excellent, and what retest rules apply?
- **Answer:** Excellent: composite at or above 90. If all three applicable components scored ≥90, retest within 12 months; if fewer than three components ≥90 and not on chronic profile, retest within six months. (2025 source snapshot—revalidate)
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22B; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-032
- **Prompt:** What composite score range is Satisfactory, and when must the member retest?
- **Answer:** Satisfactory: composite 75–89.99; retest within six months. (2025 source snapshot—revalidate)
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22B; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-033
- **Prompt:** What defines Unsatisfactory, and what is the retest window?
- **Answer:** Composite at or below 74.9 and/or one or more component minimums not met; retest within 90 days. (2025 source snapshot—revalidate)
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22B; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-034
- **Prompt:** What is the difference between component exemption and composite exemption?
- **Answer:** Component exemption: exempt from one or more components but assessed on remaining components. Composite exemption: exempt from all components.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22B; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-035
- **Prompt:** Where are assessment failures annotated, and what commander options does AFH 1 mention?
- **Answer:** Annotated in myFitness (myFSS) and considered against the individual. Commander discretion includes annotating non-current/failing assessment on the performance evaluation and documenting as referral; responses range from verbal counseling to control roster to administrative separation (details in DAFMAN 36-2905).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22B; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-036
- **Prompt:** Must members have a current fitness score before deployment, and when are they “exempt” while deployed?
- **Answer:** Yes, members must have a current fitness score on file prior to deployment. They are not considered exempt in the deployed location until their current fitness assessment expires.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22B; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-037
- **Prompt:** If medically exempt from one FA component, are remaining components still tested?
- **Answer:** Yes. Airmen with a medical profile prohibiting one or more components are still tested on components not specifically exempted.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22B; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-038
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define muscular strength versus muscular endurance?
- **Answer:** Muscular strength: maximum force generated by a specific muscle or muscle group. Muscular endurance: ability of a muscle group to execute repeated contractions over a period sufficient to cause muscular fatigue.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22B; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-039
- **Prompt:** What is aerobic (cardiorespiratory) fitness?
- **Answer:** The ability to perform large-muscle, dynamic, moderate-to-high intensity exercise for prolonged periods; depends on respiratory, cardiovascular, and skeletal muscle systems—essentially the ability to produce energy.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22B; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-040
- **Prompt:** What duration/frequency targets does AFH 1 give for moderately and vigorously intense aerobic activity?
- **Answer:** Moderate: at least 30 minutes a day / five days a week. Vigorous: at least 20–25 minutes a day / three days a week.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22B; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-041
- **Prompt:** What warm-up guidance does AFH 1 provide before vigorous exercise?
- **Answer:** A five to seven minute dynamic warm-up that increases blood flow to muscles/tendons, helps reduce injury risk, and increases joint range of motion; sequence includes slow jog/walk, joint rotation, and mimicking the activity.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22B; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-042
- **Prompt:** Why cool down after a workout?
- **Answer:** To provide gradual reduction in activity to prevent blood pooling, hasten recovery, and avoid injury, bringing the body back toward its normal resting state.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22B; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-043
- **Prompt:** Is flexibility assessed during the fitness assessment?
- **Answer:** No. Flexibility is not assessed during the FA but is an important aspect of overall health and should be part of a well-balanced routine.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22B; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-044
- **Prompt:** What stretching guidance does AFH 1 give regarding duration, repetitions, and ballistic stretching?
- **Answer:** Stretch when tissues are warmer; hold stretches 10 to 30 seconds; repeat each stretch three to four times; ballistic stretching (bouncing) is not recommended.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22B; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-045
- **Prompt:** What is the Principle of Specificity in fitness training?
- **Answer:** Target specific muscle groups or areas by assessing functional movement and incorporating specific moves so strength gains improve work-related tasks and reduce injuries.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22B; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-046
- **Prompt:** What is the Principle of Regularity?
- **Answer:** Exercise routinely to produce a training effect; establishing about three workouts per week is best for optimal gains; strive to exercise all target muscle groups at least twice each week.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22B; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-047
- **Prompt:** What recovery period should be allowed between workouts for the same muscle groups?
- **Answer:** At least a 48-hour recovery period between workouts for the same muscle groups; between-set recovery normally ranges from 30 seconds to three minutes.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22B; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-048
- **Prompt:** What do the Principles of Balance and Variety emphasize?
- **Answer:** Balance: work all major muscle groups and opposing pairs (e.g., push then pull). Variety: change equipment, exercises, volume, and intensity to improve results and engagement.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22B; B—Understanding


## Section 22C — Nutrition

### C22-FC-049
- **Prompt:** What estimated daily calorie ranges does AFH 1 print for adult women and men?
- **Answer:** About 1,600 to 2,400 calories/day for adult women and 2,000 to 3,000 for adult men; low end sedentary, high end active. (2025 source snapshot—revalidate)
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22C; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-050
- **Prompt:** What does SMART stand for in nutrition and fitness goal-setting?
- **Answer:** Specific, Measurable, Attainable, Relevant, and Timely.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22C; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-051
- **Prompt:** What primary causes of unintended weight gain does AFH 1 name?
- **Answer:** An imbalance of calorie intake and lack of physical activity.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22C; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-052
- **Prompt:** How quickly can carbohydrates become readily available energy, and what post-recovery carb-to-protein ratio is optimal?
- **Answer:** Carbohydrates can be available within 30 to 60 minutes. Optimal post-recovery composition is about a four-to-one carbohydrate-to-protein ratio.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22C; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-053
- **Prompt:** What are micronutrients, and why do they matter?
- **Answer:** Vitamins, minerals, trace elements, amino acids, and other essential nutrients required in very small amounts for healthy living and normal body function; rich foods promote brain, heart, blood, and metabolism functions and can prevent deficiency disorders.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22C; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-054
- **Prompt:** How many calories per gram do carbohydrates and protein provide?
- **Answer:** Four calories per gram each.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22C; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-055
- **Prompt:** What general percentage of total daily calories should come from carbohydrates?
- **Answer:** 45–65 percent of total daily caloric intake. (2025 source snapshot—revalidate)
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22C; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-056
- **Prompt:** How do simple and complex carbohydrates differ in blood glucose effect?
- **Answer:** Simple carbohydrates increase blood glucose rapidly; complex carbohydrates increase blood glucose slowly over a longer period, providing more sustainable energy.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22C; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-057
- **Prompt:** What fiber intake does AFH 1 recommend versus average American consumption?
- **Answer:** Recommended total fiber 20–35 grams/day; average American consumes about 12–15 grams/day. (2025 source snapshot—revalidate)
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22C; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-058
- **Prompt:** What general protein intake guidance does AFH 1 provide?
- **Answer:** 10–35 percent of total calories (0.8–1.2 grams per kilogram of body weight); lean meats and low-fat milk products preferred. (2025 source snapshot—revalidate)
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22C; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-059
- **Prompt:** How many calories per gram do fats provide, and what general calorie percentage is recommended?
- **Answer:** Nine calories per gram; general recommendation 20–35 percent of total calories. (2025 source snapshot—revalidate)
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22C; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-060
- **Prompt:** What saturated-fat limit and trans-fat guidance does AFH 1 state?
- **Answer:** Saturated fats should make up less than 10 percent of daily calories; trans fat intake should be kept as low as possible. (2025 source snapshot—revalidate)
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22C; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-061
- **Prompt:** What benefit can unsaturated fats provide according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Eating unsaturated fat can reduce risk for heart disease and improve high-density lipoprotein (good cholesterol) levels.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22C; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-062
- **Prompt:** What sodium intake limit does AFH 1 cite from the Dietary Guidelines for Americans?
- **Answer:** Adults limit sodium to 2,300 milligrams per day (about one teaspoon of table salt); certain at-risk groups about 1,500 mg/day. Average U.S. intake about 3,400 mg/day. (2025 source snapshot—revalidate)
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22C; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-063
- **Prompt:** Does the FDA test dietary supplements before they are sold, and what should buyers look for?
- **Answer:** No. Look for third-party verification/certification labels indicating testing for purity and/or quality.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22C; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-064
- **Prompt:** What supplement consultation rule applies to Airmen on flying status?
- **Answer:** Airmen on flying status must consult with their flight surgeon before taking supplements; all Airmen are encouraged to discuss supplement use with their healthcare provider.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22C; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-065
- **Prompt:** What general daily total water consumption figures does AFH 1 give for women and men?
- **Answer:** About 2.7 liters per day for women and 3.7 liters per day for men (water, beverages, and food). (2025 source snapshot—revalidate)
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22C; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-066
- **Prompt:** At what fluid-loss level does dehydration occur, and what fluid intake ceilings does AFH 1 warn about?
- **Answer:** Dehydration with fluid losses greater than two percent of body weight. Limit fluids to no more than 6 cups an hour and 48 cups a day to avoid over-hydration. (2025 source snapshot—revalidate)
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22C; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-067
- **Prompt:** Do caffeine and energy drinks hydrate according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** No. Caffeine does not hydrate; energy drinks are not meant to hydrate. Water is generally best; sports drinks may help in prolonged exertion.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22C; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-068
- **Prompt:** What alcohol drink limits and serving sizes does AFH 1 cite from the Dietary Guidelines?
- **Answer:** Limit alcohol to one drink per day for women and two for men; beer 12 oz, wine 5 oz, 80-proof spirits 1.5 oz. Alcohol does not hydrate. (2025 source snapshot—revalidate)
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22C; B—Understanding


## Section 22D — Substance Use or Abuse

### C22-FC-069
- **Prompt:** What is the USAF goal regarding tobacco, and what products does “tobacco use” include?
- **Answer:** A tobacco-free force. Includes all products that may be configured to deliver nicotine—cigars, cigarettes, e-cigarettes/vape pens, pipes, hookahs, vaporizers, smokeless products, and other nicotine delivery systems defined as tobacco products.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.18
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22D; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-070
- **Prompt:** What annual DoD cost estimate for tobacco use does AFH 1 attribute to TRICARE?
- **Answer:** About $1.7 billion annually due to increased healthcare utilization and decreased work productivity. (2025 source snapshot—revalidate)
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.18
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22D; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-071
- **Prompt:** What minimum distances from building entrances/air intakes and playgrounds apply to designated tobacco areas?
- **Answer:** At least 50 feet from building entrances and air intake ducts; at least 100 feet from playgrounds. (2025 source snapshot—revalidate)
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22D; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-072
- **Prompt:** What tobacco restrictions apply to BMT and students in technical training/PME/accession/GME?
- **Answer:** Prohibits all such students from using tobacco products while in uniform, and prohibits tobacco use at all times during basic military training.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22D; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-073
- **Prompt:** What Class rating does AFH 1 give tobacco smoke as a carcinogen, and what annual U.S. death estimate is printed?
- **Answer:** Class “A” carcinogen; estimated to cause 480,000 deaths per year, including about 41,000 from secondhand smoke. (2025 source snapshot—revalidate)
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 22D; B—Understanding

### C22-FC-074
- **Prompt:** How can tobacco use affect military-relevant performance factors named in AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Decreases endurance, night vision, and fine motor coordination; increases injury risk; poses post-operative respiratory complications; impairs/slows wound healing.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶22.20
