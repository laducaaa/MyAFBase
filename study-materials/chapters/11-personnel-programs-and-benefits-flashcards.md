## Section 11A — Benefits and Services

### C11-FC-001
- **Prompt:** Why does AFH 1 say Airmen need to understand benefits and services?
- **Answer:** Military members must be prepared to serve and support the mission at all times; wide-ranging benefits and services care for members and families. Airmen need to understand benefits applicable to themselves and to subordinates, peers, and leaders, taking responsibility and serving as good wingmen.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-002
- **Prompt:** What single-entry capability did the USAF launch in 2000, and how is portal access obtained?
- **Answer:** The Air Force Portal provides a single point of entry to web-based information, self-service applications, collaboration/networking tools, and combat support systems. Access is available from any internet-connected computer with a valid DoD PKI certificate, such as a CAC. **AFH source note:** Launch year and access method are 2025 AFH snapshots—revalidate.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-003
- **Prompt:** Name key self-service applications AFH 1 lists as accessible through the Air Force Portal.
- **Answer:** myPay; Air Force Virtual Education Center; MyFitness; Virtual Military Personnel Flight; LeaveWeb; Assignment Management System; and the Air Force E-Publishing Website. Library and Resources also provides periodicals, research, and education/training materials.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-004
- **Prompt:** What is the Military and Family Readiness Center designed to do for commanders and mission readiness?
- **Answer:** Assist commanders in assessing and supporting military welfare and building community/support; support mission readiness by helping Airmen and families adapt to expeditionary operations and the military lifestyle; coordinate with unit leadership and agencies on community readiness and personal preparedness; support work/life issues and facilitate readiness, resilience, and preparedness.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-005
- **Prompt:** What is the Commander’s Key Support Program?
- **Answer:** An official unit family readiness program designed to enhance readiness and establish a sense of community; it promotes partnerships with the unit, the M&FRC, and community agencies.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-006
- **Prompt:** What TAP components and deadlines does AFH 1 require before separation, retirement, or release from active duty?
- **Answer:** Individualized Initial Counseling and Pre-separation Counseling NLT 365 days prior; Core Curriculum (DoD Day, DOL Employment Fundamentals, VA Benefits and Services workshops, plus at least one 2-day track: Employment, Education, Vocational, or Entrepreneurship); Capstone NLT 90 days prior verifying transition plan and Career Readiness Standard deliverables. **AFH source note:** TAP timelines are 2025 AFH snapshots—revalidate for duty use.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-007
- **Prompt:** What award may recognize volunteers through the Volunteer Resources Program, and at what level?
- **Answer:** The Air Force Volunteer Excellence Award (VEA), a Chief of Staff of the Air Force (CSAF) level award and commander’s program recognizing volunteer contributions in the local civilian or military family community.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-008
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 characterize Military Family Life Counselor services?
- **Answer:** DoD-contracted confidential assistance for Airmen and dependents providing non-medical, short-term, solution-focused counseling and briefings that augment counseling services from other agencies.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-009
- **Prompt:** What three EFMP support components does AFH 1 name?
- **Answer:** Medical, assignments, and family.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-010
- **Prompt:** Whom does Air Force Families Forever support?
- **Answer:** Surviving family members of deceased Regular Air Force, U.S. Space Force, and Air Reserve Component Airmen and Guardians who died in Active Duty, Inactive Duty for Training, or Annual Training status, when the relationship was established prior to the death; it facilitates long-term case management procedures and support.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-011
- **Prompt:** What voting-assistance responsibilities does AFH 1 assign to M&FRC staff?
- **Answer:** Operate the Installation Voter Assistance Office and serve as Voting Assistance Officers responsible for providing accurate nonpartisan voting information and assistance so members and families understand the right to vote via absentee ballot.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-012
- **Prompt:** When are personnel and family readiness pre-deployment briefings mandatory?
- **Answer:** For individuals with a firm deployment tasking of 30 or more days. Spouses are welcome and encouraged to attend.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-013
- **Prompt:** What does AFH 1 say about the cost of personal financial readiness services, and what else may they cover beyond mandatory training?
- **Answer:** Services are free. Beyond mandatory training by certified financial counselors, members and families can learn basics such as budgeting, credit, large purchases, state or country liability laws, and local fraudulent business practices. **AFH source note:** Explains AFH text only—not personalized financial advice.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-014
- **Prompt:** Why are the Record of Emergency Data and SOES critically important, and how often should Airmen review them?
- **Answer:** Current RED information enables timely NOK/beneficiary notification (AFH cites CAR assistance with timelines such as notifications within 12 hours). Airmen should understand, review, verify, and certify RED and SOES at least once a year or when beneficiary information changes.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-015
- **Prompt:** What does the vRED/DD Form 93 enable Airmen to designate, and what PADD note does AFH 1 include?
- **Answer:** Those to notify if ill/injured/wounded/missing/deceased; beneficiaries of Death Gratuity and Arrears of Pay; and the Person Authorized to Direct Disposition of remains. There is no requirement for the PADD to be related to the deceased, but the PADD should know the member’s wishes and understand disposition decisions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-016
- **Prompt:** What SGLI and FSGLI coverage ceilings does AFH 1 state, and what military-to-military SOES caution applies?
- **Answer:** SGLI up to $500,000; FSGLI up to $100,000 for the spouse and $10,000 for dependent children. Military-to-military spouses who have not updated DEERS with spouse information cannot properly certify the SOES record and must add the spouse as a dependent at MPF Customer Service or the nearest RAPIDS station. **AFH source note:** Dollar ceilings are 2025 AFH snapshots—revalidate; not insurance advice.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-017
- **Prompt:** Where are legal services performed relative to the M&FRC?
- **Answer:** Legal services are not performed by or within M&FRCs; they are performed by the Office of the Staff Judge Advocate on the installation, contingent on legal staff resources and regulations prescribed by the Secretary of the Air Force.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-018
- **Prompt:** What is the Air Force Wounded Warrior Program, and who may refer someone?
- **Answer:** A congressionally mandated, federally funded program providing personalized care, services, and advocacy to seriously or very seriously wounded, ill, or injured Total Force Airmen/Guardians, their caregivers, and family members. Anyone may refer an Airman or Guardian via the program website referral function.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-019
- **Prompt:** Which members must have a family care plan according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Single-member parents with custody of children who bear sole or joint responsibility; military couples with dependents; and members solely responsible for care of a spouse, elderly family member, or adult family member with disabilities dependent on the member for financial, medical, or logistical support (including limited English, inability to drive, or inability to access basic life-sustaining facilities).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-020
- **Prompt:** What deployability standard must family care arrangements meet?
- **Answer:** Each USAF member must make and maintain dependent care arrangements that allow the member to be world-wide deployable at all times, with advance planning and sufficiently detailed short- and long-term arrangements for smooth transfer of responsibilities to a nonmilitary caregiver.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-021
- **Prompt:** What form documents family care certification, and what review cadence does AFH 1 require?
- **Answer:** DAF Form 357, Family Care Certification (AFH 1 also prints AF Form 357 in the annual signing sentence). Commanders/first sergeants counsel during inprocessing and annually brief members with family care responsibilities individually, signing when the plan is reviewed and certified; the member signs/dates to document the briefing. **AFH source note:** Preserve DAF Form 357 / AF Form 357 label split as printed.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-022
- **Prompt:** What change-notification and failure timelines does AFH 1 state for family care plans?
- **Answer:** Notify commander/first sergeant immediately or within 30 days (60 days for Selected Reserve) if status/circumstances require completing a DAF Form 357. Failure to produce a plan within 60 days of discussion with the commander, supervisor, or designated representative may result in disciplinary action or administrative separation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-023
- **Prompt:** List the major VA benefits categories AFH 1 names.
- **Answer:** Disability benefits; education benefits; vocational rehabilitation and employment; home loans; burial benefits; dependents’ and survivors’ benefits; life insurance; and health care.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-024
- **Prompt:** How is VA disability compensation graduated, and when may an additional dependent allowance apply?
- **Answer:** Graduated according to degree of disability from 10 percent to 100 percent in increments of 10 percent. An additional allowance may be added for dependents if combined disability is rated 30 percent or greater. Compensation may be offset by military retirement pay, disability severance pay, or separation incentive payments. **AFH source note:** Explains AFH text only—not a personal disability determination.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-025
- **Prompt:** How do TRICARE and the Veterans Health Administration differ at the AFH 1 level?
- **Answer:** TRICARE is DoD-operated military health care for service members and dependents. VHA provides health care for enrolled veterans and eligible family members through hospitals/clinics. They work together but have different eligibility criteria, health benefits, and costs.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-026
- **Prompt:** What are the primary TRICARE health plan options and the three dental plans named in AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Primary health options: TRICARE Prime and TRICARE Select (with remote/overseas variations and additional plans such as For Life, Reserve Select, Retired Reserve, Young Adult, U.S. Family Health Plan). Dental: active duty dental program; TRICARE dental program; TRICARE retiree dental program.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-027
- **Prompt:** What retirement eligibility thresholds does AFH 1 state for enlisted members and officers?
- **Answer:** Enlisted: 20 years TAFMS with no restrictions per DAFI 36-3203; must apply or they separate on DOS. Officers: 20 years TAFMS and 10 years total active federal commissioned service.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-028
- **Prompt:** What typical retirement application window does AFH 1 describe through myFSS?
- **Answer:** Typically up to 12 months but no less than 4 months before the desired retirement date; members must request an eligibility review through myFSS before submitting. Seven-day or three-day option applicants may be able to submit more than 12 months in advance.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-029
- **Prompt:** What does DIEUS determine, and is it adjusted for a break in service?
- **Answer:** Date Initially Entered Uniformed Service normally determines which retirement pay plan applies. DIEUS is a fixed date not subject to adjustment because of a break in service.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.8; Table 11.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-030
- **Prompt:** Compare Final Basic Pay, High-3, and BRS multipliers as printed in Table 11.1.
- **Answer:** Final Basic Pay (entered prior to 8 Sep 1980): 2.5% × years × basic pay. High-3 (and High-3 option): 2.5% × years × average of highest 36 months. BRS (entered on/after 1 Jan 2018): 2.0% × years × high-36 average. REDUX/CSB option uses 2% per year for first 20 years and 3.5% thereafter to a 75% max at 30 years. **AFH source note:** Formulas/dates are 2025 AFH snapshots—revalidate; not retirement advice.
- **Source:** AFH 1, Table 11.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-031
- **Prompt:** What automatic and matching TSP contribution rules does Table 11.1 Note 12 state for BRS members?
- **Answer:** DoD begins an automatic contribution of one percent of base pay after 60 days of service (pay date). Matching contributions up to an additional four percent begin after 2 years. Automatic and matching continue until separation, retirement, or completion of 26 years of service (pay date). For opt-in enrollments, government contributions begin the pay period following enrollment. **AFH source note:** BRS/TSP figures are 2025 AFH snapshots—revalidate; not financial advice.
- **Source:** AFH 1, Table 11.1 Note 12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-032
- **Prompt:** What does Table 11.1 Note 11 say about members with breaks in service regarding BRS enrollment?
- **Answer:** Members with breaks in service reentering AD or the selected reserve on or after 1 January 2019, who originally entered before 1 January 2018 and have less than 12 years of service (pay date) or less than 4,320 retirement points for ARC members as of reentry, may enroll in BRS within 30 days of reentry. (Extract line “11. Members with breaks…” is Table Note 11, not a chapter paragraph.)
- **Source:** AFH 1, Table 11.1 Note 11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-033
- **Prompt:** While on RegAF status, who is automatically covered by SBP at no cost?
- **Answer:** RegAF members with a spouse or dependent children are automatically covered by the survivor benefit plan at no cost while they remain on RegAF status.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-034
- **Prompt:** What annuity percentage and line-of-duty rules does AFH 1 state for SBP when a member dies before or after retirement eligibility?
- **Answer:** If not yet retirement eligible, death must be in line of duty; annuity is 55% of retired pay as if retired with a total disability rating on the date of death. If not in line of duty, an annuity may still be payable if retirement eligible; then 55% of retired pay as if retired for years of service.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-035
- **Prompt:** What SBP termination window and paid-up feature does AFH 1 describe?
- **Answer:** One-year window to terminate between the second and third anniversary of beginning receipt of retired pay (spouse/former spouse consent; premiums not refunded; termination permanent). Paid-up feature: age 70 and 360 months of premiums paid allows stopping premiums while remaining an active participant.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-036
- **Prompt:** How do Red Cross emergency communications assist commanders regarding leave?
- **Answer:** The Red Cross relays urgent messages with accurate, factual, complete, and verified descriptions of emergencies to service members worldwide; Red Cross-verified information assists commanders in making decisions regarding emergency leave.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-037
- **Prompt:** With which Air Force-related relief partner does AFH 1 say the Red Cross works under partnership agreements for financial assistance?
- **Answer:** Air Force Aid Society (also lists Army Emergency Relief, Coast Guard Mutual Assistance, and Navy-Marine Corps Relief Society). Types include emergency travel, burial of a loved one, and assistance to avoid privation or hardship.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-038
- **Prompt:** What is the Recruiter Assistance Program, and what leave character does AFH 1 associate with it?
- **Answer:** A RegAF leave program run by Air Force Recruiting Service in which an Airman or Guardian directly supports a USAF or USSF recruiter. Members may request non-chargeable leave in accordance with DAFI 36-3003.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-039
- **Prompt:** What is WEAR, and to whom may approved participants speak?
- **Answer:** We Are All Recruiters may grant permissive TDY for events that directly enhance recruiting. Approval is limited to events where personnel speak directly to potential applicants (ages 17–39) or influencers (parents, community leaders, teachers, counselors, or coaches). Commander approval is required first, then routing to AFRS Public Affairs.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-040
- **Prompt:** How should an Airman represent VA eligibility when AFH 1 only categorizes benefits?
- **Answer:** Explain the major VA categories AFH 1 lists and direct the person to www.va.gov or the closest VA department for eligibility requirements—without claiming a personal eligibility determination beyond the source. **AFH source note:** Two-track: study preserves AFH categories; duty use requires current VA channels—not advice from this card.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11A; B—Understanding

## Section 11B — Leave and Authorized Absences

### C11-FC-041
- **Prompt:** Why does AFH 1 call an effective leave program an essential military requirement?
- **Answer:** Lengthy respites from the work environment tend to benefit psychological and physical status; unit commanders establish leave programs/plans to encourage leave for the maximum benefit of the member within operational constraints.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-042
- **Prompt:** How does LeaveWeb processing flow from request to finance validation?
- **Answer:** Member requests leave → e-mail to leave approval authority (commander final authority; may delegate normally no lower than first-line supervisor) → approve/disapprove → if approved, unit leave monitor validates → sent electronically to finance → member prints approved form to hand-carry → after leave, member updates LeaveWeb and forwards for endorsement.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-043
- **Prompt:** What form may be required if LeaveWeb is unavailable?
- **Answer:** DAF Form 988, Leave Request/Authorization.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-044
- **Prompt:** What does “local area” mean for beginning and ending leave?
- **Answer:** The place of residence from which the member commutes to the duty station on a daily basis. Leave must begin and end in the local area.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-045
- **Prompt:** For leave enroute with PCS/TDY, which stations govern beginning and ending leave?
- **Answer:** The old permanent duty station is for beginning leave and the new permanent duty station is for ending leave. The financial service office uses the travel voucher to determine authorized travel and chargeable leave.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-046
- **Prompt:** Are weekends and holidays inside a leave period chargeable?
- **Answer:** Yes. Nonduty days and holidays are chargeable leave days if they occur during an authorized period of leave. A member cannot end leave Friday and begin again Monday around a weekend; successive Mon–Fri leaves are not approved except under emergency/unusual circumstances determined by the unit commander.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-047
- **Prompt:** What must a member do when requesting a leave extension, and what happens after a recall if leave resumes?
- **Answer:** Request the extension orally or in writing sufficiently in advance of expiration to return to duty on time if disapproved. If the commander authorizes resume of leave after recall duty, a new DAF Form 988 or orders must be prepared.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-048
- **Prompt:** At what rate do members accrue leave, and when do they not earn leave?
- **Answer:** 2.5 days for each month of RegAF status. Leave is not earned when AWOL/unauthorized leave, in confinement as a result of a court-martial sentence, in excess leave status, or on appellate leave.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-049
- **Prompt:** What fiscal-year leave accumulation maximum and accrued-leave payment ceiling does AFH 1 state?
- **Answer:** Accumulate a maximum of 60 days by the end of a fiscal year (carry-forward above 60 only when specifically authorized). By law, accrued leave payment up to a maximum of 60 days at certain points (reenlistment, retirement, separation under honorable conditions, or death).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-050
- **Prompt:** What DoD policy stance on using leave versus compensation does AFH 1 cite?
- **Answer:** DoD policy encourages members to use leave to relax from the pressures of duties and not as a method of compensation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-051
- **Prompt:** What is advance leave, and when does it become excess leave?
- **Answer:** Advance leave is granted based on a reasonable expectation the member will accrue at least the amount requested during remaining RegAF status, enabling resolution of emergencies/urgent situations with limited or no accrued leave. When a member has taken all advance leave representing remaining accrual, commanders change status from advance to excess leave.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-052
- **Prompt:** What are bereavement leave’s chargeability, purpose, duration, and accrued-leave eligibility cue?
- **Answer:** Non-chargeable paid leave to make arrangements required by death of a spouse or child and to attend the funeral, burial, or memorial service; up to 14 days; member must have less than 30 days of accrued ordinary leave on the date of such death.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-053
- **Prompt:** What pay and commitment effects does excess leave have?
- **Answer:** No-pay status: entitlement to pay/allowances and leave accrual stops on the first day; no disability pay if injured on excess leave; period does not count toward fulfillment of RegAF duty service commitment; charged each day plus an additional half day for every 6 days of negative leave.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-054
- **Prompt:** Who determines convalescent leave need, and who oversees the program?
- **Answer:** A medical or behavioral health provider determines the member is not yet fit for duty and recommends leave for convalescence. The Air Force Surgeon General oversees the convalescent leave program in accordance with AFMAN 41-210.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-055
- **Prompt:** Who may approve emergency leave for enlisted personnel, and what initial duration cue applies?
- **Answer:** Unit commanders approve emergency leave and can delegate leave approval to no lower than the first sergeant for enlisted personnel. Initial period is usually no more than 30 days with a possible 30-day extension; if more than 60 days, advise humanitarian/EFMP reassignment or hardship discharge.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-056
- **Prompt:** What is Emergency Leave of Absence (non-chargeable), and how often may it be granted?
- **Answer:** Up to 14 days of non-chargeable leave for a qualifying emergency (serious medical condition of an immediate family member; death of an immediate family member as defined in DAFI 36-3003; or other hardship the commander/director determines appropriate). Granted once during an entire career and only to prevent advanced or excess leave status.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-057
- **Prompt:** What enroute leave amounts does AFH 1 associate with normal PCS and with first duty station after training?
- **Answer:** Losing unit commander normally approves up to 30 days enroute with any PCS if it does not interfere with the report date. After basic or technical training, members may request 10 days enroute to a first CONUS duty station or 14 days for an overseas assignment.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-058
- **Prompt:** What characterizes terminal leave?
- **Answer:** Chargeable leave taken with retirement or separation; last day of leave coincides with last day of RegAF status; normally the member does not return to duty after it begins; amount cannot exceed the leave balance at the date of separation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-059
- **Prompt:** How do funded and unfunded environmental and morale leave differ on travel-time charging?
- **Answer:** Both are charged as ordinary leave. Funded EML: authorized military transportation; travel time is not charged as leave. Unfunded EML: space-available air transportation; travel time is charged as leave.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-060
- **Prompt:** How much non-chargeable leave does the Military Parental Leave Program authorize, and after what events?
- **Answer:** 12 weeks of non-chargeable leave after a qualifying birth event, adoption of a minor child, or placement of a minor child for adoption or long-term foster care with them to care for the child.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-061
- **Prompt:** What may commanders not do with permissive TDY relative to leave and special passes?
- **Answer:** Commanders may not authorize permissive TDY in place of leave or a special pass or in conjunction with special passes.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-062
- **Prompt:** List examples of authorized permissive TDY purposes in AFH 1.
- **Answer:** Traveling to or near a new PDS to secure off-base housing; accompanying a military or dependent patient to a designated medical treatment facility not in the local area; attending a DoD-sponsored TAP assistance seminar; attending national conventions or meetings hosted by service-connected organizations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-063
- **Prompt:** What is a pass period, and are there mileage restrictions?
- **Answer:** An authorized absence from duty for a relatively short amount of time. There are no mileage restrictions, but members may be expected to return within a reasonable time due to operational mission requirements such as recall, unit alert, or unit emergency.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-064
- **Prompt:** When does a regular pass normally begin and end on a traditional weekend schedule?
- **Answer:** Normally begins at the end of work hours on Friday afternoon and continues until the beginning of normal work hours on the following Monday when nonduty days are Saturday and Sunday.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-065
- **Prompt:** What limit applies when combining nonduty days and a public holiday for a regular pass?
- **Answer:** The combination of nonduty days and a public holiday may not exceed the four-day special pass limit. On non-traditional schedules, a regular pass period also may not exceed the four-day special pass limit.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-066
- **Prompt:** For what reasons may unit commanders award special passes, and to what level may approval be delegated?
- **Answer:** Three- or four-day special passes for special occasions, circumstances, special recognition, or compensatory time off. Approval may be delegated no lower than squadron section commander, deputies, or equivalents.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-067
- **Prompt:** When do three-day and four-day special passes start and stop?
- **Answer:** They start after normal work hours on a given day and stop at the beginning of normal work hours on the fourth day (three-day special pass) or the fifth day (four-day special pass).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-068
- **Prompt:** How does annual leave differ from convalescent leave in chargeability framing?
- **Answer:** Annual (ordinary) leave is the chargeable leave members normally use for vacations, family needs, holidays, significant events, or terminal leave. Convalescent leave is an authorized absence for recuperation when a provider determines the member is not yet fit and recommends leave—presented among AFH leave types with medical determination, not as ordinary vacation leave.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-069
- **Prompt:** How does emergency leave differ from Emergency Leave of Absence (non-chargeable)?
- **Answer:** Emergency leave is chargeable leave for immediate-family emergencies, usually up to 30 days with possible extension. Emergency Leave of Absence is up to 14 days non-chargeable leave for qualifying emergencies, granted once per career, and only to prevent advanced or excess leave status.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-070
- **Prompt:** Why is leave integrity important when representing weekends inside a leave period?
- **Answer:** AFH 1 states nonduty days/holidays inside authorized leave are chargeable, and members cannot end Friday and restart Monday to avoid charging the weekend. Accurate leave accounting reflects integrity in absence management.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11B; B—Understanding

## Section 11D — Civilian Programs

### A—Remembering (SSgt and TSgt)

### C11-FC-086
- **Prompt:** Through what organizations does the USAF provide civilian personnel services?
- **Answer:** Installation civilian personnel flights (usually located in the force support squadron) and Air Force Personnel Center. Civilian personnel sections must serve all civilian employees unless otherwise approved through HAF.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11D; A—Remembering

### C11-FC-087
- **Prompt:** Do civilian employees have a military rank? What typically grades their positions?
- **Answer:** No. Positions are graded, typically under the General Schedule, based on detailed written job descriptions/core personnel documents that determine pay, qualifications, performance expectations, and appraisal ratings.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11D; A—Remembering

### C11-FC-088
- **Prompt:** Who is responsible for core personnel document accuracy and use of standard CPDs?
- **Answer:** Supervisors are responsible for CPD accuracy and implementation of standard core personnel documents.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11D; A—Remembering

### C11-FC-089
- **Prompt:** Name groups AFH 1 says normally have mandatory selection or priority referral rights when filling competitive service positions.
- **Answer:** Those adversely affected by RIF, involuntary separation, demotion, transfer of function declinations or transfer of work outside the commuting area, overseas returnees, and the spouses of military personnel.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11D; A—Remembering

### C11-FC-090
- **Prompt:** How often does AFH 1 say the civilian resource management corporate board structure meets?
- **Answer:** At least quarterly to manage annual appropriations, revolving funds, or reimbursements used for civilian employment costs.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11D; A—Remembering

### C11-FC-091
- **Prompt:** What are the three primary sources of civilian training, and what form documents off-site training?
- **Answer:** Agency (USAF), interagency, and nongovernment. Training away from the work site uses Standard Form 182, Authorization, Agreement and Certification of Training.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.23
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11D; A—Remembering

### C11-FC-092
- **Prompt:** What happens when a civilian employee fails one or more performance elements?
- **Answer:** The employee is administered an unacceptable appraisal and is given an opportunity to improve.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.24
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11D; A—Remembering

### C11-FC-093
- **Prompt:** How many steps do GS grades and Federal Wage System grades have, and how is each pay expressed?
- **Answer:** GS: 10 steps; pay expressed as an annual salary (U.S. rates include locality pay). Federal Wage System: five steps; pay expressed as hourly wage rates.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.26
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11D; A—Remembering

### C11-FC-094
- **Prompt:** How much sick and annual leave do civilians earn, and what accumulation caps apply?
- **Answer:** 13 days sick leave each year (no accumulation cap). Annual leave 13, 20, or 26 days depending on length of service; capped at 30 days for most employees, 45 days for employees working overseas. Leave charged/taken in 15-minute increments.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.28
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11D; A—Remembering

### C11-FC-095
- **Prompt:** What bargaining-unit coverage percentage does AFH 1 state, and must covered employees be dues-paying members?
- **Answer:** Approximately 53 percent of USAF civilian employees are covered by labor agreements; they are members of the bargaining unit even if they are not dues-paying union members. **AFH source note:** Coverage percentage is a 2025 AFH snapshot—revalidate.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.29
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 11D; A—Remembering

### TSgt B—Understanding overlay (purposes and distinctions)

### C11-FC-096
- **Prompt:** Explain the purpose of core personnel documents and how they differ from military rank.
- **Answer:** Civilians do not have rank; graded positions (typically GS) rely on written job/CPD content to set pay, qualifications, performance expectations, and appraisal ratings. Expanded CPDs include recruitment qualifications and new-employee training requirements; supervisors own accuracy and use of standard CPDs to save classification time.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.20
- **Tags:** TSgt; 11D; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-097
- **Prompt:** How do competitive fill and priority referral relate when supervisors fill civilian jobs?
- **Answer:** Vacancies are filled based on organizational needs under OPM/DoD/USAF rules; referral and selection priorities must be observed for competitive service positions. Priority-referral groups (RIF-affected, involuntary separation, demotion, TOF/work-transfer issues, overseas returnees, military spouses) normally have mandatory selection or priority referral rights; AFPC screens and refers lists for competitive announcements.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.21
- **Tags:** TSgt; 11D; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-098
- **Prompt:** Distinguish agency, interagency, and nongovernment training sources and the government-first rule.
- **Answer:** Agency training is USAF-conducted (OJT, in-house, formal schools). Interagency training is sponsored by other U.S. Government agencies when agency sources are inadequate. Nongovernment sources include private seminars/courses/institutions; federal regulations require considering/selecting government sources first, using nongovernment when agency/interagency cannot satisfy the need or nongovernment is more advantageous.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.23
- **Tags:** TSgt; 11D; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-099
- **Prompt:** How do civilian leave rules in ¶11.28 differ from military leave rules in Section 11B?
- **Answer:** Civilian leave uses annual/sick earning tied to length of service, 15-minute increments, 30-/45-day annual caps, and no sick-leave cap—administered under civilian directives. Military leave in 11B uses 2.5 days/month RegAF accrual, LeaveWeb/DAF Form 988, and military leave/pass types. The systems are distinct; do not interchange rules.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶¶11.14, 11.28
- **Tags:** TSgt; 11D; B—Understanding

### C11-FC-100
- **Prompt:** Distinguish negotiated grievance procedures from administrative grievances for civilian dispute resolution.
- **Answer:** Labor-management contracts must include a negotiated grievance procedure for bargaining-unit members (often starting with ADR/mediators). Employees not covered by a CBA or not in the bargaining unit follow different procedures; administrative grievances are decided by USAF officials, usually the installation commander or designee, though outside fact finders may be used.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶11.30
- **Tags:** TSgt; 11D; B—Understanding
