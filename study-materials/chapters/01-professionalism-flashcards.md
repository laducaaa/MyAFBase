# Chapter 1 Flashcards — Professionalism

> **Status:** Draft, citation-mapped, and provisional for 27E5/27E6. These are original study prompts, not actual or recalled WAPS items.
>
> **Rank use:** All four Chapter 1 ADTC levels are identical for SSgt and TSgt. Cards are tagged for both grades.

## Section 1A — Professionalism

### C01-FC-001
- **Prompt:** What two broad dimensions does Air Force professionalism describe?
- **Answer:** Who Airmen are as a service and how they conduct themselves as Airmen and Air Force representatives.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1A; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-002
- **Prompt:** Which four requirements frame Air Force professionalism?
- **Answer:** Trust, loyalty, dignity, and personal commitment.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1A; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-003
- **Prompt:** What does AFH 1 identify as the root of professionalism?
- **Answer:** Respect, which connects each Airman's mission contribution to humble and respectful service with others.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1A; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-004
- **Prompt:** What basic obligations does professional military service place on every Airman?
- **Answer:** Follow orders, perform assigned daily duties, live up to high Air Force standards, and support good order and discipline.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1A; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-005
- **Prompt:** Why is good order and discipline connected to professionalism?
- **Answer:** It produces the reliability and coordinated conduct required for mission accomplishment.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1A; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-006
- **Prompt:** How is professional status expressed?
- **Answer:** Through attitudes, commitments, internalized military values, responsibility for one's actions, accountability of others, and appropriate action without excuse-making.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1A; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-007
- **Prompt:** What characterizes mission-focused professionalism?
- **Answer:** Readiness, selfless duty, sacrifice in answer to the nation's call, and courage to take appropriate risks when necessary.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1A; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-008
- **Prompt:** What characterizes people-oriented professionalism?
- **Answer:** Treating people as the most important resource, caring for wingmen, valuing contributions, motivating excellence, developing leaders, and honoring those who serve.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1A; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-009
- **Prompt:** Who makes up the Total Force described in Chapter 1, and what three characteristics describe it?
- **Answer:** Officer, enlisted, and civilian Airmen across Regular Air Force, Guard, Reserve, and Auxiliary Components; it is values-based, mission-focused, and people-oriented.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1A; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-010
- **Prompt:** Why are mission focus and care for people mutually supporting rather than competing ideas?
- **Answer:** People generate and sustain mission capability, while the mission gives collective purpose to their skills, development, and sacrifice.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1A; B—Understanding

## Section 1B — Profession of Arms

### C01-FC-011
- **Prompt:** Why does the profession of arms carry a higher standard than an ordinary occupation?
- **Answer:** Its members are entrusted with national security, protection of citizens, preservation of the nation's way of life, military discipline, and potentially life-threatening sacrifice.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1B; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-012
- **Prompt:** How should an Airman connect an individual duty to national defense?
- **Answer:** By understanding that every specialty supports, enables, governs, sustains, or protects the force responsible for security and defense of the homeland.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1B; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-013
- **Prompt:** What trust does an Airman accept by swearing to support and defend the Constitution?
- **Answer:** A public trust from the American people to use military authority, expertise, and capability for legitimate national purposes.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1B; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-014
- **Prompt:** What three national responsibilities does Chapter 1 associate with the profession of arms?
- **Answer:** Security of the nation, protection of its citizens, and preservation of its way of life.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1B; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-015
- **Prompt:** What distinctive expertise separates the profession of arms from other professions?
- **Answer:** Expertise in the justified application of lethal military force.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1B; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-016
- **Prompt:** What ultimate expectation demonstrates the exceptional sacrifice of the profession of arms?
- **Answer:** Members may be expected to risk or give their lives for the nation, its people, and its freedoms.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1B; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-017
- **Prompt:** How do Air Force identity and joint service relate?
- **Answer:** Airmen contribute distinctive Air Force expertise while serving as members of a joint team pursuing shared national-security objectives.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1B; B—Understanding

## Section 1C — Air Force Core Values

### C01-FC-018
- **Prompt:** What are the three Air Force core values?
- **Answer:** Integrity First; Service Before Self; Excellence In All We Do.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1C; C—Applying

### C01-FC-019
- **Prompt:** What do the core values express about Air Force service?
- **Answer:** Enduring convictions about personal character, commitment to one another and the nation, and the manner in which Airmen perform service.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1C; C—Applying

### C01-FC-020
- **Prompt:** How do virtues turn core values into professionalism?
- **Answer:** Virtues describe desired characteristics and behaviors; practicing them consistently creates habits of honorable thought and action.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1C; C—Applying

### C01-FC-021
- **Prompt:** What role does integrity play when an Airman faces temptation or an ethical challenge?
- **Answer:** It serves as a compass toward principled action and as the foundation for trust and respect.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.3.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1C; C—Applying

### C01-FC-022
- **Prompt:** What are the three virtues of Integrity First?
- **Answer:** Honesty, courage, and accountability.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.3.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1C; C—Applying

### C01-FC-023
- **Prompt:** What does honesty require when evaluating performance?
- **Answer:** Conscientiously comparing performance with standards and reporting the findings accurately.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.3.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1C; C—Applying

### C01-FC-024
- **Prompt:** How does Chapter 1 define courage as a virtue of integrity?
- **Answer:** Doing what is right despite fear, including taking necessary risks, making unpopular decisions, and admitting mistakes.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.3.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1C; C—Applying

### C01-FC-025
- **Prompt:** What behaviors demonstrate accountability?
- **Answer:** Transparency, seeking honest and constructive feedback, and owning the outcomes of one's actions and decisions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.3.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1C; C—Applying

### C01-FC-026
- **Prompt:** An Airman discovers a report gives the unit unearned credit. Which integrity virtues support correcting it?
- **Answer:** Honesty supports accurate reporting, courage supports correction despite discomfort, and accountability supports ownership and repair.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.3.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1C; C—Applying

### C01-FC-027
- **Prompt:** What priority does Service Before Self establish?
- **Answer:** Legitimate professional duties take precedence over personal desires, including when service requires sacrifice.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.3.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1C; C—Applying

### C01-FC-028
- **Prompt:** What are the three virtues of Service Before Self?
- **Answer:** Duty, loyalty, and respect.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.3.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1C; C—Applying

### C01-FC-029
- **Prompt:** What is duty?
- **Answer:** The obligation to perform what the mission requires, grounded in law, official guidance, and the individual's oath of service.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.3.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1C; C—Applying

### C01-FC-030
- **Prompt:** In what order does AFH 1 describe professional loyalty?
- **Answer:** Nation first, Air Force values and commitments second, and the men and women with whom Airmen serve third.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.3.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1C; C—Applying

### C01-FC-031
- **Prompt:** How can an Airman demonstrate loyalty while disagreeing with a proposed decision?
- **Answer:** Offer alternatives or innovation through the chain of command, then trust, follow, and execute the final lawful decision.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.3.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1C; C—Applying

### C01-FC-032
- **Prompt:** How do leaders demonstrate loyalty to those they lead?
- **Answer:** By treating them with dignity, compassion, genuine concern for well-being, and attention to professional development.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.3.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1C; C—Applying

### C01-FC-033
- **Prompt:** What three dimensions of respect appear under Service Before Self?
- **Answer:** Self-respect, mutual respect, and organizational respect.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.3.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1C; C—Applying

### C01-FC-034
- **Prompt:** What does Excellence In All We Do direct Airmen to pursue?
- **Answer:** Sustained continuous improvement, innovation, greater knowledge, stronger professional competence, and high readiness.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.3.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1C; C—Applying

### C01-FC-035
- **Prompt:** Why is excellence not the same as perfection?
- **Answer:** Excellence requires continuous advancement and learning; it does not demand flawless performance from every person in every task.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.3.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1C; C—Applying

### C01-FC-036
- **Prompt:** What continuing actions help an Airman embody excellence?
- **Answer:** Seek developmental education, strengthen physical, mental, emotional, spiritual, and moral fitness, improve competencies, and maintain skills, knowledge, and readiness.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.3.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1C; C—Applying

### C01-FC-037
- **Prompt:** What are the three virtues of Excellence In All We Do?
- **Answer:** Mission, discipline, and teamwork.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.3.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1C; C—Applying

### C01-FC-038
- **Prompt:** What does the mission virtue combine?
- **Answer:** Operational focus, stewardship, ingenuity, professional processes and relationships, innovation, and readiness to anticipate and embrace change.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.3.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1C; C—Applying

### C01-FC-039
- **Prompt:** How is discipline demonstrated?
- **Answer:** Through commitment to high standards in attitude, work ethic, effort, development, readiness, appearance, actions, and communication.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.3.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1C; C—Applying

### C01-FC-040
- **Prompt:** What does teamwork require beyond giving one's own best effort?
- **Answer:** Recognize interdependence, challenge and motivate others, build trust, carry one's share, and help wingmen when necessary.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.3.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1C; C—Applying

### C01-FC-041
- **Prompt:** How do honesty and accountability differ?
- **Answer:** Honesty concerns truthful evaluation and reporting; accountability adds transparency, feedback, and ownership of actions and outcomes.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.3.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1C; C—Applying

### C01-FC-042
- **Prompt:** Match each core value with its virtues.
- **Answer:** Integrity First—honesty, courage, accountability; Service Before Self—duty, loyalty, respect; Excellence In All We Do—mission, discipline, teamwork.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1C; C—Applying

## Section 1D — Ethical Standards

### C01-FC-043
- **Prompt:** How do moral values, ethics, and law differ?
- **Answer:** Moral values describe beliefs about right and wrong; ethics systematically reflects on those values to guide action; law establishes what is required, permitted, or prohibited.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1D; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-044
- **Prompt:** Why can minimum legal compliance be insufficient for an Airman?
- **Answer:** Ethical service also considers what should be done, motives, impartiality, public trust, and avoiding even the appearance of impropriety.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1D; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-045
- **Prompt:** How should personal values relate to Air Force ethical values?
- **Answer:** Personal values should be aligned with professional ethical commitments and must not take precedence when legitimate Air Force obligations control.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1D; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-046
- **Prompt:** Which sources does AFH 1 identify as prescribing an Airman's ethical code?
- **Answer:** Core values, oaths, the Airman's Creed, the profession of arms, DoD and Air Force guidance, the Joint Ethics Regulation, and the Uniform Code of Military Justice.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1D; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-047
- **Prompt:** What does “public service is a public trust” require?
- **Answer:** Place loyalty to the Constitution, law, and ethical principles above private gain.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1D; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-048
- **Prompt:** Why are conflicting financial interests and certain gifts ethical concerns?
- **Answer:** They can compromise—or appear to compromise—conscientious, impartial official judgment.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1D; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-049
- **Prompt:** What two ethical rules protect the government's decision-making authority from misuse?
- **Answer:** Do not make unauthorized commitments purporting to bind the government, and do not give preferential treatment to a non-federal entity or individual.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1D; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-050
- **Prompt:** What stewardship and citizenship duties appear in Chapter 1's ethics examples?
- **Answer:** Protect and conserve federal property for authorized use and satisfy lawful financial obligations, including taxes, in good faith.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1D; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-051
- **Prompt:** What are the three general sources of ethical dilemmas?
- **Answer:** Uncertainty, competing values, and potential harm.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.4.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1D; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-052
- **Prompt:** What can create uncertainty in an ethical dilemma?
- **Answer:** Incomplete facts, insufficient experience, or the absence of a clear policy, procedure, or rule.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.4.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1D; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-053
- **Prompt:** What are competing values?
- **Answer:** Conflicts between personal values and those of the institution, subordinates, peers, or supervisors that require the professional to identify the conflict, weigh options, and preserve standards.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.4.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1D; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-054
- **Prompt:** What does potential harm require an ethical decision-maker to consider?
- **Answer:** Intended and unintended consequences in both the short and long term.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.4.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1D; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-055
- **Prompt:** What decision process does Chapter 1 recommend before selecting an ethical course?
- **Answer:** Consider second- and third-order effects, order priorities, identify the strongest options, quality-check them, and apply moral tests when possible.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.4.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1D; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-056
- **Prompt:** What question is central to the Network Test?
- **Answer:** Could the Airman honorably defend the decision if it appeared on the news or social media, or would it bring shame or discredit?
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.4.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1D; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-057
- **Prompt:** In what order does the United States of America Test evaluate a decision?
- **Answer:** Whether it is good for the United States, the Air Force, the unit, and then the individual.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.4.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1D; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-058
- **Prompt:** What does the Divine Test ask an Airman to evaluate?
- **Answer:** Whether the Airman could give an honorable lifelong account of the decision without guilt or loss of trust.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.4.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1D; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-059
- **Prompt:** Distinguish valor, the three forms of courage, and sacrifice.
- **Answer:** Valor is resolute action in danger or hardship; personal courage risks one's career to do right; physical courage faces bodily harm; moral courage stands by core values when unpopular; sacrifice gives time, comfort, or life for others' needs.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.4.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1D; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-060
- **Prompt:** Who presented the Airman's Creed, when, and for what purpose?
- **Answer:** General T. Michael Moseley, the 18th Air Force Chief of Staff, presented it in 2007 to strengthen warrior ethos and create a coherent bond across the Total Force.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1D; B—Understanding

## Additional coverage cards

### C01-FC-061
- **Prompt:** How broadly should the core values guide Air Force conduct?
- **Answer:** They are universal, consistent standards for evaluating organizational ethical climate and should motivate attitudes and actions both on and off duty.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1C; C—Applying

### C01-FC-062
- **Prompt:** What general restriction does the 15 February 2025 AFH state for gifts from subordinates or lower-paid employees?
- **Answer:** Employees generally may not accept gifts from subordinates or employees who receive less pay. For real conduct, current ethics rules and official guidance control.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1D; B—Understanding; dated-rule

### C01-FC-063
- **Prompt:** What exception to the superior-subordinate gift restriction is printed in the 15 February 2025 AFH?
- **Answer:** Voluntary gifts or contributions of nominal value, not exceeding $10, for special personal occasions such as marriage or a child's birth, or occasions ending the superior-subordinate relationship such as retirement, permanent change of station, or reassignment. Apply current ethics guidance to real conduct.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1D; B—Understanding; dated-rule

### C01-FC-064
- **Prompt:** What does Chapter 1's Call to Duty connect with the Air Force's future success?
- **Answer:** Future success depends on flexible and adaptable current and next-generation Airmen who can respond faster than potential adversaries. The Air Force seeks to recruit, develop, and retain exceptional Airmen through programs that develop and care for the Total Force and strengthen Air Force culture.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.4.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1D; B—Understanding

### C01-FC-065
- **Prompt:** What major progression of ideas runs through the Airman's Creed?
- **Answer:** Airman and warrior identity; answer to the nation; fly-fight-win mission; heritage, honor, and valor; defense of freedom and country; wingman and leader roles; and commitment not to abandon, falter, or fail.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶1.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 1D; B—Understanding
