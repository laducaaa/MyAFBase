# Chapter 20 Situational Judgment Practice

> **Status:** Draft and provisional for 27E5/27E6. Chapter 20 is shared core in the historical 26E5/26E6 catalog scopes.
>
> These scenarios are wholly original. They are not actual, recalled, reconstructed, or predicted PFE items. The official Air Force uses subject-matter experts and behavioral scientists to establish operational SJT scoring keys. Chapter 20 describes AFIS, accountability, working relationships, and misconduct-response facts, but those facts alone do not establish an official behavioral scoring key. All most-effective and least-effective keys below are MyPromotion educational judgments grounded in Chapter 1 professionalism, core values, and ethical reasoning, with Chapter 20 material supplying context only. They are not official scoring keys or substitutes for current command, supervisory, IG, EO, SAPR, ADAPT, legal, investigative, or reporting procedures.
>
> **Rank / ADTC use:** Metadata reflects consolidated ADTC levels (20A/20B B for both; 20C/20D B for SSgt and C for TSgt). Scenarios that emphasize TSgt application of relationship standards or misconduct-response principles are tagged accordingly; SSgt study still uses the shared behavioral practice set.
>
> **Sensitive-topic posture:** Scenarios involving EO climate, substance misuse, or sexual assault reporting use plain, dignified language. They do not sensationalize harm and are not operational scripts.
>
> **Two-track source rule:** For exam-source study, preserve the facts in the AFH edition assigned by the controlling catalog. For duty use, separately verify current AFIS, IG, EO, SAPR, ADAPT, and adverse-action rules; duty-current information does not overwrite a catalog-controlled answer.

## How to use this set

Each scenario produces two items:

1. Select the **most effective** response.
2. Select the **least effective** response.

Read every option before choosing. Evaluate whether the response:

- represents inspection, accountability, relationship, and misconduct facts honestly;
- protects people, mission, privacy, and good order;
- uses supervisory influence without coercion, favoritism, or retaliation;
- stays within stated role and delegated authority;
- routes sensitive reports to the correct authorities without inventing investigations;
- respects people, support agencies, and the chain of command; and
- reflects integrity, accountability, loyalty, respect, and mission stewardship.

Chapter 20 supplies enforcing-standards context; it does not prescribe the behavioral sequence in every response. When a rationale depends on general professional judgment—such as honesty, courage, accountability, loyalty, respect, mission focus, or teamwork—it is identified as an application of the cited foundational Chapter 1 paragraphs.

Ten scenarios produce 20 response selections, mirroring the historical public structure announced for the [22E5/22E6 cycles in December 2021](https://www.afpc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/2874484/waps-adjusts-for-22e6-22e5-testing-cycles/). The separate [public SJT informational guide](https://www.studyguides.af.mil/Portals/15/documents/Notices/Situational-Judgment-Test-Informational-Guide.pdf) explains the four-option most/least format. The 2027 structure is unconfirmed. This set covers only selected Chapter 20 concepts and is not a full-spectrum SJT simulation.

## C20-SJT-001 — Honest self-assessment reporting

Your shop’s Self-Assessment Communicator shows a compliance shortfall caused by a known resource constraint. A peer suggests marking “compliant” so the commander and IG “see what they expect.” AFH 1 says an effective USAP depends on reporting what is true and, when compliance is not possible, reporting the constraints that prevent compliance.

### Response options

A. Mark compliant anyway so the report looks clean.  
B. Report the non-compliance accurately and document the constraint preventing compliance so the chain can see the real risk.  
C. Delete the SAC question so no one can answer it.  
D. Publicly accuse the IG of forcing false answers without documenting the shortfall.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 20.7 supplies USAP honesty context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it reports truth and constraints. Chapter 1 integrity and loyalty to the mission provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A falsifies the self-assessment and defeats AFIS’s purpose of identifying issues interfering with readiness and compliance. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It reports what leaders “want to hear,” not what is true.
- **B — Most effective:** It preserves honest self-assessment and surfaces constraints.
- **C — Ineffective:** Hiding the question conceals risk.
- **D — Ineffective:** Accusation without accurate reporting fails the USAP purpose.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶20.7 (USAP honesty context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** USAP; honesty; accountability  
**ADTC:** 20A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of self-assessment realism; local CCIP/USAP procedures control real reporting

## C20-SJT-002 — Inspection finding without duplication games

After an inspection, your team already logged a deficiency in MICT. Someone proposes rewriting the same deficiency into IGEMS as a “new” finding to look thorough. AFH 1 says IGs should not duplicate deficiencies or observations already entered into IGEMS, MICT, or other self-assessment tracking by the inspected organization.

### Response options

A. Duplicate the same deficiency into every system to inflate visibility.  
B. Avoid needless duplication; ensure the validated finding is accurately tracked in the appropriate system and worked to resolution.  
C. Erase the MICT entry so the problem disappears.  
D. Ignore the finding because documentation is optional under AFH 1.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 20.8 supplies non-duplication and validated-findings context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it preserves accuracy without gaming systems. Chapter 1 integrity and mission stewardship provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: C

C conceals a known deficiency and undermines readiness. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** AFH 1 warns against duplicating already-entered deficiencies/observations.
- **B — Most effective:** Accurate tracking without duplication games.
- **C — Least effective:** Erasing the problem is dishonest and unsafe.
- **D — Ineffective:** Findings including safety/surety/discipline issues are documented.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶20.8 (findings/non-duplication context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Inspection findings; honesty; stewardship  
**ADTC:** 20A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of IGEMS/MICT process realism

## C20-SJT-003 — Complaint rights without restriction

An Airman wants to take a lawful complaint to the IG after unsuccessful lowest-level attempts. A peer supervisor says, “If you go to the IG, I’ll make sure your career ends.” AFH 1 protects the right to present complaints without fear of reprisal and states no person may restrict a lawful communication to an IG or member of Congress.

### Response options

A. Support the Airman’s right to make a lawful IG communication and refuse any threat that would restrict that communication; use proper channels if reprisal concerns arise.  
B. Join the peer in threatening career harm if the Airman contacts the IG.  
C. Tell the Airman IG contact is always illegal.  
D. Destroy the Airman’s complaint notes so nothing can be submitted.

### Most effective: A

Paragraph 20.9 supplies complaint rights/anti-restriction context. A is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it protects lawful communication and rejects restriction. Chapter 1 integrity, respect, and courage provide the behavioral basis. Only the IG investigates reprisal/restriction under the Military Whistleblower’s Protection Act.

### Least effective: B

B is active restriction/reprisal pressure. Chapter 1 integrity and respect provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Most effective:** Protects lawful IG access and rejects restriction.
- **B — Least effective:** Threatens career harm to block IG access.
- **C — Ineffective:** False statement; IG access is protected.
- **D — Ineffective:** Obstructs complaint presentation.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶20.9 (complaints/reprisal-restriction context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Complaints resolution; anti-restriction; courage  
**ADTC:** 20B, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of whistleblower-climate realism; real reprisal allegations go to IG

## C20-SJT-004 — Public statement clearance

A coworker drafts an unofficial website post about an official unit capability and asks you to “just hit publish—PA slows everything down.” AFH 1 requires necessary review and clearance starting with public affairs before releasing proposed statements, text, or imagery to the public, including digital products on unrestricted websites.

### Response options

A. Publish immediately without PA review to save time.  
B. Pause release and obtain the necessary review and clearance starting with public affairs; ensure classification/release appropriateness.  
C. Add speculative claims about future classified plans to make the post more exciting.  
D. Imply the post is official USAF policy without any authority.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 20.11 supplies public-statement clearance context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it follows PA review/clearance before release. Chapter 1 integrity and loyalty provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: C

C adds speculation and potential classification risk. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** Skips required review/clearance.
- **B — Most effective:** Starts with PA and protects accuracy/classification.
- **C — Least effective:** Speculative/possibly classified content is especially harmful.
- **D — Ineffective:** Misrepresents official policy.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶20.11 (public statements context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Public affairs clearance; accuracy; OPSEC/classification caution  
**ADTC:** 20B, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of PA/release realism

## C20-SJT-005 — Political activity boundary

A member asks teammates to help manage a partisan campaign after duty and wear the uniform at a rally “for credibility.” AFH 1 prohibits partisan campaign management and wearing the uniform at political events/rallies, whether or not an official endorsement appears.

### Response options

A. Decline participation in prohibited partisan campaign management and refuse to wear the uniform at the political rally; remind the member to review applicable guidance before political activity.  
B. Agree to manage the campaign while in uniform at the rally.  
C. Use your official duty email to solicit political contributions from subordinates.  
D. Order junior Airmen to attend the rally as a mandatory formation.

### Most effective: A

Paragraph 20.13 supplies political-activity prohibition context. A is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it avoids prohibited participation and uniform misuse. Chapter 1 integrity and respect for lawful limits provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: C

C uses official authority to solicit political contributions—expressly prohibited. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Most effective:** Stays inside AFH 1 political-activity limits.
- **B — Ineffective:** Combines prohibited campaign management and uniform-at-event rules.
- **C — Least effective:** Misuses official authority for political solicitation.
- **D — Ineffective:** Coerces political participation through duty authority.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶20.13 (political activities context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Political activity limits; misuse of authority  
**ADTC:** 20B, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of political-activity realism; DAFI 51-508/DoDD 1344.10 control duty details

## C20-SJT-006 — Unprofessional relationship / favoritism appearance

You are a TSgt. A supervisor repeatedly grants one subordinate exclusive preferential shifts and awards while maintaining an exclusive personal social relationship that peers say shows favoritism. AFH 1 treats relationships that create the appearance of favoritism as unprofessional and matters of official concern when they erode morale, good order, discipline, respect for authority, unit cohesion, or mission.

### Response options

A. Ignore peer concerns because off-duty friendships can never be official concerns.  
B. Address the appearance of favoritism as an official concern; counsel toward professional boundaries and elevate through the chain of command/local procedures as appropriate.  
C. Punish every Airman who reported the concern.  
D. Publicly mock the subordinate to “balance” perception without correcting the supervisor’s conduct.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 20.17 supplies unprofessional-relationship context for TSgt application. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it treats favoritism appearance as official concern and seeks professional correction. Chapter 1 fairness, respect, and courage provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: C

C retaliates against those who raised concerns. Chapter 1 integrity and respect provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** AFH 1 says such relationships can become official concerns.
- **B — Most effective:** Corrects professionalism and uses proper channels.
- **C — Least effective:** Retaliation worsens climate and may implicate protected communications concerns.
- **D — Ineffective:** Targets the subordinate instead of correcting the unprofessional pattern.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶20.17 (relationship standards context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Unprofessional relationships; favoritism; fairness  
**ADTC:** 20C, C—Applying  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of relationship-intervention realism; legal/command procedures control real actions

## C20-SJT-007 — EO climate response

You become aware of repeated unwelcome race-based put-downs in the work center that a reasonable person would find hostile. AFH 1 charges supervisors to take immediate and appropriate action once aware and encourages consulting the local EO office; discriminatory harassment is unwelcome conduct based on race, color, religion, sex, and national origin.

### Response options

A. Take immediate appropriate action to stop the behavior, protect professionalism, and consult the local EO office before initiating resolution actions as AFH 1 encourages.  
B. Join the jokes to fit in.  
C. Tell complainants they must wait until the next annual climate survey before anything can be addressed.  
D. Threaten anyone who uses EO channels with unfavorable ratings.

### Most effective: A

Paragraphs 20.18–20.19 supply EO/harassment response context. A is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it acts promptly and uses EO consultation. Chapter 1 respect, courage, and loyalty to people/mission provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D is retaliatory intimidation against protected activity. Chapter 1 integrity and respect provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Most effective:** Immediate appropriate action plus EO consultation.
- **B — Ineffective:** Participating expands the hostile climate.
- **C — Ineffective:** Delays required immediate/appropriate action.
- **D — Least effective:** Threatens reprisal/retaliation against EO engagement.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶20.18–20.19 (EO/harassment context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Discriminatory harassment; supervisor responsibility; anti-retaliation  
**ADTC:** 20C, C—Applying  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of EO climate realism; local EO/legal procedures control real cases

## C20-SJT-008 — Supervisory sexual assault report duty

A subordinate in your supervisory chain privately discloses a sexual assault and asks you to “keep it completely unofficial.” AFH 1 requires military members/civilian employees (other than those authorized for confidential communications or otherwise exempted) who receive such a report about a subordinate in the supervisory chain to report to the commander, the SARC, and AFOSI.

### Response options

A. Quietly ignore the disclosure because it is uncomfortable.  
B. Report to the commander, the SARC, and AFOSI as required; treat the person with dignity and do not invent an investigation yourself.  
C. Accept it yourself as a restricted report and tell no one, even though you are the supervisor.  
D. Post the disclosure details on a unit social page to “warn the force.”

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 20.22–20.23 supply supervisory reporting and dignity/privacy context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it meets the mandatory report path without sensationalizing or investigating personally. Chapter 1 integrity, respect, and courage provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D destroys privacy and dignity. Chapter 1 respect provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** Ignores a mandatory supervisory reporting duty.
- **B — Most effective:** Reports to commander, SARC, and AFOSI; preserves dignity.
- **C — Ineffective:** Supervisors are not restricted-report recipients.
- **D — Least effective:** Public exposure is a severe privacy/harm failure.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶20.22–20.23 (SAPR reporting context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Supervisory reporting duty; dignity; privacy  
**ADTC:** 20C, C—Applying  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Mandatory human review for sensitive-topic dignity; real cases follow SARC/command/AFOSI procedures

## C20-SJT-009 — Graduated corrective action

You are a TSgt. An Airman has a first minor duty departure, accepts responsibility, and has an otherwise solid record. A peer urges an immediate Letter of Reprimand “to make an example.” AFH 1 says counseling/admonishment/reprimand should be used as part of a graduated pattern based primarily on nature/seriousness and prior record, and that counseling helps Airmen use good judgment and correct departures.

### Response options

A. Jump to the most severe paperwork available regardless of seriousness or record.  
B. Use counseling (verbal or written) matched to the minor first departure; document appropriately; set clear expectations for improvement.  
C. Place the Airman on the control roster solely because a single isolated breach occurred with no likelihood of repetition.  
D. Ignore the departure entirely and tell the flight standards do not matter.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 20.24–20.25 supply graduated corrective-action context for TSgt application. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it matches tool severity to the departure and record. Chapter 1 accountability, fairness, and respect provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D abandons standards enforcement. Chapter 1 accountability provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** Over-severe for a minor first departure under graduated discipline logic.
- **B — Most effective:** Matches counseling to seriousness/record and documents expectations.
- **C — Ineffective:** Isolated unlikely-to-repeat breach is not ordinarily control roster basis.
- **D — Least effective:** Fails to enforce standards at all.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶20.24–20.25 (corrective actions context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Graduated discipline; counseling; fairness  
**ADTC:** 20D, C—Applying  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of quality-force realism; commanders/legal control real adverse actions

## C20-SJT-010 — Substance-related misconduct referral

An Airman is involved in misconduct where alcohol appears to be a contributing factor. A coworker says to skip any assessment referral “so it stays quiet.” AFH 1 requires unit commanders to refer members for assessment when substance use/misuse is suspected as a contributing factor in any misconduct, and encourages members with problems to seek help through command, first sergeant, counselors, or medical professionals/ADAPT—while remaining accountable for unacceptable behavior.

### Response options

A. Hide the misconduct and block any commander/first-sergeant visibility.  
B. Ensure the chain of command is informed so required assessment referral can occur; support getting help while maintaining accountability for the misconduct.  
C. Promise the Airman that voluntary silence means no standards apply on or off duty.  
D. Publicly shame the Airman on social media instead of using command/medical/ADAPT channels.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 20.14–20.15 supply alcohol policy and commander’s identification context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it enables required assessment referral and balances help with accountability. Chapter 1 loyalty to people/mission and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A conceals misconduct and blocks protective referral. Chapter 1 integrity and accountability provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** Concealment increases risk to the member, others, and mission.
- **B — Most effective:** Enables proper referral and accountability.
- **C — Ineffective:** False promise; standards apply on- and off-duty.
- **D — Ineffective:** Public shaming is not the AFH help/accountability path.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶20.14–20.15 (alcohol/substance and commander’s ID context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Commander’s identification; ADAPT help-seeking; accountability  
**ADTC:** 20B, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of substance-misconduct realism; command/ADAPT/medical/legal control real cases
