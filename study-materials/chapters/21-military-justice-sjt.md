# Chapter 21 Supplemental Situational Judgment Practice

> **Status:** Draft and provisional for 27E5/27E6 preparation; the official 2027 format is unconfirmed.
>
> **Reserve-chapter status:** Chapter 21 was outside the historical 26E5 and 26E6 catalog scopes and remains reserve pending the 2027 WAPS catalogs. Prior exclusion does not prove future inclusion, weighting, or scenario testability.
>
> **Not legal advice:** These scenarios are educational judgment practice only. They are not legal advice, investigative scripts, or substitutes for SJA, ADC, Victims’ Counsel, OSTC, command, or current MCM/UCMJ procedures.
>
> **Two-track source rule:** For exam-source study, preserve the facts, dates, names, quantities, terminology, and ambiguities in the AFH edition assigned by the controlling catalog. For duty use, separately verify current military-justice organizations, procedures, forms, and authorities; duty-current information does not overwrite a catalog-controlled answer.
>
> These ten supplemental scenarios are wholly original. They are not actual, recalled, reconstructed, or predicted PFE items. They are supplemental SJT practice only, not a claim that military-justice content is scenario-tested.
>
> AFH 1 assigns Chapter 21 sections **21A–21D as B—Understanding for both SSgt and TSgt**. Those designations apply to recognizing, recalling, or explaining Chapter 21 military-justice facts; they do not make these scenarios B-level behavioral items. Chapter 21's military-justice facts provide context only and **do not establish behavioral scoring keys**. The most-effective and least-effective selections below are MyPromotion educational judgments based on Chapter 1 professionalism, core values, and ethical reasoning.
>
> The official Air Force uses subject-matter experts and behavioral scientists to establish operational SJT scoring keys. These supplemental judgments are not official keys or substitutes for current supervisory, legal, investigative, reporting, or command procedures. Any process or authority facts needed to rank an option are supplied within the scenario as invented instructional constraints.

## How to use this set

Each scenario produces two selections:

1. Select the **most effective** response.
2. Select the **least effective** response.

Read every option before choosing. Judge the learner's present-day conduct, not the wisdom of a courtroom actor or the technical excellence of a legal filing. Consider whether the response:

- represents sources, rights, authorities, and uncertainty honestly;
- accepts responsibility within the authority stated in the scenario;
- protects people, mission, and due-process boundaries without treating speed or silence as automatic answers;
- respects counsel, command, and process owners with accuracy and dignity;
- supports disciplined stewardship, readiness, and attention to detail; and
- strengthens teamwork across specialties without inventing legal outcomes.

Ten scenarios produce 20 response selections, mirroring only the historical public structure announced for the [22E5/22E6 cycles in December 2021](https://www.afpc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/2874484/waps-adjusts-for-22e6-22e5-testing-cycles/). The separate [public SJT informational guide](https://www.studyguides.af.mil/Portals/15/documents/Notices/Situational-Judgment-Test-Informational-Guide.pdf) explains the four-option most/least format. Neither source confirms the 2027 structure, and the historical 22E5/22E6 format does not show that Chapter 21 military-justice content was or will be tested through scenarios.

## C21-SJT-001 — Honest rights briefing without overclaim

You review an unclassified professional-development slide on court-martial panels. The draft closing line states that “every court-martial conviction requires a unanimous panel.” The designated baseline, AFH 1 dated 15 February 2025, states panels are not required to reach a unanimous verdict to convict except in capital cases. You may revise one content slide before delivery; the speaker must otherwise use the existing, source-cleared material.

### Response options

A. Keep the unanimous-conviction headline and hide the capital-case exception only in unread speaker notes.  
B. Delete every panel-verdict reference and end the briefing only with unit logos and names.  
C. Restore AFH 1’s non-unanimous conviction rule and capital-case exception on the closing slide.  
D. Soften the unanimous claim aloud while leaving the inaccurate headline displayed for the audience.

### Most effective: C

Paragraph 21.5 supplies panel-verdict context; it does not prescribe a behavioral scoring key for a briefing editor. C is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it uses the learner's stated revision authority to keep a clear closing within the source. Chapter 1 honesty, accountability, and disciplined communication provide the behavioral basis. Chapter 21 facts do not establish the behavioral scoring key.

### Least effective: A

A knowingly preserves an overbroad conviction claim and buries the capital-case distinction. Chapter 21 explains why the distinction matters, but Chapter 1 accurate representation and transparency provide the behavioral basis for the least-effective judgment.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It trades source-supported panel rules for a memorable but inaccurate headline.
- **B — Partially effective:** It avoids the false claim but withholds a supported conclusion the reviewer may fix.
- **C — Most effective:** It preserves a conclusive ending that matches the designated source.
- **D — Ineffective:** An oral caveat leaves the displayed closing still communicating an unsupported rule.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶21.5 (panel context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Accurate rights briefing; honesty; accountability; disciplined communication  
**ADTC context:** 21A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of briefing realism; not legal advice; approved source and public-affairs processes control real releases

## C21-SJT-002 — Article 31 pause without pressure

You are present when a supervisor begins questioning an Airman about a suspected offense. The Airman clearly asks for a lawyer. AFH 1 states that if counsel is requested, questioning must cease until counsel is present. Your role allows you to stop the discussion and notify the supervisor of that boundary; you are not the investigator of record.

### Response options

A. Stop the questioning immediately, document the counsel request, and route the matter to proper authorities.  
B. Urge the Airman to answer first so the shop can clear the issue without waiting for counsel.  
C. Remain silent and allow the supervisor to continue detailed questioning after the counsel request.  
D. Tell the Airman that counsel rights apply only after nonjudicial punishment paperwork has been served.

### Most effective: A

Paragraph 21.5 supplies Article 31/counsel-request context. A is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it uses stated authority to stop questioning and protect the counsel boundary. Chapter 1 integrity, respect, and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: B

B pressures the Airman to waive counsel through shop convenience. Chapter 1 respect for people and integrity provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Most effective:** It honors the counsel request and routes within role.
- **B — Least effective:** It pushes continued questioning after a clear counsel request.
- **C — Ineffective:** Silent observation allows prohibited continued questioning.
- **D — Ineffective:** It invents a false limitation that counsel rights await Article 15 service.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶21.5 (counsel-request context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Article 31; counsel request; integrity; respect  
**ADTC context:** 21A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review; not an interrogation script; real cases require current legal guidance

## C21-SJT-003 — Inspection versus evidence hunt

Your flight plans a dormitory “inspection” after a tip naming one Airman and one locker for stolen gear. A peer suggests calling it an inspection so no search authorization is needed. AFH 1 distinguishes inspections from searches by primary purpose and by not focusing on a particular suspect. You may approve the event framing for today’s schedule note.

### Response options

A. Label the tip-driven locker check an inspection to avoid seeking proper search authorization today.  
B. Cancel every unit inspection permanently, claiming any tip makes all inspections unlawful forever.  
C. Pause, treat the named-locker tip as a search issue, and seek competent legal and command guidance.  
D. Search only that locker yourself without notice, then fabricate an inspection log afterward.

### Most effective: C

Paragraphs 21.7–21.8 supply search/inspection context. C is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it refuses a false label and seeks proper authority within role. Chapter 1 integrity and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A knowingly mislabels a suspect-focused evidence hunt as an inspection to dodge authorization. Chapter 1 honesty provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It uses an inspection label to evade search rules.
- **B — Partially ineffective:** It overcorrects by banning lawful inspections unrelated to the tip.
- **C — Most effective:** It matches purpose/focus distinctions and seeks competent guidance.
- **D — Ineffective:** Unilateral covert search plus fabricated documentation compounds misconduct.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶21.7–21.8 (search/inspection context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Inspection vs search; honesty; lawful process  
**ADTC context:** 21B, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review; not a search warrant guide; SJA/command control real authorizations

D. Tell the Airman that turning down Article 15 is automatically punishable as insubordination today.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 21.9 supplies NJP forum-choice context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it corrects the false guilt claim and points to counsel within role. Chapter 1 honesty, respect, and loyalty to people provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A repeats a false guilt equation to rush a rights decision. Chapter 1 integrity and respect provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It coerces through a false guilt statement.
- **B — Most effective:** It restores source-accurate forum meaning and counsel access.
- **C — Ineffective:** Hiding the form obstructs a lawful notification process.
- **D — Ineffective:** It invents automatic insubordination for exercising a printed forum right.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶21.9 (NJP forum context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)

## C21-SJT-005 — Written appeal window stewardship

A member wants to appeal an Article 15 and asks whether an oral complaint to you today is enough. AFH 1 requires a written appeal within five calendar days; oral statements are not acceptable. You may give process-accurate guidance and help the member reach ADC; you cannot decide the appeal.

### Response options

A. Tell the member an oral complaint to you today fully completes the Article 15 appeal process.  
B. Advise that appeals must be written within five calendar days and help connect the member to ADC.  
C. Claim appeals become impossible as soon as any portion of the Article 15 punishment begins.  
D. Post the member’s allegation in a group chat and treat that post as the official written appeal.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 21.10 supplies appeal-form and timeline context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it states the written five-day rule and supports counsel access within role. Chapter 1 accountability and respect provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D substitutes a group-chat post for the required written appeal to the appellate authority. Chapter 1 honesty and accountability provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It misstates the appeal form requirement and falsely closes the matter.
- **B — Most effective:** It preserves the written five-day rule and counsel pathway.
- **C — Ineffective:** It invents a bar that punishments are not stayed, not that appeals vanish.
- **D — Least effective:** A group chat is not a written appeal to the appellate authority.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶21.10 (appeal context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Article 15 appeal; written process; stewardship  
**ADTC context:** 21C, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt

### Response options

A. Keep the commander-exclusive court-martial line to avoid confusing supervisors with OSTC authorities.  
B. Replace the line with OSTC exclusive court-martial authority for covered offenses as AFH 1 prints.  
C. Delete all disposition references and tell supervisors to invent local covered-offense rules instead.  
D. Merge OSTC and commander authorities into one invented shared-exclusive label that erases the distinction.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 21.2 and 21.15 supply covered-offense/OSTC context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it restores the printed exclusive-authority statement. Chapter 1 honesty and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: C

C removes disposition guidance and invites invented local rules for covered offenses. Chapter 1 integrity and accountability provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It preserves an inaccurate exclusive-authority claim for simplicity.
- **B — Most effective:** It aligns the checklist with the designated OSTC framing.
- **C — Least effective:** Removing guidance and inviting invented local rules creates risk.
- **D — Ineffective:** An invented shared-exclusive label erases the source distinction.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶21.2, 21.15 (OSTC context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Covered offenses; OSTC; accurate authority representation  
**ADTC context:** 21A/21D, B—Understanding

## C21-SJT-007 — Self-report timeline without collapse

A TSgt above E-6 privately tells you about a civilian criminal conviction dated ten days ago and asks whether waiting until day forty-four still meets every printed reporting rule. AFH 1 prints a 45-day DAFPD 36-29 rule and a 15-day AFI 1-1 rule for Airmen above E-6. You may give source-accurate guidance and urge prompt written reporting through the proper supervisor channel; you are not the designated official.

### Response options

A. Say waiting until day forty-four always satisfies every self-report rule printed in AFH 1.  
B. Collapse both printed timelines into one invented standard that allows reporting whenever convenient.  
C. Advise that above-E-6 civilian convictions require written report within fifteen days and urge prompt proper reporting.  
D. Tell the member never to report civilian convictions when the conduct occurred entirely while off duty.

### Most effective: C

Paragraph 21.3 supplies dual self-report timeline context. C is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it preserves the above-E-6 15-day rule and supports timely proper reporting. Chapter 1 accountability and integrity provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D advises non-reporting contrary to printed requirements. Chapter 1 integrity and loyalty to standards provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It ignores the separate 15-day above-E-6 rule.
- **B — Ineffective:** It invents a convenience standard that erases both timelines.
- **C — Most effective:** It applies the printed above-E-6 15-day requirement and urges proper action.
- **D — Least effective:** It directs concealment of a required report.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶21.3 (self-report context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Self-reporting; dual timelines; accountability  
**ADTC context:** 21A, B—Understanding

## C21-SJT-008 — Trial-counsel justice objective in a study huddle

D. Replace the ethics discussion with jokes about convictions so nobody examines trial-counsel duties.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 21.15 supplies trial-counsel duty context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it corrects the win-at-all-costs claim using the designated baseline. Chapter 1 integrity and excellence in professional standards provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A affirms concealment of favorable evidence. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It celebrates unethical non-disclosure as mission success.
- **B — Most effective:** It restores the justice objective and disclosure duty.
- **C — Ineffective:** Silence leaves a false professional norm uncorrected in a facilitated huddle.
- **D — Ineffective:** Deflection abandons the learning responsibility without fixing the error.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶21.15 (trial counsel context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Trial counsel ethics; disclosure; integrity
