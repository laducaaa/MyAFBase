## Section 15A — Accountability and Self-Management

### C15-FC-001
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define accountability, and how have surveys ranked it among foundational competencies?
- **Answer:** Accountability is demonstrating reliability and honesty and taking responsibility for the behaviors of self and team. In surveys of USAF members, it has been consistently rated as the most important foundational competency for success in a USAF career.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15A; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-002
- **Prompt:** What three actions does AFH 1 say accountability requires, and which Core Values do accountable Airmen embody?
- **Answer:** Leading by example, admitting mistakes, and doing the right thing even when unpopular or difficult. Accountable Airmen embody Integrity First, Service Before Self, and Excellence in All We Do.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15A; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-003
- **Prompt:** What three elements does AFH 1 say taking personal responsibility requires?
- **Answer:** Understanding the significance and potential irreversibility of failing to act; recognizing competence and ability to take needed steps (self-efficacy); and acting out of a sense of personal control (autonomy).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15A; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-004
- **Prompt:** What is self-efficacy according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** A person’s belief in their ability to address a situation, based on the skills they have and the circumstances they face.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15A; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-005
- **Prompt:** What does AFH 1 mean by autonomy in the accountability discussion?
- **Answer:** Ultimately you are your own person and make your own decisions on actions or inactions; you have freedom to choose how to respond even if so-called leaders or friends pressure you otherwise, and you can act to support the greater good.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15A; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-006
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 contrast fixed mindset with growth mindset?
- **Answer:** Fixed mindset: belief that intelligence and other personal qualities are mostly permanent. Growth mindset: belief in the capacity to fundamentally improve and significantly change the kind of person you are; past mistakes or failures don’t define who you are or can become.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15A; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-007
- **Prompt:** What patterns does AFH 1 associate with a fixed mindset?
- **Answer:** Give up easily when things get hard; constantly trying to prove intelligence or talent; if they don’t excel at first may believe little can change; overly sensitive about being wrong; may view exerting effort as evidence of inability.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15A; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-008
- **Prompt:** What growth-mindset goal behaviors does AFH 1 list?
- **Answer:** Persist in the face of setbacks; embrace challenges; value effort as the path to mastery; seek feedback and learn from criticism; find lessons and inspiration in the success of others.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15A; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-009
- **Prompt:** What four practical steps does AFH 1 suggest for becoming more perseverant if you hold a fixed mindset?
- **Answer:** 1) Remind yourself daily of fixed vs growth differences and growth-goal behaviors. 2) Ask what opportunities for learning and growth exist today, then plan and act. 3) Form a new plan when obstacles arise. 4) After success, ask what you must do to maintain and continue growth.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15A; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-010
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define self-control, and which Core Values does it connect to?
- **Answer:** Self-control is a person’s ability to override or restrain inner responses or impulses (emotions, temptations, inappropriate behavior). It is part of Integrity First and Service Before Self.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15A; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-011
- **Prompt:** Why does AFH 1 say changing the situation may beat relying only on willpower for self-control?
- **Answer:** Like a muscle, self-control is limited and depletes with heavy use. Changing the situation to reduce temptation (for example, keep chips out of the house; avoid anger triggers) is more likely to prevent inappropriate behavior than only internally controlling urges.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15A; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-012
- **Prompt:** How can self-control become stronger according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Like a muscle, it strengthens with practice. Regularly practicing small acts of self-control with rest can strengthen it over time; improving it in one domain can improve it in others. Even two weeks of deliberate daily practice to inhibit an urge can meaningfully improve impulse control.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15A; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-013
- **Prompt:** What practice method does AFH 1 describe for strengthening self-control?
- **Answer:** Set a target goal requiring self-control; keep a daily record of effort/self-control exerted; reflect on how practicing could improve self-control.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15A; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-014
- **Prompt:** What is positive reappraisal?
- **Answer:** The process of re-construing (re-interpreting) stressful events as non-threatening, meaningful, and even contributing to personal growth. It helps prepare for stressful events by embracing challenge and better cope with past events as opportunities for learning/growth.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15A; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-015
- **Prompt:** How does challenge framing differ from threat framing for a high-stakes presentation?
- **Answer:** Threat framing (focusing on task difficulty and evaluation) increases heart rate, pulse, and blood pressure and slows recovery. Challenge framing (a difficult task that can be met and overcome) is associated with less anxiety and faster physiological recovery.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15A; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-016
- **Prompt:** What reflective questions does AFH 1 recommend after negative events?
- **Answer:** Is there something you could learn? Can you envision something good coming out of dealing with the problem? How could the event change your life positively? How could you find benefit long-term?
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15A; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-017
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 distinguish cognitive reappraisal from wishful denial?
- **Answer:** Rather than wishful denial of reality, cognitive reappraisal allows actively finding meaning in adversity so people can flexibly grow and adapt longer term.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15A; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-018
- **Prompt:** What external factors does AFH 1 say also impact resilience besides how we think about stress?
- **Answer:** Support, relationships, and spirituality.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15A; B—Understanding

## Section 15B — Military Communication

### C15-FC-019
- **Prompt:** What skills does AFH 1 include in effective communication as a foundational competency?
- **Answer:** Presenting complex information articulately and concisely; tailoring communication to audience concerns; voicing differing opinions on contentious issues without triggering a defensive response; attending to nonverbal cues and communicating with sensitivity to others’ needs.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15B; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-020
- **Prompt:** What three parts does AFH 1 say communication can be broken into?
- **Answer:** The sender, the message, and the audience.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15B; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-021
- **Prompt:** How does DoD broadly define strategic communication in AFH 1?
- **Answer:** A process of purposefully using communication to advance national interests and objectives through synchronized integration of information with other elements of national power. It aligns actions, words, and images to obtain specific objectives.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15B; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-022
- **Prompt:** What qualities should enterprise-perspective strategic communication deliver?
- **Answer:** Truthful, timely, accurate, and credible information, analysis, and opinion. Truth is the foundation of all public communications; timely and agile dissemination is essential.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15B; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-023
- **Prompt:** What does AFH 1 say every Airman should be able to do regarding the USAF story?
- **Answer:** Be a communicator or spokesperson for the USAF and, through the enterprise perspective, tell their USAF story—integrating personal USAF story and experience into a message that adds credibility to USAF, DoD, and national strategic communication.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15B; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-024
- **Prompt:** What is the purpose of Public Affairs operations, and what is the PA representative’s role to the commander?
- **Answer:** Communicate timely, accurate, and useful information about USAF activities to DoD, the USAF, and domestic and international audiences. The PA representative is the commander’s principal spokesperson and advisor and a member of the personal staff.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15B; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-025
- **Prompt:** What social-media commentary does AFH 1 say Airmen may not place on internet blogs?
- **Answer:** Comments that reasonably can be anticipated or are intended to degrade morale, good order, and discipline of any members or units; are service-discrediting; or would degrade the trust and confidence of the public.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15B; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-026
- **Prompt:** What OPSEC posting restriction does AFH 1 state for social networking and non-DoD e-mail?
- **Answer:** Classified, controlled unclassified information, and other official DoD information and documents are prohibited from being posted on social networking services or transmitted via non-DoD e-mail accounts without proper authority.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15B; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-027
- **Prompt:** Why might social-network friends and followers matter for clearances?
- **Answer:** They may potentially constitute relationships that could affect background investigations and periodic reinvestigations associated with security clearances.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15B; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-028
- **Prompt:** What is JP 1-02 used for according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** It supplements English-language dictionaries and standardizes terminology used within DoD, other federal agencies, and among the United States and its allies; often used as a primary source for official correspondence and planning documents.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15B; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-029
- **Prompt:** What roles do AU-1, the GPO Style Manual, and The Tongue and Quill play in AFH 1?
- **Answer:** AU-1 guides Air University writing/editing/publishing and is valuable for grammar, mechanics, documentation, acronyms, ranks, and specialized military terms. The GPO Style Manual is the approved reference for government printing forms/styles. The Tongue and Quill is not all-inclusive but provides detailed information on most PME presentations and papers.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15B; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-030
- **Prompt:** When was the military phonetic alphabet adopted by the U.S. Armed Forces, and what organization alphabet is used?
- **Answer:** Adopted in 1956; all branches use the International Civil Aviation Organization alphabet for radio communication; also used by NATO countries and civil aviation worldwide.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15B; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-031
- **Prompt:** What is organizational communication according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** The strategic sharing of information, both internally and externally, within and across the organizational industry. Leaders need solid organizational communication skills to accomplish organization- and mission-related goals.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15B; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-032
- **Prompt:** What five attributes must an effective organizational culture of engagement have?
- **Answer:** Proactive, innovative, adaptive, leader driven, and sustainable.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15B; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-033
- **Prompt:** What does AFH 1 mean by leader-driven and sustainable engagement culture?
- **Answer:** Leader driven: leaders foster engagement, set command climate, and make themselves available for communication, especially in crisis. Sustainable: dedicated resources and manpower build enduring capabilities for a culture of engagement.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15B; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-034
- **Prompt:** What does being proactive and adaptive mean for organizational communication?
- **Answer:** Proactive: seize the initiative and be agile communicating the message; get out front with perspectives and experiences. Adaptive: adjust to changing circumstances in a fast-evolving, instantaneous, interconnected information environment.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15B; B—Understanding

## Section 15C — Preparing to Communicate

### C15-FC-035
- **Prompt:** Why does AFH 1 call preparation the most important aspect of good communication?
- **Answer:** By being prepared, speakers show audiences they value their time and the topic; preparation enhances confidence and credibility when communicating a message.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15C; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-036
- **Prompt:** What does AFH 1 say communicating intent and ideas so others understand and act on the message is?
- **Answer:** One of the primary qualities of leadership.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15C; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-037
- **Prompt:** What must happen for communication to be successful beyond the audience receiving the message?
- **Answer:** The audience must interpret the message the way the sender intended.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15C; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-038
- **Prompt:** What are the five FOCUS principles of effective communication?
- **Answer:** Focused, Organized, Clear, Understanding, and Supported.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15C; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-039
- **Prompt:** What does Focused mean in FOCUS?
- **Answer:** Understanding what the issue is, considering all aspects, and not straying—address the issue, the whole issue, and nothing but the issue.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15C; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-040
- **Prompt:** What does Supported mean in FOCUS?
- **Answer:** Support communication with information that substantiates the position but does not bring the audience to question the message; use support and logic to build credibility and trust—avoid implied data or distorted argument.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15C; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-041
- **Prompt:** List the seven steps for effective communication from DAFH 33-337 as presented in AFH 1.
- **Answer:** 1) Analyze Purpose and Audience; 2) Research Your Topic; 3) Support Your Ideas; 4) Organize and Outline; 5) Draft; 6) Edit; 7) Fight for Feedback and Get Approval.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15C; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-042
- **Prompt:** In step 1, what four purposes might you ask yourself whether you are communicating to achieve?
- **Answer:** Direct, inform, persuade, or inspire.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15C; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-043
- **Prompt:** What three review passes does AFH 1 recommend during the Edit step?
- **Answer:** 1) Big picture, main purpose, length, and flow of ideas; 2) paragraph structure, clarity, organization, and supporting ideas; 3) sentences, phrases, words, grammar, and audience perception.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15C; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-044
- **Prompt:** What should you communicate up front to reviewers in step 7?
- **Answer:** What your strengths and weaknesses are, why you selected them, and what areas you most likely need feedback on so they can focus efforts.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15C; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-045
- **Prompt:** What is one of the first steps in preparing for a job interview according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Carefully studying the job advertisement or position description so you understand the knowledge, skills, and abilities required, then aligning your KSAs to the job and gathering required application information/documentation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15C; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-046
- **Prompt:** When does AFH 1 say a job interview begins?
- **Answer:** The moment you pick up the phone or enter the property of the organization—everything the interviewer sees or hears is part of the interview.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15C; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-047
- **Prompt:** What organization information does AFH 1 suggest researching before an interview?
- **Answer:** Who the commander and senior enlisted members are, how large the organization is, and what the mission and vision statements are—plus other available mission/history information.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15C; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-048
- **Prompt:** What dual purpose do job interviews serve according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Employers find the right person for the right job, and candidates interview the prospective employer to see if the job is right for them and whether they want to commit the next few years.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15C; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-049
- **Prompt:** What post-interview action does AFH 1 say may be appropriate?
- **Answer:** A day or two later, send a short thank-you note expressing gratitude, restating interest, highlighting noteworthy points, or clarifying anything needed.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15C; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-050
- **Prompt:** When might phone or face-to-face be better than e-mail for a message?
- **Answer:** Phone when an immediate response is needed or clarification/elaboration could be lost in e-mail translation; face-to-face when the message involves multiple people or requires dialogue (sometimes recurring).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15C; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-051
- **Prompt:** What planning steps follow deciding that a meeting is appropriate?
- **Answer:** Define the purpose; decide who should be invited; decide where and when; plan for capturing meeting information; send out an agenda; be flexible based on availability of attendees, information, or other considerations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15C; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-052
- **Prompt:** What simple rules does AFH 1 list for running a successful meeting foundation?
- **Answer:** Start and stay on time, follow the agenda, understand group dynamics, and follow up with well-formatted meeting minutes—without making meetings so rigid that they become non-productive.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15C; B—Understanding

## Section 15D — Written Communication

### C15-FC-053
- **Prompt:** What key aspect does AFH 1 emphasize when choosing written communication format?
- **Answer:** Consistency in approach; attention to detail, proper format, and pertinent information ensure written communication is a professional representation of the organization.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.18
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15D; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-054
- **Prompt:** When are official memorandums used?
- **Answer:** To communicate throughout DoD and other federal agencies, and to conduct official business outside the government with vendors or contractors when the personal letter is not appropriate.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15D; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-055
- **Prompt:** How may official memorandums be addressed?
- **Answer:** To specific officials, single offices, multiple offices, multiple offices IN TURN, or to DISTRIBUTION lists.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15D; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-056
- **Prompt:** When should you use a personal letter, and what length/acronym guidance applies?
- **Answer:** When communication needs a personal touch or warmth/sincerity is preferred (for example praise, condolence, or sponsorship). Keep it brief, preferably no longer than one page, and avoid acronyms.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15D; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-057
- **Prompt:** Name the types of USAF papers listed in AFH 1.
- **Answer:** Point paper, talking paper, bullet background paper, background paper, position paper, and staff study.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15D; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-058
- **Prompt:** What is the function of a point paper?
- **Answer:** Provide a memory jogger or minimal-text outline of a single issue to quickly inform others, often extemporaneously; can give the same short message many times or cue a speaker to recite from memory. Single page using bullets/phrases with minimal data.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15D; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-059
- **Prompt:** How does a talking paper differ from a point paper?
- **Answer:** It is slightly more detailed; provides notes/outline or narrative for a planned/scheduled oral presentation on a single issue, with key reference data; can stand alone for basic understanding and serve as quick reference on key points, facts, or positions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15D; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-060
- **Prompt:** What is a bullet background paper used for?
- **Answer:** Addressing a single issue or several related issues in single- or multi-page bullet format providing background of a program, policy, problem, or procedure; concise chronology; can explain or detail an attached talking paper.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15D; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-061
- **Prompt:** What characterizes a background paper?
- **Answer:** Single issue or several related issues in multi-page format with full sentences, details, and numbered paragraphs; detailed chronology; condenses/summarizes complex issues; informs decision-makers with important details; supports oral presentations or staff discussions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15D; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-062
- **Prompt:** What is a position paper used for?
- **Answer:** Addressing a single issue or several related issues in multi-page format with full sentences, details, and numbered paragraphs—often for proposals for a new program, policy, procedure, or plan; to circulate, evaluate, or advocate a position to decision-makers.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15D; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-063
- **Prompt:** What is the purpose of a staff study?
- **Answer:** Analyze a problem, draw conclusions, and make recommendations via a multi-page research paper with detailed discussion, conclusion, and applicable recommendations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15D; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-064
- **Prompt:** Why does AFH 1 say the staff study’s thought-process value often outweighs format?
- **Answer:** Not all organizations routinely use the staff study, but it is an accepted USAF and Joint Staff problem-solution format; understanding and applying essential elements of problem analysis enables better application of any staff communication.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15D; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-065
- **Prompt:** What is a staff package used for?
- **Answer:** A writing format commonly used in the USAF for routing or coordinating correspondence through a staffing process.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.23
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15D; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-066
- **Prompt:** What is AF Form 1768, and what nicknames does AFH 1 give it?
- **Answer:** Staff Summary Sheet—the cover page (first page) of a staff package. Known as the SSS, the Triple-S, or the e-SSS.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.23
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15D; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-067
- **Prompt:** What condensed elements does the Staff Summary Sheet provide?
- **Answer:** Purpose, background, discussion, view of others (when applicable), recommendation, signature blocks, and overall contents of the staff package.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.23
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15D; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-068
- **Prompt:** Where does AFH 1 point for detailed memorandum, letter, and paper format instructions?
- **Answer:** DAFH 33-337—Chapter 14 (official memorandum), Chapter 15 (personal letter), and Chapter 16 (USAF papers samples/explanations).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶¶15.19–15.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15D; B—Understanding

## Section 15E — Spoken Communication

### C15-FC-069
- **Prompt:** What three common spoken delivery formats does AFH 1 describe?
- **Answer:** Impromptu, prepared, and manuscript.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.25
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15E; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-070
- **Prompt:** What does superb impromptu speaking require, and how does AFH 1 rank it?
- **Answer:** Great self-confidence, mastery of the subject, and ability to think on your feet. A superb impromptu speaker has achieved the highest level in verbal communications.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.25
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15E; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-071
- **Prompt:** How does prepared speaking differ from memorizing a script?
- **Answer:** Prepared delivery requires a thorough outline with careful planning and practicing, but specific words and phrases at delivery are spontaneous and sound natural—not a memorized script.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.25
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15E; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-072
- **Prompt:** What disadvantage does AFH 1 associate with manuscript briefings?
- **Answer:** Tendency to lack spontaneity and eye contact and stand behind the lectern with the script, which may lose the audience’s attention.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.25
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15E; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-073
- **Prompt:** What qualities does every good briefing have, and which quality distinguishes briefings?
- **Answer:** Accuracy, brevity, and clarity. Accuracy and clarity characterize all good speaking, but brevity distinguishes the briefing from other types of speaking.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.26
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15E; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-074
- **Prompt:** What should a briefer do if unable to answer a question?
- **Answer:** Not attempt an off-the-cuff answer; request an opportunity to research the question and follow up with an answer later.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.26
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15E; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-075
- **Prompt:** How do formal and informal teaching lectures differ?
- **Answer:** Formal lectures are generally one-way with no verbal audience participation. Informal lectures are usually presented to smaller audiences and allow verbal interaction.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.26
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15E; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-076
- **Prompt:** What are the three basic purposes of a formal speech?
- **Answer:** To inform, persuade, or entertain.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.26
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15E; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-077
- **Prompt:** What rank-related communication tip does AFH 1 give?
- **Answer:** Differences in military rank can be a real or perceived barrier. Strive to be candid, direct, and respectful with everyone, not only those senior or junior in rank.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.27
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15E; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-078
- **Prompt:** What tone guidance does AFH 1 give for formal written communication regarding emojis?
- **Answer:** Emojis do not have a place in written formal communication; recognize limitations of expressing tone in writing and pay close attention to how the message may be perceived.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.27
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15E; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-079
- **Prompt:** What pronoun guidance does AFH 1 give under Make it Personal?
- **Answer:** When appropriate use we, us, and our; use I, me, and my sparingly; be aware of how the use of you can be perceived in some situations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.27
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15E; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-080
- **Prompt:** What approximate normal spoken rate does AFH 1 cite, and why can speaking too slowly lose an audience?
- **Answer:** About 120 words a minute. People can listen four to five times faster, so speaking too slowly may lose interest of an audience processing information much faster.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.28
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15E; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-081
- **Prompt:** How can volume and pitch enhance spoken delivery?
- **Answer:** Volume can emphasize a point—softer/lower volume is often more effective. Pitch variety avoids monotone; downward inflection suggests certainty and upward inflection uncertainty.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.28
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15E; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-082
- **Prompt:** Why is eye contact important in nonverbal communication?
- **Answer:** It lets listeners know the speaker is interested, allows the speaker to receive nonverbal feedback, and enhances the speaker’s credibility.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.28
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15E; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-083
- **Prompt:** List key steps AFH 1 gives for overcoming public-speaking anxiety.
- **Answer:** Know the material; analyze the audience; envision success; practice with recording/mirror/peers; prepare mentally; present a professional image; smile; take a short walk; focus on purpose not self; encourage audience interaction when possible.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.29
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15E; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-084
- **Prompt:** What is the life raft nonverbal quirk?
- **Answer:** Seeking the safety and security of a podium as though life depends on it; when possible, venture away from the podium to connect better with the audience.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.30
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15E; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-085
- **Prompt:** What are the caged tiger and rocker quirks?
- **Answer:** Caged tiger: pacing across the stage from side to side without stopping. Rocker: settled somewhat but still rocking on heels or swaying side to side instead of standing still and talking.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.30
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15E; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-086
- **Prompt:** What is the too cool quirk?
- **Answer:** Overcompensating for fear of speaking by trying to look extremely comfortable, appearing unengaged or disinterested at the expense of reaching and keeping the audience’s attention.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.30
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15E; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-087
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 distinguish hearing from listening?
- **Answer:** Hearing occurs when ears pick up sounds. Listening involves hearing while also paying attention and giving consideration—thinking about and making sense of the message.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.31
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15E; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-088
- **Prompt:** What is informative listening, and what improvement suggestions does AFH 1 give?
- **Answer:** Primary concern is to understand information exactly as transmitted. Suggestions: keep an open mind and set aside bias; listen as if you had to teach it; take notes; ask clarifying questions; mentally repeat and absorb information to make it pertinent.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.31
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15E; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-089
- **Prompt:** What is critical listening, and when is it appropriate?
- **Answer:** Usually informative listening plus critical thinking—actively analyzing and evaluating the message. Appropriate when seeking input to a decision, evaluating work or a subordinate’s capabilities, or conducting research.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.31
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15E; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-090
- **Prompt:** When is empathic listening often useful?
- **Answer:** When communication is emotional or when the relationship between speaker and listener is just as important as the message—often appropriate during mentoring and counseling and helpful with family members; can be a prerequisite feel to informational or critical listening.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.31
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15E; B—Understanding

## Section 15F — Electronic Messaging

### Core B—Understanding cards (SSgt and TSgt)

### C15-FC-091
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define e-mail?
- **Answer:** The electronic transmission of information over computer-based messaging systems.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.32
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15F; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-092
- **Prompt:** What are the six e-mail etiquette rules in AFH 1?
- **Answer:** 1) Be Clear and Concise; 2) Watch Your Tone; 3) Be Selective About What Message You Send; 4) Be Selective About Who Gets Your Message; 5) Check Your Attachments and Support Material; 6) Keep Your E-mail Under Control.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.32
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15F; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-093
- **Prompt:** What does Rule 2 say about ALL CAPITAL letters and offensive material?
- **Answer:** Do not write using all CAPITAL letters—it is the e-mail equivalent of shouting and is considered rude. Keep e-mail clean and professional; harassing, intimidating, abusive, or offensive material is unacceptable.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.32
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15F; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-094
- **Prompt:** What types of content does Rule 3 say not to discuss in e-mail?
- **Answer:** Controversial, sensitive, classified, personal, Privacy Act, or controlled unclassified information that requires special handling; also remember OPSEC aggregation risk; no junk mail, chain letters, or personal ads.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.32
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15F; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-095
- **Prompt:** What distribution guidance does Rule 4 give?
- **Answer:** Reply to specific addressees; use reply-all sparingly; get permission before using large mail groups; double-check addresses, especially from global lists with similar last names.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.32
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15F; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-096
- **Prompt:** For what uses are government-provided messaging systems authorized?
- **Answer:** Official use and limited authorized personal use only. All government communications systems are subject to monitoring, interception, search, and seizure for authorized purposes.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.33
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15F; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-097
- **Prompt:** What auto-forward and address-release restrictions does AFH 1 state?
- **Answer:** Do not auto-forward electronic messages from the .mil domain to a commercial internet service provider, and do not indiscriminately release electronic messaging addresses to the public.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.33
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15F; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-098
- **Prompt:** What is the Defense Message System in AFH 1?
- **Answer:** The core messaging system of record for DoD and the USAF—a flexible, commercial, off-the-shelf based application providing messaging services to all DoD users (including deployed tactical users) and interfacing with other U.S. Government agencies, branches of service, and defense contractors.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.33
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15F; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-099
- **Prompt:** When sending personal information over e-mail within DoD, what subject-line and body statement cues does AFH 1 prescribe?
- **Answer:** Ensure official need and authorized recipients. Add “Controlled unclassified information” at the beginning of the subject line, followed by the subject. Apply at the beginning of the e-mail: “This e-mail contains controlled unclassified information which must be protected under The Privacy Act and AFI 33-332.” Use only when actually transmitting personal information.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.33
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15F; B—Understanding

### C15-FC-100
- **Prompt:** What conditions make limited personal internet use appropriate under AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Reasonable duration and frequency, approved by the supervisor, and must not adversely affect performance of official duties, overburden systems, or reflect adversely on the USAF or DoD.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.34
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 15F; B—Understanding

### TSgt C—Applying overlay cards (15F; no SSgt tag)

### C15-FC-101
- **Prompt:** A shop e-mail chain is noisy. Applying AFH 1 Rule 4, when should you avoid reply-all?
- **Answer:** Use reply-all sparingly; reply to specific addressees to give those not interested a break. Prefer directed replies unless every addressee truly needs the response.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.32
- **Tags:** TSgt; 15F; C—Applying

### C15-FC-102
- **Prompt:** You drafted an angry ALL-CAPS reply after a missed suspense. Applying AFH 1 Rule 2, what should you do before sending?
- **Answer:** Do not send ALL CAPS (shouting). Keep the e-mail clean and professional; be polite; treat the message like a personal conversation; avoid harassing, intimidating, abusive, or offensive material—edit tone before sending.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.32
- **Tags:** TSgt; 15F; C—Applying

### C15-FC-103
- **Prompt:** An Airman wants to e-mail Privacy Act details to a broad organizational group “for awareness.” Applying AFH 1, what constraints apply?
- **Answer:** Do not discuss Privacy Act/personal information that requires special handling without proper controls. When sending personal information within DoD, ensure official need and that all addressees are authorized; mark and statement rules apply only when actually transmitting personal information—not indiscriminately.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶¶15.32–15.33
- **Tags:** TSgt; 15F; C—Applying

### C15-FC-104
- **Prompt:** You hit send and realize the attachment is missing. Applying AFH 1 Rule 5, what habit should have prevented the follow-up?
- **Answer:** Before sending, ensure you have attached the attachments—this is a very common mistake—and that all intended information is provided so a follow-up e-mail is unnecessary.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.32
- **Tags:** TSgt; 15F; C—Applying

### C15-FC-105
- **Prompt:** A peer sets auto-forward from their .mil account to a commercial ISP for leave. Applying AFH 1, is that allowed?
- **Answer:** No. Do not auto-forward electronic messages from the .mil domain to a commercial internet service provider.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.33
- **Tags:** TSgt; 15F; C—Applying

### C15-FC-106
- **Prompt:** Someone asks you to post a controlled unclassified work document to a public commercial social site for convenience. Applying AFH 1, what is the rule?
- **Answer:** Copying and posting CUI, critical information, and/or personally identifiable information on DoD publicly accessible sites or commercial internet-based capabilities is prohibited. Classified/CUI/official DoD information also must not be posted on social networking without proper authority.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶¶15.9, 15.34
- **Tags:** TSgt; 15F; C—Applying

### C15-FC-107
- **Prompt:** An Airman asks to use a government computer for frequent personal shopping that slows work. Applying AFH 1 appropriate-use rules, what must be true for any limited personal use?
- **Answer:** Limited personal use must be of reasonable duration and frequency, approved by the supervisor, and not adversely affect official duties, overburden systems, or reflect adversely on the USAF or DoD. Unauthorized personal use is prohibited.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.34
- **Tags:** TSgt; 15F; C—Applying

### C15-FC-108
- **Prompt:** Antivirus alerts and unexpected disk access appear on a workstation. Applying AFH 1, what should you do?
- **Answer:** Report any suspected information system abnormalities (antivirus errors, virus alerts, unexpected file size increases, unexpected disk access, strange application activity) immediately to the organizational information assurance officer.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.34
- **Tags:** TSgt; 15F; C—Applying

### C15-FC-109
- **Prompt:** While on government resources in an authorized personal capacity, an Airman wants to post as if stating USAF policy. Applying AFH 1 OPSEC/internet guidance, what constraint applies?
- **Answer:** Comply with AFI 10-701 OPSEC guidance and shall not represent the policies or official position of the USAF or the Department of Defense.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.34
- **Tags:** TSgt; 15F; C—Applying

### C15-FC-110
- **Prompt:** You must send personal information to authorized DoD recipients with official need. Applying AFH 1 ¶15.33, how should you mark the message?
- **Answer:** Put “Controlled unclassified information” at the beginning of the subject line, followed by the subject, and begin the e-mail with the Privacy Act/AFI 33-332 controlled unclassified information statement. Do not apply the statement when you are not actually transmitting personal information.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶15.33
- **Tags:** TSgt; 15F; C—Applying
