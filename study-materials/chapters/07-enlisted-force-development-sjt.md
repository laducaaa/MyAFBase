# Chapter 7 Situational Judgment Practice

> **Status:** Draft and provisional for 27E5/27E6. Chapter 7 is shared core in the historical 26E5/26E6 catalog scopes.
>
> These scenarios are wholly original. They are not actual, recalled, reconstructed, or predicted PFE items. The official Air Force uses subject-matter experts and behavioral scientists to establish operational SJT scoring keys. Chapter 7 describes enlisted force development facts—leadership levels, force structure, duty titles, training, PME, CCAF, and continuing education—but those facts alone do not establish an official behavioral scoring key. All most-effective and least-effective keys below are MyPromotion educational judgments grounded in Chapter 1 professionalism, core values, and ethical reasoning, with Chapter 7 material supplying context only. They are not official scoring keys or substitutes for current command, supervisory, safety, legal, ethics, training, education, or reporting procedures.
>
> **Section 7B ADTC conflict:** The consolidated ADTC rates Section 7B B for SSgt and TSgt; the section-local chart prints B for SSgt and C for TSgt. Scenario metadata uses B—Understanding for both ranks. No separate TSgt-C behavioral overlay is included.
>
> **Two-track source rule:** For exam-source study, preserve the facts in the AFH edition assigned by the controlling catalog. For duty use, separately verify current PME, CCAF, training, funding, and program rules; duty-current information does not overwrite a catalog-controlled answer.

## How to use this set

Each scenario produces two items:

1. Select the **most effective** response.
2. Select the **least effective** response.

Read every option before choosing. Evaluate whether the response:

- represents force-development facts, program rules, and uncertainty honestly;
- uses mentoring and authority without coercion or status misuse;
- protects training-documentation integrity and readiness accuracy;
- stays within stated role and delegated authority;
- balances self-development with mission and duty requirements;
- respects people, process owners, and the chain of command; and
- reflects integrity, accountability, loyalty, respect, and mission stewardship.

Chapter 7 supplies developmental and structural context; it does not prescribe the behavioral sequence in every response. When a rationale depends on general professional judgment—such as honesty, courage, accountability, loyalty, respect, mission focus, or teamwork—it is identified as an application of the cited foundational Chapter 1 paragraphs.

Ten scenarios produce 20 response selections, mirroring the historical public structure announced for the [22E5/22E6 cycles in December 2021](https://www.afpc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/2874484/waps-adjusts-for-22e6-22e5-testing-cycles/). The separate [public SJT informational guide](https://www.studyguides.af.mil/Portals/15/documents/Notices/Situational-Judgment-Test-Informational-Guide.pdf) explains the four-option most/least format. The 2027 structure is unconfirmed. This set covers only selected Chapter 7 concepts and is not a full-spectrum SJT simulation.

## C07-SJT-001 — Mentoring versus misuse of authority

You mentor a Senior Airman who asks how to compete for a high-visibility base project. AFH 1 describes mentoring and developmental conversation as part of deliberate career progression. The project lead’s published criteria emphasize technical skill and schedule availability. You supervise the Airman’s duty section but do not own project selection. After hours, a peer suggests you “just put your troop on the team” because you outrank the project NCOIC.

### Response options

A. Tell the project NCOIC the selection is already decided and that your Airman will report Monday.  
B. Help the Airman prepare an evidence-based package against the published criteria and submit it through the project lead’s stated process.  
C. Promise the Airman selection in exchange for taking your least desirable weekend duty for the next month.  
D. Refuse to discuss the opportunity at all because mentoring on competition is inappropriate for supervisors.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 7.1 and 7.10 supply force-development and mentoring context; they do not create a behavioral scoring key for project selection. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it uses mentoring to strengthen a fair, criteria-based submission without seizing selection authority. Chapter 1 respect, loyalty, honesty, and accountability support helping the Airman compete without misusing supervisory status.

### Least effective: C

C trades duty assignments for preferential placement and converts mentoring into coercion. Chapter 7’s mentoring language does not authorize that bargain; Chapter 1 integrity, respect, and misuse-of-authority concerns provide the behavioral basis for MyPromotion’s least-effective judgment.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It usurps the project lead’s selection authority and misrepresents a decision that has not been made.
- **B — Most effective:** It mentors within published criteria and preserves the proper selection channel.
- **C — Least effective:** It conditions assistance on a personal duty trade and corrupts both mentoring and fairness.
- **D — Partially effective:** It avoids misuse of authority but abandons legitimate developmental support the scenario permits.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶7.1, 7.10 (development/mentoring context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Mentoring; misuse of authority; honesty; respect; accountability  
**ADTC:** 7D, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of mentoring realism; actual selection authorities and ethics rules control real cases

## C07-SJT-002 — Accurate PME guidance

A Staff Sergeant asks whether completing an EPME distance-learning course is still required before resident NCO Academy attendance. Your designated study baseline, AFH 1 dated 15 February 2025, states EPME DLC completion is no longer a prerequisite for resident attendance effective 26 April 2018 and that Air University no longer allows new active-component DLC enrollments, with reference-only access through AUSIS. A co-worker insists the old prerequisite still applies and wants you to tell the Airman to enroll immediately “to be safe.” Local education-office confirmation is available today.

### Response options

A. Tell the Airman the old prerequisite still controls and to enroll in a new active-component DLC seat today.  
B. Say you are unsure, then change the subject to avoid looking uninformed.  
C. Quote AFH 1’s dated policy note accurately, state that current AU/education-office confirmation should control registration actions, and walk the Airman to the education office the same day.  
D. Invent a hybrid rule that DLC is optional for some AFSCs and mandatory for others without source support.

### Most effective: C

Paragraph 7.21 supplies the PME policy snapshot; it does not score the counseling behavior. C is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it represents the assigned baseline honestly, flags volatility, and uses the available confirmation channel before a registration action. Chapter 1 honesty, accountability, and disciplined communication provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A knowingly restates a superseded prerequisite and pushes a new active-component enrollment the baseline says is no longer allowed. Chapter 7 facts identify the conflict; Chapter 1 accurate representation provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It gives actionable guidance the designated baseline contradicts.
- **B — Ineffective:** Avoiding the question leaves the Airman without help when confirmation is available.
- **C — Most effective:** It preserves source accuracy and converts uncertainty into same-day verification.
- **D — Partially effective intent, ineffective execution:** It acknowledges complexity but fabricates AFSC-specific rules.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶7.21 (PME context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Accurate PME guidance; honesty; accountability; two-track caution  
**ADTC:** 7F, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Preserve AFH snapshot; current AU enrollment rules control real counseling

## C07-SJT-003 — CCAF honesty

You help an Airman review a CCAF progress briefing for a unit professional-development hour. The draft claims the Airman has already met all 2022–2024 catalog graduate conditions, including the Journeyman 5-skill level and institutional-credit minimums. The Airman’s AUSIS degree audit shows the skill level is still pending upgrade and institutional technical-education hours are short. You may correct one slide before delivery; the speaker must otherwise use the cleared deck.

### Response options

A. Leave the “fully complete” claim on the slide and whisper the shortage only if someone asks.  
B. Replace the completion claim with the AUSIS status: skill-level upgrade pending and institutional hours still short, plus the next planned actions.  
C. Delete every CCAF reference and end with unit logos only.  
D. Broaden the claim to say every Airman in the flight has met residency requirements.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 7.24 and Table 7.1 supply CCAF requirement context; they do not create a behavioral key for briefing edits. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it uses the available revision authority to keep the public claim aligned with the verified audit. Chapter 1 honesty and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A knowingly preserves a false completion claim as the audience takeaway. Chapter 7 identifies the catalog conditions; Chapter 1 accurate reporting provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It prioritizes appearance over a verified education record.
- **B — Most effective:** It corrects the material inaccuracy with source-supported status and next steps.
- **C — Partially effective:** It avoids the false claim but withholds a supported status update the editor can fix.
- **D — Ineffective:** It expands an unsupported claim from one Airman to the whole flight.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶7.24 / Table 7.1 (CCAF context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** CCAF honesty; integrity; accountability; disciplined communication  
**ADTC:** 7G, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Catalog-era requirements are source snapshots; AUSIS/current catalog control real audits

## C07-SJT-004 — Training documentation integrity

You supervise a trainee approaching upgrade recommendation. The CFETP and work-center records show two mandatory core tasks still unsigned. The trainee asks you to backdate the certifications today so the package can meet tomorrow’s board. Task certifier support is available this afternoon, and the trainee can demonstrate both tasks before close of business.

### Response options

A. Backdate both task certifications to last month and submit the upgrade package.  
B. Mark the tasks complete without evaluation because the trainee “has been doing the job.”  
C. Schedule same-day demonstration with a qualified certifier, document completion with accurate dates, and submit only after mandatory requirements are met.  
D. Destroy the incomplete training record and create a new folder that omits the two tasks.

### Most effective: C

Paragraphs 7.15–7.17 supply upgrade and documentation context; they do not score the integrity choice. C is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it preserves documentation accuracy while still using available time to finish legitimate requirements. Chapter 1 honesty, accountability, and mission stewardship provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A falsifies training history to meet a board date. Chapter 7 explains why documentation validates qualification and readiness; Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It knowingly creates a false qualification record.
- **B — Ineffective:** It skips required evaluation and certification standards.
- **C — Most effective:** It completes real training evidence on an honest timeline.
- **D — Ineffective:** It conceals requirements rather than meeting them.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶7.15–7.17 (training documentation context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Training documentation integrity; honesty; accountability; readiness  
**ADTC:** 7E, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of upgrade-board realism; CFETP/AFECD and command procedures control real upgrades

## C07-SJT-005 — Special duty representation

You draft an unclassified information paper on selective senior enlisted positions for new supervisors. A co-author wants the paper to say every squadron Senior Enlisted Leader is automatically a Command Chief Master Sergeant and that SEA/SEL titles may be used informally for any helpful SNCO. Your AFH 1 baseline distinguishes CCM, SEL, and SEA/CEM approved-position limits and notes joint SEA/SEL titles are not always synonymous with a command chief role. You may revise the disputed paragraph before release.

### Response options

A. Keep the automatic CCM/SEL conflation because it “simplifies the chart.”  
B. State that SEA/SEL/CEM may be used for any SNCO who mentors Airmen.  
C. Restore AFH 1’s distinctions: CCM at designated levels; SEL at squadron/group/headquarters levels as approved; SEA/SEL/CEM only for designated approved positions; joint titles not always synonymous with CCM.  
D. Remove all position titles and say only that senior enlisted leaders “run the Air Force.”

### Most effective: C

Paragraph 7.9 supplies special-position context; it does not create a behavioral scoring key. C is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it uses revision authority to keep official titles accurate. Chapter 1 honesty and disciplined communication provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A knowingly collapses distinct authorities into a simpler but false equivalence. Chapter 7 draws the distinctions; Chapter 1 accurate representation provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It trades accuracy for chart simplicity despite available correction authority.
- **B — Ineffective:** Informal title use contradicts the designated-and-approved-position rule.
- **C — Most effective:** It preserves role clarity the audience needs for supervision.
- **D — Partially effective:** It avoids false labels but withholds supported distinctions the revision can include.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶7.9 (special positions context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Special duty representation; honesty; role clarity; disciplined communication  
**ADTC:** 7C, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of public-affairs/release realism; current classification directories control real titles

## C07-SJT-006 — Force development without overpromise

During a feedback session, an Airman asks whether completing a MyVECTOR Developmental Special Experience entry guarantees the next special-duty selection. AFH 1 describes DSEs as competency-building experiences listed regardless of vacancies or windows and describes development teams as using defined education, training, experience, and performance requirements. You are authorized to explain development tools but not to promise selection outcomes.

### Response options

A. Promise that the DSE catalog entry guarantees selection if the Airman finishes the application fields tonight.  
B. Explain that DSEs can build competencies and visibility, that selection still depends on defined requirements and decision authorities, and help the Airman identify honest next development actions.  
C. Tell the Airman development tools are meaningless and to stop using MyVECTOR.  
D. Say selection is random, so documentation quality does not matter.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 7.10 supplies force-development tool context; it does not authorize outcome guarantees. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it mentors without overpromising and keeps responsibility with the real selection process. Chapter 1 honesty, loyalty, and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A converts a development catalog into a false guarantee beyond the speaker’s authority. Chapter 7’s DSE description does not promise selection; Chapter 1 honesty provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It overpromises a personnel outcome the mentor does not control.
- **B — Most effective:** It gives accurate developmental help without inventing certainty.
- **C — Ineffective:** It dismisses a supported Total Force development tool without basis.
- **D — Partially effective warning, ineffective framing:** It resists false certainty but erases the value of preparation and documentation.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶7.10 (force development context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Force development without overpromise; honesty; mentoring; accountability  
**ADTC:** 7D, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of counseling realism; actual DSD/selection guidance controls real outcomes

## C07-SJT-007 — Junior leadership levels

You lead a flight professional-development discussion on leadership levels. A new supervisor argues that every Staff Sergeant should already operate primarily at the strategic vision level and that tactical expertise is “only for Airmen Basic.” AFH 1 states the majority of enlisted Airmen operate at tactical expertise and operational competence, and it describes strategic vision as enterprise-level leadership of complex, multi-tiered organizations. You may correct one talking point before the discussion continues.

### Response options

A. Endorse the claim that all SSgts should primarily exercise strategic vision and that tactical expertise ends at Airman Basic.  
B. Avoid the disagreement and ask the group to discuss sports instead.  
C. Correct the talking point: leadership levels are developmental emphases; most enlisted Airmen operate at tactical expertise and operational competence, and strategic vision describes a broader enterprise focus rather than a rank-automatic default for every SSgt.  
D. Tell junior Airmen that followership no longer matters once they supervise anyone.

### Most effective: C

Paragraph 7.2 supplies leadership-level context; it does not score facilitation behavior. C is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it restores the source’s developmental distinction without humiliating the speaker. Chapter 1 honesty, respect, and disciplined communication provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A amplifies a misunderstanding that compresses leadership levels into rank slogans. Chapter 7 distinguishes the levels; Chapter 1 accurate teaching provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It teaches an overstated rank-to-level mapping the baseline contradicts.
- **B — Ineffective:** It abandons the learning objective when a correction is authorized.
- **C — Most effective:** It preserves accurate developmental framing the flight needs.
- **D — Ineffective:** It discards followership, which AFH 1 still associates with tactical development and leader/follower understanding.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶7.2 (leadership levels context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Junior leadership levels; honesty; respect; teaching accuracy  
**ADTC:** 7A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of facilitation tone; local PD objectives control real sessions

## C07-SJT-008 — Continuing education balance with duty

An Airman asks approval to enroll in two overlapping evening courses that would require leaving the duty section early three days this week during a published high-tempo inspection window. Military tuition assistance may cover tuition within policy caps, but the scenario states the courses are not mission-essential this week and equivalent later sections are available next month. You may approve, modify, or recommend deferral within your delegated scheduling authority.

### Response options

A. Approve both early releases without discussing mission impact because education always outranks local taskings.  
B. Deny all future education permanently because duty and school can never coexist.  
C. Order the Airman to use tuition assistance for prohibited textbook purchases to “make the week worthwhile.”  
D. Explain the inspection-window impact, disapprove the early releases for this week, and help the Airman plan enrollment in the later sections while keeping long-term education goals intact.

### Most effective: D

Paragraphs 7.25–7.26 supply continuing-education context; they do not create an automatic priority over stated mission windows. D is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it protects current duty while preserving a realistic education path. Chapter 1 mission focus, accountability, loyalty, and respect provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: C

C directs a tuition-assistance misuse the baseline prohibits and ignores the scheduling conflict. Chapter 7 lists TA restrictions; Chapter 1 integrity and accountability provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It treats education as an automatic trump card over a stated high-tempo mission window.
- **B — Partially effective boundary, ineffective extremity:** It protects duty now but shuts down legitimate long-term development.
- **C — Least effective:** It pairs mission neglect with prohibited TA use.
- **D — Most effective:** It balances duty and development through a deferred, still-supportive plan.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶7.25–7.26 (continuing education context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Continuing education balance with duty; mission focus; integrity; loyalty  
**ADTC:** 7H, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of scheduling realism; current TA and local duty policies control real approvals

## C07-SJT-009 — Enlisted structure role clarity

A Technical Sergeant newly arrived to your section tells junior Airmen that SNCOs alone mentor and that NCOs are “just technicians with no leadership lane.” AFH 1 places Staff Sergeants and Technical Sergeants in the NCO tier with dual technician-and-leader responsibilities, while SNCOs advise, supervise, mentor, and participate in broader decision-making. You are the section NCOIC and may correct the message before close of business.

### Response options

A. Let the statement stand to avoid correcting another NCO in front of Airmen.  
B. Privately and then, as needed, with the team, clarify that the NCO tier includes leadership, supervision, management, and mentoring responsibilities alongside occupational expertise, while SNCO roles expand those leadership demands—without belittling either tier.  
C. Announce that junior enlisted Airmen should ignore all TSgt guidance from now on.  
D. Agree publicly that NCOs have no leadership lane and that only chiefs may correct substandard performance.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 7.6–7.7 supply enlisted-structure context; they do not score correction technique. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it restores role clarity while preserving dignity and teamwork. Chapter 1 respect, loyalty, accountability, and courage provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D validates a structural falsehood that would strip NCO leadership responsibility. Chapter 7 defines the NCO lane; Chapter 1 accountability and mission stewardship provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** Silence leaves junior Airmen with a misleading leadership map.
- **B — Most effective:** It corrects the structure accurately and maintains professional respect.
- **C — Ineffective:** It overcorrects by undermining lawful NCO authority wholesale.
- **D — Least effective:** It teaches junior Airmen to disregard NCO leadership duties the force structure assigns.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶7.6–7.7 (enlisted structure context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Enlisted structure role clarity; respect; courage; accountability  
**ADTC:** 7B, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Section 7B ADTC conflict documented in header; core scenario remains B for both ranks

## C07-SJT-010 — Two-track exam versus current policy

You prepare a study handout on AFCOOL funding using AFH 1, dated 15 February 2025, which cites a $4,500 career maximum. A teammate finds an unofficial social-media post claiming the career maximum is now unlimited and wants the handout updated before tonight’s study group. The education office can confirm current policy tomorrow morning; tonight’s group is optional practice, not an official records action.

### Response options

A. Replace the AFH figure with the social-media “unlimited” claim and present it as settled current policy.  
B. Keep the AFH 1 $4,500 figure as the exam-source snapshot, label it as the designated baseline, note that duty-current funding may differ, and tell the group the education office will confirm current policy tomorrow before any funding action.  
C. Delete all funding numbers and tell Airmen policy research is unnecessary for WAPS or duty.  
D. Tell Airmen to claim unlimited funding on applications tonight so finance will “catch up later.”

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 7.24 supplies the AFCOOL funding snapshot; it does not itself adjudicate every later policy change. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it preserves the two-track distinction—exam-source fidelity versus duty verification—without turning rumor into action. Chapter 1 honesty, accountability, and disciplined management of uncertainty provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D encourages false or unsupported benefit claims before confirmation. Chapter 7’s funding figure is a source snapshot, not a license to invent entitlements; Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It overwrites the designated study baseline with unverified social media and presents rumor as settled policy.
- **B — Most effective:** It keeps exam-source and duty-current tracks distinct and defers action until confirmation.
- **C — Partially effective caution, ineffective extremity:** Removing numbers avoids rumor but denies useful sourced study content and dismisses needed verification.
- **D — Least effective:** It pushes premature benefit claims that can create false records and expectations.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶7.24 (AFCOOL context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Two-track exam vs current policy; honesty; accountability; uncertainty management  
**ADTC:** 7G, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Preserve $4,500 as source snapshot; education office/current AFCOOL rules control real funding
