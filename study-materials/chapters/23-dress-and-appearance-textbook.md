## Why this chapter matters

Chapter 23 answers how Airmen present a professional military image and wear authorized uniforms:

1. **What image is required?** Pride, esprit de corps, and five appearance elements: neatness, cleanliness, safety, uniformity, and military image.
2. **What grooming and body standards apply?** Hair, nails, cosmetics, tattoos/brands/body markings, piercings, and alterations.
3. **What uniform families exist?** Formal/mess, semi-formal, service dress, OCP, maternity variants, PTG, flight duty uniforms, organizational/specialty uniforms.
4. **How are insignia and awards worn?** Outerwear, headgear, jewelry/accessories, badges, ribbons/medals, and religious accommodation as printed.

A useful chapter-wide sequence is:

> Professional image → personal appearance → choose the right uniform → configure accoutrements → know when accommodation/ETP applies

That sequence is a **MyPromotion memory aid**, not an official wear checklist. Real wear depends on current DAFI 36-2903 and local direction. (AFH 1, ¶¶23.1–23.36)

# Section 23A — Professional Image

## Learning objectives

### SSgt and TSgt — ADTC level B

At ADTC level B, an Airman should be able to:

1. Explain why professional military image matters and the key on-/off-duty expectations AFH 1 lists for members in uniform.
2. Summarize the five elements of dress and personal appearance standards and who must comply/enforce them.
3. Compare male and female (and accommodation-related) hair standards, including bulk, length, accessories, mustaches, and beards.
4. Explain fingernail, cosmetic, tattoo/brand/body-marking, piercing, and body-alteration rules as printed, including unauthorized content/locations and ETP documentation cues.
5. Distinguish religious/medical beard authorization pointers from the general “beards not authorized” rule. (AFH 1, ¶¶23.1–23.5)

## 1. Professional military image (¶23.1)

According to AFI 1-1, first impressions are often drawn based upon appearance. For military members, presenting a professional image matters just as much as professional behavior. Pride in personal appearance and uniform wear enhances esprit de corps. Projecting a professional military image reflects on the individual and the USAF, both on and off duty. (AFH 1, ¶23.1)

| Expectation (not all-inclusive) | Rule as printed |
|---|---|
| Tobacco in uniform | Do not smoke or use smokeless tobacco products except when authorized in designated smoking areas |
| Personal electronics while walking | Use of personal electronic media devices (cellular phones, earpieces, speaker phones, or text messaging) while walking in uniform is authorized; **military customs and courtesies take precedence** |
| Food / beverage | Do not consume food while walking in uniform; beverage consumption is authorized; commanders may authorize food consumption while walking during special functions; customs and courtesies take precedence |
| Public displays of affection | Do not engage in PDA except when briefly permitted in commonly accepted etiquette situations (weddings, graduations, promotions, retirement ceremonies, or departure for/return from deployment) |

(AFH 1, ¶23.1)

## 2. Dress and personal appearance standards (¶23.2)

Dress and personal appearance standards ensure every Airman reflects pride, professionalism, and esprit de corps. Rooted in USAF heritage, they provide visible examples of self-discipline, commitment, and a willingness to set aside individuality for the betterment of the whole. As outlined in DAFI 36-2903, standards consist of five elements:

| Element | Study cue |
|---|---|
| Neatness | Looks orderly and serviceable |
| Cleanliness | Clean and presentable |
| Safety | Does not compromise protective fit/mission safety |
| Uniformity | Matches authorized combinations and placement |
| Military image | Projects the disciplined professional force |

It is every Airman’s responsibility, regardless of position or rank, to comply with and respectfully enforce the prescribed standards. More restrictive standards regarding personal appearance may be imposed on or off duty for highly visible special mission requirements or special duty positions. (AFH 1, ¶23.2)

## 3. Hair standards — shared rules (¶23.3)

Hair will be conservative, clean, well-groomed, and present a professional appearance. Regardless of hairstyle, hair must not exceed length and bulk standards and must allow proper wear of headgear, helmet, or chemical mask for safety. Hair will not contain excessive grooming aids, appear lopsided, touch either eyebrow (**exception: female bangs**), or end below an imaginary line across the forehead at the top of the eyebrows parallel to the ground. (AFH 1, ¶23.3)

| Topic | Key rule |
|---|---|
| Dyes/tints/bleaches/frostings | Must result in **natural** hair colors, present a natural appearance, and be complementary to facial hair (eyebrows, lashes, authorized beards, etc.) |
| Natural color examples | Brown, blonde, brunette, natural red, gray, or black |
| Natural color wear | All Airmen may wear natural color hair regardless of natural-born hair color; when highlighted/frosted, colors must blend naturally (e.g., salt-and-pepper authorized) |
| Unauthorized colors | Burgundy, purple, orange, fluorescent, or neon (not all-inclusive); unnatural ombre or drastic contrasts (e.g., black with blonde highlights / blonde with black) |
| Unauthorized styles (shared) | Mohawk, mullet, or etched design; societal trends that reasonably appear unnatural |
| Wigs/hairpieces/extensions | Authorized if they meet natural-hair standards, fit properly, and meet safety/function/professional standards; synthetic additions must be natural and blend; **extensions prohibited for males**; not used to cover unauthorized styles |
| Medical indoor caps | Commanders may authorize plain single-color caps (black, navy, or tan) indoors for medical conditions (e.g., alopecia, radiation/chemotherapy); outdoor headgear still required; head scarves not authorized |
| Hairnets | Only when required for health/safety as determined by applicable commander; conservative solid color similar to hair color; no metal fasteners |

(AFH 1, ¶23.3)

### Male hair, sideburns, mustache, beard (¶23.3)

| Item | Distinctive requirement |
|---|---|
| Male hair shape | Tapered appearance on sides/back; conforms to shape of head; no eccentric directional flow, twists, or spiking; block-cut permitted with taper |
| Male bulk / termination | Bulk not exceed **2 ½ inches** regardless of length; **1/4-inch** at natural termination point; only closely cut/shaved hair on back of neck may touch collar |
| Part | One cut/clipped/shaved front-to-back straight-line part; not slanted/curved; not exceed **4 inches** length or **1/4-inch** width |
| Male-only unauthorized styles | Dreadlocks, coils, braids, twists, designs, and/or hair extensions |
| Authorized cuts | Cleanly shaven heads, military high-and-tight, or flattop |
| Sideburns | Straight and even width (not flared); not below bottom of ear orifice; end in clean-shaven horizontal line |
| Mustache | Authorized; not below lip line of upper lip; not beyond horizontal line across corners of mouth; not more than **1/4-inch** beyond vertical line from corner of mouth; natural appearance to head hair color; handlebars, twist, curls, and goatee prohibited |
| Beards (general) | **Not authorized** unless medical authorization or religious accommodation |
| Medical beard | Trimmed to same length; not exceed **1/4-inch**; may not be shaped into goatees/faded styles; natural appearance; not bleached/dyed |
| Religious beard | Governed by DAFI 36-2903, Chapter 11 (see also ¶23.25) |

(AFH 1, ¶23.3)

### Female hair / unshorn hair religious accommodations (¶23.3)

| Item | Distinctive requirement |
|---|---|
| Length/bulk | No minimum length; maximum bulk **4 inches** from scalp; allow proper headgear wear |
| Appearance | Symmetric (around axis of head) or tapered when viewed from all angles |
| Collar line | Hair ends above bottom edge of collar and not below invisible ground-parallel line unless pulled back/secured or in authorized ponytail/equivalent/long braid(s) |
| Pulled-back gather | Not gathered at crown or worn on top of head; radius not exceed **6 inches** left or right (**12 inches** total width); **6 inches** bulk; allow headgear |
| Ponytail / braid length | One or two braids or a single ponytail/equivalent down the back; length not below horizontal line between top of each sleeve inseam at underarm through shoulder blades; not worn over shoulder or pulled in front |
| PTG exception | While wearing PTG, long hair will be secured but may have loose ends and may extend below that shoulder-blade line |
| Bangs | May touch eyebrows but will not touch or cover eyes |
| Bun | Single bun; all loose ends tucked/secured; not starting at crown / worn on top of head |
| Accessories | Black or match hair color; headbands/fabric scrunchies not exceed **2 inches** width; ornaments (ribbons, beads, jeweled pins) not authorized |
| Locs/braids/twists/cornrows | Authorized; uniform dimension; braid/twist no wider than **1 inch** with natural spacing; multiple locs/braids/twists no larger than **1/4 inch** diameter and no more than **1/4-inch** scalp between; tightly interwoven; braid/twist continues to end without design (**exception: micro-braids/twists**) |
| Female-only unauthorized | Partially shaved sides and/or back with long hair on top requiring ponytail/bun/braid |

(AFH 1, ¶23.3)

### Fingernails and cosmetics (¶23.3)

| Topic | Rule |
|---|---|
| Nail length | Not exceed **1/4-inch** beyond tip of finger; clean and well groomed; must not interfere with duties or safety/uniform fit |
| Male nail polish | Not authorized |
| Female nail polish | Single conservative color that does not detract from uniform; no designs; no two-tone/multi-tone; **exception: white-tip French manicures authorized** |
| Male cosmetics | Not authorized |
| Female cosmetics | Conservatively styled; lipstick conservative shade; eyeliner cannot extend past corners of eye opening (winged liner not authorized); cosmetics not worn during field conditions |
| Eyelash extensions | Natural eyelash color; not exceed **14 mm** total length; not touch eyebrow; natural appearance; must not hinder protective eyewear/headgear |

(AFH 1, ¶23.3)

### Tattoos / brands / body markings (¶23.3)

**Definitions (DAFI 36-2903 purposes as printed in AFH 1):** tattoo = picture/design/marking made by indelible dye or other method (including UV/invisible ink); brand = burned marking; body markings = permanent scarring/marking by other means. Failure to obey mandatory provisions in DAFI 36-2903, paragraphs 3.2.1, 3.2.1.1, and 3.2.2 constitutes a violation of Article 92(1), UCMJ (with Title 32 status notes for ANG). (AFH 1, ¶23.3)

| Family | Key rules |
|---|---|
| Unauthorized locations | Head, face, tongue, lips, eyes, and scalp |
| Unauthorized content (in/out of uniform) | Obscene; gang/extremist/supremacist; sexual/racial/ethnic/religious discrimination; content prejudicial to good order and discipline or tending to bring discredit upon the DAF |
| Cover-up ban | May **not** cover unauthorized-content tattoos/brands/markings with bandages or make-up to comply |
| Removal duty | Members with unauthorized-content markings must initiate removal/alteration; DoD MTF voluntary removal at commander discretion on space/resource available basis; otherwise member expense; permissive TDY not authorized for this purpose |
| Authorized locations | Chest and back (below open collar uniform), arms, legs, feet, hands, and neck |
| Neck tattoo | One authorized; not exceed **1 inch** in any direction; only behind vertical lines at ear orifices around the back (includes behind ear) |
| Ring tattoos | Single band on one finger of each hand; not more than **3/8-inch** width; below knuckle and above finger joint closest to palm |
| Hand tattoos | In addition to ring tattoo, single tattoo on each hand not exceeding **1 inch** in any direction |
| Visibility | Hand/arm/leg/neck/ring tattoos may be exposed in any uniform combination; chest/back tattoos will not be visible through any uniform combination or while wearing open-collar uniform |
| ETP | Location/size ETP per DAFI 36-2903 ¶15.5 **before** receiving tattoo; document on **DAF Form 4428**; ETP for unauthorized **content** will not be considered |
| Cosmetic tattooing | Authorized when medically directed; otherwise women: permanent facial makeup (eyebrows/eyeliner); men: scalp micropigmentation for natural hair appearance; must be natural and conservative |
| Accessions | Disclose non-compliant tattoos/brands; recruiting-service review; unauthorized content/excessive markings otherwise require complete removal/alteration before acceptance |
| Hand ETP for applicants | Component senior waiver authority may allow hand tattoos (anywhere on hand) no more than **25%** of hand for exceptionally qualified applicants in undermanned critical fields; still subject to content restrictions; document on DAF Form 4428 |
| Commander restrictiveness | Per DAFI 36-2903 ¶2.14.4, commanders retain authority to be more restrictive for covering tattoos, body ornaments, and personal grooming based on legal, safety, sanitary, and/or host-nation agreements |

(AFH 1, ¶23.3) **2025 source snapshot—revalidate:** tattoo location/size/content and ETP rules are volatile policy.

## 4. Body piercing / ornamentation (¶23.4)

In civilian attire on official duty and in uniform on or off a military installation: with the exception of earrings as listed in DAFI 36-2903 ¶7.3.1.1, all members are prohibited from attaching, affixing, or displaying objects, articles, jewelry, or ornamentation to or through the ear, nose, tongue, eyebrows, lips, or any exposed body part (includes visible through the uniform). (AFH 1, ¶23.4)

**Dental ornamentation:** Teeth (natural, capped, or veneered) will not be ornamented with designs, jewels, initials, etc. Yellow/white gold or platinum caps merely for ornamentation (not dental/medical necessity) are prohibited. Waivers not required for permanent gold/platinum caps applied for dental/medical necessity. Members with official documentation of permanent ornamental caps received prior to **13 June 2011** are exempted; no other dental ornamentation is exempted. (AFH 1, ¶23.4)

## 5. Body alterations and modifications (¶23.5)

Intentional alterations/modifications that result in a visible, physical effect that disfigures, deforms, or otherwise detracts from a professional military image are prohibited. Examples (not limited to): tongue splitting or forking, tooth filing, visible disfiguring skin implants, and gauging (piercing holes large enough to permit light to shine through). Members who intentionally alter/modify their bodies to achieve such effects may be subject to disciplinary action or involuntary separation as determined by the commander. Failure by RegAF members, AFR members on active duty or inactive duty for training, and ANG members in Title 10 status to obey the mandatory provisions constitutes a violation of Article 92(1), UCMJ; ANG members in Title 32 status may be held accountable through similar state military-code provisions. (AFH 1, ¶23.5)

# Section 23B — Military Uniforms

## Learning objectives

### SSgt and TSgt — ADTC level B

At ADTC level B, an Airman should be able to:

1. Explain USAF uniform responsibility (procure/maintain mandatory items; neatness/cleanliness/safety/uniformity/military image) and when to seek clarification.
2. Distinguish formal/mess dress, enlisted semi-formal, Class A/B service dress, OCP, maternity variants, PTG, FDU/DFDU/2PFDU, and major organizational/specialty uniforms by purpose and distinctive wear rules.
3. Summarize outerwear, headgear, jewelry/accessories, footwear, and undergarment families that cut across combinations.
4. Explain badge/ribbon/medal wear ceilings, precedence cues, and religious-accommodation approval lanes as printed.
5. Recognize that DAFI 36-2903 and local supplements control detailed measurements and volatile configurations. (AFH 1, ¶¶23.6–23.36)

## 1. USAF uniform — responsibility baseline (¶23.6)

Wearing the USAF uniform means carrying on a tradition that represents the profession of arms. The uniform is plain yet distinctive and presents the appearance of a military professional. All USAF members in uniform will adhere to neatness, cleanliness, safety, uniformity, and military image. Members will procure and maintain all mandatory clothing items; follow local supplements and procedures; keep uniforms neat, clean, pressed, buttoned, and properly maintained; know authorized combinations; and display correct placement of ribbons, insignia, and other items. When in doubt, seek clarification through supervisors, first sergeants, or commanders. Additional details are in DAFI 36-2903. (AFH 1, ¶23.6)

## 5. Operational Camouflage Pattern (OCP) (¶23.10)

### Wear, care, and procurement

| Rule family | Key points |
|---|---|

| Procurement | AAFES MCSS / Individual Equipment Elements (or contract equivalent) certified compliant; AF not responsible for non-AAFES personal purchases that are noncompliant |
| Authorized types for all Airmen | Non-fire-retardant and improved hot weather combat OCP only, with OSHA fire-retardant exception for certain jobs; firefighters/EOD may wear 2PFDU configuration per DAFI 36-2903 ¶9.12 (firefighter badge in lieu of HHQ patch on left sleeve) |
