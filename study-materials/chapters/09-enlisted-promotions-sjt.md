# Chapter 9 Situational Judgment Practice

> **Status:** Draft and provisional for 27E5/27E6. Chapter 9 is shared core in the historical 26E5/26E6 catalog scopes. All sections are treated as B—Understanding for both SSgt and TSgt (B/B).
>
> These scenarios are wholly original. They are not actual, recalled, reconstructed, or predicted PFE items. The official Air Force uses subject-matter experts and behavioral scientists to establish operational SJT scoring keys. Chapter 9 describes enlisted promotion systems, cycles, preparation responsibilities, testing, and evaluation boards, but those facts alone do not establish an official behavioral scoring key. All most-effective and least-effective keys below are MyPromotion educational judgments grounded in Chapter 1 professionalism, core values, and ethical reasoning, with Chapter 9 material supplying context only. They are not official scoring keys or substitutes for current command, supervisory, safety, legal, ethics, testing, personnel, or reporting procedures.
>
> **Test-security rule:** Scenarios involving study materials or testing integrity reinforce refusal, non-sharing, official channels, and reporting/avoidance per AFH 1 / DAFMAN 36-2664. They never provide, request, or facilitate compromised test content.
>
> **Two-track source rule:** For exam-source study, preserve the facts in the AFH edition assigned by the controlling catalog. For duty use, separately verify current promotion, PECD, testing, and personnel rules; duty-current information does not overwrite a catalog-controlled answer.

## How to use this set

Each scenario produces two items:

1. Select the **most effective** response.
2. Select the **least effective** response.

Read every option before choosing. Evaluate whether the response:

- protects promotion-testing integrity and refuses compromise;
- represents PECD/DVR, catalog, and score-notice rules honestly;
- uses mentoring without sharing prohibited materials;
- stays within stated role and proper personnel channels;
- accepts or declines promotion commitments with integrity;
- respects privacy, people, process owners, and the chain of command; and
- reflects integrity, accountability, loyalty, respect, and mission stewardship.

Chapter 9 supplies promotion-system context; it does not prescribe the behavioral sequence in every response. When a rationale depends on general professional judgment—such as honesty, courage, accountability, loyalty, respect, mission focus, or teamwork—it is identified as an application of the cited foundational Chapter 1 paragraphs.

Ten scenarios produce 20 response selections, mirroring the historical public structure announced for the [22E5/22E6 cycles in December 2021](https://www.afpc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/2874484/waps-adjusts-for-22e6-22e5-testing-cycles/). The separate [public SJT informational guide](https://www.studyguides.af.mil/Portals/15/documents/Notices/Situational-Judgment-Test-Informational-Guide.pdf) explains the four-option most/least format. The 2027 structure is unconfirmed. This set covers only selected Chapter 9 concepts and is not a full-spectrum SJT simulation.

## C09-SJT-001 — PECD/DVR accuracy

You are promotion-eligible and reviewing your Data Verification Record in ARMS/PRDA before the PECD. You discover a missing decoration and an incorrect CAFSC entry that would affect how you compete. The MPF can assist today. A coworker suggests ignoring the errors because “boards figure it out” and warns that contacting MPF might “flag you as a problem.”

### Response options

A. Leave the errors uncorrected and hope the board reconstructs your record from memory.  
B. Immediately contact the MPF, request corrections in MilPDS/DVR, and re-review the updated record to verify accuracy before PECD.  
C. Edit a printed DVR with a pen and place it in your selection folder as if it were the system of record.  
D. Ask a peer to change your CAFSC in a shared unofficial spreadsheet and treat that as the official fix.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 9.13–9.14 supply PECD/DVR context; they do not create a behavioral scoring key. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because AFH 1 requires eligible Airmen to review the DVR and immediately contact the MPF for errors, then verify updates. Chapter 1 honesty, accountability, and courage support timely correction through the proper channel.

### Least effective: C

C fabricates a personnel record outside official systems. Chapter 9 identifies MPF/MilPDS as the correction path; Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It abandons required DVR stewardship and risks uncorrected competition data.
- **B — Most effective:** It uses the mandated MPF channel and verifies the fix.
- **C — Least effective:** It falsifies record evidence instead of updating the system of record.
- **D — Ineffective:** Unofficial spreadsheets do not update MilPDS/DVR.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶9.13–9.14 (PECD/DVR context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** PECD/DVR accuracy; honesty; accountability; proper channels  
**ADTC:** 9C, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of MPF realism; current personnel procedures control real corrections

## C09-SJT-002 — Refusing test compromise

Two days before your testing window, a peer offers to text you a list of “questions people remembered from last cycle” and invites you to a group chat “so nobody fails.” AFH 1 prohibits group study for promotion testing and prohibits sharing recalled test questions. Your test control officer contact information is available.

### Response options

A. Join the chat, save the recalled questions, and thank the peer for helping the team.  
B. Refuse the material, do not join the group study chat, and report the offer through the proper test-control/command channels so integrity can be protected.  
C. Accept the list privately “just to compare later” but promise not to tell anyone.  
D. Ask the peer to send only the hardest questions so the compromise is “smaller.”

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 9.11 supplies test-compromise context; it does not score the reporting sequence. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it refuses prohibited material, avoids group study for testing, and uses official channels rather than participating. Chapter 1 integrity, courage, and loyalty to the institution provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A knowingly accepts recalled test content and joins prohibited group study. Chapter 9 forbids that conduct; Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It participates in and encourages compromise.
- **B — Most effective:** It refuses, avoids, and reports through proper channels.
- **C — Ineffective:** Private acceptance is still receiving prohibited recalled content.
- **D — Ineffective:** Partial compromise remains compromise.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶9.11 (test compromise context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Refusing test compromise; reporting/avoidance; integrity; courage  
**ADTC:** 9C, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Reinforces reporting/avoidance only; never facilitates cheating; DAFMAN 36-2664/local procedures control real reporting

## C09-SJT-003 — Honest study-reference use

You are building a personal study plan using the EPRRC listed for your cycle. A friend offers a commercial study-guide app and suggests installing it on a shared government computer in the work center “so everyone can use it on break,” then highlighting official PDFs together after hours. AFH 1 allows personal commercial materials without USAF endorsement but prohibits placing commercial study-guide software on government computers, creating/storing personal study notes on government computers, sharing highlighted references, and group study for promotion testing.

### Response options

A. Install the commercial app on the government computer and host a group highlighting session.  
B. Use official references from approved sources for individual study; if you choose commercial materials, keep them personal/off government systems, do not share highlighted references, and decline group promotion-test study.  
C. Transfer your personal study notes to the shared drive so the flight can split chapters.  
D. Skip official EPRRC references entirely because the commercial app “covers everything.”

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 9.11–9.12 and 9.16 supply study-reference and compromise context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it preserves official-reference access, keeps any commercial use personal and non-endorsed, and refuses prohibited sharing/group study. Chapter 1 honesty and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A combines prohibited commercial software on a government computer with group promotion-test study and sharing of marked references. Chapter 9 forbids those acts; Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It stacks multiple explicit prohibitions.
- **B — Most effective:** It follows official-reference rules and keeps study individual/lawful.
- **C — Ineffective:** Personal notes on government systems and sharing violate restrictions.
- **D — Partially effective caution about overreliance, ineffective execution:** Abandoning EPRRC references undercuts the official study pathway.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶9.11–9.12, 9.16 (study-reference/compromise context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Honest study-reference use; integrity; individual study  
**ADTC:** 9C, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Reinforces official EPRRC pathway; no facilitation of compromise

## C09-SJT-004 — Accepting/declining promotion integrity

You are a Master Sergeant-select with more than 18 years TAFMS by the effective date of promotion. The MPF presents a Promotion Statement of Understanding requiring acknowledgment of two years retainability and a two-year ADSC. You also have a pending retirement application. A peer suggests signing nothing, keeping the retirement paperwork quiet, and “sorting it out after pin-on.”

### Response options

A. Ignore the 10-duty-day window, keep the retirement application in place, and assume you can remain selected anyway.  
B. Within 10 duty days, honestly complete the Statement of Understanding requirements—including withdrawing the retirement application if you will accept the promotion—or decline the promotion in writing through the MPF with the required understanding that reinstatement will not be authorized.  
C. Sign the statement while secretly intending not to obtain retainability.  
D. Ask a coworker to forge your signature so you do not have to decide.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 9.6 and 9.8 supply accept/decline context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it meets the stated timeline with an honest accept-and-comply path or a proper written declination. Chapter 1 honesty, accountability, and courage provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D uses forgery to evade a required personnel decision. Chapter 9 requires the member’s own statement or declination; Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** Failure to withdraw retirement within 10 duty days results in removal from the selection list.
- **B — Most effective:** It makes a timely, truthful accept-or-decline choice through proper channels.
- **C — Ineffective:** Signing without intent to meet retainability/ADSC is dishonest.
- **D — Least effective:** Forgery corrupts the process and the record.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶9.6, 9.8 (accept/decline context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Accepting/declining promotion integrity; honesty; accountability  
**ADTC:** 9B, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of MPF counseling realism; current AFI 36-2502 procedures control real cases

## C09-SJT-005 — Score-notice stewardship

After releases, a fellow NCO asks you to pull their promotion score notice from vMPF “because you’re logged in anyway,” then forward it to the flight’s group chat so supervisors can “mentor off the numbers.” AFH 1 states score notices should never be provided to or used by anyone other than the individual and the commander, and scores cannot be disclosed without the Airman’s written consent. Supervisors and first sergeants are not authorized access.

### Response options

A. Open the score notice on your session, screenshot it, and post it to the group chat.  
B. Refuse to access or share another Airman’s scores; tell them to retrieve their own notice on vMPF, and remind the flight that scores are limited to the individual and commander for notification/advisory counseling.  
C. Read the scores aloud in the hallway so “everyone gets the same mentoring.”  
D. Trade access—share one Airman’s scores if they share yours.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 9.21 supplies score-notice context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it protects privacy, refuses unauthorized access/dissemination, and points the Airman to the proper self-retrieval channel. Chapter 1 respect, loyalty, and integrity provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A both accesses and disseminates protected score information without written consent. Chapter 9 forbids that use; Chapter 1 respect and integrity provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It violates disclosure limits and unauthorized-access rules.
- **B — Most effective:** It protects stewardship and uses proper retrieval.
- **C — Ineffective:** Public reading is further unauthorized dissemination.
- **D — Ineffective:** Consent-by-trade still improperly spreads protected scores.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶9.21 (score-notice context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Score-notice stewardship; privacy; respect; integrity  
**ADTC:** 9E, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Privacy emphasis; commander counseling authorities control real notifications

## C09-SJT-006 — Board-process honesty

You facilitate a professional-development discussion on SNCO evaluation boards. A slide claims board members see each competitor’s weighted scores, privately negotiate every initial score, and leave knowing who was selected. AFH 1 states members lack weighted-score access, score independently by secret ballot with no discussion (except split resolution), and do not know selects when they disband. You may correct the slide before the session.

### Response options

A. Keep the inaccurate slide because it “makes the board sound more transparent.”  
B. Replace the claims with AFH 1’s process: independent secret-ballot scoring, no weighted-score access, trial-run consistency, split-vote resolution rules, and post-board electronic combination of scores before selects are known.  
C. Add that Airmen should contact panel members after the board for unofficial score feedback.  
D. Delete all board content and say process knowledge does not matter.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 9.19–9.20 supply board-process context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it uses revision authority to restore accurate process teaching. Chapter 1 honesty and disciplined communication provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A knowingly teaches false process features for rhetorical effect. Chapter 9 describes the actual safeguards; Chapter 1 accurate representation provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It prioritizes a false narrative over source accuracy.
- **B — Most effective:** It corrects the material with supported process facts.
- **C — Ineffective:** Encouraging unofficial panel contact conflicts with process integrity and member non-knowledge of selects.
- **D — Partially effective caution, ineffective extremity:** Removing errors helps, but withholding correct process denies needed education.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶9.19–9.20 (board process context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Board-process honesty; teaching accuracy; integrity  
**ADTC:** 9E, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of PD facilitation tone; current board procedures control real boards

## C09-SJT-007 — Mentoring without sharing compromised material

A Senior Airman you mentor is anxious about an upcoming WAPS cycle and asks you to share your underlined AFH study pages, your personal note sheets, and “anything you remember seeing on the test.” AFH 1 prohibits sharing personal study materials, underlined/highlighted references, and recalled test questions, and requires individual study. You may mentor on process and responsibility without sharing prohibited content.

### Response options

A. Hand over your highlighted references and describe questions you think you remember.  
B. Explain individual-study and compromise rules, help the Airman locate official EPRRC references and build a lawful self-study plan, and refuse to share personal marked materials or any recalled test content.  
C. Host a two-person “promotion test prep” review of marked pages tonight.  
D. Say mentoring on promotion preparation is always prohibited, then end the conversation without pointing to official references.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 9.10–9.12 and 9.16 supply preparation and compromise context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it mentors toward lawful individual study and official references while refusing prohibited sharing. Chapter 1 loyalty, respect, honesty, and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A shares exactly the prohibited categories AFH 1 lists. Chapter 9’s compromise rules forbid it; Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It transmits prohibited study/recalled content.
- **B — Most effective:** It mentors within the rules and points to official references.
- **C — Ineffective:** Group study for promotion testing is prohibited.
- **D — Partially effective boundary, ineffective extremity:** Refusal of compromise is right, but abandoning lawful mentoring/help locating official references is unnecessary.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶9.10–9.12, 9.16 (preparation/compromise context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Mentoring without compromised material; integrity; loyalty; individual study  
**ADTC:** 9C, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Reinforces refusal/non-sharing; never facilitates cheating

## C09-SJT-008 — Two-track catalog vs current

You prepare a flight study handout using AFH 1, dated 15 February 2025, which states the EPRRC is published annually on 1 October and lists study references for authorized tests. A teammate finds an unofficial social-media post claiming “this year’s catalog dropped early and deleted half the references” and wants the handout changed tonight before an optional study-skills session. The WAPS monitor can confirm the controlling EPRRC tomorrow morning; tonight’s session is not an official records action.

### Response options

A. Replace the AFH/EPRRC snapshot with the unverified social-media claim and tell Airmen to ignore official listings.  
B. Keep the AFH 1 publication-cadence and EPRRC role as the exam-source baseline, label it as the designated snapshot, note that the controlling catalog/EPRRC edition and WAPS monitor confirmation govern actual study-reference lists, and defer any duty action until confirmation.  
C. Tell Airmen reference lists never matter because test questions are unrelated to publications.  
D. Instruct Airmen to file delay requests tonight based solely on the social-media rumor.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 9.12 and 9.16 supply EPRRC context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it preserves two-track fidelity—exam-source accuracy versus duty verification—without converting rumor into action. Chapter 1 honesty, accountability, and disciplined uncertainty management provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A overwrites the designated baseline with unverified social media and directs Airmen away from official listings. Chapter 9 ties questions to EPRRC-listed publications; Chapter 1 accurate representation provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It treats rumor as settled policy and discards official reference governance.
- **B — Most effective:** It keeps source and duty tracks distinct and seeks confirmation before action.
- **C — Ineffective:** It contradicts the EPRRC-to-question relationship in AFH 1.
- **D — Ineffective:** Delay requests should rest on actual lack of access to required references, not unverified posts.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶9.12, 9.16 (EPRRC context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Two-track catalog vs current; honesty; accountability; uncertainty management  
**ADTC:** 9D, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Controlling catalog/EPRRC edition governs exam-source study; WAPS monitor confirmation controls duty reference access

## C09-SJT-009 — Quota misconceptions

During mentoring, an Airman insists promotion quotas mean “the top scorers Air Force-wide get promoted regardless of AFSC,” and that statutory E-8/E-9 percentage limits make within-AFSC competition irrelevant. AFH 1 states SSgt–CMSgt quotas are tied to FY-end strength and affected by funding, regulation, and projected vacancies, and that WAPS selections are made within each AFSC, not across all AFSCs. You may correct the misunderstanding before the Airman briefs peers.

### Response options

A. Endorse the Air Force-wide top-scorer myth so the Airman stays motivated.  
B. Correct the misconception: quotas constrain opportunities by grade/strength factors, while WAPS selections are made within each AFSC under quota limitations; competing peers are those in the AFSC (or applicable identifier), not a single cross-AFSC scoreboard.  
C. Say quotas are fake and every eligible is always promoted.  
D. Tell the Airman to ignore AFSC competition and only study for a force-wide cutoff rumor.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 9.2–9.3 supply quota and within-AFSC selection context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it restores accurate system mechanics without overpromising. Chapter 1 honesty and disciplined communication provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A knowingly teaches a false selection model. Chapter 9 states within-AFSC selection; Chapter 1 accurate mentoring provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It affirms a misconception that can misdirect preparation and expectations.
- **B — Most effective:** It separates quota constraints from within-AFSC competition accurately.
- **C — Ineffective:** It denies quota realities the source describes.
- **D — Ineffective:** It steers the Airman toward an unsupported force-wide cutoff framing.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶9.2–9.3 (quota/WAPS context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Quota misconceptions; honesty; mentoring accuracy  
**ADTC:** 9A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Preserve statutory percentages/quota drivers as snapshots; current strength guidance controls duty counseling

## C09-SJT-010 — Individual responsibility

You supervise a promotion-eligible Staff Sergeant who will be TDY during the entire testing window. Correct test booklets can be made available for early testing before departure. The Airman asks you to “handle eligibility, study references, and scheduling” so they can focus only on the trip. AFH 1 states preparing for promotion testing is an individual responsibility and that members unavailable for the entire cycle must be prepared to test before departure.

### Response options

A. Accept full ownership of the Airman’s eligibility status, EPRRC references, and study plan, and tell them they need take no action.  
B. Clarify that preparation remains the Airman’s responsibility; help them verify eligibility, obtain official references, and schedule authorized early testing before departure while they execute their own self-study plan.  
C. Advise skipping the cycle without coordination because TDY automatically excuses testing.  
D. Offer to take the test in their place if early testing falls through.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 9.10 supplies individual-responsibility context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it preserves the Airman’s ownership while providing lawful supervisory support for early testing and official preparation. Chapter 1 accountability, loyalty, respect, and mission stewardship provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D proposes proxy testing that destroys individual assessment integrity. Chapter 9 centers individual knowledge and responsibility; Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It erases the individual-responsibility requirement the source emphasizes.
- **B — Most effective:** It supports without transferring ownership of preparation or integrity.
- **C — Ineffective:** Unavailability directs early testing preparation, not uncoordinated skipping.
- **D — Least effective:** Proxy testing is an integrity failure.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶9.10 (individual responsibility context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Individual responsibility; accountability; integrity; early testing readiness  
**ADTC:** 9C, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of TDY/testing coordination realism; test control officer procedures control real early testing
