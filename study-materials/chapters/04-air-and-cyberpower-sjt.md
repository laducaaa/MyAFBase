# Chapter 4 Supplemental Situational Judgment Practice

> **Status:** Draft and provisional for 27E5/27E6 preparation; the official 2027 format is unconfirmed.
>
> **Reserve-chapter status:** Chapter 4 was outside the historical 26E5 and 26E6 catalog scopes and remains reserve pending the 2027 WAPS catalogs. Prior exclusion does not prove future inclusion, weighting, or scenario testability.
>
> **Two-track source rule:** For exam-source study, preserve the facts, dates, names, quantities, terminology, and ambiguities in the AFH edition assigned by the controlling catalog. For duty use, separately verify current organizations, missions, policies, terminology, force counts, locations, inventories, and authorities; duty-current information does not overwrite a catalog-controlled answer.
>
> These ten supplemental scenarios are wholly original. They are not actual, recalled, reconstructed, or predicted PFE items. They are supplemental SJT practice only, not a claim that air and cyberpower systems content is scenario-tested.
>
> AFH 1 assigns Chapter 4 section **4A as B—Understanding for both SSgt and TSgt**, and sections **4B and 4C as A—Remembering for SSgt and B—Understanding for TSgt**. Those designations apply to recognizing, recalling, or—where rated B—explaining Chapter 4 systems and airpower facts; they do not make these scenarios A-level or B-level behavioral items. Chapter 4's systems facts provide context only and **do not establish behavioral scoring keys**. The most-effective and least-effective selections below are MyPromotion educational judgments based on Chapter 1 professionalism, core values, and ethical reasoning.
>
> The official Air Force uses subject-matter experts and behavioral scientists to establish operational SJT scoring keys. These supplemental judgments are not official keys or substitutes for current supervisory, safety, technical, legal, ethics, public-affairs, reporting, munitions, cyber, or command procedures. Any process or authority facts needed to rank an option are supplied within the scenario as invented instructional constraints.

## How to use this set

Each scenario produces two selections:

1. Select the **most effective** response.
2. Select the **least effective** response.

Read every option before choosing. Judge the learner's present-day conduct, not the wisdom of a historical actor or the technical excellence of a weapon system. Consider whether the response:

- represents sources, capabilities, status, and uncertainty honestly;
- accepts responsibility within the authority stated in the scenario;
- protects mission requirements without treating caution or speed as automatic answers;
- respects partners, components, and process owners with accuracy and dignity;
- supports disciplined stewardship, readiness, and attention to detail; and
- strengthens teamwork across specialties, components, and partner audiences.

Ten scenarios produce 20 response selections, mirroring only the historical public structure announced for the [22E5/22E6 cycles in December 2021](https://www.afpc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/2874484/waps-adjusts-for-22e6-22e5-testing-cycles/). The separate [public SJT informational guide](https://www.studyguides.af.mil/Portals/15/documents/Notices/Situational-Judgment-Test-Informational-Guide.pdf) explains the four-option most/least format. Neither source confirms the 2027 structure, and the historical 22E5/22E6 format does not show that Chapter 4 air and cyberpower content was or will be tested through scenarios.

## C04-SJT-001 — Capability without overclaim

You are the reviewer for an unclassified public briefing on the A-10 Thunderbolt II. The draft closing slide states that the A-10 "dominates modern air-superiority fights against advanced fighters." The cited AFH 1 extract instead describes a close-air-support platform with secondary roles of air interdiction, airborne forward air control, and combat search and rescue. You may replace one conclusion slide before delivery; the speaker must otherwise use the existing, source-cleared material.

### Response options

A. Keep the air-superiority claim as a memorable headline and place sourced roles on the next slide.  
B. Remove every capability claim and end only with cleared silhouette photos and names.  
C. Replace the ending with AFH 1's close-air-support focus and listed secondary roles.  
D. Keep the slide but have the speaker orally soften the claim if questioned.

### Most effective: C

Paragraph 4.6 supplies systems context for the A-10's primary and secondary roles; it does not prescribe a behavioral scoring key for a briefing editor. C is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it uses the learner's stated revision authority to keep a clear closing that remains within the source. Chapter 1 honesty, accountability, and disciplined communication provide the behavioral basis. Chapter 4 systems facts do not establish the behavioral scoring key.

### Least effective: A

A knowingly preserves a capability claim broader than the approved extract supports and presents overstatement as the audience's takeaway. The Chapter 4 passage explains why the roles matter, but Chapter 1 accurate representation and transparency provide the behavioral basis for the least-effective judgment.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It trades source-supported capability language for rhetorical force despite available revision authority.
- **B — Partially effective:** It avoids the false claim but withholds a supported conclusion the reviewer is authorized to fix.
- **C — Most effective:** It preserves a conclusive ending that matches the designated source without inventing air superiority.
- **D — Ineffective:** An oral caveat leaves the displayed closing still communicating an unsupported capability claim.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶4.6 (systems context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Accurate capability briefing without overclaim; honesty; accountability; disciplined communication  
**ADTC context:** 4B, A—Remembering (SSgt); B—Understanding (TSgt)  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of public-briefing realism; approved source and public-affairs processes control real releases
