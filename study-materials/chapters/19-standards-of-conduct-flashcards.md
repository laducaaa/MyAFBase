## Section 19A — Way of Life

### C19-FC-001
- **Prompt:** Who must comply with prescribed USAF standards of conduct, and when?
- **Answer:** USAF employees must comply in all official matters and when off-duty. Military and civilian Airmen (RegAF, Air Force Reserve, and Air National Guard) are expected to maintain high standards of honesty, responsibility, and accountability.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19A; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-002
- **Prompt:** Which three USAF core values must Airmen adhere to under standards of conduct?
- **Answer:** Integrity First, Service Before Self, and Excellence In All We Do.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19A; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-003
- **Prompt:** According to AFI 1-1 as cited in AFH 1, how do USAF standards compare to civilian life?
- **Answer:** The importance of the USAF mission and its inherent responsibility to the nation require members to adhere to higher standards than those expected in civilian life.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19A; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-004
- **Prompt:** What must leaders ensure regarding USAF standards of conduct?
- **Answer:** Leaders must ensure members are kept informed of USAF standards and take timely and appropriate actions so standards meet the spirit and intent of USAF policy on proper conduct.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19A; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-005
- **Prompt:** In accordance with what publication shall the USAF prepare publications?
- **Answer:** DAFI 90-160, Publications and Forms Management.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19A; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-006
- **Prompt:** What are DAFPDs, and whose orders are they?
- **Answer:** DAF Policy Directives are orders of the Secretary of the Air Force containing directive policy statements that guide DAF implementation of DoD issuances or other binding external authorities requiring DAF action; they also initiate, govern, delegate authorities/responsibilities, and/or regulate actions within specified areas of responsibility.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19A; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-007
- **Prompt:** What do DAFIs, AFIs, and SPFIs generally instruct readers to do?
- **Answer:** They generally instruct readers on “what to do”—direct action and ensure compliance to standard actions across the DAF.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19A; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-008
- **Prompt:** May DAFIs, AFIs, and SPFIs be supplemented below the DAF level?
- **Answer:** Yes—they may be supplemented at any level below the DAF unless otherwise stated in the publication.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19A; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-009
- **Prompt:** What do DAFMANs, AFMANs, and SPFMANs generally instruct readers on?
- **Answer:** They generally instruct readers on “how to” and may be either an extension of a DAFI/AFI/SPFI with detailed procedure/technical guidance, or an alternative to a DAFI/AFI/SPFI if appropriate.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19A; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-010
- **Prompt:** When may manuals include more specialized and technical language?
- **Answer:** When intended for use only by Air and Space Professionals who have graduated from special schools (such as flight training, intelligence, or maintenance schools).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19A; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-011
- **Prompt:** How are publication series numbers organized according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Series numbers of publications are organized based on USAF Specialty Code; Table 19.1 is provided as a quick reference.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.2; Table 19.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19A; B—Understanding

- **Answer:** 1—Air Force Culture; 10—Operations; 11—Flying Operations; 36—Personnel.
- **Source:** AFH 1, Table 19.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19A; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-013

- **Source:** AFH 1, Table 19.1

### C19-FC-014

### C19-FC-015
- **Prompt:** Are nondirective publications mandatory?
- **Answer:** Complying with nondirective publications is expected, but not mandatory. They are informational and suggest guidance that may be modified to fit existing or forecasted circumstances; personnel use them as reference aids or guides.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19A; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-016
- **Prompt:** List examples of nondirective publications from AFH 1.
- **Answer:** Pamphlets; basic and operational doctrine; tactics, techniques, and procedures documents; directories; handbooks; catalogs; visual aids; and product announcements.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19A; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-017
- **Prompt:** Are Airmen subject to duty only during normal duty hours?
- **Answer:** No. Airmen are always subject to duty, including weekends, holidays, and while on leave. If directed by competent authority, they must report for duty at any time, at any location, for as long as necessary.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19A; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-018
- **Prompt:** In what four dimensions does AFH 1 say Airmen must strive to be resilient?
- **Answer:** Physically, mentally, emotionally, and spiritually.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19A; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-019
- **Prompt:** What are General Orders, and why must members comply with them?
- **Answer:** General Orders often provide clear, concise guidance tailored to good order and discipline in the deployed setting, including host-nation restrictions. Strict compliance produces discipline and resilience needed for mission success; failure may bring adverse administrative action, nonjudicial punishment, or UCMJ prosecution.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19A; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-020
- **Prompt:** How must USAF standards be known, applied, and enforced?
- **Answer:** Uniformly known, consistently applied, and non-selectively enforced.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19A; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-021
- **Prompt:** Beyond following standards, what responsibility do Airmen have regarding standards of conduct?
- **Answer:** Learn standards well enough to follow them, articulate them clearly to subordinates, and enforce proper observation by other members.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19A; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-022
- **Prompt:** Which additional ethics authorities does AFH 1 ¶19.4 point readers to?
- **Answer:** 5 CFR 2635, Standards of Ethical Conduct for Employees of the Executive Branch; the Joint Ethics Regulation; and AFI 1-1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19A; B—Understanding

## Section 19B — Law of War

### C19-FC-023
- **Prompt:** What is another name for the law of war, and how does DoD define it?
- **Answer:** Also called the Law of Armed Conflict (LOAC); DoD defines it as the treaties and customary international law binding on the United States that regulate resort to armed force; conduct of hostilities and protection of war victims in international and non-international armed conflict; belligerent occupation; and relationships between belligerent, neutral, and non-belligerent states.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-024
- **Prompt:** From what desire does the law of war arise, and what does it aim to prevent?
- **Answer:** It arises from civilized nations’ humanitarian desire to lessen the effects of conflicts and aims to keep conflicts from degenerating into savagery and brutality, thereby helping restore peace.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-025
- **Prompt:** Whom does the law of war protect from unnecessary suffering, and who receives fundamental protections when falling into enemy hands?
- **Answer:** It protects combatants and noncombatants, including civilians, from unnecessary suffering; fundamental protections apply particularly to prisoners of war, civilians, and military wounded, sick, and shipwrecked.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-026
- **Prompt:** What does DoDD 2311.01 require military departments to implement regarding the law of war?
- **Answer:** Effective programs that ensure law-of-war observance, prevent violations, ensure prompt reporting of alleged violations, and appropriately train all forces.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-027
- **Prompt:** Which groups receive specialized law-of-war training for unique situations?
- **Answer:** Certain groups such as aircrews, medical personnel, and security forces.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-028
- **Prompt:** What is the constitutional status of U.S. treaty obligations relevant to the law of war?
- **Answer:** Article VI of the U.S. Constitution states treaty obligations are the “supreme law of the land”; treaties and international agreements to which the United States is a party enjoy equal status to laws passed by Congress and signed by the President.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-029
- **Prompt:** Who must observe law-of-war obligations when planning or executing operations?
- **Answer:** All persons subject to United States law, as well as military personnel, civilians, and contractors authorized to accompany the U.S. Armed Forces when planning or executing operations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-030
- **Prompt:** List the five important law-of-war principles addressed in AFH 1.
- **Answer:** Military necessity, humanity, distinction, proportionality, and honor.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-031
- **Prompt:** Define military necessity as a law-of-war principle.
- **Answer:** It justifies the use of all measures needed to defeat the enemy as quickly and efficiently as possible that are not prohibited by the law of war; attacks must be limited to military objectives.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-032
- **Prompt:** Which persons are military objectives and may be made the object of attack?
- **Answer:** Combatants, unprivileged belligerents, and civilians taking a direct part in hostilities.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-033
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define military objectives as to objects?
- **Answer:** Objects which by their nature, location, purpose, or use make an effective contribution to military action and whose total or partial destruction, capture, or neutralization, at the time, offer a definite military advantage.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-034
- **Prompt:** Give examples of military objective objects from AFH 1.
- **Answer:** Tanks, military aircraft, bases, supplies, lines of communication, and headquarters.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-035
- **Prompt:** Can military necessity authorize murder of prisoners of war or taking hostages?
- **Answer:** No. Under no circumstances may military necessity authorize actions specifically prohibited by the law of war, such as murder of POWs, ill treatment of POWs or internees, taking hostages, or execution/reprisal against persons or objects specifically protected from reprisal.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-036
- **Prompt:** What does the principle of humanity forbid?
- **Answer:** The infliction of suffering, injury, or destruction unnecessary to accomplish a legitimate military purpose; once a military purpose has been achieved, inflicting more suffering is unnecessary and must be avoided.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-037
- **Prompt:** What does *hors de combat* mean in the humanity example, and what follows?
- **Answer:** Taken out of the fight (e.g., severely wounded or captured); no military purpose is served by continuing to attack that person.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-038
- **Prompt:** What requirement does distinction impose?
- **Answer:** Distinguish (discriminate) between military forces and the civilian population, and between unprotected and protected objects; military force may be directed only against military objectives, not civilian objects.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-039
- **Prompt:** Give examples of civilian objects protected from attack under distinction.
- **Answer:** Places of worship, schools, hospitals, and dwellings (unless they lose protected status by contributing to military action).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-040
- **Prompt:** Why is use of human shields a fundamental violation of distinction?
- **Answer:** Employment of voluntary or involuntary human shields to protect military objectives or individual military units or personnel is a fundamental violation of the law-of-war principle of distinction.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-041
- **Prompt:** Does law-of-war proportionality require that no incidental damage result from attacks?
- **Answer:** No. It creates obligations to refrain from attacks where expected incidental harm would be excessive in relation to the concrete and direct military advantage anticipated, and to take feasible precautions to reduce risk to civilians and other protected persons and objects.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-042
- **Prompt:** What does honor require between opposing military forces?
- **Answer:** A certain amount of fairness in offense and defense and a certain mutual respect; parties must accept certain limits on hostilities and not resort to means that would constitute a breach of trust with the enemy.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-043
- **Prompt:** List five good-faith prohibitions under honor during hostilities.
- **Answer:** (1) Killing or wounding by perfidy (treachery); (2) misusing certain signs; (3) fighting in the enemy’s uniform; (4) feigning non-hostile relations to seek military advantage; (5) compelling nationals of a hostile party to take part in operations of war directed against their own country.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-044
- **Prompt:** Does honor forbid all deception against the enemy?
- **Answer:** No. Honor does not forbid parties from using ruses and other lawful deceptions against which the enemy ought to take measures to protect itself.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-045
- **Prompt:** What do the 1949 Geneva Conventions aim to protect?
- **Answer:** All persons taking no active part in hostilities—including members of military forces who laid down their arms and combatants out of the fight due to sickness, wounds, detention, or any other cause—and also civilians and private property.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-046
- **Prompt:** If doubt exists about a captured individual’s status, what protections apply?
- **Answer:** The individual receives the protections of the Geneva Prisoner of War Convention until actual status is determined.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-047
- **Prompt:** Who are lawful or “privileged” combatants according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** (1) Members of the military forces of a state party to a conflict (aside from certain medical and religious personnel); (2) under certain conditions, militia/volunteer corps belonging to a state but not part of its military forces; (3) inhabitants in a *levée en masse* defending against foreign invaders.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-048
- **Prompt:** What four characteristics describe a combatant’s conduct of operations under AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Commanded by a person responsible for subordinates; wears fixed distinctive emblems/uniforms recognizable at a distance; carries arms openly; conducts combat operations according to the law of war.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-049
- **Prompt:** Who are noncombatants, and may they be attacked?
- **Answer:** Certain military personnel not authorized to engage in combatant activities, such as permanent medical and religious personnel; they must be respected and protected and may not be made the object of attack.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-050
- **Prompt:** When do civilians forfeit protection from being made the object of attack?
- **Answer:** When they take an active or direct part in hostilities (with the *levée en masse* exception noted regarding authorization to take part).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-051
- **Prompt:** How does the DoD Law of War Manual define unprivileged belligerent (as quoted in AFH 1)?
- **Answer:** Includes lawful combatants who forfeited combatant privileges by engaging in spying or sabotage, and private persons who forfeited one or more protections of civilian status by engaging in hostilities; an individual not authorized by a state party to take part in hostilities but who does so anyway.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-052
- **Prompt:** When may military objectives not be attacked regarding incidental civilian harm?
- **Answer:** When expected incidental loss of civilian life, injury to civilians, and damage to civilian objects would be excessive in relation to the concrete and direct military advantage expected to be gained.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-053
- **Prompt:** May civilians be used as shields or hostages?
- **Answer:** No. Civilians must not be made the object of attack and must not be used as shields or hostages; measures of intimidation or terrorism against the civilian population are prohibited.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-054
- **Prompt:** Give examples of military operations short of violence against civilians that AFH 1 says are not prohibited.
- **Answer:** Stopping/searching civilians for weapons and verifying they are civilians; temporarily detaining them for mission, self-defense, or safety; collecting intelligence/interrogating; restricting or directing movement away from operations for protection; seeking to influence enemy civilians with propaganda.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-055
- **Prompt:** On what basis must commanders make targeting decisions when information is imperfect?
- **Answer:** In good faith based on the information available to them at the time; they may direct and conduct operations so long as they make a good-faith assessment of available information.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-056
- **Prompt:** List protected objects specifically named in AFH 1 ¶19.8.
- **Answer:** Medical units/establishments; transports of wounded and sick; military and civilian hospital ships; Geneva Conventions safety zones; religious, cultural, and charitable buildings; monuments; and prisoner-of-war camps.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-057
- **Prompt:** When may protected objects lose protected status?
- **Answer:** If they are used for military purposes.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-058
- **Prompt:** May enemy military aircraft in neutral airspace or territory be attacked?
- **Answer:** No. Enemy military aircraft may be attacked and destroyed unless in neutral airspace or territory.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-059
- **Prompt:** May airmen parachuting from a disabled aircraft who offer no resistance be attacked?
- **Answer:** No. Airmen who parachute from a disabled aircraft and offer no resistance may not be attacked. Those who resist in descent or are downed behind their own lines and continue to fight may be subject to attack.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-060
- **Prompt:** When are military medical aircraft entitled to protection from attack?
- **Answer:** While flying at heights, times, and on routes specifically agreed upon between the parties to the conflict; if found in violation of agreements, they could be lawfully attacked after other means of control (e.g., directing to land and submit to search) are exhausted.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-061
- **Prompt:** Do contractors accompanying deployed forces have to comply with the law of war?
- **Answer:** Yes. All DoD personnel, including contractors when assigned to or accompanying deployed armed forces, comply with the law of war during all armed conflicts and military operations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-062
- **Prompt:** What duty do service members have regarding clearly illegal orders to commit law-of-war violations?
- **Answer:** Each member has a duty to comply with the law of war, which includes refusal to comply with clearly illegal orders to commit law-of-war violations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-063
- **Prompt:** How may members be prosecuted for law-of-war violations?
- **Answer:** By courts-martial under the UCMJ or through an international military tribunal.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-064
- **Prompt:** To whom should DoD personnel promptly report a suspected law-of-war violation?
- **Answer:** To their immediate commander or the proper authority. If the allegation involves or may involve a U.S. commander, report to the next higher U.S. command authority. Circumstances may require reporting to the nearest judge advocate, IG, OSI special agent, or security forces member.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-065
- **Prompt:** What do rules of engagement set parameters for?
- **Answer:** When, where, how, why, and against whom commanders and their Airmen may use force—to ensure use of force accords with national policy goals, mission requirements, and the rule of law.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-066
- **Prompt:** Who approves and who issues the standing rules of engagement?
- **Answer:** Approved by the U.S. President and Secretary of Defense; issued by the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-067
- **Prompt:** Where are mission-specific ROE generally contained?
- **Answer:** In an annex to execution orders, operations plans, and operations orders.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-068
- **Prompt:** What is the fundamental U.S. self-defense policy stated in the standing ROE?
- **Answer:** The rules do not limit a commander’s inherent authority and obligation to use all necessary means available to take all appropriate actions in self-defense of the commander’s unit and other U.S. Armed Forces in the vicinity.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-069
- **Prompt:** What is de-escalation under ROE self-defense?
- **Answer:** When time and circumstances permit, forces committing hostile acts or demonstrating hostile intent should be warned and given the opportunity to withdraw or cease threatening actions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-070
- **Prompt:** What does necessity mean in the ROE self-defense context?
- **Answer:** Whether the individual perceives a hostile act has occurred or hostile intent is being demonstrated—force or threats of force against the United States, U.S. Armed Forces, designated persons/property, or intended to impede the mission of U.S. Armed Forces.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-071
- **Prompt:** What does proportionality mean in the ROE self-defense context?
- **Answer:** Reasonableness of the response: use only the amount of force necessary to decisively counter a hostile act or demonstration of hostile intent and ensure continued safety; force must be reasonable in nature, duration, and scope based on facts known at the time.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-072
- **Prompt:** When may U.S. forces pursue a hostile force under ROE, and what may limit pursuit?
- **Answer:** They can pursue and engage a hostile force that continues to commit hostile acts or demonstrate hostile intent; applicable ROE may restrict or limit pursuit/engagement across an international border.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19B; B—Understanding

## Section 19C — Code of Conduct

### C19-FC-073
- **Prompt:** What kind of code is the Code of Conduct, and are violations criminally punishable per se?
- **Answer:** It is a moral code providing a standard of conduct all members are expected to measure up to. Violations are not criminally punishable per se, but actions that also violate the UCMJ may subject members to disciplinary action.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19C; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-074
- **Prompt:** Besides POW situations, to what other hostile detention does the Code of Conduct apply?
- **Answer:** Other hostile detention such as hostage scenarios.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19C; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-075
- **Prompt:** What factors determine the level of Code of Conduct knowledge members need?
- **Answer:** How likely they are to be captured, their exposure to sensitive information, and how useful or valuable a captor considers them to be.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19C; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-076
- **Prompt:** What is Code of Conduct Level A training?
- **Answer:** Entry Level Training—the minimum level of understanding needed for all members of the U.S. Armed Forces, imparted during entry training.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19C; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-077
- **Prompt:** What is Code of Conduct Level B training, and who needs it?
- **Answer:** Training After Assumption of Duty Eligibility—an enhanced Level A; minimum for members whose jobs, specialties, or assignments entail moderate risk of capture (e.g., ground combat units); conducted as soon as assumption of duty makes them eligible.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19C; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-078
- **Prompt:** What is Code of Conduct Level C training, and who needs it?
- **Answer:** Training Upon Assumption of Duties or Responsibilities—enhanced Levels A and B; minimum for significant or high risk of capture and greater-than-average exploitation risk by position, rank, or seniority (e.g., aircrews and special mission forces such as pararescue teams).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19C; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-079
- **Prompt:** Who first published the Code of Conduct, when, and who later amended it with gender-neutral language?
- **Answer:** President Dwight D. Eisenhower on 17 August 1955; President Ronald W. Reagan amended it with gender-neutral language in March 1988.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19C; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-080
- **Prompt:** State Article I of the Code of Conduct.
- **Answer:** I am an American, fighting in the forces which guard my country and our way of life. I am prepared to give my life in their defense.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19C; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-081
- **Prompt:** Distinguish surrender from capture under Article II.
- **Answer:** Surrender is the willful act of giving oneself up to the enemy. Capture occurs when a member has no means to resist, evasion is impossible, and further fighting would lead to death of the U.S. member with no significant loss to the enemy; capture dictated by overwhelming enemy strength and futility of fighting is not dishonorable.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19C; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-082
- **Prompt:** Under Article III, what must a POW not accept, and what are parole agreements?
- **Answer:** Neither parole nor special favors from the enemy. Parole agreements are promises made to the captor to fulfill stated conditions (e.g., not to bear arms) in exchange for special privileges (e.g., release or lessened restraint).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19C; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-083
- **Prompt:** Why are continued escape efforts critical under Article III?
- **Answer:** A successful escape causes the enemy to divert forces that may otherwise be fighting, provides the United States valuable information about the enemy and other POWs, and serves as a positive example to all members of the U.S. Armed Forces.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19C; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-084
- **Prompt:** Under Article IV, how must POWs organize, and who takes command?
- **Answer:** Organize in a military manner under the senior military POW, regardless of military service; if the senior is incapacitated or unable to act, the next senior POW assumes command.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19C; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-085
- **Prompt:** What four items is a POW required to give when questioned under Article V?
- **Answer:** Name, rank, service number, and date of birth.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19C; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-086
- **Prompt:** What conversations with captors are allowed but not required under Article V?
- **Answer:** Filling out a Geneva Conventions capture card, writing letters home, and communicating with captors on matters of health and welfare; the senior POW must represent POWs in camp administration, health, welfare, and grievances.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19C; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-087
- **Prompt:** Under Article VI, what is the difference between the Code of Conduct and the UCMJ?
- **Answer:** The Code of Conduct is a moral code; the UCMJ is a legal code. Failure to follow the Code could ultimately lead to misconduct punishable under the UCMJ.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19C; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-088
- **Prompt:** In operations other than war, why may detained personnel lack Geneva POW protections?
- **Answer:** Basic protections available to POWs under the Geneva Conventions may not be adhered to during operations other than war; detained personnel may be subject to the domestic criminal laws of the detaining nation and should use the Code of Conduct as a moral guide.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19C; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-089
- **Prompt:** What should detainees ask for immediately when held by an unfriendly government, and what information should they provide?
- **Answer:** Ask immediately and continually to see U.S. embassy personnel or a representative of an allied or neutral government; provide name, rank, service number, date of birth, and the innocent circumstances leading to detention; limit further discussion to health/welfare, fellow detainees’ conditions, and going home.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19C; B—Understanding

### C19-FC-090
- **Prompt:** How does DoD policy on escape from unfriendly government detention differ from POW escape duty, and what is the terrorist-hostage rapport guidance?
- **Answer:** Escape from unfriendly government detention is not recommended by DoD policy except under life-threatening circumstances (unlike Article III POW escape emphasis). For terrorist hostages, DoD policy accepts and promotes efforts to establish rapport so hostages are seen as people rather than symbols; avoid inflaming topics (cause, politics, religion); during rescue, take cover, remain stationary when practicable, and do not attempt to help rescuers.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶19.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 19C; B—Understanding
