## Section 12A — Individual Finances and Allowances

### C12-FC-001
- **Prompt:** Why does AFH 1 say “fiscal fitness” matters, and what two key aspects of individual financial responsibility does it name?
- **Answer:** Fiscal fitness is as important to overall well-being as physical fitness. Two key aspects are establishing a personal budget and effectively managing debt.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12A; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-002
- **Prompt:** Besides budgeting and debt management, what three travel/pay habits does AFH 1 highlight for financial responsibility?
- **Answer:** Review LES regularly, file travel vouchers on a timely basis, and use the GTCC to pay costs related to official government travel.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12A; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-003
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define official government travel?
- **Answer:** Travel under official orders while performing duties pertaining to official government assignments such as TDY and PCS.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12A; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-004
- **Prompt:** What is basic pay, and how are annual military pay raises set?
- **Answer:** Basic pay is the fundamental and typically largest component of military pay. Annual raises are set by Congress and the President in the NDAA; by federal law the 1 January raise automatically matches private-sector wage increases measured by the prior-year Employment Cost Index, though the President may propose a lower raise in certain national emergencies or serious economic conditions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12A; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-005
- **Prompt:** What does the military pay date determine, and what can cause negative pay-date adjustments?
- **Answer:** It determines length of service for pay purposes. Negative adjustments may result from AWOL, desertion, and sickness or injury due to personal misconduct.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12A; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-006
- **Prompt:** What is the LES formally titled, and what information does it comprehensively show?
- **Answer:** DFAS Form 702, Defense Finance and Accounting Service Military Leave and Earnings Statement. It shows entitlements, deductions, allotments, leave information, tax withholdings, and Traditional and Roth TSP information.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12A; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-007
- **Prompt:** Why should Airmen apply increased attention to the LES during PCS?
- **Answer:** Entitlements such as BAH and COLA often change when moving duty locations, and PCS usually occurs more frequently than events such as birth, marriage, or divorce.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12A; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-008
- **Prompt:** Around which dates do mid-month and end-of-month pay processing cutoffs generally occur?
- **Answer:** Generally around the 6th for mid-month and the 20th for end-of-month paydays, though cutoff dates fluctuate.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12A; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-009
- **Prompt:** When are local cash payments normally authorized, and what limits apply to partial payments?
- **Answer:** Normally only overseas where on-base military banking is not readily available (with exceptions for classified/contingency operations). Partial payments are limited to pay and allowances accrued to the payment date and are recouped in full on the next available payday.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12A; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-010
- **Prompt:** When may a stateside member receive an emergency partial payment?
- **Answer:** Under extenuating circumstances if deemed time sensitive and required within 24 hours due to unforeseen circumstances.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12A; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-011
- **Prompt:** What is the purpose of allowances, and what embedded benefit does AFH 1 note about many allowances?
- **Answer:** Allowances are provided for specific needs such as food or housing when the government does not provide for that need. Many allowances are not taxable.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12A; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-012
- **Prompt:** What is BAS, and how do missed-meal reimbursements work?
- **Answer:** BAS is a nontaxable allowance used to offset meal costs. When certified by the commanding officer or designee, members may claim reimbursement for missed meals.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12A; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-013
- **Prompt:** What is the intent of BAH, and are members in privatized quarters entitled to it?
- **Answer:** BAH provides accurate and equitable housing compensation based on local civilian housing markets and is payable when government quarters are not provided. Members in privatized quarters are entitled to BAH.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12A; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-014
- **Prompt:** Who may qualify for partial BAH or BAH-differential?
- **Answer:** Members without dependents in government single-type quarters are entitled to partial BAH unless quarters exceed minimum single-quarters standards for their grade. Members in single-type government quarters who pay court-ordered child support may qualify for BAH-differential.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12A; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-015
- **Prompt:** How does OHA reimburse housing costs overseas?
- **Answer:** It reimburses actual rental costs not to exceed the maximum OHA rate for locality, grade, and dependency status; monthly OHA includes rent up to the rental allowance plus the utility/recurring maintenance allowance. Move-in housing allowance (if qualified) is based on average move-in costs.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12A; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-016
- **Prompt:** What does Family Separation Housing pay, and how do FSH-BAH and FSH-OHA amounts relate to without-dependent rates?
- **Answer:** FSH pays for added housing expenses from enforced separation from dependents. FSH-BAH equals the without-dependent BAH rate for grade and PDS; FSH-OHA is up to the without-dependent OHA rate for grade and PDS.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12A; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-017
- **Prompt:** How do clothing replacement allowance-basic and -standard differ in timing?
- **Answer:** CRA-basic is paid annually between the 6th and 36th month of RegAF status; CRA-standard automatically replaces CRA-basic after 36 months of RegAF status.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12A; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-018
- **Prompt:** When does FSA-restricted apply versus FSA-temporary?
- **Answer:** FSA-restricted: dependent transportation not authorized at government expense and dependents do not live in the vicinity of the PDS. FSA-temporary: member on TDY away from PDS continuously more than 30 days and dependents not residing at or near the TDY station (including TDY before reporting to initial station of assignment).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12A; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-019
- **Prompt:** What overseas station allowances may DoD authorize, and where do members get entitlement information?
- **Answer:** Temporary lodging allowance and cost-of-living allowance at certain overseas locations. Members receive information during in-processing and may also consult local finance upon notification of a pending overseas assignment.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12A; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-020
- **Prompt:** What is the Basic Needs Allowance eligibility threshold as AFH 1 prints it?
- **Answer:** A taxable monthly allowance for Airmen and Guardians who voluntarily apply and whose Gross Household Income and household size place them below 150 percent of Federal poverty guidelines for their geographical location. **AFH source note:** 2025 snapshot—revalidate current eligibility rules.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12A; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-021
- **Prompt:** What federal tax advantage does AFH 1 attribute to BAH and BAS?
- **Answer:** Service members do not pay federal or state taxes on BAH or BAS, significantly increasing purchasing power for food and housing; members also enjoy combat tax exclusion and fully deductible mortgage interest.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12A; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-022
- **Prompt:** Who receives CONUS COLA?
- **Answer:** Members assigned to designated “high cost” areas within the CONUS.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12A; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-023
- **Prompt:** Name examples of special and incentive pay AFH 1 lists.
- **Answer:** Hazardous duty incentive pay, imminent danger pay, special duty assignment pay, enlisted flying duty incentive pay, and hardship duty pay-location; enlistment and retention bonuses are also considered special and incentive pay.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12A; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-024
- **Prompt:** What are involuntary deductions, and what AFRH deduction maximum does AFH 1 print?
- **Answer:** Payroll deductions of certain amounts of pay imposed by law (examples include FICA, FITW, SITW, AFRH). Monthly AFRH deductions from regular enlisted pay are up to a maximum of $1. **AFH source note:** 2025 snapshot—revalidate.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12A; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-025
- **Prompt:** What allotment uses are limited for nondiscretionary allotments, and what allotments are members not authorized to start?
- **Answer:** Nondiscretionary allotments have limited uses such as charitable contributions, AFAS loan repayments, garnishment for commercial debts, and delinquent travel charge card debt. Members are not authorized to start allotments for purchase, lease, or rental of personal property.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12A; B—Understanding

## Section 12B — Official Travel Expenses and Allowances

### C12-FC-026
- **Prompt:** What does AFH 1 say individuals must do regarding reimbursable official travel costs?
- **Answer:** Understand and adhere to specific travel regulations accurately and within a timely manner.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12B; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-027
- **Prompt:** What is a PCS advance payment, and who needs commander approval?
- **Answer:** An advance of up to three months of basic pay less mandatory deductions and debts currently deducted, for extraordinary PCS-related expenses not covered by other entitlements. E-3 and below need immediate commander approval; anyone requesting repayment >12 months or more than one month’s basic pay also needs immediate commander approval. Repayment >12 months only for financial hardship.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12B; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-028
- **Prompt:** What normally determines authorized HHG weight allowances, and what happens if a member ships excess weight?
- **Answer:** Grade and number of dependents. If goods exceed the authorized weight allowance, payments are based on the authorized weight allowance.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12B; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-029
- **Prompt:** When is Dislocation Allowance payable to members with and without dependents?
- **Answer:** With dependents: when dependents relocate HHG in conjunction with a PCS. Without dependents: if not assigned permanent government quarters upon arrival at the new PDS.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12B; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-030
- **Prompt:** What is the commercial-air maximum for unaccompanied baggage when the expedited mode is commercial air?
- **Answer:** A maximum of 1,000 pounds (net). **AFH source note:** 2025 snapshot—revalidate.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12B; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-031
- **Prompt:** When may members ship one POV at government expense?
- **Answer:** When authorized and ordered PCS to, from, or between locations overseas. POV storage may be provided/authorized when shipment is prohibited or restricted.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12B; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-032
- **Prompt:** What air-carrier rule applies when the government funds commercial air travel?
- **Answer:** Unless an authorizing/order-issuing official determines U.S.-certificated air carriers are unavailable, U.S.-certificated air carriers must be used; documentation explaining unavailability must be provided.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12B; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-033
- **Prompt:** What reimbursement limit applies if a member personally procures common carrier transportation despite CTO policy, and what exception applies to transoceanic travel?
- **Answer:** Reimbursement up to the authorized amount, not exceeding cost for authorized transportation/accommodations over a usually traveled direct route; no reimbursement is authorized for personally procured transoceanic travel.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12B; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-034
- **Prompt:** How do Temporary Lodging Expense and Temporary Lodging Allowance differ in location and payment method?
- **Answer:** TLE helps CONUS PCS temporary lodging costs and is paid on a travel voucher. TLA helps OCONUS PCS temporary lodging costs and is paid in military pay, reflected on the LES.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12B; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-035
- **Prompt:** Within how many business days must a traveler file a voucher after return to PDS, and what does the signature certify?
- **Answer:** Within five business days. The signature indicates the claim is accurate, statements are true and complete, and the traveler is aware of liability for filing a false claim.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12B; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-036
- **Prompt:** What settlement and disbursement rules apply to travel claims in DTS?
- **Answer:** EFT is mandatory settlement. Split disbursement will be used to pay the GTCC vendor for IBA charges. For TDY of 45 days or more, interim vouchers or scheduled partial payments are set up every 30 days with split disbursement; extended TDY does not excuse on-time GTCC payments.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12B; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-037
- **Prompt:** How is TDY per diem structured for lodging versus meals and incidentals?
- **Answer:** Travelers are paid a prescribed amount for meals and incidental expenses plus the actual amount for lodging, not to exceed the maximum lodging rate for the location; rates depend on government facility availability.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.18
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12B; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-038
- **Prompt:** What is the purpose of the GTCC program?
- **Answer:** Facilitate and standardize DoD travel with a safe, effective, convenient commercial payment method for official travel expenses (including local travel); improve cash management; reduce administrative workload; facilitate better service; and produce DoD cost savings via the refund feature.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12B; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-039
- **Prompt:** Who is an APC, and who designates the APC?
- **Answer:** An Agency Program Coordinator—uniformed member, employee, contractor, or foreign national—designated in writing by a commander or director as responsible for GTCC program management within the hierarchy.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12B; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-040
- **Prompt:** When must DoD personnel use the GTCC, and may incidental non-reimbursable charges be placed on it?
- **Answer:** Unless exempted, for all expenses arising from official government travel (lodging, transportation, local ground transportation, authorized rental cars). While in official travel status, non-reimbursable incidentals such as rental movies, personal calls, exercise fees, and beverages may be charged when not part of a separate room billing or meal; all charges must be paid via split disbursement.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12B; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-041
- **Prompt:** What ATM-advance timing and fee rule applies to the GTCC?
- **Answer:** ATM advances will not be obtained earlier than three working days before scheduled travel and are limited to authorized expenses exempt from mandatory card usage (meals, incidentals, miscellaneous). ATM fees are part of the incidental-expense portion of per diem, not separately reimbursable.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12B; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-042
- **Prompt:** What APC notifications and account actions occur during PCS?
- **Answer:** Cardholder notifies losing APC before departure and gaining APC upon reporting. Losing APC sets “mission critical,” future deactivation based on RNLTD, and transfer request. Gaining APC processes transfer, updates account, and clears deactivation date. Bills must stay current during PCS.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12B; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-043
- **Prompt:** What happens at GTCC delinquency milestones of 31, 61, 75, 96, and 181 days as AFH 1 prints them?
- **Answer:** 31 days: suspended; contact APC. 61 days: due-process letter and impending salary offset unless paid in 30 days or Reduced Payment Plan reached. Day 75: first late fee (two cycles +15), then each cycle. 96 days: submitted to DFAS for salary offset; offset enrollment fees not reimbursable. 181 days: charged off; no longer eligible for reinstatement even if paid. **AFH source note:** 2025 snapshot—revalidate.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12B; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-044
- **Prompt:** What is GTCC misuse, and what may result?
- **Answer:** Using the GTCC other than while in official travel status. Misuse will not be tolerated; cardholders are subject to appropriate administrative or disciplinary action.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12B; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-045
- **Prompt:** What miscellaneous TDY expenses does AFH 1 list as potentially reimbursable?
- **Answer:** Travel from home/lodging to the servicing transportation terminal by taxi, limousine, bus, or POV; plus passports, visas, and rental vehicles when authorized on travel orders.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.18
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12B; B—Understanding

## Section 12C — Manpower Management

### C12-FC-046
- **Prompt:** Why does AFH 1 call manpower a constrained resource?
- **Answer:** It comprises a significant portion of the USAF budget; missions and programs compete for authorized military and civilian end-strength and grade distributions and must comply with congressional guidelines.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12C; A—Remembering

### C12-FC-047
- **Prompt:** How must manpower be programmed according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** In accordance with validated manpower requirements, within fiscal limits and acceptable risks, as identified in defense planning and programming guidance.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12C; A—Remembering

### C12-FC-048
- **Prompt:** List the five surge/short-term alternatives AFH 1 says MAJCOMs should use instead of programmed manpower change requests.
- **Answer:** (1) Available funds/civilian employment plans; (2) overtime, temporary full-time, part-time, or overhire civilians; (3) ARC support via military pay appropriation funds; (4) TDY military and civilian personnel; (5) contract service.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12C; A—Remembering

### C12-FC-049
- **Prompt:** What three types of manpower conduct the USAF mission?
- **Answer:** Military personnel, in-service civilian employees, and contracted services.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12C; A—Remembering

### C12-FC-050
- **Prompt:** To which positions does DoD guidance say services should assign military personnel?
- **Answer:** Positions that directly contribute to the prosecution of war; are military by law, custom, or tradition; or are needed for overseas rotation, operational deployment augmentation, or career-field sustainment.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12C; A—Remembering

### C12-FC-051
- **Prompt:** For what phase of the planning-scenario construct are manpower requirements sized?
- **Answer:** The most demanding phase, including rotational forces needed for prolonged conflicts.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12C; A—Remembering

### C12-FC-052
- **Prompt:** How does AFI 38-101, as cited in AFH 1, define a manpower requirement, and how can it be documented?
- **Answer:** Manpower needed to accomplish a job, mission, or program; documented as a funded manpower authorization or an unfunded requirement.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12C; A—Remembering

### C12-FC-053
- **Prompt:** What is the Unit Manpower Document?
- **Answer:** The primary document reflecting manpower required to accomplish the unit mission—a computer-generated product listing funded and unfunded requirements used to help manage manpower resources.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12C; A—Remembering

### C12-FC-054
- **Prompt:** Name key UMD data elements AFH 1 lists.
- **Answer:** Position number, AFSC, functional account code, office symbol code, grade, personnel accounting symbol, and reason code.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12C; A—Remembering

### C12-FC-055
- **Prompt:** How is an existing UMD requirement changed?
- **Answer:** Submit a manpower change request with justification to the servicing manpower organization; approved changes appear via a change notice from the manpower programming and execution system.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12C; A—Remembering

### C12-FC-056
- **Prompt:** What are the core competencies of Manpower and Organization?
- **Answer:** Organization structure, requirement determination, program allocation and control, and process improvement.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.23
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12C; A—Remembering

### C12-FC-057
- **Prompt:** What day-to-day services does the Manpower and Organization Flight provide?
- **Answer:** UMD management, assistance with manpower change requests, change notices, and organizational structure changes (plus other services such as performance management, commercial activity services, Airmen Powered by Innovation, continuous process improvement, and management advisory studies).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.23
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12C; A—Remembering

### C12-FC-058
- **Prompt:** What are the three principal goals of Commercial Services Management?
- **Answer:** Sustain readiness; improve performance and quality by doing business more efficiently and cost effectively; and focus available personnel and resources on core USAF missions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.24
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12C; A—Remembering

### C12-FC-059
- **Prompt:** What will Commercial Services Management not affect?
- **Answer:** Military-essential skills or those functions that are inherently governmental.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.24
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12C; A—Remembering

### C12-FC-060
- **Prompt:** Why must command-specific military and civilian manpower requirement changes be validated by HAF before programming use?
- **Answer:** Because manpower is constrained and programmed against validated requirements within fiscal limits; HAF validation/approval is required before those changes can be used in the programming and resourcing process.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.20
- **Tags:** TSgt; 12C; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-061
- **Prompt:** How do funded authorizations, unfunded requirements, the UMD, change requests, and change notices relate?
- **Answer:** A requirement may be a funded authorization or unfunded requirement; the UMD lists both for unit management; justified change requests go to servicing manpower; approved changes appear as change notices from the programming and execution system.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶¶12.22–12.23
- **Tags:** TSgt; 12C; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-062
- **Prompt:** How do Manpower and Organization Flight services connect requirement determination to commercial services management?
- **Answer:** Flights assist by quantifying requirements (peacetime standards, wartime requirements) and support commercial services actions such as public-private competition, in-sourcing, and business process reengineering, within core competencies of structure, requirements, allocation/control, and process improvement.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶¶12.21, 12.23–12.24
- **Tags:** TSgt; 12C; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-063
- **Prompt:** How does competitive sourcing differ in purpose from in-sourcing?
- **Answer:** Competitive sourcing compares in-house versus private-sector cost to find the more economical/efficient provider while keeping the mission essentially unchanged. In-sourcing converts a contracted function to DoD civilian/military performance (or vice versa) based on business-case analysis and stated applicability conditions—and certain inherently governmental/problem contracts are in-sourced regardless of cost.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.25
- **Tags:** TSgt; 12C; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-064
- **Prompt:** Why should short-term surge workload use alternatives other than programmed manpower change requests?
- **Answer:** Programmed changes require validated/approved requirement changes for enduring programming; short-term/surge needs should use civilian employment flexibilities, ARC support, TDY, or contracts instead of treating surge as a programmed manpower change.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.20
- **Tags:** TSgt; 12C; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-065
- **Prompt:** How do military-assignment criteria relate to Commercial Services Management’s focus on core missions?
- **Answer:** Military members are assigned only to war-prosecution, military-by-law/custom/tradition, or rotation/deployment/career-sustainment positions; Commercial Services Management aims to focus personnel/resources on core missions and will not affect military-essential or inherently governmental functions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶¶12.21, 12.24
- **Tags:** TSgt; 12C; B—Understanding

## Section 12D — Resource Management

### C12-FC-066
- **Prompt:** What does the USAF resource management system focus on?
- **Answer:** Outputs and resources used; managers effectively using resources; measuring actual versus planned performance; and using financial plans and accounting to enhance management controls at each organizational level.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.26
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12D; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-067
- **Prompt:** What resource stewardship responsibility does every USAF member have, and what additional responsibility do commanders and supervisors have?
- **Answer:** Every member has a principal responsibility to ensure resources are used in the most cost-effective manner. Commanders and supervisors are responsible for efficient and economical use of all resources in their organizations and for budgeting, allocation, composition, and distribution of those resources.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.27
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12D; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-068
- **Prompt:** What do responsibility center managers and cost center managers do differently?
- **Answer:** RCMs plan, direct, and coordinate subordinate activities; analyze plans; identify imbalances; analyze alternatives; and balance programs. Cost center managers regulate consumption of work hours, supplies, equipment, and services within the basic production flight/work center and shift resources among tasks.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.27
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 12D; B—Understanding

### C12-FC-069
- **Prompt:** Who appoints resource advisors, and what do they do?
- **Answer:** Appointed in writing by the responsibility center manager. They monitor/help prepare resource estimates; help develop obligations and expense fund targets; monitor use versus projected consumption; and serve as primary POC on resource management matters for their responsibility center.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶12.27
