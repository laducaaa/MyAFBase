## Section 20A — Air Force Inspection System

### Core B—Understanding cards (SSgt and TSgt)

### C20-FC-001
- **Prompt:** What does AFIS provide commanders, and what publication purpose statement does AFH 1 cite for AFIS?
- **Answer:** A credible, independent assessment process to measure capabilities of assigned forces. DAFI 90-302 purpose includes enabling/strengthening mission effectiveness and efficiency; motivating/promoting military discipline; improving unit performance and management excellence; and identifying/reporting/analyzing issues interfering with readiness, economy, efficiency, discipline, effectiveness, compliance, performance, and surety.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20A; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-002
- **Prompt:** What role do supervisors play in the AFIS continual-evaluation process?
- **Answer:** Supervisors at all levels play an integral part through continual assessment of personnel, programs, and their areas of responsibility.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20A; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-003
- **Prompt:** What is The Inspector General’s (TIG) role regarding the DAF IG system?
- **Answer:** TIG serves as the functional and force management proponent for the DAF IG system; communicates directly with SecAF, CSAF, CSO, and other USAF/USSF command elements on IG matters; and provides guidance and oversight to AFIA and AFOSI.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20A; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-004
- **Prompt:** What is the IG mission according to AFH 1, and which programs support it?
- **Answer:** Independently assess and report on discipline, readiness, economy, efficiency, and overall readiness of the DAF to the SecAF through specified inquiries, investigations, and inspections. Supporting programs include AFIS; the Complaints Resolution Program; and portions of the Insider Threat and Antiterrorism Programs.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20A; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-005
- **Prompt:** Why is the IG role aligned as it is, and what is the IG’s primary charge?
- **Answer:** Aligned to remove perceived conflicts of interest, lack of independence, or apprehension regarding the chain of command. Primary charge: sustain a credible DAF IG system by ensuring responsive complaint resolutions, including investigations, characterized by objectivity, integrity, and impartiality.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20A; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-006
- **Prompt:** What do Wing, Delta, and equivalent IGs do for the commander?
- **Answer:** Execute the commander’s inspection program; validate and verify unit self-assessments; provide an independent assessment of unit effectiveness; inform of potential concern areas/trends; act as fact finder and honest broker in complaint resolution; educate/train on IG rights and responsibilities; and help prevent, detect, and correct FWA and mismanagement.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20A; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-007
- **Prompt:** What attributes and qualifications should Airmen/Guardians selected for IG duties possess?
- **Answer:** Highest professional standards and moral character; potential for future service and continued promotion; credibility for those seeking assistance; ability to attain/hold required security clearance; no civil conviction record except minor offenses; neither court-martial conviction, NJP, nor UIF in the OMPF; and good military bearing and appearance. NSI inspectors need prior nuclear experience or applicable MAJCOM system experience.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20A; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-008
- **Prompt:** What is the purpose of the Air Force Scheduling Process (Gatekeeper), and what authority do Gatekeepers have?
- **Answer:** Synchronize inspection efforts to maximize unit training days and inspection resource usage, build out-year schedules, and align non-AF inspection requirements; ensure independent/efficient inspection and that commander priorities take precedence over non-mission-essential activities. Authority to approve, disapprove, schedule, deconflict, and eliminate duplication between all inspection-type activities on behalf of their commanders.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20A; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-009
- **Prompt:** Why is a validated, accurate, trusted CCIP called the cornerstone of AFIS, and what is CCIP’s purpose?
- **Answer:** It should provide the right information at the right time to assess risk, identify improvement areas, determine root causes, and focus limited resources aligned with the Commander’s priorities/timeline. Purpose: improve readiness, efficiency, discipline, effectiveness, compliance, and surety at the Wing level.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20A; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-010
- **Prompt:** What is the purpose of a Nuclear Surety Inspection (NSI)?
- **Answer:** Evaluate a certified nuclear-capable unit’s ability to manage assigned nuclear resources and execute nuclear missions in a safe, secure, and reliable manner.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20A; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-011
- **Prompt:** What is the Unit Effectiveness Inspection (UEI), and what are the four Major Graded Areas?
- **Answer:** A multi-year external continual evaluation of Wing performance based on four MGAs conducted by MAJCOM IGs and DAFIA on wings and wing-equivalents. MGAs: managing resources, leading people, improving the unit, and executing the mission.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20A; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-012
- **Prompt:** What does the UEI evaluate regarding CCIP and the Wing?
- **Answer:** Evaluates CCIP for accuracy, adequacy, and relevance, while providing an independent assessment of the Wing’s resource management, leadership, process improvement efforts, and ability to execute the mission.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20A; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-013
- **Prompt:** What is a primary AFIS objective related to self-assessment, and what is USAP’s primary purpose?
- **Answer:** Foster a culture of critical self-assessment and continuous improvement and reduce reliance on external inspection teams. USAP purpose: accurately identify and report issues so Airmen feel they can safely report what is true, not what they believe commander/IG expects.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20A; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-014
- **Prompt:** What is MICT, and what are Self-Assessment Communicators (SACs)?
- **Answer:** MICT is a USAF program of record and a tool for managing USAPs. SACs are tools that improve compliance with published guidance and communicate risk/program health up and down the chain; they ask shop-level Airmen/Guardians to self-report compliance or non-compliance and may be tailored at lowest Wing administrative levels.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20A; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-015
- **Prompt:** How are validated inspection findings categorized, and what duplication should IGs avoid?
- **Answer:** Categorized as strengths, deficiencies, or recommended improvement areas. IGs should not duplicate deficiencies/observations already entered into IGEMS, MICT, or other self-assessment documentation/tracking by the inspected organization.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20A; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-016
- **Prompt:** Who may report systematic, programmatic, or procedural weaknesses to the IG according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Airmen and Guardians at all levels may report suspected systematic, programmatic, or procedural weaknesses, or seek further assurance that resources are used effectively and efficiently, by contacting the IG office.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20A; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-017
- **Prompt:** What special consideration do nuclear weapon systems and assigned units require under AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Special consideration because of political and military importance, destructive power, and potential consequences of a nuclear weapons incident or accident.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20A; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-018
- **Prompt:** What must an effective USAP depend on when Airmen cannot comply with directives?
- **Answer:** Reporting the status of compliance and, when they cannot comply, reporting what constraints prevent them from doing so.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20A; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-019
- **Prompt:** What inspection-report content must IGs ensure is documented besides categorized findings?
- **Answer:** Inspection activity findings including safety, surety, or issues of military discipline, with validated data categorized as strengths, deficiencies, or recommended improvement areas.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20A; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-020
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 describe who IGs are selected as, and from where?
- **Answer:** Selected for a DAF IG assignment as functional experts with broad contemporary experience from across the USAF.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20A; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-021
- **Prompt:** What does assessing CCIP components provide critical data about?
- **Answer:** Adequacy of policy, training, manpower and personnel, funds, equipment, and facilities.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20A; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-022
- **Prompt:** What risk area does the UEI help the wing commander understand?
- **Answer:** Areas of greatest risk from undetected non-compliance.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20A; B—Understanding

## Section 20B — Individual Accountability

### Core B—Understanding cards (SSgt and TSgt)

### C20-FC-023
- **Prompt:** With what does enforcing military standards begin according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Individual accountability. Some activities/behaviors, while arguably not illegal, are inconsistent with good order and discipline.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20B; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-024
- **Prompt:** What is the Air Force Complaints Resolution Program as a leadership tool?
- **Answer:** A leadership tool indicating where command involvement is needed to correct systematic, programmatic, or procedural weaknesses; resolving underlying causes may prevent reduced performance, accidents, poor quality work, or poor morale.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20B; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-025
- **Prompt:** What rights and responsibilities apply when submitting complaints under AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Right to present a complaint without fear of reprisal. Members should try lowest-level resolution first, but no person may restrict lawful communication to an IG or member of Congress. Responsibility to report FWA/gross mismanagement; violations; injustice; abuse of authority; inappropriate conduct; or misconduct through supervisory channels or the IG. Only the IG may investigate reprisal and restriction under the Military Whistleblower’s Protection Act.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20B; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-026
- **Prompt:** When will IG channels process a complaint that another grievance channel mishandled a matter?
- **Answer:** When complainants provide some relevant evidence the process was mishandled or handled prejudicially. Dissatisfaction/disagreement with outcome or findings alone is not sufficient to warrant an IG investigation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20B; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-027
- **Prompt:** What inherent authority/responsibility do commanders have while preserving expression rights?
- **Answer:** Authority and responsibility to take action to ensure the mission is performed, including lawful restrictions on dissident/protest activities; unauthorized material; off-limits establishments; illegal discrimination or activities detrimental to good order/discipline/mission; interfering with mission or law and order; or presenting a clear danger to loyalty, discipline, or morale.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20B; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-028
- **Prompt:** Whose responsibility is issuing public statements on official USAF matters, and what must members obtain before release?
- **Answer:** Cognizant unit or installation commanders and their public affairs representatives. Each member must obtain necessary review and clearance, starting with public affairs, before releasing any proposed statement, text, or imagery to the public, including digital products on unrestricted websites.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20B; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-029
- **Prompt:** What qualities should official USAF information presented to the public have?
- **Answer:** Accurate, prompt, and factual; confined to particular areas of expertise; avoid hypothetical/speculative nature; accurately reflect USAF policy; presented simply and honestly; comply with SecDef principles for public information.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20B; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-030
- **Prompt:** When may individual expressions of sincerely held beliefs be limited, and what must leaders balance?
- **Answer:** Limited if expressions would adversely impact military readiness, unit cohesion, good order, discipline, health and safety, or mission accomplishment. Leaders must balance free exercise protections (including their own expressions) with the prohibition against governmental establishment of religion, ensuring words/actions cannot reasonably be construed as officially endorsing, disapproving, or preferring any faith, belief, or absence of belief.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20B; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-031
- **Prompt:** What must Airmen do while a religious accommodation request is pending, and what standards apply if it is not approved?
- **Answer:** Continue to comply with directives, instructions, and lawful orders until approved. Non-approval must be based on facts presented; directly relate to compelling government interest (readiness, cohesion, good order, discipline, health, safety, or mission); and use the least restrictive means necessary.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20B; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-032
- **Prompt:** What political participation is prohibited for USAF members on RegAF status according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Partisan political fundraising, rallies, conventions (including speeches), campaign management, or debates—on one’s own behalf or another’s—whether in uniform or not, and regardless of appearance of official endorsement. Participation includes more than mere spectator attendance. Also prohibited: wearing uniform at political events/speeches/interviews/picket lines/rallies; using official authority to interfere with elections, solicit votes, or solicit political contributions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20B; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-033
- **Prompt:** What extremist/gang advocacy is prohibited, and what UCMJ article may apply to prohibited political activities listed in DoDD 1344.10 and DAFI 51-508?
- **Answer:** Must not actively advocate supremacist, extremist, or criminal gang doctrine/ideology/causes including illegal discrimination or force/violence/criminal activity or depriving civil rights. Members engaging in specific prohibited activities listed in DoDD 1344.10 and DAFI 51-508 are subject to prosecution under Article 92, UCMJ, plus other applicable UCMJ/Federal law.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20B; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-034
- **Prompt:** Within what timelines must allegations of DoDI 1325.06-prohibited activity be reported to Wing/Delta/servicing IG?
- **Answer:** No later than 30 calendar days after receiving the allegation (Wing/Delta/servicing IG). For Reserve Component officials, not later than 60 calendar days.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20B; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-035
- **Prompt:** What is USAF alcohol policy under AFI 1-1 as summarized in AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Prevent alcohol abuse and alcoholism among personnel and dependents; assist in resolving alcohol-related problems; ensure humane management and administrative disposition of those unable or unwilling to be restored to full, effective functioning. Exercise good judgment; obey drinking-age laws on/off duty; alcohol must not adversely affect duty performance/conduct or recall ability when on-call.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20B; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-036
- **Prompt:** What BAC standards and suspension does AFH 1 state for impaired driving on installations?
- **Answer:** BAC greater than .08 is considered impaired even without other evidence. BAC 0.05 but less than 0.08 may be presumed impaired and considered with other competent evidence. One-year driving privilege suspension for driving/physical control while under the influence at BAC 0.08% or greater.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20B; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-037
- **Prompt:** How does DAFMAN 44-197 (via AFH 1) describe drug abuse, and what does “wrongful” mean?
- **Answer:** Wrongful use, possession, distribution, or introduction onto a military installation/supervised property of a controlled substance, prescription medication, OTC medication, or intoxicating substance (other than alcohol). Wrongful means without legal justification/excuse, including use contrary to manufacturer/prescriber directions and use of intoxicating substances not intended for human ingestion. Also includes huffing and non-prescribed steroid use.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20B; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-038
- **Prompt:** Where are members with substance abuse problems encouraged to seek assistance, and what is ADAPT’s scope?
- **Answer:** Unit commander, first sergeant, substance abuse counselor, or military medical professional through ADAPT. ADAPT and drug demand reduction include prevention, education, treatment, and urinalysis testing. Patients diagnosed with a substance use disorder and entered into ADAPT are recommended for limited duty (not worldwide qualified). Members remain accountable for unacceptable behavior.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20B; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-039
- **Prompt:** When must unit commanders refer members for substance-use assessment (Commander’s Identification)?
- **Answer:** When substance use/misuse is suspected to be a contributing factor in any misconduct—examples include DUI, public intoxication, drunk and disorderly, spouse/child abuse and maltreatment, underage drinking, positive drug test, or when notified by medical personnel.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20B; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-040
- **Prompt:** Who is subject to USAF drug testing, and what is the caution about commander-directed testing?
- **Answer:** Military members are subject to testing regardless of grade, status, or position. Commander-directed testing should only be used as a last resort because results may not be used for UCMJ disciplinary action or to characterize an administrative discharge.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20B; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-041
- **Prompt:** What is an inspection under Military Rule of Evidence 313 in the drug-testing context?
- **Answer:** An examination conducted as an incident of command whose primary purpose is to determine and ensure security, military fitness, or good order and discipline of a unit/organization/installation; may use reasonable natural/technological aids; with or without notice. Positive results may support substance-use evaluation, UCMJ discipline, administrative discharge, and characterization considerations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20B; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-042
- **Prompt:** What is required for a probable cause search under MRE 315 for a urine specimen?
- **Answer:** Search and seizure authorization from the appropriate commander, military judge, military magistrate, or other competent search authority. Probable cause exists when there is a reasonable belief evidence of illegal drug use will be found in the member’s system. Results may support UCMJ discipline or administrative discharge.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20B; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-043
- **Prompt:** How does voluntary self-identification of a substance-use problem protect a member under AFH 1?
- **Answer:** For members not currently under investigation or pending action who seek assistance, commanders may not use voluntary self-identification against the member in a UCMJ action or when determining characterization of service in an administrative discharge action.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20B; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-044
- **Prompt:** Through which channels may complaints be submitted under the Complaints Resolution Program?
- **Answer:** In person, by phone, through electronic means, or in writing to supervisors, first sergeants, commanders, members of any level of the IG system, someone higher in the chain of command, or members of Congress.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20B; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-045
- **Prompt:** What publication pointer does AFH 1 give for IG complaint procedures?
- **Answer:** DAFI 90-301, Inspector General Complaints Resolution.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20B; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-046
- **Prompt:** What political rights/duties do USAF members retain as citizens according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Right and duty to vote and voice opinions concerning political matters, careful that personal opinions/activities are not directly or by implication represented as those of the USAF.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20B; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-047
- **Prompt:** What happens if a military member fails to comply with an order to provide a urine sample?
- **Answer:** Subject to punitive action under the UCMJ.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20B; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-048
- **Prompt:** May results of valid medical examinations be used to identify drug abusers under AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Yes. Results of examinations conducted for a valid medical purpose—including emergency treatment, periodic physicals, and other diagnostic/treatment examinations—may identify drug abusers and may be used as evidence for UCMJ discipline or administrative discharge.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20B; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-049
- **Prompt:** Why does AFH 1 say substance use/abuse is incompatible with USAF expectations?
- **Answer:** Incompatible with USAF core values, maintenance of high standards of behavior, performance, readiness, and the discipline necessary to accomplish the Air Force mission; can cause serious physical/mental health problems and jeopardize safety.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20B; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-050
- **Prompt:** What classification requirement applies to information revealed publicly, official or unofficial?
- **Answer:** Members must ensure information revealed is appropriate for release according to classification requirements in DoDM5200.01V1_DAFMAN16-1404V1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20B; B—Understanding

## Section 20C — Appropriate Working Relationships

### Core B—Understanding cards (SSgt and TSgt)

### C20-FC-051
- **Prompt:** When do personal relationships between USAF members become matters of official concern?
- **Answer:** When they adversely affect or have the reasonable potential to affect the USAF by eroding morale, good order, discipline, respect for authority, unit cohesion, or mission accomplishment.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-052
- **Prompt:** Why are professional relationships more critical in the military than in civilian organizations?
- **Answer:** The military mission requires absolute confidence in command and unhesitating adherence to orders that may result in inconvenience, hardships, and at times injury or death.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-053
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define unprofessional relationships?
- **Answer:** Relationships, on- or off-duty, that detract from the authority of superiors, or result in, or reasonably create the appearance of, favoritism, misuse of office or position, or abandonment of organizational goals for personal interests.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-054
- **Prompt:** Between which groups must unprofessional relationships be avoided?
- **Answer:** Between officers; between enlisted members; between officers and enlisted members; between military personnel and civilian employees or contractor personnel; and within and across the military branches.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-055
- **Prompt:** What is fraternization under Article 134, and who is primarily responsible?
- **Answer:** An unprofessional relationship between an officer and enlisted member prohibited by Article 134, UCMJ, putting the enlisted member on terms of military equality with the officer in a way that prejudices good order and discipline or brings discredit upon the Armed Forces. The officer is held primarily responsible and is the only member subject to discipline for fraternization under Article 134; an enlisted member in consensual fraternization is still in an unprofessional relationship and may face Article 92 discipline.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-056
- **Prompt:** What surrounding circumstances help determine whether contact constitutes fraternization?
- **Answer:** Whether the conduct compromised the chain of command; resulted in the appearance of partiality; or otherwise undermined good order, discipline, authority, or morale. The prohibition extends beyond organizational/chain lines and across services.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-057
- **Prompt:** What is the primary objective of the USAF Equal Opportunity Program?
- **Answer:** Eradicate unlawful discrimination and foster a positive human relations environment.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.18
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-058
- **Prompt:** What are commanders and supervisors charged to do once aware of inappropriate EO-related behaviors or allegations?
- **Answer:** Take immediate and appropriate actions to address them and are encouraged to consult with their local equal opportunity office before initiating action to resolve such concerns.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.18
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-059
- **Prompt:** What constitutes unlawful discrimination against military members, and when is it unacceptable?
- **Answer:** Any unlawful action that denies equal opportunities based on race, color, religion, national origin, or sex—including verbal, physical, non-verbal, and social media forms. Unacceptable on- or off-base, 24 hours a day.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.18
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-060
- **Prompt:** What bases does AFH 1 list for unlawful discrimination against DoD civilian employees?
- **Answer:** Race, color, religion, national origin, sex, age (40 or older), genetic information, physical or mental disability, or reprisal—regarding hiring/promotion, discharge, compensation/terms/conditions/privileges, or limiting/segregating/classifying employees/applicants.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.18
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-061
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define harassment in the EO context?
- **Answer:** Any behavior unwelcome or offensive to a reasonable person—oral, written, or physical—that creates an intimidating, hostile, or offensive environment, including electronic/social media/other communications and in person. Proper military/governmental-purpose activities (e.g., combat survival training) are not harassment.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-062
- **Prompt:** What six distinct forms of harassment does the Air Force EO Program cover?
- **Answer:** Discriminatory, sexual, bullying, hazing, retaliation, and reprisal.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-063
- **Prompt:** What is discriminatory harassment under AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Conduct that is unwelcome based on race, color, religion, sex, and national origin.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-064
- **Prompt:** What three conditions can make unwelcome sexual conduct sexual harassment under AFH 1?
- **Answer:** (1) Submission is made, explicitly or implicitly, a term/condition of job, pay, or career; (2) submission/rejection is used as a basis for career/employment decisions; or (3) conduct unreasonably interferes with work performance or creates an intimidating/hostile/offensive environment so severe or pervasive that a reasonable person would perceive it as hostile or offensive.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-065
- **Prompt:** Is concrete psychological harm required for behavior to constitute sexual harassment under AFH 1?
- **Answer:** No. Behavior is sufficient if so severe or pervasive that a reasonable person would perceive, and the complainant does perceive, the environment as hostile or offensive.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-066
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 distinguish bullying from properly directed command activities?
- **Answer:** Bullying: aggression with nexus to military service intending physical/psychological harm without proper military/governmental purpose; consent does not excuse; prohibited including off-duty/unofficial settings. Does not include properly directed command/organizational activities serving a proper purpose or requisite training such as command-authorized PT.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-067
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define hazing’s purpose element, and what does hazing not include?
- **Answer:** Physically/psychologically injure or create risk of injury for initiation into, admission into, affiliation with, change in status/position within, or as a condition for continued membership in any military or DoD civilian organization—without proper military/governmental purpose but with nexus to military service. Does not include properly directed command activities or requisite training such as administrative corrective measures, EMI, or command-authorized PT.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-068
- **Prompt:** What is retaliation under AFH 1, and which UCMJ article prohibits it?
- **Answer:** Taking/threatening adverse personnel action, or wrongfully withholding/threatening to withhold favorable personnel action, because a person reported a criminal offense or made/planned a protected communication. Prohibited by Article 132, UCMJ.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-069
- **Prompt:** Who may file a military EO complaint, and may third parties file on someone’s behalf?
- **Answer:** Only military personnel, their family members, and retirees—and the individual must be the subject of the alleged unlawful discrimination or sexual harassment. Third parties (including commanders, supervisors, or co-workers) may not file on behalf of another; EO refers them to the chain of command.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-070
- **Prompt:** What are key features of the military informal EO complaint process?
- **Answer:** Attempt resolution at the lowest possible level via addressing the alleged offender, coworker intervention, ADR, or chain of command. No time limit for filing informal complaints and no commander-approval requirement before accepting them.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-071
- **Prompt:** What is the filing window for military formal EO complaints, and what evidence standard does clarification use?
- **Answer:** Filed within 60 calendar days of the alleged offense (installation commander may waive for good cause). Clarification determines whether a preponderance of evidence demonstrates unlawful discrimination occurred.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-072
- **Prompt:** Where are formal complaints of sexual harassment referred under the military EO process?
- **Answer:** Referred to security forces for an independent investigation pursuant to procedures in 10 USC § 1561.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-073
- **Prompt:** Who may file civilian EO complaints, and what is the informal process completion timeline?
- **Answer:** Only USAF employees, former employees, and applicants for employment. EO must complete informal process within 30 calendar days unless complainant grants extension not to exceed 60 additional calendar days.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-074
- **Prompt:** What are the key civilian formal EO complaint timing rules in AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Initial contact within 45 calendar days of the alleged discriminatory matter (or personnel-action effective date/awareness). Formal complaint filed with EO director/designee within 15 calendar days of notice of right to file. Investigation completion target within 180 calendar days from filing date for accept/dismiss/partial-dismiss determinations allowing investigation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-075
- **Prompt:** What is the installation SARC’s role according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Single point of contact at an installation or geographic area reporting to the installation commander; oversees sexual assault awareness/prevention/response training; coordinates medical treatment including emergency care; tracks services from initial report through final disposition and resolution.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-076
- **Prompt:** What are the Victims’ Counsel’s three main roles?
- **Answer:** Advocate (zealous advocacy protecting rights); Advise (legal advice on the investigatory and military justice system); Empower (removing barriers to full participation in the military justice process). Primary responsibility is to their client.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-077
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define sexual assault and consent?
- **Answer:** Sexual assault: intentional sexual contact characterized by force, threats, intimidation, abuse of authority, or when the victim does not/cannot consent; encompasses offenses under Articles 120, 120b, and 80 UCMJ. Consent: words or overt acts indicating freely given agreement by a competent person; lack of consent expressed means no consent; force-induced submission is not consent; relationship/dress is not consent; no consent if sleeping or incapacitated (age, alcohol/drugs, mental incapacity).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-078
- **Prompt:** What must a supervisor do upon receiving a sexual assault report about a subordinate in the supervisory chain?
- **Answer:** Any military member or civilian employee (other than those authorized for confidential communications or otherwise exempted) who receives such a report will report the matter to the commander, the SARC, and AFOSI.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-079
- **Prompt:** Who may receive restricted reports of sexual assault, and may a restricted report be converted?
- **Answer:** Only SARCs, SAPR VAs, VVAs, and healthcare personnel. A victim may convert a restricted report to unrestricted at any time. Chaplains, legal assistance attorneys, and VC will not accept a restricted report but will facilitate contact with SARC/SAPR VA/VVA if the victim wishes to file one.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.23
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-080
- **Prompt:** What is an unrestricted report, and can it later become restricted?
- **Answer:** Any report made by the victim through normal reporting channels (chain of command, law enforcement, AFOSI/other criminal investigative services). Victim will not be permitted to change from unrestricted to restricted. The recipient notifies the SARC.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.23
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-081
- **Prompt:** What immediate steps must commanders take when notified of a sexual assault via unrestricted report?
- **Answer:** Take immediate steps to ensure the victim’s physical safety, emotional security, and medical treatment needs are met, and that AFOSI or appropriate criminal investigative agency and SARC are notified.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.23
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-082
- **Prompt:** Why is collateral misconduct by a sexual assault victim a significant reporting barrier, and who initially disposes of it?
- **Answer:** Fear of punishment for collateral misconduct (e.g., underage drinking, adultery, drug abuse, fraternization). Authority to dispose of collateral misconduct in sexual assault cases is initially reserved to the special court-martial convening authority under DAFI 51-201.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.23
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-083
- **Prompt:** What is the Safe-to-Report Policy threshold, and who decides minor vs non-minor?
- **Answer:** Whether alleged collateral misconduct is “minor” or “non-minor.” Authority withheld to the Initial Disposition Authority (IDA). If minor, Safe-to-Report applies and the victim will not be disciplined; if non-minor, it does not apply and the victim could face discipline.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.23
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-084
- **Prompt:** What publication establishes responsibilities for maintaining professional relationships according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** AFI 36-2909, Air Force Professional Relationships and Conduct.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-085
- **Prompt:** What 24-hour hotline number does AFH 1 print for sexual harassment/unlawful discrimination reporting?
- **Answer:** 1-888-231-4058 (Air Force Sexual Harassment/Unlawful Discrimination Hotline)—verify for duty use as a 2025 snapshot.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.18
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-086
- **Prompt:** What protected communication elements appear in AFH 1’s retaliation discussion?
- **Answer:** Communication to a Member of Congress or an IG, or to certain other authorities disclosing evidence of a violation of law/regulation (including sexual harassment and unlawful discrimination), or relating to gross mismanagement, FWA, or substantial danger to public health and safety.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20C; B—Understanding

### TSgt C—Applying overlay cards (20C; no SSgt tag)

### C20-FC-087
- **Prompt:** A TSgt notices a supervisor frequently giving the same subordinate preferential schedule and awards while socializing exclusively with that Airman off duty in ways peers say show favoritism. Applying AFH 1, how should you classify this concern?
- **Answer:** As a potential unprofessional relationship: relationships that detract from superior authority or result in/create the appearance of favoritism, misuse of position, or abandoning organizational goals for personal interests—matters of official concern when they erode morale, good order, discipline, respect for authority, unit cohesion, or mission.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.17
- **Tags:** TSgt; 20C; C—Applying

### C20-FC-088
- **Prompt:** An officer and enlisted member form a personal relationship on terms of military equality that peers say compromises the chain of command. Applying AFH 1 fraternization rules, who is primarily responsible for fraternization under Article 134, and what still applies to the enlisted member?
- **Answer:** The officer is primarily responsible and is the only member subject to disciplinary action for fraternization under Article 134; the enlisted member in consensual fraternization is still engaged in an unprofessional relationship and may be subject to discipline under Article 92.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.17
- **Tags:** TSgt; 20C; C—Applying

### C20-FC-089
- **Prompt:** A TSgt becomes aware of repeated unwelcome race-based put-downs creating a hostile climate. Applying AFH 1 EO/harassment guidance, what supervisory response does AFH 1 charge?
- **Answer:** Take immediate and appropriate action to address inappropriate behaviors/allegations once aware, and consult the local EO office before initiating action to resolve such concerns. Discriminatory harassment is unwelcome conduct based on race, color, religion, sex, and national origin.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶¶20.18–20.19
- **Tags:** TSgt; 20C; C—Applying

### C20-FC-090
- **Prompt:** An Airman wants to try resolving alleged unlawful discrimination at the lowest level without starting a formal EO case. Applying AFH 1 military informal EO process, what options and timing rules apply?
- **Answer:** Orally address/write the alleged offender, request coworker intervention, use ADR, or use the chain of command. There is no time limit for informal complaints and no commander-approval requirement before accepting them.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.20
- **Tags:** TSgt; 20C; C—Applying

### C20-FC-091
- **Prompt:** A subordinate in your supervisory chain privately discloses a sexual assault. Applying AFH 1, what reporting action must you take?
- **Answer:** Report the matter to the commander, the SARC, and AFOSI (unless you are among those authorized to receive confidential communications or otherwise exempted by law/regulation/policy).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.22
- **Tags:** TSgt; 20C; C—Applying

### C20-FC-092
- **Prompt:** An eligible victim wants medical care and support without triggering an investigation. Applying AFH 1, which reporting option fits, who may receive it, and can it later change?
- **Answer:** Restricted reporting to SARC, SAPR VA, VVA, or healthcare personnel only; intended to remove barriers to care/support and increase victim control. The victim may convert restricted to unrestricted at any time; unrestricted cannot later become restricted.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.23
- **Tags:** TSgt; 20C; C—Applying

## Section 20D — Addressing Misconduct

### Core B—Understanding cards (SSgt and TSgt)

### C20-FC-093
- **Prompt:** What are administrative counseling, admonitions, and reprimands intended to do?
- **Answer:** Improve, correct, and instruct subordinates who depart from standards of performance, conduct, bearing, and integrity on- or off-duty, and whose actions degrade the individual and the unit mission.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.24
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20D; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-094
- **Prompt:** What is a Personnel Information File (PIF), and what form is required if one is established?
- **Answer:** A file commanders/supervisors keep for personnel management functions authorized by DAFI 36-2608. If established, AF Form 10A is required. Kept current and secured in a locked area/container; member may access anytime per AFI 33-332.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.24
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20D; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-095
- **Prompt:** What mandatory documents go in a UIF according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Suspended or unsuspended Article 15 punishment of more than one month (31 days or more); court-martial conviction; civilian conviction where penalty/actions equivalent to guilty finding resulted in confinement of one year or more or could have resulted in confinement for more than one year or death; and control roster actions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.24
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20D; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-096
- **Prompt:** How long does a member have to acknowledge/respond before a commander finalizes UIF filing, and when must commanders review UIFs?
- **Answer:** Three duty days to acknowledge and provide pertinent information. Commanders review within 90 days of assuming/being appointed to command; when considered for promotion, reenlistment, PCS/assignment, reclassification/retraining; and annually with SJA assistance.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.24
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20D; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-097
- **Prompt:** What is a control roster, what form places someone on it, and what are key limits?
- **Answer:** A rehabilitative observation period for substandard performance or failure to meet standards. DAF Form 1058 places the member on notice; three duty days to respond; mandatory UIF entry; initially six months (may remove early); not ordinarily for a single isolated breach unlikely to repeat; not a substitute for more appropriate administrative/judicial/nonjudicial action.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.24
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20D; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-098
- **Prompt:** What evidence standard applies to administrative corrective actions, and how should LOC/LOA/LOR be used?
- **Answer:** Preponderance of the evidence—the greater weight of credible evidence. Use as part of a graduated pattern of discipline for repeated departures; decide based primarily on nature/seriousness and previous disciplinary record. Written actions are more meaningful and may be used in subsequent proceedings.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.25
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20D; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-099
- **Prompt:** How do LOC, LOA, and LOR differ in severity and filing cues?
- **Answer:** LOC (or DAF Form 174) helps develop good judgment/responsibility/problem-solving. LOA is more severe than counseling but not serious enough for reprimand; for officers, if not in UIF must be in PIF. LOR is more severe than LOC/LOA (stronger official censure); may be filed in UIF for enlisted; mandatory UIF filing for officers.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.25
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20D; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-100
- **Prompt:** What are example reasons for administrative demotion, and what should demotion not replace?
- **Answer:** Examples: failing to fulfill Airmen/NCO/SNCO responsibilities, failure to keep fit, student status termination. Should not replace action under the UCMJ; when appropriate give opportunity to overcome deficiencies first (AFI 36-2502).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.26
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20D; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-101
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 distinguish required, voluntary, and involuntary separation?
- **Answer:** Required: continue in another military status (e.g., AFR/ANG) or accept commission/warrant appointment. Voluntary: early separation for convenience of government if criteria met (officer training, pregnancy, conscientious objection, hardship, early release for school). Involuntary: convenience-of-government reasons (physical conditions, parental/military-duty inability, insufficient retainability), defective enlistment, or for cause (unsatisfactory performance, substance abuse, misconduct, national security).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.27
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20D; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-102
- **Prompt:** How do honorable, general, and UOTHC characterizations differ under AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Honorable: service generally met standards, or so meritorious other characterization inappropriate; ETS and convenience-of-government separations characterized honorable. General: honest/faithful but significant negative aspects outweigh positives. UOTHC: pattern or act/omission significantly departing from expected conduct; only if offered a discharge board or unconditionally requested in lieu of court-martial.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.28
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20D; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-103
- **Prompt:** What may the Discharge Review Board review, within what time, and is personal appearance a right?
- **Answer:** Former members may request review of discharge (except BCD, DD, or dismissal by GCM) within 15 years; consider changing characterization, reason, and RE code when applicable based on propriety/equity. Personal appearance is a statutory right (in person or VTC).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.29
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20D; B—Understanding

### C20-FC-104
- **Prompt:** What is the AFBCMR, must applicants exhaust other remedies, and is personal appearance a statutory right?
- **Answer:** Highest level of administrative review for correcting military records; recommending body forwarding favorable recommendations to SecAF/designee. Applicants must exhaust other reasonably available administrative avenues or DD Form 149 is returned without action. Personal appearance is not a statutory right—granted at board discretion if it materially adds understanding.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.30
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 20D; B—Understanding

### TSgt C—Applying overlay cards (20D; no SSgt tag)

### C20-FC-105
- **Prompt:** An Airman’s first minor lateness with otherwise solid performance occurs. Applying AFH 1 corrective-action concepts, which tool emphasis fits best as a starting point?
- **Answer:** Counseling (verbal or written LOC/DAF Form 174) to improve, correct, and instruct—used as part of a graduated pattern; written documentation is more meaningful for later proceedings. Decision based primarily on nature/seriousness and prior record.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶¶20.24–20.25
- **Tags:** TSgt; 20D; C—Applying

### C20-FC-106
- **Prompt:** Repeated departures continue after counseling; the infraction is more serious than counseling supports but not yet reprimand-level in your judgment. Applying AFH 1, which letter documents that middle severity?
- **Answer:** Letter of Admonishment—documents an infraction warranting more severe action than counseling but not serious enough for a reprimand.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.25
- **Tags:** TSgt; 20D; C—Applying

### C20-FC-107
- **Prompt:** A commander considers control roster after one isolated, unlikely-to-repeat standard breach with no prior history. Applying AFH 1, is control roster ordinarily appropriate?
- **Answer:** No. A single incident of substandard performance or isolated breach not likely to be repeated is not ordinarily a basis for control roster action. Control roster is rehabilitative observation after considering prior incidents/failures/counseling; not a substitute for more appropriate action.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.24
- **Tags:** TSgt; 20D; C—Applying

### C20-FC-108
- **Prompt:** You are preparing paperwork and need the administrative evidence standard. Applying AFH 1, what standard must the greater weight of credible evidence meet?
- **Answer:** Preponderance of the evidence—the greater weight of credible evidence that misconduct occurred. Without it, administrative action may be legally unsupportable and could be set aside.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.25
- **Tags:** TSgt; 20D; C—Applying

### C20-FC-109
- **Prompt:** A member’s service was honest and faithful overall, but significant negative conduct outweighs the positives in the current enlistment. Applying AFH 1 characterizations, which characterization fits?
- **Answer:** General (under honorable conditions).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶20.28
- **Tags:** TSgt; 20D; C—Applying

### C20-FC-110
- **Prompt:** A former member wants characterization/reason/RE-code review of an administrative discharge within 10 years and wants to appear. Applying AFH 1, which board applies, and is appearance a right?
- **Answer:** Air Force Discharge Review Board (within 15 years; not for BCD/DD/GCM dismissal). Personal appearance is a statutory right. (AFBCMR is highest records-correction level and requires exhausting other remedies; appearance there is not a statutory right.)
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶¶20.29–20.30
- **Tags:** TSgt; 20D; C—Applying
