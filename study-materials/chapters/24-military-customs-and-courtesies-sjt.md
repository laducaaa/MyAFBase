# Chapter 24 Situational Judgment Practice

> **Status:** Draft and provisional for 27E5/27E6. Chapter 24 is shared core in the historical 26E5/26E6 catalog scopes.
>
> These scenarios are wholly original. They are not actual, recalled, reconstructed, or predicted PFE items. The official Air Force uses subject-matter experts and behavioral scientists to establish operational SJT scoring keys. Chapter 24 describes customs, flag respect, individual courtesies, ceremonies, drill, and heritage facts, but those facts alone do not establish an official behavioral scoring key. All most-effective and least-effective keys below are MyPromotion educational judgments grounded in Chapter 1 professionalism, core values, and ethical reasoning, with Chapter 24 material supplying context only. They are not official scoring keys or substitutes for current command, protocol, honor-guard, or ceremony procedures.
>
> **Rank / ADTC use:** Metadata reflects consolidated ADTC levels (24A/24B/24D/24E B for both; 24C/24F B for SSgt and C for TSgt). Scenarios that emphasize TSgt application of respect or heritage-preservation principles are tagged accordingly; SSgt study still uses the shared behavioral practice set.
>
> **Two-track source rule:** For exam-source study, preserve the facts in the AFH edition assigned by the controlling catalog. For duty use, separately verify current protocol, flag, drill, and ceremony rules; duty-current information does not overwrite a catalog-controlled answer.

## How to use this set

Each scenario produces two items:

1. Select the **most effective** response.
2. Select the **least effective** response.

Read every option before choosing. Evaluate whether the response:

- shows respect for symbols, people, and ceremonies;
- protects dignity, safety, and mission;
- uses supervisory influence without humiliation or showmanship;
- stays within stated role and seeks protocol/honor-guard help when needed;
- models customs and courtesies without inventing local “always” rules beyond AFH 1; and
- reflects integrity, respect, loyalty, and mission stewardship.

Chapter 24 supplies customs-and-courtesies context; it does not prescribe the behavioral sequence in every response. When a rationale depends on general professional judgment—such as honesty, courage, accountability, loyalty, respect, mission focus, or teamwork—it is identified as an application of the cited foundational Chapter 1 paragraphs.

Ten scenarios produce 20 response selections, mirroring the historical public structure announced for the [22E5/22E6 cycles in December 2021](https://www.afpc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/2874484/waps-adjusts-for-22e6-22e5-testing-cycles/). The separate [public SJT informational guide](https://www.studyguides.af.mil/Portals/15/documents/Notices/Situational-Judgment-Test-Informational-Guide.pdf) explains the four-option most/least format. The 2027 structure is unconfirmed. This set covers only selected Chapter 24 concepts and is not a full-spectrum SJT simulation.

## C24-SJT-001 — Anthem respect on the installation

You are outdoors in uniform near the flagstaff when the national anthem begins. A coworker keeps walking and talking, saying “it’s just music.” AFH 1 says Airmen in uniform outdoors render the military salute from the first note to the last note.

### Response options

A. Keep walking and talking to avoid looking “too military.”  
B. Stop, face the flag, and render the salute from the first note to the last note; quietly model the standard for others.  
C. Yell at the coworker during the anthem to embarrass them publicly.  
D. Sit on the curb and scroll your phone until the music ends.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 24.5 supplies outdoor anthem-salute context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it performs the required respect and models the standard without spectacle. Chapter 1 respect and integrity provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A openly ignores a core courtesy and signals that standards are optional. Chapter 1 accountability and respect provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** Continues disrespect during the anthem.
- **B — Most effective:** Executes the outdoor uniform salute correctly and models professionalism.
- **C — Ineffective:** Public humiliation during the anthem compounds the problem.
- **D — Ineffective:** Sitting/phone use fails the respect requirement.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶24.5 (anthem outdoors context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** National anthem; respect; modeling standards  
**ADTC:** 24A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of anthem-scenario realism

- **C — Least effective:** Uses the flag as a cover/drop cloth.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶24.15 (care/respect context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** Applies the wrong courtesy and adds disrespect.
- **B — Most effective:** Applies the exception and coaches correctly.
- **C — Least effective:** Ignores the senior official and the team’s bearing.
- **D — Ineffective:** Invents continuous indoor saluting contrary to AFH 1.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶24.18 (position of honor / room attention context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)

- **B — Most effective:** Applies reveille requirement and rank-recognition distinction correctly.
- **C — Ineffective:** Flag dipping is not a substitute salute rule.
- **D — Ineffective:** Disrespectful and irrelevant.
