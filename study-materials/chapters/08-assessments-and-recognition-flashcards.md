## Section 8A — Airman Comprehensive Assessment

### Core B—Understanding cards (SSgt and TSgt)

### C08-FC-001
- **Prompt:** What is the performance feedback assessment, and what does it discuss?
- **Answer:** A formal two-way communication between a rater and ratee to discuss standards, responsibilities, expectations, and goals. Raters document the session to assess or discuss objectives, standards, behavior, and performance with the ratee.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8A; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-002
- **Prompt:** During feedback sessions, where must raters refer ratees for Air Force benefits information?
- **Answer:** MyAirForceBenefits website (https://www.myairforcebenefits.us.af.mil).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8A; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-003
- **Prompt:** What happens to the completed performance feedback assessment worksheet after the session?
- **Answer:** Raters give the original, completed, and signed worksheet to the ratee and maintain copies of all completed performance feedback assessments and all signed performance feedback assessment notices, or appropriate statements (RegAF only).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8A; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-004
- **Prompt:** What are unit commanders responsible for regarding performance feedback assessments?
- **Answer:** Developing a tracking mechanism for performance feedback assessments and ensuring they are conducted properly.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8A; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-005
- **Prompt:** Who monitors that performance feedback assessments are conducted as required between the rater and forced distributor/HLR?
- **Answer:** Raters’ raters/reviewing officials of evaluations between the rater and the forced distributor/higher level reviewers (HLR) will monitor personnel to ensure performance feedback assessments are conducted, as required.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8A; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-006
- **Prompt:** Who conducts performance feedback when a lower-level rater is not available due to unusual circumstances?
- **Answer:** The raters’ rater will conduct performance feedback assessment sessions in place of the rater when the lower-level rater is unavailable due to unusual circumstances or when officially assuming the subordinate rater’s responsibilities.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8A; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-007
- **Prompt:** What must ratees do if a required or requested performance feedback assessment does not take place?
- **Answer:** Notify the rater and, if necessary, the raters’ rater. Ratees are also responsible for knowing when their sessions are due.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8A; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-008
- **Prompt:** For whom are performance feedback assessments mandatory?
- **Answer:** Officers up through the grade of Colonel, and all enlisted RegAF and Air Reserve Component personnel.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8A; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-009
- **Prompt:** When is a performance feedback assessment not required for students, and what may serve in-lieu of mid-term feedback?
- **Answer:** Not required for student officers receiving DAF Form 475, Education/Training Report, or enlisted personnel in initial or advanced skills training (may be given at school leadership discretion). For non-rated initial or advanced skills training students, documented academic progress reports such as AETC Form 156, Student Training Report, serve in-lieu of the mandatory mid-term performance feedback assessment.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8A; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-010
- **Prompt:** How is the mid-term performance feedback assessment treated relative to the performance evaluation?
- **Answer:** It is a mandatory supporting document to be routed with the performance evaluation but will not be made a matter of official record.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8A; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-011
- **Prompt:** How should raters conduct performance feedback assessment sessions under AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Impartially with honest, realistic feedback (may include video conferencing). Prefer a private, face-to-face session; telephone only in unusual circumstances where face-to-face is impractical. Feedback must be specific—positive comments reinforce behavior; negative comments focus attention where improvement is needed—and clarify what must be done before the evaluation is due.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8A; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-012
- **Prompt:** Which sections of the ACA worksheet does the ratee complete or review before the session?
- **Answer:** The ratee completes Section III on their own and reviews Section IX (AB thru Technical Sergeant) or Section VIII (Master Sergeant thru Chief Master Sergeant) prior to the feedback session. Areas following Section III are completed by the rater.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8A; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-013
- **Prompt:** Which three Airman Comprehensive Assessment worksheets does AFH 1 designate by ratee grade?
- **Answer:** AF Form 724 (2Lt thru Col); AF Form 931 (AB thru TSgt); AF Form 932 (MSgt thru CMSgt).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8A; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-014
- **Prompt:** How is AF Form 724-A used for officers versus SNCOs?
- **Answer:** Raters are required to use AF Form 724-A, Airman Comprehensive Assessment Addendum, for officers. It is optional for raters to use AF Form 724-A for SNCOs as an informal guiding document to supplement performance feedback.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8A; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-015
- **Prompt:** When does the MPF typically provide ACA notices, and what if a notice is not received?
- **Answer:** In most cases, within 30 days of when supervision begins and again halfway between supervision begin and the projected performance report close-out date. Failure to receive a notice does not justify failing to hold a required session. The Air National Guard does not currently have an automated process and may use an alternate form of communication.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8A; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-016
- **Prompt:** Within what timeframe must initial ACA sessions be conducted, and when are mid-term and end-of-period sessions due?
- **Answer:** Initial: within the first 60 days of when supervision begins (only initial until CRO). Mid-term: midway between supervision begin and next evaluation projected close-out. End-of-reporting period: within 60 days after the evaluation has been accomplished. Ratees may request feedback if 60 days have passed since the last session.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8A; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-017
- **Prompt:** What unique ACA feedback requirement applies to Chief Master Sergeants and colonels?
- **Answer:** The initial performance feedback assessment is the only feedback required.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8A; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-018
- **Prompt:** If an annual evaluation is due and supervision is less than 150 days, when does the rater conduct ACA feedback?
- **Answer:** Approximately 60 days before the projected evaluation close-out date.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8A; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-019
- **Prompt:** For AB, Amn, or A1C with less than 20 months TAFMS, and for Lt–Capt CRO feedback, what special rules apply?
- **Answer:** After initial feedback, mid-term ACA sessions are conducted every 180 days until the rater writes a performance report or a CRO occurs. For Lieutenant through Captain only, if an evaluation is due to a CRO, the new rater will do initial feedback; documentation is not required though the ACA worksheet may be used.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8A; B—Understanding

### TSgt C—Applying overlay cards (8A; no SSgt tag)

### C08-FC-020
- **Prompt:** A TSgt ratee’s rater deployed unexpectedly before a required mid-term feedback. Applying AFH 1, who should conduct the session?
- **Answer:** The raters’ rater conducts the performance feedback assessment in place of the rater when the lower-level rater is unavailable due to unusual circumstances or when officially assuming the subordinate rater’s responsibilities. The mid-term obligation remains; the primary rater’s absence does not cancel it.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.1
- **Tags:** TSgt; 8A; C—Applying

### C08-FC-021
- **Prompt:** You supervise a ratee who says mid-term feedback can wait because no MPF notice arrived. Applying AFH 1, what is the correct action?
- **Answer:** Conduct the required mid-term anyway. The notice reminds raters a session is due, but failure to receive a notice does not justify failing to hold a required session. Use supervision-begin and projected close-out dates to schedule midway feedback.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.4
- **Tags:** TSgt; 8A; C—Applying

### C08-FC-022
- **Prompt:** A ratee reports that requested feedback never occurred and more than 60 days have passed since the last session. Applying AFH 1, what should you ensure happens?
- **Answer:** Ratees may request feedback when 60 days have passed since the last session. If a required or requested session does not take place, the ratee notifies the rater and, if necessary, the raters’ rater. Ensure the session is scheduled and conducted rather than deferred indefinitely.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶¶8.1, 8.4
- **Tags:** TSgt; 8A; C—Applying

### C08-FC-023
- **Prompt:** You are preparing ACA documentation for an AB–TSgt ratee versus an MSgt ratee. Applying AFH 1, which forms and pre-session reviews apply?
- **Answer:** Use AF Form 931 for AB thru TSgt and AF Form 932 for MSgt thru CMSgt. The ratee completes Section III and reviews Section IX (AB thru TSgt) or Section VIII (MSgt thru CMSgt) before the session; the rater completes areas following Section III.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.3
- **Tags:** TSgt; 8A; C—Applying

### C08-FC-024
- **Prompt:** A non-rated initial skills training student is due a performance evaluation. Applying AFH 1, what may serve in-lieu of mandatory mid-term ACA feedback?
- **Answer:** Documented academic progress reports, such as the AETC Form 156, Student Training Report, serve in-lieu of the mandatory mid-term performance feedback assessment. When an ACA mid-term is used, it is a mandatory supporting document routed with the evaluation but is not made a matter of official record.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.1
- **Tags:** TSgt; 8A; C—Applying

### C08-FC-025
- **Prompt:** You must deliver ACA feedback covering strengths and deficiencies before evaluation close-out. Applying AFH 1 session-conduct guidance, what standards shape your delivery?
- **Answer:** Provide impartial, honest, realistic feedback in a private face-to-face session (video conferencing permitted; telephone only when face-to-face is impractical). Make comments specific so positive feedback reinforces behavior and negative feedback focuses improvement, and state what must be done before the evaluation is due.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.2
- **Tags:** TSgt; 8A; C—Applying


## Section 8B — Performance Evaluations

### Core B—Understanding cards (SSgt and TSgt)

### C08-FC-026
- **Prompt:** What is the performance evaluation system designed to provide?
- **Answer:** A reliable, long-term, cumulative record of performance and potential. Key aspects center on how well the individual does the job; supervisors help subordinates understand strengths and weaknesses and how their efforts contribute to the mission.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8B; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-027
- **Prompt:** How must evaluations be handled regarding access and classified information?
- **Answer:** Evaluations are “Controlled Unclassified Information” forms and must be marked, protected, and accessed accordingly; the custodial office determines whether a requestor’s official duties require access. Do not enter classified information in any section (including attachments, referral documents, and endorsements); use “Data Masked” where an entry would release classified information, and for classified organization/location enter “Data Masked” for organization nomenclature and nothing more. Specific completion instructions are in AFI 36-2406.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8B; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-028
- **Prompt:** What four objectives should guide use of the officer and enlisted evaluation systems?
- **Answer:** Communicate performance standards/expectations and provide meaningful feedback; provide a reliable long-term cumulative record of performance and promotion potential based on that performance; provide sound information for talent management decisions; and document in the permanent record any substantiated allegation of a sex-related offense against an Airman, regardless of grade, resulting in conviction by court-martial, non-judicial punishment, or other adverse administrative action.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8B; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-029
- **Prompt:** Which grade determines the appropriate performance report form?
- **Answer:** The ratee’s grade or projected grade on the static close-out date.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8B; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-030
- **Prompt:** Name the myEval performance briefs and their offline ETP counterparts listed in AFH 1.
- **Answer:** Officer ALQ Evaluation in myEval – Officer Performance Brief; Enlisted ALQ Evaluation in myEval – Enlisted Performance Brief. Offline: AF Form 715, Officer Performance Brief (ETP required); AF Form 716, Enlisted Performance Brief (ETP required).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8B; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-031
- **Prompt:** Which evaluation-related forms does AFH 1 list in addition to the ACA worksheets?
- **Answer:** DAF Form 77 (LOE); DAF Form 78 (GO PRF); DAF Form 475 (Education/Training Report); DAF Form 709 (Promotion Recommendation); AF Forms 715/716 (offline briefs, ETP); AF Forms 724/724-A/931/932 (ACA); DAF Form 948 (Application for Correction/Removal of Evaluation Reports).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8B; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-032
- **Prompt:** What training and sex-related offense record-review duties do unit commanders have for evaluations?
- **Answer:** Ensure supervisors are properly trained and educated on writing performance evaluations. Conduct a record review of all personnel assigned to and/or transferred into the command for knowledge of sex-related offense history resulting in court-martial conviction, NJP, or other adverse administrative action—by the immediate commander at the lowest unit level; these responsibilities will not be delegated.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8B; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-033
- **Prompt:** What must raters, reviewers, Senior Raters, and HLRs consider when preparing a performance evaluation?
- **Answer:** Contents of Unfavorable Information Files or Personal Information Files. They must assess what the ratee did, how well he or she did it, and potential based on that performance throughout the rating period.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8B; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-034
- **Prompt:** What does a ratee’s signature on an evaluation mean, and what if the ratee disagrees with content?
- **Answer:** Signing acknowledges receipt and certifies review for administrative errors—it is not concurrence with content. Raise typos, spelling errors, and inaccurate data before the evaluation becomes a matter of record. Disagreement with comments and/or ratings may be appealed after the evaluation becomes a matter of record.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8B; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-035
- **Prompt:** What two elements must a performance statement include?
- **Answer:** A standalone sentence that includes (1) the behavior or action taken by an Airman and (2) the impact, results, or outcome of that behavior or action. Include at least one performance statement in each section of the evaluation being accomplished.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8B; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-036
- **Prompt:** Why must misconduct that deviates from USAF core values be documented on evaluations?
- **Answer:** Failure to document such misconduct is a disservice to all Airmen who serve with honor and distinction. Fair and accurate evaluations help ensure the best qualified members are identified for positions of higher responsibility.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8B; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-037
- **Prompt:** What standard applies to comments about misconduct in performance evaluations?
- **Answer:** Comments must be specific, outlining the event and any corrective action taken. Vague phrases such as “conduct unbecoming…” or “an error in judgment led to an off-duty incident…” are too vague. Valid examples name the misconduct and the action taken (e.g., Article 15 or Letter of Reprimand).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8B; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-038
- **Prompt:** What factors may need consideration when assessing misconduct for an accurate evaluation?
- **Answer:** Impact on the USAF as an institution; impact on and relationship to the ratee’s duties; impact on the mission; grade, assignment, and experience of the ratee; number and frequency of violations; consequences (death, injury, property loss/damage); other dissimilar misconduct in the period; and unique, unusual, or extenuating circumstances.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8B; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-039
- **Prompt:** For evaluation adverse-action policy, which civilian dispositions are reportable (other than motor vehicle violations not requiring court appearance)?
- **Answer:** Any finding of guilt; any plea of guilty; any plea of no contest or nolo contendere; any plea of guilty in exchange for a deferred prosecution or diversion program; or any other similar disposition of civilian criminal charges.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8B; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-040
- **Prompt:** What mandatory notation applies when sex-related offense complaints result in court-martial conviction, NJP, or adverse administrative action?
- **Answer:** A mandatory notation on the member’s next performance report or training report and promotion recommendation form (if not already documented on an evaluation or court-martial in the selection record). Sex-related offenses include UCMJ violations or attempts to commit related offenses.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8B; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-041
- **Prompt:** When are behavior comments mandatory after court-martial conviction or adverse information filed in a selection record?
- **Answer:** Comments relating to the ratee’s behavior are mandatory on the next performance or training report and promotion recommendation form (if not already documented). The evaluation becomes a referral for the performance report or training report.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8B; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-042
- **Prompt:** How may a rater seek waiver of the mandatory requirement to document civilian convictions?
- **Answer:** Request a waiver for good cause. The waiver routes from the rater, through any required additional rater and the ratee’s commander, to the ratee’s senior rater, and, if endorsed, to the major command commander or authorized final approval authority.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8B; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-043
- **Prompt:** What should supervisors understand about employing evaluation systems?
- **Answer:** Supervisors must understand how and when to employ the officer and enlisted evaluation systems and the civilian performance program.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8B; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-044
- **Prompt:** What talent-management and record uses are associated with evaluation forms/briefs?
- **Answer:** They are considered when making promotion recommendations, selections or propriety actions, selective continuations, involuntary separations, selective early retirements, assignments, school nominations and selections, and other management decisions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8B; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-045
- **Prompt:** What is the expectation for performance evaluations regarding fair treatment?
- **Answer:** Fair and equal treatment of all, and enforcement of the same behavior in subordinates. The goal is fair and accurate evaluations so the best qualified members are identified for positions of higher responsibility.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8B; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-046
- **Prompt:** If a commander or military law-enforcement official learns that a USAF member under another military department’s jurisdiction has a reportable civilian conviction, what must happen?
- **Answer:** The commander or military law-enforcement official receiving such information shall forward it to the member’s immediate commander.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8B; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-047
- **Prompt:** What does AFH 1 say about entering classified information on evaluations?
- **Answer:** Do not enter classified information in any section of the evaluation, including attachments to evaluations, referral documents, and endorsements to referral documents. Use “Data Masked” in place of an entry that would release classified information.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8B; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-048
- **Prompt:** What must ratees do before an evaluation becomes a matter of record?
- **Answer:** Review the evaluation prior to it becoming a matter of record and bring typos, spelling errors, and inaccurate data to the rater’s attention at that time.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8B; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-049
- **Prompt:** What is an adverse action for purposes of the evaluation documentation policy described in AFH 1?
- **Answer:** An adverse action includes reportable civilian offenses or convictions, other than convictions for motor vehicle violations that do not require a court appearance, with the listed findings/pleas/dispositions that must be reported.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8B; B—Understanding

### TSgt C—Applying overlay cards (8B; no SSgt tag)

### C08-FC-050
- **Prompt:** A ratee signs an evaluation but later says the signature meant agreement with every rating. Applying AFH 1, how do you correct that misunderstanding?
- **Answer:** Explain that signature acknowledges receipt and certifies administrative review—it is not concurrence with content. Content disagreements are addressed by appeal after the evaluation becomes a matter of record, while administrative errors should have been raised beforehand.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.8
- **Tags:** TSgt; 8B; C—Applying

### C08-FC-051
- **Prompt:** Your draft evaluation says only “conduct unbecoming” after an Article 15 for DUI. Applying AFH 1, what must you change?
- **Answer:** Replace vague wording with specific comments outlining the event and corrective action taken—for example, that the member drove while intoxicated and received an Article 15—so the record is fair, accurate, and usable.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.9
- **Tags:** TSgt; 8B; C—Applying

### C08-FC-052
- **Prompt:** A performance statement lists only what an Airman did, with no outcome. Applying AFH 1, what is missing?
- **Answer:** The impact, results, or outcome of the behavior or action. A performance statement must be a standalone sentence including both the behavior/action and its impact/results/outcome, with at least one such statement in each evaluation section.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.9
- **Tags:** TSgt; 8B; C—Applying

### C08-FC-053
- **Prompt:** You learn a ratee has a substantiated sex-related offense resulting in NJP that is not yet on an evaluation. Applying AFH 1, what documentation obligation applies?
- **Answer:** A mandatory notation is required on the member’s next performance report or training report and promotion recommendation form (if not already documented on an evaluation or court-martial in the selection record).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶¶8.6, 8.9
- **Tags:** TSgt; 8B; C—Applying

### C08-FC-054
- **Prompt:** While drafting an evaluation for a classified unit, a peer wants the real organization name entered “for clarity.” Applying AFH 1, what do you do?
- **Answer:** Do not enter classified information. Enter “Data Masked” for organization nomenclature and nothing more when the evaluator is assigned to a classified organization or location. Protect the evaluation as CUI and limit access to official-need requestors.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.5
- **Tags:** TSgt; 8B; C—Applying

### C08-FC-055
- **Prompt:** A rater wants to omit known misconduct from an evaluation to “protect the Airman’s career.” Applying AFH 1, why is that approach incorrect?
- **Answer:** Failure to document misconduct that deviates from USAF core values is a disservice to Airmen who serve with honor and distinction. Fair and accurate evaluations identify the best qualified for higher responsibility; omit only when a properly routed waiver of mandatory civilian-conviction documentation is approved for good cause.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.9
- **Tags:** TSgt; 8B; C—Applying


## Section 8C — Reenlistments and Continuation

### C08-FC-056
- **Prompt:** What is the Selective Reenlistment Program, and whom does it evaluate?
- **Answer:** A program applying to all enlisted personnel by which commanders/civilians, directors, and supervisors evaluate first-term, second-term, and career Airmen to ensure the USAF retains those who consistently demonstrate the capability and willingness of maintaining high professional standards.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8C; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-057
- **Prompt:** When do first-term Airmen receive selective reenlistment consideration?
- **Answer:** When they are within 15 months of their expiration of term of service.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8C; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-058
- **Prompt:** When do second-term and career Airmen with less than 19 years TAFMS receive SRP consideration?
- **Answer:** Within 13 months of the original expiration of term of service.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8C; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-059
- **Prompt:** When do career Airmen receive SRP consideration at/around 20 years and beyond?
- **Answer:** Within 13 months of completing 20 years TAFMS; those who have served beyond 20 years TAFMS receive consideration each time they are within 13 months of their original ETS.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8C; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-060
- **Prompt:** What do commanders/civilian directors consider when determining if a member may reenlist?
- **Answer:** Enlisted performance report ratings, unfavorable information from any substantiated source, the Airman’s willingness to comply with USAF standards, and the Airman’s ability (or lack thereof) to meet required training and duty performance levels.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8C; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-061
- **Prompt:** What should supervisors do before recommending SRP decisions to commanders/civilian directors?
- **Answer:** Carefully evaluate the Airman’s duty performance and review the Airman’s personnel records, to include the DAF Form 1137, Unfavorable Information File Summary, if applicable.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8C; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-062
- **Prompt:** What form documents SRP non-selection, and what appeal timing rules apply?
- **Answer:** DAF Form 418, Selective Reenlistment Program (SRP) Consideration/Denial of Continued Service for Airmen. The Airman has up to three calendar days to render an appeal intent and must submit the appeal to the MPF within 10 calendar days of the date the appeal intent is rendered on the form. The commander must ensure the Airman understands the right to appeal.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8C; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-063
- **Prompt:** Who is the SRP appeal authority for first-term Airmen and for career Airmen completing at least 20 years TAFMS on current ETS?
- **Answer:** The respective group commander. The appeal authority’s decision is final.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8C; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-064
- **Prompt:** Who is the SRP appeal authority for second-term and career Airmen completing fewer than 16 years TAFMS on current ETS?
- **Answer:** The respective wing commander.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8C; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-065
- **Prompt:** Who is the SRP appeal authority for second-term and career Airmen completing at least 16 but fewer than 20 years TAFMS on current ETS?
- **Answer:** The Secretary of the Air Force.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8C; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-066
- **Prompt:** May commanders conduct SRP consideration outside the standardized window?
- **Answer:** Yes. Commanders may also conduct selective reenlistment consideration at any time outside the standardized window.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8C; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-067
- **Prompt:** What is the Noncommissioned Officer Career Status Program?
- **Answer:** It extends the length of reenlistments for RegAF Airmen. Airmen selected for continued service by their commander/civilian director (including those with approved waivers) who have at least 12 years TAFMS on date of discharge (day prior to reenlistment) reenlist for an unspecified period and serve up to High Year of Tenure based on current or projected grade unless sooner separated. Airmen with 20 or more years TAFMS may retire, if otherwise eligible, no later than the first day of the month following HYT. The program streamlines reenlistment/extension processes.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8C; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-068
- **Prompt:** Why does the Career Job Reservation Program exist, and who must have an approved CJR?
- **Answer:** To manage first-term Airmen reenlistments by AFSC and prevent surpluses and shortages when career force size/composition restrictions limit authorized first-term reenlistments. All eligible first-term Airmen must have an approved CJR to reenlist.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8C; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-069
- **Prompt:** When are Airmen automatically placed on the career job applicant list for CJR?
- **Answer:** On the first duty day of the month during which they complete 35 months on their current enlistment (59 months for six-year enlistees), but no later than the last duty day of the month during which they complete 43 months (67 months for six-year enlistees). To keep an approved CJR, Airmen must reenlist on or before the CJR expiration date.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8C; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-070
- **Prompt:** How do constrained and unconstrained CJR AFSCs differ?
- **Answer:** Constrained: limited quotas; first-term Airmen compete; commanders/civilian directors recommend; approval at group commander level or equivalent; Airmen outside the CJR window are ineligible; evaluations must reflect minimum expectations and the Airman must not have a UIF, lost time, or active NJP on the current enlistment. Unconstrained: Airmen do not compete; CJR is automatically awarded on the same 35/43 (or 59/67) month window if selected for continued service under SRP. Approved CJRs do not expire until the Airman’s date of separation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8C; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-071
- **Prompt:** What happens if an AFSC is removed from the constrained list while an Airman is on the CJR waiting list?
- **Answer:** Airmen on the waiting list whose AFSC is removed from the constrained list will receive a CJR. Airmen removed from the waiting list before the AFSC is removed will not receive supplemental consideration.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8C; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-072
- **Prompt:** Under what conditions may Airmen request an enlistment extension, and what limits apply?
- **Answer:** If the Airman has a service-directed retainability reason and the extension is in the best interest of the USAF. Extensions are in whole-month increments; voluntary extensions are limited to a maximum of 48 months per enlistment. Once approved, an extension has the legal effect of extending obligated service and can be canceled only when the reason no longer exists and the Airman has not already entered the extension. Airmen may request extension to establish a DOS at HYT; normally they must be within two years of HYT.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8C; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-073
- **Prompt:** What is the Selective Retention Bonus Program intended to do?
- **Answer:** Provide a monetary incentive to Airmen in certain selected critical military skills who reenlist for additional obligated service, encouraging reenlistment of sufficient qualified enlisted personnel in skills with demonstrated retention shortfalls or high training costs. Airmen in SRB skills who reenlist or extend on active duty for at least three years may be eligible if they meet DAFI 36-2606 criteria, and they can expect to serve in the SRB specialty for the entire enlistment for which the bonus was paid.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8C; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-074
- **Prompt:** What are the SRB zones by TAFMS, and what is the maximum SRB per zone?
- **Answer:** Zone A: 17 months to 6 years; Zone B: 6 to 10 years; Zone C: 10 to 14 years; Zone E: 18 to 20 years. Eligible Airmen may receive an SRB in each zone but only one SRB per zone. Maximum SRB per zone is $100,000. Calculation uses one month’s base pay × years reenlisting/extending × SRB multiple; base pay on the date of discharge is used.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8C; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-075
- **Prompt:** What is the USAF Retraining Program’s primary purpose?
- **Answer:** A force management tool used primarily to balance career fields across all AFSCs and ensure sustainability, and to return disqualified Airmen to a productive status. Although involuntary retraining may occur based on USAF needs, it also allows a limited number of Airmen to pursue other career paths. The Online Retraining Advisory on myFSS is a living document maintained by AFPC. Refer to AFMAN 36-2100 for eligibility and procedures.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8C; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-076
- **Prompt:** When may CONUS first-term Airmen apply for the First-Term Airmen Retraining Program?
- **Answer:** Not earlier than the first duty day of the month they complete 35 months of their current enlistment (59 months for six-year enlistees), and not later than the last duty day of the 43rd month (67th month for six-year enlistees).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8C; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-077
- **Prompt:** When may OCONUS first-term Airmen apply for retraining?
- **Answer:** Nine to 15 months prior to the date they are eligible to return from overseas if serving within their normal first-term window (35th month for four-year enlistees or 59th month for six-year enlistees). Airmen with an indefinite return-from-overseas date must complete the original tour length before departing for retraining.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8C; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-078
- **Prompt:** What quality indicators does AFPC use when selecting the most eligible Airmen for first-term retraining?
- **Answer:** Most recent performance report rating, current grade, projected grade, previous two performance report ratings, date of rank, total active federal military service date, aptitude qualification examination score (electrical, mechanical, administrative, general), Air Force Enlisted Classification Directory Part II, and requested AFSC preferences—selected on the last duty day of each month.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8C; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-079
- **Prompt:** How do NCO Retraining Program Phase I and Phase II differ?
- **Answer:** Phase I seeks volunteer applicants from identified overage AFSCs to fill shortage specialty codes; Airmen with retraining “out” objectives may apply for available “in” specialties for which they qualify. If applications are insufficient and fiscal-year objectives are unmet, Phase II selects Airmen for mandatory retraining based on USAF needs using the master vulnerability list.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8C; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-080
- **Prompt:** What officer crossflow/reclassification tools does AFH 1 summarize?
- **Answer:** Nonrated Line Crossflow Program (panel when needed to select best-qualified officers for vacancies); Missileer Crossflow Program (balances 13N by crossflowing excess officers at the four-year point back to donor career fields); plus out-of-cycle crossflow requests and initial skills training reclassification to balance officer career fields.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8C; B—Understanding


## Section 8D — Awards and Decorations

### C08-FC-081
- **Prompt:** What is the purpose of the Department of the Air Force Military Awards and Decorations Program?
- **Answer:** As outlined in DAFMAN 36-2806, it recognizes units, organizations, and individuals and fosters morale, provides incentive, and instills esprit de corps. Individuals or units must clearly demonstrate sustained and superior performance. Acts of valor, heroism, exceptional service, and outstanding achievement deserve special recognition.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.18
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-082
- **Prompt:** What proof should military members keep for service and campaign award entitlement?
- **Answer:** Copies of their travel orders and their travel vouchers as documented proof of entitlement.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-083
- **Prompt:** What does the Prisoner of War Medal recognize?
- **Answer:** Awarded to any person who, while serving in any capacity with the U.S. Armed Forces, was taken prisoner and held captive while engaged in action against an enemy of the U.S., engaged in military operations involving conflict with an opposing foreign force, or served with friendly forces engaged in an armed conflict against an opposing armed force in which the U.S. was not a belligerent party.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-084
- **Prompt:** What does the National Defense Service Medal recognize?
- **Answer:** Honorable active military service (including Selected Reserve in good standing, other than Selected Reserve called to active duty, USAFA cadets, and DAF members who completed Basic Training) during periods of conflict or national crisis designated by SecDef.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-085
- **Prompt:** What participation thresholds does AFH 1 cite for the Armed Forces Expeditionary Medal?
- **Answer:** Participation/direct support for 30 consecutive days in the area of operations (or the full period), or 60 non-consecutive days when engaged in actual combat or equally hazardous duty with an armed opposition, when wounded/injured and medically evacuated, or when accumulating 15 days as an assigned aircrew member flying sorties in direct support. Members will not be awarded more than one expeditionary medal for services during a specific period or duties during an operation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-086
- **Prompt:** When were the GWOT Expeditionary and Service Medals established, and what start date do they share?
- **Answer:** Both were established on 12 March 2003 and recognize qualifying service on or after 11 September 2001 (GWOT-S until a future date to be determined). Designated operations are in DAFMAN 36-2806.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-087
- **Prompt:** What periods do the Afghanistan and Iraq Campaign Medals cover, and what replaced GWOT-E for those theaters after 1 May 2005?
- **Answer:** Afghanistan Campaign Medal: 11 September 2001 to 31 August 2021. Iraq Campaign Medal: 9 March 2003 to 31 December 2011. Effective 1 May 2005, members deployed to Afghanistan or Iraq receive the respective campaign medal in lieu of the Global War on Terrorism Expeditionary Medal. Both medals were established 29 November 2004.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-088
- **Prompt:** What start date does AFH 1 associate with the Inherent Resolve Campaign Medal?
- **Answer:** Qualifying service on or after 15 June 2014; area of eligibility and designated campaigns are in DAFMAN 36-2806.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-089
- **Prompt:** What eligibility window does AFH 1 cite for the Korean Defense Service Medal?
- **Answer:** Approved February 2004 for RegAF, AFR, and ANG personnel for military service in the Republic of Korea and surrounding waters after 28 July 1954 until a future date to be determined.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-090
- **Prompt:** What period does the Kosovo Campaign Medal cover?
- **Answer:** Established 3 May 2000; recognizes qualifying service in Kosovo from 24 March 1999 to 31 December 2013.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-091
- **Prompt:** How do the Armed Forces Service Medal and Humanitarian Service Medal differ in AFH 1’s descriptions?
- **Answer:** AFSM: awarded for participation as military members in a JCS-deemed significant military operation with no foreign armed opposition or imminent threat of hostile action. HSM: awarded for meritorious direct (hands-on) participation in a significant military act or operation of a humanitarian nature.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-092
- **Prompt:** What do the Military Outstanding Volunteer Service Medal and Armed Forces Reserve Medal recognize?
- **Answer:** MOVSM (established 9 January 1993): outstanding volunteer community service of a sustained, direct, and consequential nature. AFRM: Reserve Component members/former members who completed 10 years of service within 12 consecutive years, or upon mobilization or contingency MPA order to RegAF.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-093
- **Prompt:** What integrity rules govern unit awards according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Approved only for acts/services clearly and distinctly outstanding by nature and magnitude, placing the unit above similar units and of such importance they cannot be recognized another way. Only one unit award for the same achievement/service; the unit’s entire service must have been honorable. Higher organizations may not share a subordinate’s award; designated subordinate units (except foreign units) may. Assigned/attached people who served during the period are authorized the ribbon if they directly contributed to the mission and accomplishments.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-094
- **Prompt:** What are the five most common unit awards worn by USAF members today, and what is the nation’s highest unit award?
- **Answer:** Common five: Joint Meritorious Unit Award, Gallant Unit Citation, Meritorious Unit Award, Air Force Outstanding Unit Award, and Air Force Organizational Excellence Award. Highest: Presidential Unit Citation, for extraordinary heroism in action against an armed enemy on or after 7 December 1941; the degree of heroism required is the same as that which would be required of an Air Force Cross to an individual.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-095
- **Prompt:** What do the Gallant Unit Citation and Meritorious Unit Award recognize (post-11 Sep 2001)?
- **Answer:** GUC (approved March 2004): extraordinary heroism in action against an armed enemy while engaged in military operations involving conflict with an opposing foreign force on or after 11 September 2001; normally for single/successive actions over relatively brief spans. MUA (approved March 2004): exceptionally meritorious conduct in outstanding achievement or service in direct support of combat operations for at least 90 continuous days on or after 11 September 2001; combat-zone service not required but service must relate to the combat effort; not awarded if AFOUA/AFOEA or other service unit awards already cover the same act/service.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-096
- **Prompt:** How do the Air Force Outstanding Unit Award and Air Force Organizational Excellence Award differ?
- **Answer:** Both established/awarded in the name of the Secretary of the Air Force on 6 January 1954 with the same guidelines and approval authority, and only 10 percent of similar units assigned to a command are recommended annually. AFOUA is awarded only to numbered units or Numbered Air Forces, air divisions, wings, groups, and squadrons. AFOEA is awarded to unnumbered organizations such as a major command, FOA, DRU, Office of the Chief of Staff, other Air Staff, and deputy assistant chief of staff agencies.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-097
- **Prompt:** Who is ineligible for DoD campaign, expeditionary, or service awards unless criteria specify otherwise, and which form verifies service medals?
- **Answer:** U.S. civilians, foreign nationals, and foreign military personnel are not eligible unless specified. Nominations are submitted on AF Form 104, Service Medal Award Verification, approved by the commander, and submitted to the servicing MPF, which procures and provides the initial issue of all achievement medals and ribbons.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-098
- **Prompt:** What does the Combat Action Medal require?
- **Answer:** Awarded to military members (grades E-1 through O-6) who actively participated in ground or air combat on or after 11 September 2001—deliberately going into an enemy’s domain or defending the base while conducting official duties and experiencing enemy fire by lethal weapons or firing upon the enemy at the risk of grave danger.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-099
- **Prompt:** What sustained-readiness criteria apply to the Combat Readiness Medal?
- **Answer:** Awarded for sustained individual combat or mission readiness/preparedness for direct weapon-system employment, subject to combat readiness reporting, individually certified combat and/or mission ready, subject to a continuous individual positional evaluation program, and upon completion of 24 months of sustained combat or mission readiness.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-100
- **Prompt:** How is the Air Force Good Conduct Medal awarded?
- **Answer:** Awarded to enlisted personnel for exemplary conduct while on active military service; award is automatic unless denied by the unit commander for reasons of less than exemplary conduct.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-101
- **Prompt:** What service lengths apply to the Air Reserve Forces Meritorious Service Medal?
- **Answer:** On or after 1 April 1965, for 4 continuous years of service, or on or after 1 July 1975, for 3 continuous years of service, recognizing exemplary behavior, efficiency, and fidelity in an enlisted status in the ANG and AFR. Creditable service ends when called to active duty.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-102
- **Prompt:** What does the Remote Combat Effects Campaign Medal require?
- **Answer:** On or after 11 September 2001, DAF military members who distinguished themselves by direct participation in a DoD combat operation: assigned/attached to a unit directly supporting such an operation; served in an RPA, cyber, space, or ISR career field; personally provided hands-on employment of a weapon system with direct and immediate impact on a combat operation; and were not physically exposed to hostile actions or at risk of exposure to hostile action.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-103
- **Prompt:** What do the Air and Space Expeditionary Service Ribbon and Longevity Service Award recognize?
- **Answer:** AESR: Air Expeditionary Force deployed status—45 consecutive days or 90 non-consecutive days in deployed status. Longevity Service Award: active-duty personnel based on an aggregate of 4 years of honorable active federal military service with any U.S. Armed Forces branch or Reserve components; Reserve Component members credited for each 4 years of satisfactory service creditable for retirement.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-104
- **Prompt:** What is a decoration, and what limits apply to receiving decorations?
- **Answer:** Formal recognition for personal excellence requiring individual nomination and USAF or DoD approval, for acts of valor, heroism, courage, exceptional/meritorious service, or outstanding achievement that clearly place members above their peers and of such importance they cannot be properly recognized another way. Determining factors include level of responsibility, achievements, manner of performance, and impact. Each decoration has specific requirements, and an individual may receive only one decoration for any act, achievement, or period of service.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-105
- **Prompt:** Who may recommend a decoration, and what information must not be included in regular recommendations?
- **Answer:** Although responsibility primarily falls on the immediate supervisor, any person other than the individual being recommended who has firsthand knowledge may recommend or contribute through the supervisor and chain of command. Do not include CUI, classified, highly sensitive, or special-category information requiring special handling in regular decoration recommendations. A memorandum/letter of justification with concrete examples may accompany the recommendation; a memorandum/letter is required for foreign military, separated/retired veterans, or other-service members.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-106
- **Prompt:** What are the Medal of Honor criteria and presentation custom noted in AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Highest decoration for heroism in military action; awarded for conspicuous gallantry and intrepidity at the risk of life above and beyond the call of duty in the specified combat/conflict circumstances. Generally presented by the U.S. President to the recipient or primary next of kin. Members of the uniformed services are encouraged to render salutes to MoH recipients as a matter of respect and courtesy whether or not they are in uniform and regardless of rank or status. First USAF recipient noted: Maj Bernard F. Fisher, presented by President Lyndon B. Johnson on 19 January 1967. Space Force members are eligible for the Department of the Air Force version.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-107
- **Prompt:** How do the Air Force Cross and Silver Star relate to the Medal of Honor in AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Air Force Cross: second-highest decoration; extraordinary heroism in the specified combat circumstances; above other combat decorations but does not merit the Medal of Honor. Silver Star: third-highest military combat decoration; gallantry in action in the specified circumstances; above other combat decorations but does not merit the Medal of Honor or Air Force Cross.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-108
- **Prompt:** What do the Distinguished Service Medal, Legion of Merit, Distinguished Flying Cross, and Airman’s Medal recognize?
- **Answer:** DSM: exceptionally meritorious service to the nation in a duty of great responsibility. Legion of Merit: exceptionally meritorious conduct in outstanding duties performed in a clearly exceptional manner (also may be awarded to foreign military for outstanding services to the United States). DFC: heroism or extraordinary achievement while participating in aerial flight for entirely distinctive, non-routine operations. Airman’s Medal: heroism involving voluntary risk of life under conditions other than conflict with an armed enemy of the United States.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-109
- **Prompt:** What do the Bronze Star, Purple Heart, Air Medal, Aerial Achievement Medal, MSM, and Commendation/Achievement Medals recognize?
- **Answer:** Bronze Star: heroism, valor, or meritorious achievement/service (not aerial flight) in specified conflict circumstances; valor to a lesser degree than Silver Star or Legion of Merit. Purple Heart: wounds received or death after being wounded in specified hostile circumstances; earned entitlement rather than recommendation; since 11 September 2001 may be awarded for certain foreign terrorist organization attacks targeting the member’s Armed Forces status (unless willful misconduct). Air Medal: heroism or meritorious achievement in aerial flight above expected professional performance but not warranting DFC. Aerial Achievement Medal: sustained meritorious achievement in aerial flight. MSM: outstanding meritorious achievement. Air and Space Commendation Medal: distinctive meritorious achievement and service. Air and Space Achievement Medal: outstanding achievement or meritorious service.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶8.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding

### C08-FC-110
- **Prompt:** What makes special trophies and awards unique, and what foreign/non-military award rules apply?
- **Answer:** Special trophies/awards: MAJCOM, FOA, and DRU commanders must nominate individuals (usually annually); nomination alone is meaningful recognition. Examples: 12 Outstanding Airmen of the Year Award and the Lance P. Sijan Award (DAFMAN 36-2806 lists programs). Foreign items: DAF does not issue foreign certificates/medals/ribbons/emblems/devices/badges (commercial purchase at individual expense). Foreign decorations may be accepted only for combat service or outstanding/unusually meritorious performance; foreign unit and campaign/service awards (including NATO) only after official DoD acceptance. Non-military awards from non-Federal entities require official approval-authority approval before acceptance.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶¶8.23–8.25
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 8D; B—Understanding
