# Chapter 10 Supplemental Situational Judgment Practice

> **Status:** Draft and provisional for 27E5/27E6 preparation; the official 2027 format is unconfirmed.
>
> **Reserve-chapter status:** Chapter 10 was outside the historical 26E5 and 26E6 catalog scopes and remains reserve pending the 2027 WAPS catalogs. Prior exclusion does not prove future inclusion, weighting, or scenario testability.
>
> **Two-track source rule:** For exam-source study, preserve the facts, dates, names, quantities, terminology, and ambiguities in the AFH edition assigned by the controlling catalog. For duty use, separately verify current assignment policies, occupational codes, special-duty procedures, and authorities; duty-current information does not overwrite a catalog-controlled answer.
>
> These ten supplemental scenarios are wholly original. They are not actual, recalled, reconstructed, or predicted PFE items. They are supplemental SJT practice only, not a claim that assignments and occupational-codes content is scenario-tested.
>
> AFH 1 assigns Chapter 10 section **10A as B—Understanding for both SSgt and TSgt**, section **10B as A—Remembering for SSgt and B—Understanding for TSgt**, and section **10C as B—Understanding for both SSgt and TSgt**. Those designations apply to recognizing, recalling, or—where rated B—explaining Chapter 10 assignment and classification facts; they do not make these scenarios A-level or B-level behavioral items. Chapter 10's assignment and occupational-code facts provide context only and **do not establish behavioral scoring keys**. The most-effective and least-effective selections below are MyPromotion educational judgments based on Chapter 1 professionalism, core values, and ethical reasoning.
>
> The official Air Force uses subject-matter experts and behavioral scientists to establish operational SJT scoring keys. These supplemental judgments are not official keys or substitutes for current supervisory, personnel, legal, SAPR/FAP, ethics, public-affairs, reporting, or command procedures. Any process or authority facts needed to rank an option are supplied within the scenario as invented instructional constraints.

## How to use this set

Each scenario produces two selections:

1. Select the **most effective** response.
2. Select the **least effective** response.

Read every option before choosing. Judge the learner's present-day conduct, not the wisdom of a historical actor or the technical excellence of a personnel system. Consider whether the response:

- represents sources, eligibility rules, codes, and uncertainty honestly;
- accepts responsibility within the authority stated in the scenario;
- protects mission requirements without treating caution or speed as automatic answers;
- respects Airmen, families, process owners, and partners with accuracy and dignity;
- supports disciplined stewardship, readiness, and attention to detail; and
- strengthens teamwork across specialties, components, and support agencies.

Ten scenarios produce 20 response selections, mirroring only the historical public structure announced for the [22E5/22E6 cycles in December 2021](https://www.afpc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/2874484/waps-adjusts-for-22e6-22e5-testing-cycles/). The separate [public SJT informational guide](https://www.studyguides.af.mil/Portals/15/documents/Notices/Situational-Judgment-Test-Informational-Guide.pdf) explains the four-option most/least format. Neither source confirms the 2027 structure, and the historical 22E5/22E6 format does not show that Chapter 10 assignments and occupational-codes content was or will be tested through scenarios.

## C10-SJT-001 — Honest retainability counseling

You help review an unclassified flight professional-development tip sheet on PCS retainability. The draft claims that declining required retainability “has no career impact” for career Airmen. The designated baseline, AFH 1 dated 15 February 2025, states that declining retainability makes a career Airman ineligible for promotion and reenlistment. You may replace one conclusion sentence before the tip sheet is emailed; the rest of the cleared content must remain.

### Response options

A. Keep the “no career impact” line because it sounds reassuring and put the AFH consequence only in a footer nobody reads.  
B. Delete every retainability reference and send only a unit logo.  
C. Replace the closing sentence with AFH 1’s promotion-and-reenlistment ineligibility consequence for career Airmen who decline retainability.  
D. Leave the false line and tell Airmen orally that the tip sheet is “just motivational.”

### Most effective: C

Paragraph 10.5 supplies retainability context; it does not prescribe a behavioral scoring key for a tip-sheet editor. C is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it uses the learner’s stated revision authority to keep a clear closing that remains within the source. Chapter 1 honesty, accountability, and disciplined communication provide the behavioral basis. Chapter 10 assignment facts do not establish the behavioral scoring key.

### Least effective: A

A knowingly preserves a claim broader and softer than the approved extract supports and presents understatement of career risk as the audience takeaway. Chapter 10 explains the consequence, but Chapter 1 accurate representation and transparency provide the behavioral basis for the least-effective judgment.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It trades source-supported consequences for a memorable but inaccurate reassurance despite available revision authority.
- **B — Partially effective:** It avoids the false claim but withholds a supported conclusion the reviewer is authorized to fix.
- **C — Most effective:** It preserves a conclusive ending that matches the designated source without inventing “no impact.”
- **D — Ineffective:** An oral caveat leaves the emailed tip sheet still communicating an unsupported claim.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶10.5 (retainability context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Honest retainability counseling; honesty; accountability; disciplined communication  
**ADTC context:** 10A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of counseling realism; MPS/assignment authorities control real retainability actions

## C10-SJT-002 — EQUAL preference currency

### Response options

B. Require them to update preferences to reflect the current status change and verify the update is submitted, while explaining Airman ownership of accuracy.  
C. Quietly change their preferences yourself without their knowledge “to save time.”  
D. Joke that marriage guarantees join-spouse approval so preference updates are optional.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 10.7 supplies EQUAL preference-responsibility context; it does not establish a behavioral answer key for a supervisor. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it reinforces personal ownership and drives a timely, accurate update after a status change. Chapter 1 accountability, respect, and mission stewardship provide the behavioral basis. Chapter 10 EQUAL facts do not establish the behavioral scoring key.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It dismisses a source-supported responsibility and mission tool.
- **B — Most effective:** It pairs ownership with a concrete update after a status change.
- **C — Least effective:** It secretly manipulates preferences and erases accountability.
- **D — Ineffective:** It substitutes a false join-spouse guarantee for preference diligence.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶10.7 (EQUAL context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Preference currency; accountability; integrity; respect  
**ADTC context:** 10A, B—Understanding

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It creates a false guarantee that can drive irreversible personal decisions.
- **B — Partially effective:** It avoids falsehood but withholds a needed caution the mentor can give.
- **C — Most effective:** It preserves both mission criteria and the no-guarantee caution.
- **D — Ineffective:** It erases manning/skills requirements the source keeps central.

B. Redesign around career-group digits, skill/qualification levels, prefix vs suffix, and AFECD purpose, with a clear note that full career-field detail remains in AFH 1.  
C. Replace all code content with unit morale slogans.  
D. Invent simplified codes that “merge” 1B, 1D, and 3D into one label for ease.

### Most effective: B

B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it aligns the handout to the stated learning objective and preserves source fidelity without false completeness. Chapter 1 accountability, discipline, and honest representation provide the behavioral basis. Chapter 10 code facts do not establish the behavioral scoring key.

### Least effective: D

D manufactures a composite code family the designated extract does not support as a single label. Chapter 10 distinguishes those fields, but Chapter 1 accurate representation provides the behavioral basis for the least-effective judgment.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It overwhelms the objective and crowds out structure/purpose learning.
- **B — Most effective:** It teaches how to read codes and points learners to the controlling full list.
- **C — Partially effective:** It avoids error but abandons the authorized learning objective.
- **D — Least effective:** It invents agreement by erasing source distinctions.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶10.11–10.12 (codes context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Structure-first code teaching; honesty; accountability; discipline

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It preserves a known terminology error as the takeaway.
- **B — Most effective:** It restores prefix vs suffix meaning before training delivery.
- **C — Partially effective:** It avoids the false shred claim but removes usable baseline teaching.
- **D — Ineffective:** Oral softening leaves the displayed error in force.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶10.11; Table 10.1 (structure context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)

## C10-SJT-006 — Special-duty nomination integrity

Your flight commander asks for developmental special-duty nomination inputs. A coworker suggests nominating a popular Airman who recently modeled poor discipline “because the special duty will fix them.” AFH 1 states nominees must be the best in the USAF and ambassadors/role models for core values and discipline. You may submit one recommendation note today.

### Response options

A. Nominate the struggling Airman as a corrective placement and omit the discipline concerns.  
B. Recommend against nomination for that Airman, cite the ambassador/role-model standard, and identify a stronger nominee who meets it if one is available.  
C. Refuse to participate in any nomination discussion.  
D. Nominate everyone in the flight to “be fair,” including those who do not meet the standard.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 10.14 supplies developmental special-duty nomination context; it does not establish a behavioral answer key for nomination inputs. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it protects the quality gate and gives usable counsel within the stated task. Chapter 1 integrity, accountability, and respect for the institution provide the behavioral basis. Chapter 10 special-duty facts do not establish the behavioral scoring key.

### Least effective: A

A conceals known discipline issues and treats a developmental leadership role as remediation theater. Chapter 1 integrity and responsibility provide the behavioral basis for the least-effective judgment.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It falsifies fitness for a role explicitly tied to modeling core values and discipline.
- **B — Most effective:** It applies the nomination standard honestly and offers a constructive alternative when possible.
- **C — Partially effective:** It avoids a bad nomination but withholds required input the commander requested.
- **D — Ineffective:** Blanket nomination erases the “best Airmen” quality gate.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶10.14 (special-duty context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.2 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Nomination integrity; integrity; accountability; leadership stewardship  
**ADTC context:** 10C, B—Understanding
