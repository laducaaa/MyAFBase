# Chapter 16 Situational Judgment Practice

> **Status:** Draft and provisional for 27E5/27E6. Chapter 16 is a **TSgt-priority overlay**: historically in **26E6** catalog scope and outside **26E5** scope.
>
> These scenarios are wholly original. They are not actual, recalled, reconstructed, or predicted PFE items. The official Air Force uses subject-matter experts and behavioral scientists to establish operational SJT scoring keys. Chapter 16 describes knowledge programs, cognitive processes, informed decision-making, and empowerment/innovation facts, but those facts alone do not establish an official behavioral scoring key. All most-effective and least-effective keys below are MyPromotion educational judgments grounded in Chapter 1 professionalism, core values, and ethical reasoning, with Chapter 16 material supplying context only. They are not official scoring keys or substitutes for current command, innovation-program, legal, or reporting procedures.
>
> **Section-title note:** ADTC/curriculum name for 16A is **What We Know**; source heading variant is **Accountability and Self-Management**.
>
> **Two-track source rule:** For exam-source study, preserve the facts in the AFH edition assigned by the controlling catalog. For duty use, separately verify current innovation, reading-list, and decision-process guidance; duty-current information does not overwrite a catalog-controlled answer.

## How to use this set

Each scenario produces two items:

1. Select the **most effective** response.
2. Select the **least effective** response.

Read every option before choosing. Evaluate whether the response:

- represents knowledge, decision-process, and innovation facts honestly;
- protects candid dialogue and resists groupthink or concealment;
- refuses normalized deviance, false consensus, or abusive conflict;
- stays within stated role and delegated authority;
- uses the two-track caution when source snapshots may differ from current rules; and
- reflects integrity, accountability, loyalty, respect, and mission stewardship.

Chapter 16 supplies developing-ideas context; it does not prescribe the behavioral sequence in every response. When a rationale depends on general professional judgment—such as honesty, courage, accountability, loyalty, respect, mission focus, or teamwork—it is identified as an application of the cited foundational Chapter 1 paragraphs.

Ten scenarios produce 20 response selections, mirroring the historical public structure announced for the [22E5/22E6 cycles in December 2021](https://www.afpc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/2874484/waps-adjusts-for-22e6-22e5-testing-cycles/). The separate [public SJT informational guide](https://www.studyguides.af.mil/Portals/15/documents/Notices/Situational-Judgment-Test-Informational-Guide.pdf) explains the four-option most/least format. The 2027 structure is unconfirmed. This set covers only selected Chapter 16 concepts and is not a full-spectrum SJT simulation.

## C16-SJT-001 — GAIN idea honesty

You supervise a Staff Sergeant who drafted a GAIN-style improvement suggestion that could cut process time. AFH 1 describes GAIN as soliciting Airmen ideas that contribute to effectiveness, efficiency, enterprise replication, cost savings/avoidance, and other improvements. A peer asks the Staff Sergeant to inflate the projected savings numbers “so leadership will fund it,” and the Staff Sergeant asks whether you will sign as a co-submitter if the numbers are padded.

### Response options

A. Agree to pad the savings figures and sign as co-submitter to “help innovation win.”  
B. Refuse falsified savings claims, help the Airman document realistic effectiveness/efficiency evidence, and submit through the proper innovation channel without misrepresentation.  
C. Tell the Airman innovation ideas are only for officers and destroy the draft.  
D. Post the padded claim on social media as already approved funding.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 16.4 supplies GAIN improvement-outcome context; it does not score the integrity choice. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it keeps the idea channel honest while still supporting legitimate improvement. Chapter 1 honesty and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A knowingly falsifies projected savings to obtain funding. Chapter 16’s improvement purpose assumes real contribution; Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It fabricates savings to manipulate approval.
- **B — Most effective:** It supports innovation with truthful evidence and proper channels.
- **C — Ineffective:** It falsely restricts innovation and destroys work product.
- **D — Ineffective:** It publicizes false approval status.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶16.4 (GAIN context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Innovation honesty; integrity; accountability  
**ADTC:** 16A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of innovation-channel realism; current GAIN/command processes control real submissions

## C16-SJT-002 — Resisting groupthink

Your flight is rushing a manning workaround. Two senior voices insist “we’re all on board,” and a junior Airman who spots a safety gap stays quiet after being told not to “slow the train.” AFH 1 warns that groupthink trades critical thinking for conformity/unanimity and that dissenting views can improve decisions.

### Response options

A. Reinforce silence by telling the junior Airman that unanimity is more important than raising risks.  
B. Pause the rubber-stamp, invite the dissenting safety concern into the discussion, and require the team to examine disconfirming data before deciding.  
C. Mock the junior Airman publicly for “not being a team player.”  
D. Order everyone to sign a pre-written “full agreement” memo with no discussion.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 16.8–16.9 supply groupthink/dissent context; they do not score facilitation behavior. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it restores candid analysis and protects mission risk discovery. Chapter 1 courage, respect, and mission focus provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A actively enforces conformity that suppresses safety-relevant dissent. Chapter 16 identifies unanimity pressure as a groupthink symptom; Chapter 1 integrity and courage provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It silences needed dissent for false unanimity.
- **B — Most effective:** It invites healthy disagreement and examines warning signs.
- **C — Ineffective:** It uses personal attack (affective conflict) instead of idea analysis.
- **D — Ineffective:** It manufactures fake consensus without analysis.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶16.8–16.9 (groupthink/dissent context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Groupthink resistance; candid dialogue; courage  
**ADTC:** 16B, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Safety/command processes control real risk decisions

## C16-SJT-003 — Cognitive versus affective conflict

During a process redesign meeting, two NCOs disagree about sequencing. One begins attacking the other’s competence and motives. AFH 1 distinguishes constructive cognitive conflict (ideas/issues) from destructive affective conflict (personal/emotional outbursts).

### Response options

A. Join the personal attacks to “show strength.”  
B. Reset ground rules: keep debate on the sequencing options and evidence, stop personal attacks, and require respectful engagement with each analytical style.  
C. End all disagreement permanently so the room stays quiet.  
D. Leak selective quotes to the chain to embarrass one NCO.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 16.11 supplies cognitive/affective conflict context; it does not score facilitation technique. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it preserves needed issue debate while stopping personal attacks. Chapter 1 respect and teamwork provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A intensifies affective conflict. Chapter 16 rejects personal outbursts as constructive; Chapter 1 respect provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It escalates personal conflict.
- **B — Most effective:** It recenters cognitive conflict with ground rules.
- **C — Ineffective:** It creates a Culture of Yes silence problem.
- **D — Ineffective:** It weaponizes information and harms trust.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶16.11 (conflict context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Cognitive conflict; respect; facilitation  
**ADTC:** 16B, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Local meeting norms/command climate apply in real units

## C16-SJT-004 — Normalized deviance / close call

B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it rejects acceptance of deviance and seeks transparent root-cause correction. Chapter 1 accountability and integrity provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: C

C conceals ongoing standard violations. Chapter 16 warns deviance becomes invisible when unexamined; Chapter 1 honesty provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It endorses lowering standards.
- **B — Most effective:** It restores standards and analyzes the deviation.
- **C — Least effective:** It hides deviance from oversight.
- **D — Ineffective:** It scapegoats juniors while preserving the bad norm.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶16.14 (normalized deviance/practical drift context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)

**ADTC:** 16B, B—Understanding

## C16-SJT-005 — CRAAP / information integrity

D. Ban the Airman from ever recommending process ideas again.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 16.16 supplies CRAAP/digital-literacy context; it does not score mentoring tone. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it applies source evaluation without crushing initiative. Chapter 1 honesty and disciplined judgment provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: C

C fabricates authority credentials. Chapter 16’s Authority/Accuracy elements assume truthful evaluation; Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It skips critical evaluation.
- **B — Most effective:** It teaches CRAAP and demands better evidence.
- **C — Least effective:** It falsifies source authority.
- **D — Ineffective:** It punishes engagement instead of improving rigor.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶16.16 (CRAAP context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Information literacy; honesty; mentoring  
**ADTC:** 16C, B—Understanding

## C16-SJT-006 — Deferred judgment in brainstorming

You are facilitating Idea Finding for a work-center problem. Midway through brainstorming, a supervisor starts grading every idea as “stupid” or “genius,” and juniors stop offering unusual options. AFH 1’s Osborn-Parnes model requires deferred judgment during divergent idea generation.

### Response options

A. Continue scoring each idea instantly to “save time.”  
B. Pause evaluation, restate deferred judgment, capture ideas—including far-out ones—to a numeric goal, and only then move to convergent evaluation.  
C. Cancel brainstorming and announce your preferred solution as Level I with no explanation.  
D. Allow only SNCOs to speak for the rest of the session.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 16.18 supplies deferred-judgment/Idea Finding context; it does not score facilitation craft. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it protects divergent thinking before evaluation. Chapter 1 respect and teamwork provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A violates deferred judgment and chills contribution. Chapter 16 warns extrinsic evaluation can undermine divergent thinking; Chapter 1 respect for others’ inputs supports the least-effective judgment.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It evaluates too early and suppresses ideas.
- **B — Most effective:** It restores deferred judgment and later convergence.
- **C — Ineffective:** It abandons needed idea generation without rationale discipline.
- **D — Ineffective:** It reimposes hierarchy stigma that blocks contribution.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶16.18 (Osborn-Parnes context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Deferred judgment; inclusive facilitation  
**ADTC:** 16C, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Facilitation norms vary by unit; scenario is educational

## C16-SJT-007 — Decision involvement / false façade

You gathered group input on a schedule change (Level III-style). You already decided beforehand and used the meeting only for show. Afterward, members say their input was a façade. AFH 1 warns that if leaders decide differently after group input without valued consideration, members may undermine implementation.

### Response options

A. Deny that any meeting occurred and alter the attendance roster.  
B. Acknowledge the process failure, explain the actual decision rationale, identify where inputs did or did not change the outcome, and rebuild a transparent process for future decisions.  
C. Punish anyone who questions the façade as disloyal.  
D. Promise unanimous private veto power to every member forever.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 16.13 and 16.19 supply legitimacy/Level III risk context; they do not score apology technique. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it restores honesty and process legitimacy. Chapter 1 accountability and respect provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A falsifies records to hide a legitimacy failure. Chapter 16 stresses transparent weighing of perspectives; Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It conceals process failure with false records.
- **B — Most effective:** It owns the error and rebuilds fair process.
- **C — Ineffective:** It replaces legitimacy with coercion.
- **D — Ineffective:** It overcorrects into unworkable Culture of No dynamics.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶16.13, 16.19 (fairness/Level III context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Procedural legitimacy; honesty; accountability  
**ADTC:** 16C, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Command decision authorities control real schedule changes

## C16-SJT-008 — Empowerment versus empty delegation

A section chief “delegates” a problem but micromanages every click, then blames the team for lacking ownership. AFH 1 states delegation is not empowerment; empowerment requires freedom/authority to creatively accomplish tasks, plus trust and appropriate recognition.

### Response options

A. Keep micromanaging and publicly blame the team for “not wanting ownership.”  
B. Clarify constraints and intent, grant real authority within boundaries, provide support/resources, and recognize contributions that advance the mission.  
C. Remove all standards and call that empowerment.  
D. Take back the task secretly while still claiming the team owns it in briefing slides.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 16.21 supplies empowerment context; it does not score leadership style as an official key. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it pairs clear constraints with genuine ownership and recognition. Chapter 1 loyalty, respect, and mission stewardship provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D fakes ownership while retaining covert control—dishonest toward both the team and the chain. Chapter 16’s trust cornerstone and Chapter 1 integrity support the least-effective judgment.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It denies real empowerment then blames followers.
- **B — Most effective:** It enables constrained ownership with support and recognition.
- **C — Ineffective:** It confuses empowerment with abandonment of standards.
- **D — Least effective:** It misrepresents ownership.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶16.21 (empowerment context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Empowerment; trust; honesty  
**ADTC:** 16D, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Supervisory authorities and local constraints control real tasking
