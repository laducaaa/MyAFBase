# Chapter 8 Situational Judgment Practice

> **Status:** Draft and provisional for 27E5/27E6. Chapter 8 is shared core in the historical 26E5/26E6 catalog scopes.
>
> These scenarios are wholly original. They are not actual, recalled, reconstructed, or predicted PFE items. The official Air Force uses subject-matter experts and behavioral scientists to establish operational SJT scoring keys. Chapter 8 describes assessments, evaluations, reenlistments, and awards facts, but those facts alone do not establish an official behavioral scoring key. All most-effective and least-effective keys below are MyPromotion educational judgments grounded in Chapter 1 professionalism, core values, and ethical reasoning, with Chapter 8 material supplying context only. They are not official scoring keys or substitutes for current command, supervisory, safety, legal, ethics, evaluation, reenlistment, awards, or reporting procedures.
>
> **Rank / ADTC use:** Metadata reflects consolidated ADTC levels (8A/8B B for SSgt and C for TSgt; 8C/8D B for both). Scenarios that emphasize TSgt application of feedback/evaluation standards are tagged accordingly; SSgt study still uses the shared behavioral practice set.
>
> **Two-track source rule:** For exam-source study, preserve the facts in the AFH edition assigned by the controlling catalog. For duty use, separately verify current evaluation, reenlistment, and awards rules; duty-current information does not overwrite a catalog-controlled answer.

## How to use this set

Each scenario produces two items:

1. Select the **most effective** response.
2. Select the **least effective** response.

Read every option before choosing. Evaluate whether the response:

- represents performance, feedback, and recognition facts honestly;
- protects evaluation, counseling, and award-documentation integrity;
- uses supervisory authority without coercion, favoritism, or record falsification;
- stays within stated role and delegated authority;
- treats Airmen fairly in SRP, awards, and feedback decisions;
- respects people, process owners, and the chain of command; and
- reflects integrity, accountability, loyalty, respect, and mission stewardship.

Chapter 8 supplies assessment and recognition context; it does not prescribe the behavioral sequence in every response. When a rationale depends on general professional judgment—such as honesty, courage, accountability, loyalty, respect, mission focus, or teamwork—it is identified as an application of the cited foundational Chapter 1 paragraphs.

Ten scenarios produce 20 response selections, mirroring the historical public structure announced for the [22E5/22E6 cycles in December 2021](https://www.afpc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/2874484/waps-adjusts-for-22e6-22e5-testing-cycles/). The separate [public SJT informational guide](https://www.studyguides.af.mil/Portals/15/documents/Notices/Situational-Judgment-Test-Informational-Guide.pdf) explains the four-option most/least format. The 2027 structure is unconfirmed. This set covers only selected Chapter 8 concepts and is not a full-spectrum SJT simulation.

## C08-SJT-001 — Honest ACA feedback

You are a rater preparing a mid-term Airman Comprehensive Assessment for a Technical Sergeant whose production metrics are strong but whose recent counseling record shows repeated missed suspenses that affected a supported unit. The ratee asks you to mark every performance area at the top block and keep deficiencies “off the worksheet” so the upcoming evaluation looks clean. Face-to-face feedback is scheduled today, and AFH 1 requires honest, realistic, specific feedback.

### Response options

A. Mark every area at the top block and omit the missed-suspense pattern because the metrics look good.  
B. Deliver impartial, specific feedback that reinforces strong production and clearly addresses the missed-suspense pattern and expectations before evaluation close-out; document the session on the correct ACA worksheet.  
C. Cancel the session and tell the ratee feedback is optional when metrics are favorable.  
D. Promise top-block marks in exchange for the ratee taking your least desirable weekend duty.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 8.1–8.3 supply ACA purpose, conduct, and documentation context; they do not create an official behavioral scoring key. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it applies honest, specific two-way feedback and preserves an accurate developmental record. Chapter 1 honesty, courage, and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D trades evaluation-related feedback for a personal duty bargain and corrupts both feedback integrity and fairness. Chapter 8’s honesty requirement does not authorize that bargain; Chapter 1 integrity, respect, and misuse-of-authority concerns provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It conceals a performance problem AFH 1 expects feedback to address specifically.
- **B — Most effective:** It balances recognition of strengths with honest improvement focus and proper documentation.
- **C — Ineffective:** Mid-term feedback remains a required supporting action; favorable metrics do not waive it.
- **D — Least effective:** It conditions feedback outcomes on a personal duty trade.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶8.1–8.3 (ACA context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Honest ACA feedback; integrity; courage; accountability  
**ADTC:** 8A, C—Applying  
**Ranks:** TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of counseling realism; AFI 36-2406 and command procedures control real feedback/evaluation actions

## C08-SJT-002 — Evaluation integrity

You draft an enlisted performance brief. A coworker suggests omitting a substantiated Letter of Reprimand for improper sexually suggestive and harassing comments “because the Airman is otherwise talented,” and replacing any comment with “an error in judgment led to an off-duty incident.” AFH 1 requires specific adverse comments and warns that failing to document misconduct that deviates from core values is a disservice to Airmen who serve with honor.

### Response options

A. Omit the misconduct entirely to protect the Airman’s career competitiveness.  
B. Use only the vague “error in judgment” phrase with no event or corrective action.  
C. Include a specific comment outlining the harassing comments and the Letter of Reprimand, and complete required referral/adverse documentation processes through the proper chain.  
D. Enter classified investigative details in an unprotected attachment to “prove seriousness.”

### Most effective: C

Paragraph 8.9 supplies adverse-documentation context; it does not score the integrity choice by itself. C is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it preserves fair, specific, required documentation. Chapter 1 honesty, courage, and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A knowingly withholds misconduct documentation AFH 1 treats as necessary for fair evaluation of who is best qualified for higher responsibility. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It prioritizes competitive appearance over required honesty in the permanent record.
- **B — Ineffective:** AFH 1 labels that style of vagueness insufficient.
- **C — Most effective:** It documents the event and action specifically through proper channels.
- **D — Ineffective:** It risks improper handling of sensitive/classified information contrary to evaluation rules.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶8.5, 8.9 (evaluation/adverse context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Evaluation integrity; honesty; courage; accountability  
**ADTC:** 8B, C—Applying  
**Ranks:** TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of adverse-action realism; legal/command guidance controls real cases

## C08-SJT-003 — SRP fairness

You supervise a first-term Airman approaching the selective reenlistment window. Duty performance has been consistently substandard, and the personnel record includes unfavorable information you have verified. A peer asks you to recommend retention anyway because “manpower is short” and to hide the unfavorable information from the commander. AFH 1 expects supervisors to evaluate duty performance and review records, including DAF Form 1137 if applicable, before recommending career potential.

### Response options

A. Recommend retention and withhold the unfavorable information so the commander never sees it.  
B. Refuse to make any recommendation and tell the Airman SRP does not apply to first-term Airmen.  
C. Provide an honest recommendation based on performance, standards compliance, and the reviewed record—including unfavorable information—so the commander can make an informed SRP decision.  
D. Promise the Airman selection if the Airman agrees not to appeal any future non-selection.

### Most effective: C

Paragraphs 8.10–8.11 supply SRP context; they do not create a behavioral key for withholding information. C is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it gives the commander a fair, complete basis for retention decisions. Chapter 1 honesty, loyalty to the institution, and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A conceals substantiated unfavorable information and distorts a force-management decision. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It falsifies the decision picture by hiding adverse information.
- **B — Ineffective:** First-term Airmen are expressly covered by SRP consideration timing.
- **C — Most effective:** It aligns supervisory recommendation with the factors commanders must consider.
- **D — Ineffective:** It bargains away appeal rights and conditions honesty on silence.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶8.10–8.11 (SRP context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** SRP fairness; honesty; loyalty; accountability  
**ADTC:** 8C, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of SRP realism; DAFI 36-2606 and command procedures control real decisions

## C08-SJT-004 — Award nomination honesty

You are drafting a decoration recommendation for an Airman who completed a demanding project. A teammate wants the justification to claim solo leadership of a combat rescue that never occurred, arguing “stronger bullets win.” You have firsthand knowledge of the real achievement and may revise the package before it enters the chain of command. AFH 1 requires concrete examples of the action performed and prohibits including CUI/classified/special-category information in regular decoration recommendations.

### Response options

A. Insert the fabricated combat-rescue claim because award boards “expect bold wording.”  
B. Keep only verified accomplishments with concrete examples, route through the supervisor/chain, and exclude unauthorized sensitive content.  
C. Submit the package with blank justification and let reviewers invent achievements.  
D. Self-nominate yourself as co-recipient by altering the Airman’s form without chain awareness.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 8.22 supplies decoration-recommendation context; it does not itself score honesty. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it preserves truthful recognition grounded in firsthand knowledge. Chapter 1 honesty and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A knowingly falsifies valor/achievement content. Chapter 8’s concrete-example standard does not authorize fabrication; Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It converts recognition into a false record.
- **B — Most effective:** It nominates honestly within process and content rules.
- **C — Ineffective:** It abandons the justification responsibility the process requires.
- **D — Ineffective:** It misuses the nomination process and misrepresents recipients.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶8.22 (decorations context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Award nomination honesty; integrity; accountability  
**ADTC:** 8D, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of awards realism; DAFMAN 36-2806 controls real nominations

## C08-SJT-005 — Documentation accuracy

An evaluation package is due tomorrow. Two performance statements still lack outcomes, and a date in the administrative block is wrong. The ratee is available to review the draft now. AFH 1 requires performance statements with action and impact, and it expects ratees to review evaluations for administrative errors before they become a matter of record.

### Response options

A. Submit as-is and tell the ratee to “just sign” without reading because the deadline is tight.  
B. Invent outcome metrics you cannot verify so every bullet looks complete.  
C. Correct the administrative date, finish each performance statement with verified impact/results, and give the ratee time to review for administrative errors before signature.  
D. Backdate the close-out and hide the unfinished sections by copying another Airman’s bullets.

### Most effective: C

Paragraphs 8.8–8.9 supply review and performance-statement context. C is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it restores required content accuracy and preserves the ratee’s administrative review. Chapter 1 honesty and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D falsifies dates and appropriates another Airman’s performance. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It blocks required administrative review and risks known errors becoming of record.
- **B — Ineffective:** Unverified invented results corrupt the performance record.
- **C — Most effective:** It fixes content and process defects before signature.
- **D — Least effective:** It combines falsification with plagiarism of performance.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶8.8–8.9 (evaluation documentation context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Documentation accuracy; honesty; accountability  
**ADTC:** 8B, B—Understanding

## C08-SJT-006 — Counseling timing

Supervision began 50 days ago. Initial ACA is not yet done. The ratee asks for feedback now on standards and goals. A peer says to wait until an MPF notice arrives, even if that is after day 60. AFH 1 requires initial feedback within the first 60 days of supervision and states failure to receive a notice does not justify failing to hold a required session.

### Response options

A. Wait indefinitely for an MPF notice before any initial feedback.  
B. Schedule and conduct the initial ACA immediately within the 60-day window, using the correct worksheet and honest two-way discussion of standards, responsibilities, expectations, and goals.  
C. Tell the ratee initial feedback is never required after day 45.  
D. Conduct feedback only by unprotected group text with no worksheet because “timing matters more than process.”

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 8.1 and 8.4 supply timing and purpose context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it meets the required window without using notice failure as an excuse. Chapter 1 accountability and respect provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A treats a reminder notice as a prerequisite and risks missing the mandatory 60-day initial window. Chapter 1 accountability provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It invents a notice prerequisite AFH 1 rejects.
- **B — Most effective:** It satisfies timing, medium, and content expectations.
- **C — Ineffective:** It invents an exemption inconsistent with the 60-day rule.
- **D — Partially effective urgency, ineffective execution:** Speed without proper documentation/process undermines the ACA purpose.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶8.1, 8.4 (ACA timing context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Counseling timing; accountability; respect  
**ADTC:** 8A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of scheduling realism; local tracking mechanisms control real calendars

## C08-SJT-007 — Two-track policy

You prepare a study handout on Selective Retention Bonus zones using AFH 1, dated 15 February 2025, which lists Zones A/B/C/E and a $100,000 maximum per zone. A teammate finds an unofficial post claiming Zone E now pays an unlimited amount and wants the handout changed before tonight’s optional study group. The career assistance advisor can confirm current SRB policy tomorrow; no one is reenlisting tonight.

### Response options

A. Replace the AFH maximum with “unlimited” and present it as settled current policy.  
B. Keep the AFH 1 zone and $100,000-per-zone snapshot as the exam-source baseline, label it as such, note that duty-current SRB rules may differ, and direct confirmation with the career assistance advisor before any reenlistment/bonus action.  
C. Delete all SRB numbers and tell Airmen policy research is unnecessary for WAPS or duty.  
D. Tell Airmen to claim unlimited Zone E money on paperwork tonight so finance will “catch up later.”

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 8.15 supplies the SRB snapshot; it does not adjudicate every later policy change. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it preserves exam-source fidelity while routing duty actions to confirmation. Chapter 1 honesty, accountability, and disciplined management of uncertainty provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D encourages unsupported benefit claims before confirmation. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It overwrites the designated baseline with unverified social media.
- **B — Most effective:** It keeps exam-source and duty-current tracks distinct.
- **C — Partially effective caution, ineffective extremity:** Removing numbers avoids rumor but denies sourced study content.
- **D — Least effective:** It pushes premature, unsupported financial claims.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶8.15 (SRB context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Two-track policy; honesty; accountability; uncertainty management  
**ADTC:** 8C, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Preserve $100,000/zone snapshot; current DAFI 36-2606/AFPC listings control real SRB actions

## C08-SJT-008 — Avoiding test compromise adjacent to evals

Your section is studying Chapter 8 for WAPS while several members also have evaluations closing out. A peer offers what they claim is a “recalled” set of exact current PFE questions on evaluations and SRP and asks you to distribute them in the work center chat “to help the flight,” then use the same bullets as mandatory evaluation text. You may share original study materials you created from AFH 1, but you must not traffic in claimed exam compromise material.

### Response options

A. Forward the claimed recalled questions to the flight chat and tell everyone to memorize them for both the test and evaluation wording.  
B. Refuse to distribute claimed exam questions, remind the team to study from authorized materials such as AFH 1, and keep evaluation content based on actual performance rather than alleged test items.  
C. Trade the alleged question file for preferential evaluation stratification language.  
D. Say nothing while the file circulates, then quietly use it yourself.

### Most effective: B

Chapter 8 evaluation and SRP facts are legitimate study content; claimed recalled exam items are not a valid study method and threaten test integrity. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it rejects compromise adjacent to evaluation work and keeps ratings tied to real performance. Chapter 1 integrity and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: C

C pairs exam compromise with corrupt evaluation influence. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** Distributing claimed recalled questions risks test compromise and contaminates evaluation writing.
- **B — Most effective:** It protects exam integrity and evaluation honesty together.
- **C — Least effective:** It bargains compromised material for unfair evaluation advantage.
- **D — Ineffective:** Silence enables compromise and still uses tainted material.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶8.5–8.11 (evaluation/SRP study context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Avoiding test compromise adjacent to evals; integrity; accountability  
**ADTC:** 8B, B—Understanding

### Option analysis

- **C — Partially effective anti-favoritism impulse, ineffective extremity:** Avoiding all recognition abandons legitimate award stewardship.
- **D — Ineffective:** It converts recognition into a personal duty bargain.

You are a Technical Sergeant rater. Mid-term ACA is due for a Staff Sergeant who asks you to skip face-to-face feedback and instead send a one-line text: “You’re doing fine—sign the blank form later.” The ratee also wants you to ignore a pattern of late work because “the evaluation is what matters.” AFH 1 expects impartial, honest, specific face-to-face (or authorized alternate) feedback that sets expectations before the evaluation, with the mid-term routed as a supporting document.

### Response options

A. Send the one-line text, leave the ACA blank, and promise an inflated evaluation later.  
B. Conduct a private, honest, specific feedback session on the correct worksheet that reinforces strengths, addresses the late-work pattern with clear expectations, and retains the mid-term as a supporting document for the evaluation process.  
C. Have an unrelated Airman from another flight complete and sign the ACA as if they were the rater.  
D. Tell the ratee that TSgts are not allowed to give feedback to SSgts.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 8.1–8.4 supply ACA conduct and timing context for TSgt-level application. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it applies feedback standards rather than treating the evaluation as a substitute for counseling. Chapter 1 honesty, courage, respect, and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A abandons required feedback content and process while promising an inflated record. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It replaces honest ACA standards with empty assurance and evaluation inflation.
- **B — Most effective:** It applies the feedback standards AFH 1 describes before evaluation close-out.
- **C — Ineffective:** It falsifies rater identity/responsibility.
- **D — Ineffective:** It invents a grade restriction inconsistent with rater responsibilities.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶8.1–8.4 (ACA application context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Applying feedback standards; honesty; courage; accountability
