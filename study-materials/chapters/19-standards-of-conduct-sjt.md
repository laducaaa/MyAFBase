# Chapter 19 Situational Judgment Practice

> **Status:** Draft and provisional for 27E5/27E6. Chapter 19 is **shared core**: historically in both **26E5** and **26E6** catalog scope.
>
> These scenarios are wholly original. They are not actual, recalled, reconstructed, or predicted PFE items. The official Air Force uses subject-matter experts and behavioral scientists to establish operational SJT scoring keys. Chapter 19 describes standards of conduct, law-of-war facts, and Code of Conduct facts, but those facts alone do not establish an official behavioral scoring key. All most-effective and least-effective keys below are MyPromotion educational judgments grounded in Chapter 1 professionalism, core values, and ethical reasoning, with Chapter 19 material supplying context only. They are not official scoring keys or substitutes for current command, legal, ROE, LOAC, detention, or reporting procedures.
>
> **Not legal advice:** These scenarios are promotion-study practice only. They are not legal advice, ROE authority, LOAC counsel, Code of Conduct certification, or detention/hostage guidance.
>
> **Two-track source rule:** For exam-source study, preserve the facts in the AFH edition assigned by the controlling catalog. For duty use, separately verify current law-of-war, ROE, Code of Conduct, ethics, and publications guidance; duty-current information does not overwrite a catalog-controlled answer.

## How to use this set

Each scenario produces two items:

1. Select the **most effective** response.
2. Select the **least effective** response.

Read every option before choosing. Evaluate whether the response:

- represents standards, law-of-war, and Code of Conduct facts honestly;
- protects people, mission discipline, and accountability;
- refuses selective enforcement, concealment of violations, or illegal orders;
- stays within stated role and uses proper reporting channels;
- uses the two-track caution when source snapshots may differ from current rules; and
- reflects integrity, accountability, loyalty, respect, and mission stewardship.

Chapter 19 supplies standards-of-conduct context; it does not prescribe the behavioral sequence in every response. When a rationale depends on general professional judgment—such as honesty, courage, accountability, loyalty, respect, mission focus, or teamwork—it is identified as an application of the cited foundational Chapter 1 paragraphs.

Ten scenarios produce 20 response selections, mirroring the historical public structure announced for the [22E5/22E6 cycles in December 2021](https://www.afpc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/2874484/waps-adjusts-for-22e6-22e5-testing-cycles/). The separate [public SJT informational guide](https://www.studyguides.af.mil/Portals/15/documents/Notices/Situational-Judgment-Test-Informational-Guide.pdf) explains the four-option most/least format. The 2027 structure is unconfirmed. This set covers only selected Chapter 19 concepts and is not a full-spectrum SJT simulation.

## C19-SJT-001 — Off-duty standard honesty

You supervise a Staff Sergeant who argues that off-duty misconduct “doesn’t count” because it happened on a weekend and “AFI standards are for the office.” AFH 1 states USAF employees must comply with prescribed standards in official matters and when off-duty, and that members adhere to higher standards than civilian life.

### Response options

A. Agree that weekends are a free zone and tell the Airman to hide the issue from leadership.  
B. Correct the misconception: standards apply on and off duty; reinforce honesty, responsibility, and accountability; and address the conduct through the proper chain without selective excuses.  
C. Publicly mock the Airman in a group chat to “make an example.”  
D. Ignore the issue because documenting it might be inconvenient.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 19.1 supplies the on-/off-duty and higher-standards context; it does not score the leadership choice. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it restores accurate standards and accountability without humiliation. Chapter 1 honesty, accountability, and respect provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A knowingly endorses a false “off-duty free zone” and concealment. Chapter 1 integrity and ¶19.1’s off-duty obligation provide the least-effective basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** Excuses and conceals misconduct.
- **B — Most effective:** Corrects the standard, preserves dignity, uses proper channels.
- **C — Ineffective:** Public humiliation undermines respect and due process norms.
- **D — Ineffective:** Avoidance abandons accountability.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶19.1 (standards context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Off-duty standards; honesty; accountability  
**ADTC:** 19A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of supervisory realism; current disciplinary and legal advice control real cases

## C19-SJT-002 — Selective enforcement

Two Airmen commit the same clear standards violation. A peer urges you to “go easy on the high performer” and “make an example of the other one.” AFH 1 requires standards to be uniformly known, consistently applied, and non-selectively enforced.

### Response options

A. Punish only the less popular Airman and quietly excuse the high performer.  
B. Apply the standard consistently to both, document facts, and use appropriate processes without favoritism.  
C. Announce on social media that you will invent harsher rules for one Airman only.  
D. Tell both Airmen standards do not matter as long as the mission looks good on paper.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 19.4 supplies non-selective enforcement context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it preserves fairness and public trust. Chapter 1 integrity, justice-minded accountability, and respect provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A is selective enforcement by popularity/performance favoritism. Chapter 1 integrity and ¶19.4 provide the least-effective basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** Selective enforcement.
- **B — Most effective:** Consistent, fact-based accountability.
- **C — Ineffective:** Public, arbitrary escalation.
- **D — Ineffective:** Abandons standards entirely.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶19.4 (accountability context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Non-selective enforcement; accountability; integrity  
**ADTC:** 19A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Current disciplinary procedures control real enforcement

## C19-SJT-003 — General Orders compliance

While deployed, a coworker proposes ignoring a General Order restriction because “we do that at home all the time” and suggests the team hide the violation. AFH 1 links General Orders to host-nation relations, good order and discipline, and possible UCMJ consequences for noncompliance.

### Response options

A. Join the violation and help conceal it to keep morale “fun.”  
B. Refuse, remind the team that General Orders bind the deployed force, and report/stop the planned violation through the proper chain.  
C. Post the planned violation plan on a public commercial site.  
D. Argue that General Orders replace the need for any other standard forever.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 19.3 supplies General Orders context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it protects discipline, host-nation relations, and accountability. Chapter 1 integrity, courage, and loyalty provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A both violates and conceals. Chapter 1 integrity and ¶19.3 compliance expectations provide the least-effective basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** Violates and conceals.
- **B — Most effective:** Stops harm and uses proper channels.
- **C — Ineffective:** Publicizes misconduct.
- **D — Ineffective:** Misstates the role of General Orders.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶19.3 (General Orders context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Deployed discipline; integrity; reporting  
**ADTC:** 19A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Current General Orders and command guidance control real deployed conduct

## C19-SJT-004 — Clearly illegal order / LOAC

A peer claims a supervisor “said to keep hitting” a captured, severely wounded enemy combatant who is *hors de combat* and no longer resisting. AFH 1 states humanity forbids unnecessary suffering once a military purpose is achieved, and members must refuse clearly illegal orders to commit law-of-war violations.

### Response options

A. Follow the claimed order without question and continue the attack.  
B. Refuse the clearly illegal action, protect the hors-de-combat person from further attack, and promptly report through the proper authority.  
C. Film the abuse for entertainment and share it.  
D. Tell everyone LOAC does not apply to DoD personnel.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 19.6 and 19.9 supply humanity and illegal-order/reporting context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it refuses illegality and reports. Chapter 1 courage, integrity, and respect for humanity provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A complies with a clearly illegal law-of-war violation. Chapter 1 integrity and ¶19.9 refusal duty provide the least-effective basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** Executes a clearly illegal act.
- **B — Most effective:** Refuses, protects, reports.
- **C — Ineffective:** Compounds abuse and exploitation.
- **D — Ineffective:** False statement about LOAC applicability.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶19.6, 19.9 (LOAC/humanity/reporting context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Humanity; illegal orders; reporting  
**ADTC:** 19B, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Not legal advice; current LOAC/ROE/JA guidance control real use-of-force decisions

## C19-SJT-005 — Reporting a suspected LOAC violation

You learn information that might reasonably be viewed as a law-of-war violation involving personnel in your unit. A coworker says to “stay quiet because reporting is career suicide.” AFH 1 requires prompt reporting to the immediate commander or proper authority.

### Response options

A. Stay silent and delete your notes so nothing can be traced.  
B. Promptly report to your immediate commander or the proper authority (or next higher U.S. command if a U.S. commander may be involved), preserving available facts.  
C. Accuse random bystanders publicly without facts.  
D. Trade silence for a personal favor from the coworker.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 19.9 supplies reporting-duty context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it meets the prompt-reporting obligation. Chapter 1 courage, integrity, and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A conceals a possible law-of-war violation. Chapter 1 integrity and ¶19.9 provide the least-effective basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** Concealment.
- **B — Most effective:** Prompt proper-channel reporting.
- **C — Ineffective:** Reckless false accusation.
- **D — Ineffective:** Corrupt bargain to suppress reporting.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶19.9 (reporting context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** LOAC reporting; courage; accountability  
**ADTC:** 19B, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Current reporting channels and legal advice control real allegations

## C19-SJT-006 — Distinction and civilian objects

During a planning discussion, someone jokes that a clearly marked hospital with no military use “is close enough to shoot anyway for convenience.” AFH 1 protects hospitals as civilian/protected objects unless used for military purposes and requires distinction.

### Response options

A. Laugh and encourage targeting the hospital for convenience.  
B. Stop the joke-as-plan, restate that protected objects such as hospitals are not lawful objects of attack when not used for military purposes, and elevate for proper targeting/JA review if a real question exists.  
C. Propose using civilians as shields “to keep the other side honest.”  
D. Say distinction only applies to finance regulations.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 19.6 and 19.8 supply distinction/protected-object context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it rejects unlawful targeting talk and seeks proper review. Chapter 1 integrity, courage, and mission discipline provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: C

C proposes human shielding, a fundamental distinction violation. Chapter 1 integrity and ¶19.6 provide the least-effective basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** Encourages unlawful targeting.
- **B — Most effective:** Corrects and escalates properly.
- **C — Least effective:** Proposes human shields.
- **D — Ineffective:** Misstates distinction.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶19.6, 19.8 (distinction/protected objects context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Distinction; protected objects; integrity  
**ADTC:** 19B, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Not targeting authority; current ROE/LOAC/JA processes control real targeting

## C19-SJT-007 — ROE self-defense de-escalation honesty

In training, a teammate argues that de-escalation never matters and that “maximum force first” is always required even when time and circumstances clearly permit a warning. AFH 1 states that when time and circumstances permit, forces committing hostile acts or demonstrating hostile intent should be warned and given opportunity to withdraw or cease.

### Response options

A. Teach that warnings are never allowed under any standing ROE.  
B. Correct the overstatement: when time and circumstances permit, warn and allow withdrawal/cessation; still preserve the inherent right of self-defense and follow current mission ROE.  
C. Invent a personal ROE that ignores the President/SecDef-approved standing ROE.  
D. Tell trainees ROE are optional suggestions like nondirective pamphlets.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 19.10 supplies de-escalation and self-defense context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it preserves both de-escalation when feasible and lawful self-defense. Chapter 1 honesty and disciplined mission focus provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: C

C fabricates unauthorized ROE. Chapter 1 integrity and ¶19.10 authority chain provide the least-effective basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** False absolute.
- **B — Most effective:** Accurate de-escalation + self-defense balance.
- **C — Least effective:** Invents unauthorized ROE.
- **D — Ineffective:** Misstates ROE as optional.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶19.10 (ROE/self-defense context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** De-escalation; ROE honesty; self-defense  
**ADTC:** 19B, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Current ROE/training materials control real force application

## C19-SJT-008 — Code of Conduct vs UCMJ clarity

An Airman preparing for a high-risk assignment says, “The Code of Conduct is just vibes; nothing in it can ever matter under the UCMJ.” AFH 1 describes the Code as a moral code not criminally punishable per se, but actions that also violate the UCMJ may be punished, and failure to follow the Code could lead to UCMJ misconduct.

### Response options

A. Agree that the Code is meaningless and advise ignoring Articles III–V if captured.  
B. Explain the moral-code vs legal-code distinction accurately: Code violations are not punishable per se, but overlapping UCMJ violations can be; urge completing the appropriate Code training level for the assignment’s capture/exploitation risk.  
C. Tell the Airman to sign a parole agreement in advance “to make captivity easier.”  
D. Replace Code training with a social-media quiz invented by peers.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 19.11–19.12 supply Code/UCMJ and training-level context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it is factually accurate and training-focused. Chapter 1 honesty and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A dismisses the Code and encourages ignoring resistance/escape/faithfulness duties. Chapter 1 integrity and ¶¶19.11–19.12 provide the least-effective basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** Rejects Code duties.
- **B — Most effective:** Accurate distinction + proper training path.
- **C — Ineffective:** Encourages parole contrary to Article III.
- **D — Ineffective:** Replaces required training with unofficial content.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶19.11–19.12 (Code/UCMJ context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Moral vs legal code; training levels; integrity  
**ADTC:** 19C, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Not legal advice; current Code of Conduct training requirements control real qualification

## C19-SJT-009 — Keeping faith / no informing

In a SERE-style discussion, a peer jokes that if captured, “the smart move is to point out who has the classified background so you get better treatment.” AFH 1 forbids informing or actions harmful to fellow prisoners and forbids seeking special favors at others’ expense.

### Response options

A. Endorse informing on fellow prisoners to gain favors.  
B. Reject the joke’s premise: keep faith with fellow prisoners, give no information harmful to comrades, refuse special favors at others’ expense, and organize under the senior military POW.  
C. Advise writing propaganda confessions early “to get it over with.”  
D. Say Article IV applies only to officers.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 19.12 Articles III–IV supply keep-faith / no-informing / no-special-favors / senior-command context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it preserves loyalty to fellow prisoners. Chapter 1 loyalty, integrity, and courage provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A endorses informing for personal advantage. Chapter 1 loyalty and ¶19.12 Article IV provide the least-effective basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** Informing for favors.
- **B — Most effective:** Keep faith; refuse exploitation bargains; military organization.
- **C — Ineffective:** Encourages harmful statements.
- **D — Ineffective:** Misstates Article IV applicability.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶19.12 (Articles III–IV context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Keep faith; no informing; loyalty  
**ADTC:** 19C, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Training discussion only; current SERE/Code guidance controls real preparation

## C19-SJT-010 — OOTW detention representation

A teammate role-playing unfriendly-government detention says the first move is a televised “confession” for crimes never committed, and that asking for the U.S. embassy is “weak.” AFH 1 directs asking immediately and continually for U.S. embassy or allied/neutral representation, limiting further discussion, and avoiding statements/documents that enable exploitation.

### Response options

A. Support volunteering a false televised confession for the detainer’s propaganda.  
B. Correct the role-play: ask immediately/continually for U.S. embassy or allied/neutral representation; provide name, rank, service number, date of birth, and innocent circumstances; limit further talk to health/welfare, fellow detainees, and going home; avoid statements/documents.  
C. Recommend escaping immediately in every government-detention case contrary to the stated DoD-policy caution.  
D. Tell the teammate to debate the detainer’s politics and religion aggressively.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 19.13 supplies OOTW government-detention guidance. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it matches AFH representation and anti-exploitation limits. Chapter 1 discipline, integrity, and loyalty provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A enables political exploitation through a false confession. Chapter 1 integrity and ¶19.13 provide the least-effective basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** Feeds exploitation/propaganda.
- **B — Most effective:** Representation + limited disclosures + avoid statements.
- **C — Ineffective:** Ignores DoD-policy escape caution for government detention (except life-threatening circumstances).
- **D — Ineffective:** Inflames/exploitable debate (closer to terrorist-hostage “avoid topics” caution; still unhelpful in government detention exploitation).

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶19.13 (OOTW detention context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Embassy representation; anti-exploitation; two-track caution  
**ADTC:** 19C, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Not detention advice; current DoD/USAF isolation/detention guidance controls real situations
