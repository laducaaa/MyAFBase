## Why this chapter matters

Chapter 12 connects personal fiscal fitness to institutional stewardship:

1. **Individual finances:** budgets, LES vigilance, pay, allowances, deductions, and financial obligations.
2. **Official travel:** PCS and TDY entitlements, vouchers, GTCC discipline.
3. **Manpower:** constrained end-strength, requirements, UMD, and workforce tools.
4. **Resources:** managers, PPBE, equipment and facilities, FWA prevention, liability investigations.
5. **Environment:** EMS culture, compliance support, and continuous improvement.

A useful chapter-wide sequence is:

> Fiscal fitness → entitlements and deductions → travel integrity → manpower requirements → resource roles and PPBE → property and FWA → environmental management

# Section 12A — Individual Finances and Allowances

## Learning objectives

At ADTC level B, an Airman should be able to:

1. Explain fiscal fitness, personal budget and debt management, LES review, timely travel vouchers, and GTCC use for official travel.
2. Explain basic pay, military pay date, and how prior service or misconduct can adjust pay date.
3. Describe the LES (DFAS Form 702), myPay uses, regular pay schedule, and local/partial/emergency partial payments.
4. Distinguish major allowances and entitlements (BAS, BAH, OHA, FSH, CRA, FSA, overseas station allowances, Basic Needs Allowance, CONUS COLA, special/incentive pays) as AFH 1 presents them.
5. Explain federal tax advantage for BAH/BAS and related source notes.
6. Distinguish involuntary and voluntary deductions, including TSP/BRS and SGLI snapshots.
7. Explain financial obligations, complaint handling, government debt collection, and waiver/remission concepts.

## 1. Individual financial responsibilities

According to AFI 1-1 as cited in AFH 1, “fiscal fitness” is as important to overall well-being as physical fitness. Two key aspects of individual financial responsibility are establishing a personal budget and effectively managing debt. Members should review Leave and Earnings Statements (LES) regularly, file travel vouchers promptly, and use the government travel charge card (GTCC) to pay costs related to official government travel. Official government travel means travel under official orders while performing duties pertaining to official government assignments such as temporary duty (TDY) and permanent change of station (PCS). (AFH 1, ¶12.1)

Additional information on financial responsibilities, pay and allowances, and related entitlements is found in the Joint Travel Regulation; DoD Financial Management Regulation (DoDFMR) 7000.14-R, Volume 7A, *Military Pay Policy—Active Duty and Reserve Pay*; and AFMAN 65-116, Volume 1, *Defense Joint Military Pay System Active Component (DJMSAC) Financial Management Flight (FMF) Procedures*. Those references are study pointers, not substitutes for current finance-office guidance. (AFH 1, ¶12.1)

> **MyPromotion memory aid:** **Budget → debt → LES → voucher → GTCC.** Unofficial personal-finance study cue, not counseling advice.

## 2. Military (basic) pay

Basic pay is the fundamental—and typically largest—component of military pay. Annual military pay raises are set by Congress and the U.S. President in the National Defense Authorization Act. In accordance with federal law, the 1 January annual pay raise automatically matches private-sector wage increases as measured by the Employment Cost Index for the previous year. If national emergencies or serious economic conditions affect general welfare, the President may propose that Congress instill a lower annual pay raise. (AFH 1, ¶12.2)

**Military pay date.** The military pay date determines length of service for pay purposes. In general, the pay date should be the same date the individual entered on RegAF status if there was no prior service before entering the USAF. If the individual previously served in certain governmental agencies, the USAF adjusts the pay date to reflect credit for those periods. Negative pay-date adjustments may result from absence without leave, desertion, and sickness or injury due to personal misconduct. (AFH 1, ¶12.2)

## 3. Leave and Earnings Statement

The LES, titled DFAS Form 702, *Defense Finance and Accounting Service Military Leave and Earnings Statement*, is a comprehensive statement of entitlements, deductions, allotments, leave information, tax withholdings, and Traditional and Roth Thrift Savings Plan information. PCS is a time for Airmen to “apply increased attention” to their LES because entitlements such as BAH and COLA often change when moving duty locations, and PCS usually occurs more frequently than events such as birth, marriage, or divorce. The myPay system allows members to view their LES and initiate changes to selected items affecting pay. If pay varies significantly, consult the servicing finance office. (AFH 1, ¶12.3)

### Regular pay schedule

Military members are paid monthly and may receive payments once or twice per month. Members receive a statement (net pay advice) of net pay and the financial statement at mid-month if receiving a payment, and a comprehensive LES at month’s end via myPay. Statements are created centrally by DFAS. The pay system has processing cutoff dates; while they fluctuate, they generally occur around the 6th for mid-month and the 20th for end-of-month paydays. (AFH 1, ¶12.3)

### Local, partial, and emergency partial payments

Local cash payments are normally authorized only for overseas areas where on-base military banking facilities are not readily available. Exceptions may be granted for classified or contingency operations. The member’s commander may authorize immediate cash payments up to the amount of accrued entitlement to date when deemed appropriate to the mission. Non-cash partial payments may be made via electronic funds transfer, normally in two to three business days, limited to pay and allowances accrued to the payment date, and recouped in full on the next available payday. Under extenuating circumstances, a stateside member may receive an emergency partial payment if deemed time sensitive and required within 24 hours due to unforeseen circumstances. (AFH 1, ¶12.3)

## 4. Military allowances and entitlements

Allowances are provided for specific needs such as food or housing. Monetary allowances are provided when the government does not provide for that need. For example, members in government housing do not receive full housing allowances; members not in government housing receive allowances to assist with commercial housing costs. Many allowances are not taxable—an embedded benefit of military pay. A complete list of military benefits and entitlements is available on MyAirForceBenefits; the annual Personal Statement of Military Compensation (PSMC) on myPay can show total compensation for comparisons or credit applications. (AFH 1, ¶12.4)

**Basic Allowance for Subsistence (BAS).** A nontaxable allowance used to offset meal costs. Junior enlisted members in single-type government quarters at the permanent duty station are often required to eat in government dining facilities, receive BAS, and are charged the discounted meal rate deducted from pay. When certified by the commanding officer or designee, members may claim reimbursement for missed meals. (AFH 1, ¶12.4)

**Basic Allowance for Housing (BAH).** Intended to provide accurate and equitable housing compensation based on local civilian housing markets. Payable when government quarters are not provided. Many installations have privatized quarters; members in privatized quarters are entitled to BAH, with rental agreements typically requiring rent not exceeding the BAH entitlement paid via allotment. Members without dependents in government single-type quarters are entitled to partial BAH unless quarters (including government-leased) exceed minimum single-quarters standards for their grade. Members in single-type government quarters who pay court-ordered child support may qualify for BAH-differential. (AFH 1, ¶12.4)

**Overseas Housing Allowance (OHA).** A cost-reimbursement allowance helping defray housing costs at a permanent duty station outside the United States. Members are reimbursed actual rental costs not to exceed the maximum OHA rate for locality, grade, and dependency status. Move-in housing allowance (for those who qualify) is based on average move-in costs. Monthly OHA includes rent (up to the rental allowance) plus the utility/recurring maintenance allowance. (AFH 1, ¶12.4)

**Family Separation Housing (FSH).** Pays a member for added housing expenses from enforced separation from dependents. FSH-BAH is payable monthly equal to the without-dependent BAH rate for the member’s grade and permanent duty station. FSH-OHA is payable monthly up to the without-dependent OHA rate for grade and permanent duty station. (AFH 1, ¶12.4)

## 5. Clothing Replacement Allowance

Enlisted members receive an annual allowance to help maintain, repair, and replace initial-issue uniform items. Clothing replacement allowance-basic is a preliminary replacement allowance paid annually between the 6th and 36th month of RegAF status. Clothing replacement allowance-standard automatically replaces CRA-basic after 36 months of RegAF status. Entitlement depends on the “entered on RegAF status date” in the master military pay account. (AFH 1, ¶12.5)

## 6. Family Separation Allowance

Family separation allowance (FSA) is payable to members with dependents in addition to other allowances or per diem. It compensates qualified members for added expenses from enforced family separation. **FSA-restricted** applies when dependent transportation is not authorized at government expense and dependents do not live in the vicinity of the permanent duty station. **FSA-temporary** applies when a member is on TDY away from the permanent duty station continuously for more than 30 days and dependents are not residing at or near the TDY station, including members beginning TDY before reporting to the initial station of assignment. (AFH 1, ¶12.6)

## 7. Station allowances outside the continental United States

Overseas station allowances help defray higher-than-normal cost of living or housing costs overseas. Allowances authorized by DoD at certain overseas locations may include temporary lodging allowance and cost-of-living allowance. Members receive entitlement information during in-processing and may also consult local finance upon notification of a pending overseas assignment. (AFH 1, ¶12.7)

## 8. Basic Needs Allowance

### 2025 source snapshot—revalidate

The Basic Needs Allowance provides a taxable monthly allowance for Airmen and Guardians who voluntarily apply and whose Gross Household Income (GHI) and household size place them below 150 percent of Federal poverty guidelines (FPG) for their geographical location. (AFH 1, ¶12.8)

## 9. Federal tax advantage

Service members do not pay federal or state taxes on BAH or BAS, which significantly increases purchasing power for food and housing. In addition to the federal tax advantage, military members also enjoy combat tax exclusion and fully deductible mortgage interest. (AFH 1, ¶12.9)

## 10. CONUS Cost-of-Living Allowance

CONUS COLA is payable to members assigned to designated “high cost” areas within the CONUS. AFH 1 points to DoDFMR 7000.14-R, Volume 7A, Chapter 67 for additional information. (AFH 1, ¶12.10)

## 11. Special and incentive pay

Special and incentive pays recognize certain aspects of duty, such as hazardous duty incentive pay, imminent danger pay, special duty assignment pay, enlisted flying duty incentive pay, and hardship duty pay-location. Enlistment and retention bonuses are also considered special and incentive pay. (AFH 1, ¶12.11)

## 12. Involuntary deductions

Involuntary deductions are payroll deductions of certain amounts of pay imposed by law. (AFH 1, ¶12.12)

**Withholding income tax.** All pay is considered income for federal and state income tax purposes, including basic pay, incentive pay, special pay, lump-sum payment of accrued leave, and separation pay. The LES reflects current-month and year-to-date income for social security, federal income tax, and state income tax under “FICA TAXES,” “FED TAXES,” and “STATE TAXES.” (AFH 1, ¶12.12)

**FICA taxes.** The Federal Insurance Contributions Act requires federal agencies to withhold FICA (Social Security and Medicare) taxes from the basic pay of military members covered by the Social Security Act and to pay matching FICA taxes to the Social Security Administration. (AFH 1, ¶12.12)

**Federal Income Tax Withholding (FITW).** Used to provide for national programs such as defense, community development, and law enforcement. A member may authorize an additional monthly FITW amount. (AFH 1, ¶12.12)

**State Income Tax Withholding (SITW).** Refers to tax laws of the state where the member is a legal resident. Amount depends on state tax rate; one-time payments may also be subject to state tax. The state for tax purposes appears in the first column on the LES under STATE TAXES. (AFH 1, ¶12.12)

**Armed Forces Retirement Home (AFRH).** Monthly AFRH deductions from the pay of regular enlisted members, up to a maximum of $1, are set by the Secretary of Defense after consulting with the AFRH board. Deductions help support the U.S. Soldiers’, Airmen’s, and Naval Homes. (AFH 1, ¶12.12)

## 13. Voluntary deductions

Members may establish voluntary deductions such as allotments to administer personal finances, support family members, and pay insurance premiums. Certain discretionary allotments may be controlled through myPay. Nondiscretionary allotments have limited uses, such as charitable contributions, Air Force Aid Society loan repayments, garnishment for commercial debts, and delinquent travel charge card debt. Members are not authorized to start allotments for purchase, lease, or rental of personal property. (AFH 1, ¶12.13)

Allotments should be requested 30 days before the desired month to allow processing time. If paid twice a month, the allotment is normally deducted in equal amounts from mid-month and end-of-month pay; if paid once a month, the entire amount is deducted from the monthly paycheck. (AFH 1, ¶12.13)

### Thrift Savings Plan

TSP is a voluntary deduction—a retirement savings and investment plan established for federal employees under the Federal Employees’ Retirement Act of 1986, offering tax-deferred advantages similar to an IRA or 401(k). Traditional TSP contributions can come out of pay before taxes; Roth TSP contributions come out after income is taxed, and qualified future withdrawals of those contributions are tax-free because taxes were already paid. (AFH 1, ¶12.13)

### 2025 source snapshot—revalidate (BRS / TSP)

A member enlisted on or after 1 January 2018 is enrolled in the Blended Retirement System (BRS) and automatically enrolled in TSP at 5% individual contribution from monthly basic pay or inactive duty pay, beginning with the first pay period following the 60th day of service from Pay Entry Base Date (PEBD). If contributing from basic pay, the member may contribute bonuses, incentive, or special pay. As of January 2023, the maximum TSP contribution amount is $22,500 for deferred and Roth contributions and up to $66,000 total combined deferred/exempt Traditional and Roth contributions. Members may not contribute more than 92 percent of basic pay so required Social Security and Medicare deductions can be made. (AFH 1, ¶12.13)

### Servicemembers’ Group Life Insurance

### 2025 source snapshot—revalidate (SGLI)

Maximum SGLI coverage is $500,000; members are covered by law at the maximum rate unless they decline or reduce coverage. SGLI automatically covers an eligible member against death when on RegAF status and/or training for an ordered period of more than 30 days. Less coverage may be chosen in amounts divisible by $50,000, or no coverage, in writing. Family SGLI covers spouses and dependent children when the member also participates in SGLI, including military spouses. Spousal coverage has a monthly deduction based on coverage amount; each child is covered for $10,000 at no cost, and the member may not elect less than $10,000 per child. Premiums are deducted monthly; the military personnel section is OPR for administering the program. (AFH 1, ¶12.13)

## 14. Financial obligations

Military members will pay financial obligations in a proper and timely manner; provide adequate financial support of a spouse, child, or any other relative for which the member receives additional support allowances; comply with court-order or written-support-agreement financial support provisions; and respond to applications for involuntary allotments of pay within established suspense dates. DAFI 36-2906, *Personal Financial Responsibility*, provides administrative and management guidelines and rules. (AFH 1, ¶12.14)

**Handling complaints.** Complainants often address correspondence to the installation commander, Staff Judge Advocate, or force support squadron. Complaints are forwarded to the individual’s immediate commander, and the complainant is notified of the referral. Commanders will attempt to respond within 15 days. Failure to pay debts or support dependents can lead to administrative or disciplinary action. Commanders must actively monitor complaints until resolved. If the complaint reflects adversely on the member, the action should be included in the unfavorable information file. If the member has separated with no further military service or has retired, the complainant is notified that the member is no longer under USAF jurisdiction and the USAF is unable to assist. Retired members’ retirement pay can be garnished for child support or alimony. (AFH 1, ¶12.14)

**Federal government collection of debts.** A USAF member who owes debts to the government does not have to consent for collection. Generally, for debts exceeding $100, the individual must receive notification and a chance to repay before withholding; due process need not be completed before collection starts if estimated separation date is insufficient to complete collection and collection would be unlikely, or when collection can be completed within two monthly pay periods. The USAF may also collect debts involving any federal agency, unserved retention-bonus portions, delinquent hospital bills for family members, excess household-goods shipment, loss or damage to government property, and erroneous payments. (AFH 1, ¶12.14)

**Waiver and remission.** Members may request relief from valid debts by applying for waiver or remission; the local financial services office has guidance. For erroneous pay or allowances, a waiver may be granted when there is no indication of fraud, misrepresentation, fault, or lack of good faith by the member or any other person interested in obtaining the waiver; DFAS rules on all waivers. A RegAF or separated member, or the commander, may apply for remission of indebtedness; the USAF may not remit or cancel debt due to non-collection of court-martial forfeiture. Circumstances creating the debt, good faith, and financial hardship may be factors. (AFH 1, ¶12.14)

# Section 12B — Official Travel Expenses and Allowances

## Learning objectives

At ADTC level B, an Airman should be able to:

1. Explain that official travel costs may be reimbursable when regulations are followed accurately and timely.
2. Describe major PCS allowances and expense rules (advances, HHG, DLA, UAB, mobile home, POV, transportation modes, dependent travel, TLE/TLA).
3. Explain official travel reimbursement timing, claim certification, EFT, and split disbursement.
4. Distinguish TDY per diem, transportation, and miscellaneous expense concepts.
5. Explain GTCC purpose, APC role, mandatory use, PCS coordination, payment, abuse, and delinquency timelines as AFH 1 prints them.

## 1. Authorized expenses and allowances

Individuals often travel for PCS or TDY, including PME and training. Costs associated with official travel are often reimbursable, but individuals must understand and adhere to specific travel regulations accurately and within a timely manner. (AFH 1, ¶12.15)

## 2. Permanent Change of Station allowances and expenses

When ordered to PCS, members may receive travel allowances for transportation, lodging, household goods, and related expenses. Individually billed account (IBA) GTCC usage is authorized and highly encouraged for all PCS-related expenses. (AFH 1, ¶12.16)

**PCS advance payments.** Advance payments of pay and allowances provide funds for extraordinary expenses directly related to government-ordered relocation and not covered by other entitlements. A PCS advance is an advance of up to three months of basic pay, less mandatory deductions and debts currently being deducted. All Airmen E-3 and below must have immediate commander approval to receive advance pay. All Airmen who request a repayment period greater than 12 months or an amount greater than one month’s basic pay must have immediate commander approval. Repayment periods greater than 12 months are approved only in cases of financial hardship. (AFH 1, ¶12.16)

**Shipment of household goods.** A member ordered on PCS may ship household goods within certain weight limitations at government expense. Authorized weight allowances normally depend on grade and number of dependents. Members may be reimbursed for personally arranging shipment; the government cost limit is based on maximum HHG weight allowance. If the member transports goods in excess of the authorized weight allowance, payments are based on the authorized weight allowance. (AFH 1, ¶12.16)

**Dislocation Allowance (DLA).** Paid at a rate determined by the Secretary of Defense. Payable to all members with dependents when dependents relocate their household goods in conjunction with a PCS. Also payable to members without dependents if they are not assigned permanent government quarters upon arrival at the new permanent duty station. (AFH 1, ¶12.16)

**Unaccompanied baggage.** The portion of the PCS weight allowance ordinarily transported separately from bulk HHG, usually by an expedited mode. When the expedited mode is commercial air, a maximum of 1,000 pounds (net) may be transported. (AFH 1, ¶12.16)

**Mobile home shipment.** Members who own a mobile home should contact the traffic management office to arrange transportation in lieu of HHG transportation. In certain circumstances, members may arrange or contract personally for movement. (AFH 1, ¶12.16)

**POV shipment.** When authorized, members may ship one privately owned vehicle at government expense when ordered PCS to, from, or between overseas locations. POV storage may be provided or authorized when shipment is prohibited or restricted. (AFH 1, ¶12.16)

**Government-procured transportation.** Unless an authorizing/order-issuing official determines U.S.-certificated air carriers are unavailable, U.S.-certificated air carriers must be used for all commercial air transportation of persons or property when the government funds the air travel. Documentation explaining unavailability must be provided. Travel time for government conveyance (except government automobile) or common carriers obtained by government-procured transportation is allowed for actual time needed over the direct route, including necessary delays. (AFH 1, ¶12.16)

**Privately owned conveyance.** Uniformed service policy is to authorize/approve POC travel if acceptable to the member and advantageous to the government based on the facts. Other options include government conveyance or commercial carrier. Parking fees, ferry fares, and road/bridge/tunnel tolls are authorized over the most direct route. The member is also authorized a flat per diem rate for each PCS travel day between authorized points, up to allowable travel time. (AFH 1, ¶12.16)

**Personally procured transportation.** DoD policy mandates use of the commercial travel office for all official transportation requirements. A member who nonetheless procures common carrier transportation at personal expense is authorized reimbursement (except transoceanic travel, for which no reimbursement is authorized) up to the authorized amount, not exceeding cost for authorized transportation and accommodations over a usually traveled direct route. Consult the Joint Travel Regulation. (AFH 1, ¶12.16)

**Mixed modes.** When both government-procured and personally procured modes are used, the USAF uses a combination of JTR-governed rules; the local financial service office can provide guidance. (AFH 1, ¶12.16)

**Dependent travel.** A member receives monetary allowance in lieu of transportation and flat-rate per diem for the official distance dependents travel with the member by POV. If dependents purchase commercial common carrier transportation, the member may be reimbursed actual transportation cost not exceeding government cost for ordered travel, and receives a per diem allowance for dependents. When the USAF restricts dependent travel overseas, dependents may move at government expense to any approved/authorized change-of-station designated location within the CONUS or designated overseas location with special approval. (AFH 1, ¶12.16)

**Temporary Lodging Expense (TLE).** A member arriving or departing PCS at a CONUS location may receive TLE to help defray added living expenses while occupying temporary lodging. TLE is paid on a travel voucher. (AFH 1, ¶12.16)

**Temporary Lodging Allowance (TLA).** A member arriving or departing PCS at a location outside the CONUS may receive TLA for added temporary-lodging living expenses. TLA is paid in military pay and reflected on the LES. (AFH 1, ¶12.16)

> **MyPromotion distinguisher:** **TLE = CONUS / travel voucher; TLA = OCONUS / military pay (LES).** Unofficial cue only.

## 3. Official travel reimbursement

Travelers are responsible for electronically filing a voucher in the Defense Travel System (DTS) or preparing DD Form 1351-2, *Travel Voucher or Subvoucher*, to claim reimbursement for official travel within five business days upon return to permanent duty station. The traveler’s signature indicates the claim is accurate, statements are true and complete, and the traveler is aware of liability for filing a false claim. Electronic funds transfer is the mandatory settlement means. The split disbursement option in DTS will be used, permitting direct EFT payment to the GTCC vendor for IBA charges. For TDY of 45 days or more, an interim voucher or scheduled partial payments will be set up in DTS every 30 days with split disbursements for on-time payments. Extended TDY does not excuse on-time GTCC payments. (AFH 1, ¶12.17)

## 4. Temporary duty entitlements

**Per diem.** Helps defray quarters, meals, and certain incidentals. Rates depend on TDY location. Travelers are paid a prescribed amount for meals and incidental expenses plus the actual amount for lodging, not to exceed the maximum lodging rate for the location. Rates depend on availability of government facilities such as quarters and dining facilities. (AFH 1, ¶12.18)

**Transportation.** Policy mandates use of available commercial travel offices to arrange official travel. The mode used between points designated in the order determines the transportation entitlement. If authorized to travel at personal expense, reimbursement is limited to what the government would have incurred for the authorized mode. Consult the transportation office or commercial travel office. (AFH 1, ¶12.18)

**Miscellaneous expenses.** Reimbursable travel-related expenses include travel from home or lodging to the servicing transportation terminal by taxi, limousine, bus, or POV. Additional miscellaneous expenses include passports, visas, and rental vehicles when authorized on travel orders. (AFH 1, ¶12.18)

## 5. Government Travel Charge Card (GTCC)

The GTCC program facilitates and standardizes DoD travel, providing a safe, effective, convenient commercial method to pay expenses incident to official travel, including local travel. It improves cash management, reduces administrative workload, facilitates better traveler service, and—because of the refund feature—results in DoD cost savings. (AFH 1, ¶12.19)

**Agency Program Coordinators (APC).** An APC is an individual (uniformed member, employee, contractor, or foreign national) designated in writing by a commander or director as responsible for GTCC program management within the hierarchy. DoD GTCC Regulation describes DAF GTCC program policies and procedures. (AFH 1, ¶12.19)

**Card use.** Unless otherwise exempted, all DoD personnel are required to use the government-sponsored, vendor-issued GTCC for all expenses arising from official government travel, including lodging, transportation, local ground transportation, and rental car expenses authorized on travel orders. While in official travel status, cardholders may use the card for non-reimbursable incidental travel expenses such as rental movies, personal telephone calls, exercise fees, and beverages when these charges are not part of a separate room billing or meal. The cardholder must pay all charges—reimbursable or not—via split disbursement. (AFH 1, ¶12.19)

Cash advances may be made with the GTCC for travel-related expenses. ATM advances will not be obtained earlier than three working days before scheduled travel and are limited to authorized expenses exempt from mandatory card usage (meals, incidentals, and miscellaneous expenses). ATM transaction fees are part of the incidental-expense portion of per diem and are not separate reimbursable expenses. (AFH 1, ¶12.19)

**PCS coordination.** Individuals are required to use the GTCC during a PCS. Credit-limit increases and deferred payment options are available for extended PCS travel times. Bills must be kept current while in PCS status. The cardholder must notify the losing APC before departing and the gaining APC upon reporting. The losing APC updates the account to “mission critical” status and sets a future deactivation date based on the PCS travel-order report-no-later-than date, and submits a transfer request so the individual is removed from that unit’s reporting hierarchy. The gaining APC ensures the transfer is processed on arrival, updates account information, and clears the deactivation date. (AFH 1, ¶12.19)

**How to pay.** The vendor provides detailed monthly bills. Cardholders are responsible for payment in full of outstanding balances due in the monthly billing statement and should pay promptly within the current billing cycle. Military members must use the DTS split-disbursement feature, which automatically pays the credit-card vendor for charges incurred on official travel. (AFH 1, ¶12.19)

**Card abuse.** The cardholder will only use the GTCC while in official travel status. Commanders or supervisors will not tolerate misuse; cardholders who misuse the GTCC are subject to appropriate administrative or disciplinary action. (AFH 1, ¶12.19)

### 2025 source snapshot—revalidate (delinquency timeline)

Accounts 31 days past due will be suspended; the cardholder contacts the APC to resolve. At 61 days past due, the vendor sends a due-process letter and impending salary-offset procedures, advising intent to submit for salary offset unless paid in full within 30 days or an acceptable Reduced Payment Plan is reached. The first late fee is charged at two cycles plus fifteen days past due (day 75), then each cycle thereafter until the past-due amount is paid. Accounts 96 days past due are submitted for salary-offset processing to DFAS; DFAS calculates start/stop dates and payment amounts; fees added at enrollment in salary offset are not reimbursable. Accounts 181 days past due are considered charged off regardless of continuing salary-offset payments and are no longer eligible for reinstatement even when paid off. (AFH 1, ¶12.19)

# Section 12C — Manpower Management

## Learning objectives

### SSgt — ADTC level A

An Airman should be able to:

1. Recognize that manpower is constrained and programmed against validated requirements within fiscal limits.
2. Recall the three types of manpower used to conduct the USAF mission.
3. Recall what the Unit Manpower Document is and key data elements.
4. Recall core Manpower and Organization Flight competencies and services.
5. Recall Commercial Services Management goals and that competitive sourcing and in-sourcing are workforce tools.

### TSgt — ADTC level B

Retain every SSgt recall fact and explain:

1. Why missions compete for end-strength and grade distributions under congressional guidelines.
2. How military-assignment criteria, wartime sizing, and Manpower and Organization Flight services relate.
3. How UMD funded/unfunded requirements, change requests, and change notices relate.
4. How Commercial Services Management, competitive sourcing, and in-sourcing differ in purpose and triggers.

## 1. Manpower requirements

Manpower is a constrained resource that comprises a significant portion of the USAF budget. All missions and programs compete for authorized military and civilian end-strength and established grade distributions and must comply with congressional guidelines. Manpower must be programmed in accordance with validated manpower requirements, within fiscal limits and acceptable risks, as identified in defense planning and programming guidance. The Directorate of Manpower, Organization and Resources, Program Development Division allocates programmed manpower resources by command identifier, program element code, resource identification code, and country state code. These resources are translated into manpower authorizations by updating the unit manpower document. (AFH 1, ¶12.20)

Major commands should use means other than programmed manpower change requests for short-term mission or surge workload, such as: (1) available funds and command civilian employment plans to employ civilians; (2) overtime, temporary full-time, part-time, or overhire of civilian personnel; (3) Air Reserve Component support via military pay appropriation funds; (4) TDY military and civilian personnel; and (5) contract service. Changes to command-specific military and civilian manpower requirements must be validated and approved by HAF before use in programming and resourcing. (AFH 1, ¶12.20)

## 2. Determining manpower requirements

Three types of manpower conduct the USAF mission: military personnel, in-service civilian employees, and contracted services. In accordance with DoD guidance, the services assign military personnel only to positions that directly contribute to the prosecution of war; are military by law, custom, or tradition; or are needed for overseas rotation, operational deployment augmentation, or career-field sustainment. The USAF manpower requirements determination process systematically identifies minimum-essential manpower for the most effective and economical accomplishment of approved missions and functions within organizational and resource constraints. Determining correct military manpower for the most stringent wartime missions is key to the national military strategy. (AFH 1, ¶12.21)

Defense planning guidance defines planning scenarios used to size and shape the Total Force. Manpower requirements are sized for the most demanding phase of the scenario construct, including rotational forces for prolonged conflicts. These scenarios drive force structure and manpower military budgeting decisions. Manpower and organization flight personnel assist commanders and functional managers by objectively quantifying manpower requirements for distribution of USAF manpower resources. Key services include peacetime manpower standards development, wartime manpower requirements, and commercial services management actions such as public-private competition, in-sourcing, and business process reengineering. (AFH 1, ¶12.21)

## 3. Unit Manpower Document

According to AFI 38-101, a manpower requirement is the manpower needed to accomplish a job, mission, or program. It can be documented as a funded manpower authorization or an unfunded requirement. The Unit Manpower Document (UMD) is the primary document reflecting manpower required to accomplish the unit mission—a computer-generated product listing funded and unfunded requirements used to help manage manpower resources. UMD data elements identify unique position attributes, including position number, Air Force Specialty Code, functional account code, office symbol code, grade, personnel accounting symbol, and reason code. (AFH 1, ¶12.22)

Supervisors should routinely check the UMD for accuracy and use it to track authorized manpower strength. To change an existing UMD requirement, a manpower change request with justification is submitted to the servicing manpower organization. Approved changes are reflected by a change notice generated by the manpower programming and execution system. (AFH 1, ¶12.22)

### TSgt overlay note

At level B, explain the relationship: **requirement → funded authorization or unfunded requirement → UMD visibility → justified change request → approved change notice**. Do not invent local programming shortcuts. (AFH 1, ¶¶12.20–12.22)

## 4. Manpower and Organization Flight

The installation Manpower and Organization Flight helps manage manpower resources. Core competencies encompass organization structure, requirement determination, program allocation and control, and process improvement. Day-to-day services include UMD management, assistance with manpower change requests, change notices, and organizational structure changes. Other management services include performance management, commercial activity services, the Airmen Powered by Innovation Program, continuous process improvement, and management advisory studies. (AFH 1, ¶12.23)

## 5. Commercial Services Management

Commercial Services Management improves USAF functions using tools including competitive sourcing, in-sourcing, and post-competition accountability. The major command Manpower Organization and Resources Office provides commercial-services oversight at command level and guidance to Manpower and Organization Flights for wing implementation. Three principal goals: sustain readiness; improve performance and quality by doing business more efficiently and cost effectively; and focus available personnel and resources on core USAF missions. Commercial Services Management will not affect military-essential skills or inherently governmental functions. The Air Force Manpower Analysis Agency develops and maintains tools, templates, and guidebooks; administers the inherently governmental/commercial activities inventory; monitors post-competition accountability; and supports field initiatives. (AFH 1, ¶12.24)

## 6. Competitive sourcing and in-sourcing

**Competitive sourcing** generates savings by finding more efficient ways to accomplish a function. A competitive sourcing study is a public-private competition comparing total cost of in-house government operation to private-sector performance of the same activity to determine the most economical or efficient approach. The mission remains essentially unchanged; workforce composition changes. Where military personnel initially performed the mission, the resulting provider will be civil servants or private-sector contract employees. The study frees military personnel to perform other core military-essential activities. (AFH 1, ¶12.25)

**In-sourcing** is conversion of a contracted function to DoD civilian or military performance, or vice versa. An in-sourcing business case analysis compares contracted-function cost to DoD civilian-employee cost for the same activity, including certifications validating contract cost, in-house manning, available labor pool, and that the activity meets a valid and enduring mission requirement. The process applies when the function: (1) has been performed by DoD civilian employees at any time during the previous 10 years; (2) is closely associated with performance of an inherently governmental function; (3) has been performed under a contract awarded on a noncompetitive basis; or (4) has been determined by a contracting officer to have been performed poorly during the five years preceding the determination because of excessive costs or inferior quality. Contracted functions found to be inherently governmental, exempt from contract performance, unauthorized personal services, or experiencing contract-administration problems shall be in-sourced regardless of cost, per Deputy Secretary of Defense implementation guidance cited in AFH 1. (AFH 1, ¶12.25)

# Section 12D — Resource Management

## Learning objectives

At ADTC level B, an Airman should be able to:

1. Explain the resource management system’s focus on outputs, stewardship, performance versus plan, and financial controls.
2. Distinguish commander, comptroller, responsibility center manager, cost center manager, resource advisor, FMB, and FWG roles.
3. Explain PPBE purpose and the four phases; describe FYDP dimensions and update timing as AFH 1 prints them.
4. Explain equipment management, property-custodian responsibilities, civil-engineer work-request types, and facility stewardship concepts.
5. Define fraud, waste, and abuse; explain reporting, privacy, and whistleblower notes.
6. Explain when financial liability investigation applies and the role of DD Form 200.

## 1. Resource requirements

All USAF resources have a value or cost. The resource management system focuses on outputs and resources used; managers effectively using resources; measuring actual versus planned performance; and using financial plans and accounting to enhance management controls at each organizational level. It provides a way to establish priorities, choose policies, and act to get desired results and required resources at acceptable costs. (AFH 1, ¶12.26)

## 2. Resource management responsibilities

Although base-level resource managers do not control initial allocation of all resources, they must effectively manage them—including stewardship of money, manpower, and equipment. Stewardship involves more than legal accountability. Every USAF member has a principal responsibility to ensure resources are used in the most cost-effective manner; commanders and supervisors are responsible for efficient and economical use of all resources in their organizations and for budgeting, allocation, composition, and distribution of those resources. (AFH 1, ¶12.27)

**Commanders.** Review, validate, and balance the financial plan; actively review work-center financial programs; inquire about program conditions; review causes; weigh alternatives; direct action; and ensure resource-management success by allocating sufficient resources to RMS training and resource management team efforts. (AFH 1, ¶12.27)

**Comptrollers.** Support the mission by providing sound financial management and advice; promote responsible, proper financial management consistent with statutory and regulatory requirements; and apply policies enabling accounting, budget, and cost functions. (AFH 1, ¶12.27)

**Responsibility center managers.** Plan, direct, and coordinate subordinate organization activities; analyze subordinate plans; identify imbalances; analyze alternatives; and balance programs. (AFH 1, ¶12.27)

**Cost center managers.** The cost center is the basic production flight or work center. The cost center manager regulates consumption of work hours, supplies, equipment, and services to do tasks within the cost center and shifts resources among production tasks for the proper mix or emphasis. (AFH 1, ¶12.27)

**Resource advisors.** Monitor and help prepare resource estimates; help develop obligations and expense fund targets; monitor resource use versus projected consumption; serve as primary POC on resource management matters for their responsibility center; and are appointed in writing by the responsibility center manager. (AFH 1, ¶12.27)

**Financial Management Board (FMB).** Established by the senior or host commander at each base; determines program priorities and ensures effective resource allocation; reviews and approves or disapproves financial working group recommendations for balanced, valid financial programs and consideration of known or anticipated requirements. (AFH 1, ¶12.27)

**Financial Working Group (FWG).** Composed of line and staff resource advisors and responsibility center managers; manages commodities and resources integral to base or unit operating activities; develops requirements and revisions for the financial plan; reviews appropriated-fund financial plans; recommends to the FMB; presents recommendations on unfunded-requirement prioritization and fund-target adjustments; and provides technical guidance on primary-responsibility resources. (AFH 1, ¶12.27)

**Organizational finance and budgeting.** The operating budget covers costs associated with operation of all USAF organizations. Higher-headquarters approval gives obligation authority to accomplish the mission. The budget program operates on a fiscal year basis (1 October through the following 30 September). (AFH 1, ¶12.27)

## 3. Planning, Programming, Budgeting, and Execution (PPBE)

PPBE is DoD’s resource allocation system. Its ultimate objective is to provide the best mix of forces, equipment, manpower, and support attainable within fiscal constraints according to DoDD 7045.14. The goal is to achieve defense objectives established by the President and Secretary of Defense in strategic planning and joint planning guidance. The system helps leaders establish and forecast a budget and increases management effectiveness by applying judgment and experience to programs, resource limitations, adjustments, and alternatives. (AFH 1, ¶12.28)

PPBE consists of four interrelated and overlapping phases: planning, programming, budgeting, and execution. (AFH 1, ¶12.28)

**Planning.** Translates top-down guidance into plans and requirements for which a future year’s defense program can be developed; defines and examines alternative strategies; analyzes external conditions and trends; examines changes and long-term implications; helps prioritize objectives and assess strategies; links planning and programming; and provides an objective against which execution success can be measured. (AFH 1, ¶12.28)

**Programming.** Addresses planning policies and guidance and establishes initial program costs; matches available resources (fiscal, manpower, and materiel) against validated requirements to achieve the strategic plan and submit program proposals. The key objective is to develop a balanced, capabilities-based Air Force Program Objective Memorandum (POM). The USAF defends the POM during program and budget review and adjusts for OSD reviews and changing situations. Tradeoffs are inevitably involved. (AFH 1, ¶12.28)

**Budgeting.** Occurs concurrently with programming. Each DoD Component submits a proposed budget estimate with a POM. Budgeting provides detailed review of program pricing, phasing, and executability. It principally addresses years to be justified in the President’s budget. Analysts identify risk of OSD or congressional reduction; the comptroller applies latest inflation figures and flying-hour and manpower rates; the program is put into OSD budget format with justification documentation—yielding the POM/budget estimate submission or program budget review. Budgeting continues with program budget review submission and fact-of-life changes via notification document in the off-year. (AFH 1, ¶12.28)

**Execution.** Focuses on running the USAF day to day at applicable organizational levels. MAJCOMs and HAF receive obligation authority to execute missions in accordance with approved integrated priority lists for centrally managed programs. Because the budget being executed was compiled over a year earlier, some assumptions will have changed. Congress allows some operating-budget flexibility without requiring permission, but installations are expected to execute to the integrated priority list for centrally managed programs. A key part of execution is the MAJCOM O&M operation plan. Program execution is reviewed during budget execution reviews in February, April (concurrent with internal USAF mid-year review), and July. (AFH 1, ¶12.28)

## 4. Future Years Defense Program

The FYDP summarizes forces, resources, and equipment associated with all DoD programs. It displays total DoD resources and force structure in three basic dimensions: organizations affected (military departments and defense agencies); appropriations accounts (for example, RDT&E and O&M); and major force programs (for example, strategic forces and mobility forces). The FYDP is updated twice during the PPBE cycle—first upon POM submission (usually July/August) and again upon submission of the President’s budget (early February the following year). The FYDP is composed of 11 major force programs and over 3,600 active program elements. (AFH 1, ¶12.29)

## 5. USAF Equipment Management System and property stewardship

The USAF equipment management system provides worldwide visibility of all equipment assets; supports reporting capitalized asset depreciation; determines equipment requirements based on allowance standards; supports the budget and buy program; and identifies equipment types and quantities required to accomplish the mission. Allowance standards identify specific items and quantities approved by the functional manager for wartime and peacetime operations and are accessible online from the USAF Equipment Management System and the Allowance Standard Retrieval System website. (AFH 1, ¶12.30)

All military and civilian personnel must operate and maintain government systems, equipment, supplies, and real property in the best possible condition and readiness and in the absolute minimum quantity necessary to accomplish assigned tasks. Airmen at all levels must accurately maintain property records; ensure careful, economical use and safeguarding; provide adequate security, protection, and storage; and make recommendations for preventing fraud, waste, and abuse. USAF members must have supply discipline to conserve, protect, and maintain available government supplies, equipment, and real property for operational requirements. (AFH 1, ¶12.30)

**Commanders and supervisors.** Responsible for prudent management, control, storage, and cost-effective use of government property; establish controls to eliminate uneconomical equipment management; ensure personnel are taught proper care and safeguard principles and enforce them; properly manage public property; provide instructions on specific responsibilities; maintain auditable records; appoint representatives; designate property custodians and facility managers; and ensure representatives attend logistics readiness squadron training. (AFH 1, ¶12.30)

**Property custodian.** Appointed by an accountable property officer, commander, or chief of staff agency to accept responsibility for property; directly responsible for physical custody, inventory, accounting, and reconciliation of items on the account; may be held financially liable for loss, theft, destruction, or damage under control; and must report losses or irregularities to commanders or accountable officers at discovery. Refer to DoDI 5000.64 and AFI 23-111 for additional information. (AFH 1, ¶12.30)

**Installation and civil engineer responsibilities.** The installation commander has overall responsibility and accountability for installation operation. The MAJCOM and installation commander, assisted by the base civil engineer, develop, operate, maintain, and control use of facilities in compliance with DoD and USAF policies; develop comprehensive asset management plans; identify facility lifecycle requirements; implement common levels of service; assess asset-condition impact on mission through key performance indicators; and develop and execute real property construction, sustainment, restoration, and modernization programs. (AFH 1, ¶12.30)

The base civil engineer provides, operates, maintains, restores, and protects built and natural infrastructure necessary to support the mission and is the focal point for construction, sustainment, restoration, and modernization of real property and associated real property installed equipment. The customer service unit typically manages the facility manager program, including initial and recurring training. (AFH 1, ¶12.30)

**Civil engineer work requests.** The customer service unit receives and reviews work requests for validity, verifies scope, and ensures coordination with agencies such as fire, safety, and environmental. Approved work may be executed in-house by priority by the operations flight or as a contract managed by the engineering flight. Facility managers identify work requests using AF Form 332, *Base Civil Engineer Work Request*, or designated IT systems. (AFH 1, ¶12.30)

- **Direct scheduled work:** does not need detailed planning; sent directly to the shop (example: leaky faucet).
- **Planned work:** complex; needs detailed planning, multiple shops, and lead time for materials (example: relocating a doorway and exit signs).
- **Emergency work:** corrects an issue posing an immediate threat to mission, life, safety, or health; request and accomplish by the quickest means, including verbal or phone. (AFH 1, ¶12.30)

## 6. Fraud, waste, and abuse prevention

The USAF loses millions of dollars yearly to abuse, waste, and fraud. Prevention is the primary concern; detection and prosecution deter, but the key is preventing resource loss. The Secretary of the Air Force, Inspector General, provides policy guidance, develops procedures, and establishes and evaluates Air Force Complaints; the IG at every level establishes and directs these programs. (AFH 1, ¶12.31)

**Fraud.** Any intentional deception designed to unlawfully deprive the USAF of something of value or to secure from the USAF for an individual a benefit, privilege, allowance, or consideration to which he or she is not entitled. Fraud may also be any theft or diversion of resources for personal or commercial gain. (AFH 1, ¶12.31)

**Waste.** The extravagant, careless, or needless expenditure of USAF funds or consumption of USAF property resulting from deficient practices, systems controls, or decisions. Waste also includes improper practices not involving prosecutable fraud. Wartime and emergency operations, legitimate stockpiles, and wartime reserves are not considered waste. (AFH 1, ¶12.31)

**Abuse.** The intentionally wrongful or improper use of USAF resources. Examples include misuse of rank, position, or authority that causes loss or misuse of resources such as tools, vehicles, computers, or office equipment. (AFH 1, ¶12.31)

**Complaints.** USAF personnel have a duty to promptly report FWA to an appropriate supervisor or commander, to an IG or other appropriate inspector, or through an established grievance channel. Complaints may also go to the Air Force Audit Agency, AFOSI, security forces, or other proper authorities, by memorandum, in person, or by FWA hotlines, including anonymously. Members should try resolving FWA issues at the lowest possible level using command channels before addressing a higher level or the IG. Disclosures require factual, unbiased, specific information; individuals remain subject to punitive or adverse administrative action for knowingly making false statements or submitting other unlawful communications. (AFH 1, ¶12.31)

**Complainant privacy.** Privacy is safeguarded to encourage cooperation and openness. The IG safeguards personal identity and complaints of individuals seeking assistance or participating in an IG process. Communications to an IG are not privileged or confidential, but disclosure of communications and identity is strictly limited to official need-to-know, when required by law or regulation, when necessary to take adverse action against a subject, or with SAF/IG approval. (AFH 1, ¶12.31)

**Whistleblower rights.** Whistleblower witnesses have additional rights. Nature of allegation and findings determines releasable information. All releases follow FOIA and the Privacy Act. “Third-party” complainants are not entitled to a response regarding alleged wrongs not directly affecting them unless authorized via FOIA or Privacy Act release. (AFH 1, ¶12.31)

## 7. Financial liability investigation

Upon discovery of loss, damage, destruction, or theft of government-owned property (real or personal) of any value, the immediate supervisor within the chain of command shall conduct an informal inquiry to determine if a formal investigation is warranted. An investigation shall be conducted for all loss, damage, destruction, or theft of government-owned equipment with an initial acquisition cost of $5,000 or greater; all sensitive, classified, or leased (capital lease) property regardless of initial acquisition cost; and any real property. Investigations may also be conducted when circumstances warrant, such as when small amounts of property are lost frequently enough to suggest a pattern of wrongdoing. DD Form 200, *Financial Liability Investigation of Property Loss*, documents the facts and circumstances. Refer to DoDR 7000.14, Volume 12, Chapter 7 for additional information. (AFH 1, ¶12.32)

Preserve the $5,000 initial-acquisition-cost threshold as printed in AFH 1. (AFH 1, ¶12.32)

## 1. Environmental responsibilities

The USAF is a leader and devoted guardian of the environment. As trustee to over 8 million acres of natural habitat, the USAF takes considerable measures to defend and enhance America’s natural resources and cultural heritage. Over the last 20 years, the USAF has followed a compliance-based approach resulting in an unparalleled record of responsiveness to regulation, community interests, and ecological needs. ## 2. Environmental Management System

DoD, as the nation’s largest user of federal lands, consumer of energy, and operator of large industrial complexes, must strive for efficient operations while reducing its environmental footprint. All USAF commanders, supervisors, and process owners shall integrate environmental considerations into daily work activities and at all levels of decision-making for current or proposed actions that may adversely impact the environment. The EMS is a set of processes and practices that enable reduction in environmental impact and increase in operating efficiency. (AFH 1, ¶12.34)

**Priorities.** Three priorities: compliance, risk reduction, and continuous improvement. Compliance will be maintained with all environmental legal obligations and regulatory guidelines. Risk reduction will be achieved through a standardized approach to protect USAF assets, personnel, and material by effectively identifying and managing risks from environmental encroachment. Instilling a culture that encourages and supports continuous improvement will reduce environmental impacts and regulatory burden. (AFH 1, ¶12.34)

**Phases.** The EMS has four phases to help the USAF maintain compliance with environmental goals and obligations as outlined in DoDI 4715.17, *Environmental Management Systems*. (AFH 1, ¶12.34)

| Phase | Source focus |
|---|---|
| Plan | Scope, environmental policy and commitment statement, aspects and impacts, legal and other requirements, objectives and targets, and action plans; leadership focuses resources on mitigating risks that present the greatest threat to mission capability |
| Do | Environmental risk-reduction strategy for aspects associated with processes or activities that generate pollutants; each facility identifies opportunities to optimize processes for pollutant reduction, lower energy use, reduced natural-resource use, water conservation, and health/safety improvements |
| Check | Environmental monitoring and measuring to increase leadership awareness of compliance issues, identify and analyze non-conformance/non-compliance trends, identify improvement areas, provide lessons learned, and minimize or avoid environmental litigation risks |
| Act | Management review to assess suitability, adequacy, and effectiveness of the management system |

## 3. Environmental Management System guidance

Program areas identified in AFI 32-7001 and other environmental AFIs include environmental protection initiatives aligned with DoD and USAF policies and programs. Program areas include air quality; water supply; hazardous materials; hazardous waste; solid waste; toxic substances; petroleum, oil, and lubricants; storage tanks; pest management; cultural resources; natural resources; environmental planning; environmental restoration; environmental baseline surveys; and overseas compliance policy. (AFH 1, ¶12.35)

## 4. Environmental compliance

The Air Force Civil Engineer Center (AFCEC) is responsible for managing USAF compliance, restoration, sustainability, and National Environmental Policy Act programs. AFCEC provides HAF, major commands, and installations with environmental compliance support—delivering compliance programming and execution expertise, core knowledge, and leadership required to ensure installations comply with regulatory programs related to air emissions, water quality, wastewater discharges, hazardous waste generation, and natural and cultural resource management. AFCEC also serves as the central point for collection and analysis of environmental data and includes subject-matter experts and program managers in regulatory compliance, environmental restoration, hazardous waste management, pollution prevention, and environmental assessments. (AFH 1, ¶12.36)
