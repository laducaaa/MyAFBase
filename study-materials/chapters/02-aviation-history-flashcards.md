## Section 2A — Aviation Fundamentals

### C02-FC-001
- **Prompt:** What does AFH 1 identify as the purpose of studying early aviation history?
- **Answer:** To understand airpower’s foundations and how wars and conflicts drove innovation and technological advances; its dates provide a general chronological timeline because events may overlap.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2A; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-002
- **Prompt:** What four general suggestions does AFH 1 give for naming operations?
- **Answer:** Make names meaningful, target key audiences, avoid fashion, and make them memorable.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2A; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-003
- **Prompt:** Which two early French balloon milestones occurred in 1783 and 1794?
- **Answer:** Two French brothers launched a man-carrying hot-air balloon in 1783; in 1794, French Aerostatic Corps balloons accompanied the Armies of the French Revolution.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2A; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-004
- **Prompt:** What features distinguished Sir George Cayley’s 1853 glider?
- **Answer:** Fixed wings, a cambered airfoil, and horizontal and vertical stabilizers.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2A; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-005
- **Prompt:** What did the Union Army Balloon Corps do, and when did the Army disband it?
- **Answer:** Its seven balloons provided aerial observation and reconnaissance during the Civil War beginning in 1861; the Army disbanded it in 1863.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2A; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-006
- **Prompt:** What aviation first did Orville and Wilbur Wright achieve after more than 1,000 glides?
- **Answer:** The first powered, sustained, controlled airplane flight, opening the age of heavier-than-air aviation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2A; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-007
- **Prompt:** When was the Aeronautical Division established, and what specifications governed its first military airplane?
- **Answer:** President Theodore Roosevelt established it in the Army Signal Corps on 1 August 1907. The airplane had to carry two people weighing no more than 350 pounds combined and fly 125 miles at an average 40 miles per hour.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2A; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-008
- **Prompt:** Who was killed in the Wright aircraft crash of 17 September 1908?
- **Answer:** Passenger Lieutenant Thomas E. Selfridge was killed, and Orville Wright was seriously injured.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2A; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-009
- **Prompt:** What aircraft did the Army accept in 1909, and which future air leader did the Wright brothers later train?
- **Answer:** The Army accepted the Wright Company’s Signal Corps No. 1; the Wright brothers later trained Henry H. “Hap” Arnold.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2A; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-010
- **Prompt:** Who were the first Aeronautical Division enlisted men schooled in balloon support, and what did they learn?
- **Answer:** Eddie Ward and Joseph Barrett; they learned fabric handling, folding, stitching, buoyant-gas manufacture, and balloon inflation and control at Leo Stevens’ Balloon Factory.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2A; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-011
- **Prompt:** What duties did early enlisted airplane mechanicians perform?
- **Answer:** They maintained and repaired engines, airframes, controls, and systems; improved aircraft safety; and developed skills as mechanics, riggers, and fitters.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2A; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-012
- **Prompt:** What was the Aeronautical Division’s strength by October 1912?
- **Answer:** 11 aircraft, 14 flying officers, and 39 enlisted mechanics.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2A; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-013
- **Prompt:** Which squadron was activated in 1913 and is recognized as the USAF’s oldest?
- **Answer:** The 1st Aero Squadron.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2A; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-014
- **Prompt:** What first is associated with Corporal Frank Scott?
- **Answer:** On 28 September 1912, his first and only flight with Lieutenant Rockwell ended when engine trouble on landing killed both men. He was the first enlisted person to die in a military-aircraft accident; Scott Field, now Scott Air Force Base, was named for him.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2A; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-015
- **Prompt:** Who is recognized as the first American enlisted pilot, and when did he earn his license?
- **Answer:** Corporal Vernon L. Burge, in June 1912 while serving as Captain Frank P. Lahm’s crew chief in the Philippines.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2A; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-016
- **Prompt:** What later career path did Vernon L. Burge follow?
- **Answer:** After 10 enlisted years, he was commissioned during World War I and served another 25 years as an officer.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2A; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-017
- **Prompt:** Who became the third enlisted pilot on 20 April 1914?
- **Answer:** Sergeant William C. Ocker, joining first enlisted pilot Vernon Burge and second enlisted pilot Lamkey.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2A; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-018
- **Prompt:** What device did William C. Ocker develop to combat pilot disorientation in clouds?
- **Answer:** A flight integrator: an electrically driven gyroscope with a moving sky-and-cloud scroll and an airplane silhouette correctly oriented to the horizon.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2A; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-019
- **Prompt:** Why is William C. Ocker called the “Father of Blind Flight”?
- **Answer:** In June 1930, he flew about 900 miles from San Antonio to Scott Field in an enclosed cockpit; the USAF posthumously awarded him the Legion of Merit in January 1955 for his pioneering training devices.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2A; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-020
- **Prompt:** What created the Aviation Section, Signal Corps, and what dates define it?
- **Answer:** Passage of U.S. House Resolution 5304 authorized it on 18 July 1914; the AFH date range ends on 20 May 1918.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2A; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-021
- **Prompt:** What personnel and pay provisions does AFH 1 give for the Aviation Section?
- **Answer:** It first states 60 officers and 260 enlisted men, then states a total limit of 40 with no more than 12 enlisted men; those limits conflict in the source. The aviation-mechanician rating brought a 50-percent pay increase for instructed enlisted men on flying status.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2A; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-022
- **Prompt:** What force did Captain Benjamin Foulois initially take on the 1916 Pancho Villa Expedition?
- **Answer:** The 1st Aero Squadron’s 11 pilot officers, 82 enlisted men, one civilian mechanic, eight Curtiss JN-3 Jennies, 10 trucks, and one automobile; he later added two enlisted hospital corpsmen and an engineering section.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2A; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-023
- **Prompt:** What aircraft result and field lesson came from the Pancho Villa Expedition?
- **Answer:** Weather, dust, temperature, and 5,000-foot elevations left only two of eight aircraft working within a month. The expedition showed that field aviation required adequate maintenance and backup aircraft; the pursuit ended after almost a year.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2A; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-024
- **Prompt:** Which major aviation roles made Benjamin D. Foulois a “man of many firsts”?
- **Answer:** He helped test the Army’s first semi-rigid dirigible and Signal Corps No. 1, demonstrated the airplane at Fort Sam Houston in 1910, organized the 1st Aero Squadron, commanded the Pancho Villa Expedition, helped execute the $640 million World War I aviation program, and became Chief of the Air Corps in 1931.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2A; A—Remembering

## Section 2B — Aviation and Operations in World War I

### C02-FC-025
- **Prompt:** Which powers did AFH 1 place in the initial Entente and the opposing Central Powers?
- **Answer:** The initial Entente was the French Republic, British Empire, and Russian Empire; Italy, Japan, Belgium, Serbia, Greece, Montenegro, and Romania later joined it. The Central Powers were the German Empire, Austria-Hungary, Ottoman Empire, and Kingdom of Bulgaria.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2B; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-026
- **Prompt:** When does AFH 1 date the start of World War I and the U.S. declaration of war on Germany?
- **Answer:** World War I began in July 1914, days after Archduke Franz Ferdinand’s assassination in Sarajevo—attributed by the source to “Yugoslav forces”; the United States declared war on Germany on 6 April 1917.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2B; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-027
- **Prompt:** What was the condition of U.S. Army aviation when America entered World War I?
- **Answer:** Only the 1st Aero Squadron was fully equipped, manned, and organized. The Aviation Section had fewer than 250 airplanes, 131 officers, and 1,087 enlisted men.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2B; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-028
- **Prompt:** Which emerging World War I aviation specialties relied on trained enlisted personnel?
- **Answer:** Supply, construction, photo-reconnaissance, communication, armament, welding, rigging, sail making, and especially aircraft mechanics.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2B; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-029
- **Prompt:** What were the airplane’s most important early World War I missions?
- **Answer:** Observation, artillery spotting, and reconnaissance; pursuit aircraft emerged by 1915 to deny enemy use of airspace.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2B; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-030
- **Prompt:** What innovation did Roland Garros use, and what fighter did Germany develop after capturing him?
- **Answer:** Garros put steel deflector plates on his propeller so a machine gun could fire through its arc; the source credits this with the first aerial victory. After his 19 April 1915 capture, Germany developed the Fokker Eindecker, described as the first true fighter plane.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2B; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-031
- **Prompt:** What was the original threshold for a flying ace, and which enlisted aircrew could also earn the title?
- **Answer:** Five or more aerial victories, although the threshold later varied; aerial gunners and observers could also be called aces for air-to-air victories.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2B; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-032
- **Prompt:** Why was Edward V. Rickenbacker called America’s World War I “Ace of Aces”?
- **Answer:** He achieved 26 confirmed aerial victories, received the Distinguished Service Cross a record eight times, and had one converted to the Medal of Honor in 1930.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2B; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-033
- **Prompt:** What happened to the Lafayette Escadrille’s American members in 1918?
- **Answer:** They transferred from the French Air Service into the U.S. Army Air Service as the 103d Aero Squadron; the French formed the Escadrille Jeanne d’Arc.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2B; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-034
- **Prompt:** What aviation first and combat record are associated with Eugene Bullard?
- **Answer:** He was the first African American military pilot, flew more than 20 air-combat missions for France, earned the Croix de Guerre, and claimed two victories as the “Black Swallow of Death.”
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2B; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-035
- **Prompt:** What four-day organization transferred Army aviation from the Signal Corps toward the Air Service?
- **Answer:** The Division of Military Aeronautics, 20–24 May 1918. President Wilson’s executive order transferred aviation control to the Secretary of War so administration, assets, and personnel could pass to the Air Service.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2B; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-036
- **Prompt:** What dates define the Air Service, U.S. Army, and how severe was its postwar drawdown?
- **Answer:** 24 May 1918–2 July 1926. Personnel fell from 190,000 to fewer than 20,000, and aviation funding fell from $952 million in 1919 to $28 million in 1920.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2B; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-037
- **Prompt:** What did Billy Mitchell’s 1921 bombing trials demonstrate?
- **Answer:** His 1st Provisional Air Brigade sank the 27,000-ton former German battleship *Ostfriesland*; further tests used USS *New Jersey* and USS *Virginia* to demonstrate airpower’s value.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2B; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-038
- **Prompt:** What future attack did Billy Mitchell predict, and where was the prediction published?
- **Answer:** His 1924 report predicted war with Japan and a surprise attack on Hawaii; it appeared in his 1925 book *Winged Defense*, about 16 years before Pearl Harbor.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2B; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-039
- **Prompt:** What did Sergeant Ulysses “Sam” Nero accomplish in Mitchell’s airpower demonstration?
- **Answer:** As an NBS-1 bombardier, he scored two hits that sank USS *New Jersey* in just over three minutes, then put his final bomb directly on USS *Virginia*.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2B; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-040
- **Prompt:** Who marked the end of the enlisted-pilot era?
- **Answer:** Master Sergeant George H. Holmes, the last serving member of about 2,500 enlisted-pilot-training graduates; he retired from the Air Force in 1957.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2B; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-041
- **Prompt:** When was the World War I Armistice, and which air missions had shown promise by then?
- **Answer:** 11 November 1918—the eleventh hour of the eleventh day of the eleventh month. Close air support, interdiction, and strategic bombardment showed promise alongside observation, reconnaissance, and artillery spotting.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2B; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-042
- **Prompt:** What dates define the U.S. Army Air Corps, and what did its creation recognize?
- **Answer:** 2 July 1926–20 June 1941; it was a step toward recognizing aviation’s autonomy and role in modern warfare while aviation remained in the Army command structure.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2B; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-043
- **Prompt:** What lineage connects the Air Corps Tactical School to today’s Air University?
- **Answer:** The Air Service Tactical School became the Air Corps Tactical School in 1926, moved to Maxwell Field, was replaced by the Army Air Force School of Applied Tactics, and is now recognized as Air University at Maxwell Air Force Base.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2B; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-044
- **Prompt:** Where and when was the Air Corps Technical School established, and where had it expanded by 1938?
- **Answer:** It was established at Chanute Field, Illinois, in 1926 and had branched to Lowry Field, Colorado, and Scott Field, Illinois, by 1938.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2B; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-045
- **Prompt:** What did Carl A. Spaatz and the *Question Mark* crew demonstrate in January 1929?
- **Answer:** Aerial refueling’s range potential: 150 hours 40 minutes aloft, 37 air-to-air refuelings, 5,600 gallons hand-pumped, and 11,000 miles traveled. Spaatz later became the first Air Force Chief of Staff.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2B; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-046
- **Prompt:** What role did Ira C. Eaker perform in Operation Point Blank?
- **Answer:** He directed the American daylight strategic-bombing campaign while the British conducted night area bombing, striking Germany’s military and industrial base around the clock.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2B; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-047
- **Prompt:** What was the primary role of GHQ Air Force from 1935 to 1939?
- **Answer:** It controlled aviation combat units alongside and under the Army Air Corps, a “half-step” toward an independent Air Force.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2B; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-048
- **Prompt:** What 1939 action preceded GHQ Air Force’s massive expansion?
- **Answer:** President Roosevelt requested a $300 million military-aviation appropriation; the resulting expansion evolved into the largest air organization in U.S. history.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2B; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-049
- **Prompt:** What did the U.S. Army Air Forces replace in June 1941, and when did its AFH date range end?
- **Answer:** It replaced both the U.S. Army Air Corps and GHQ Air Force to unify all air elements; its date range is 20 June 1941–17 September 1947, before the National Security Act reorganization took effect on 18 September.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2B; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-050
- **Prompt:** Who commanded the U.S. Army Air Forces in World War II, and how large did it become?
- **Answer:** General Henry H. “Hap” Arnold; under him it expanded to nearly 2.5 million members and 75,000 aircraft.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2B; A—Remembering

## Section 2C — Aviation and Operations in World War II

### C02-FC-051
- **Prompt:** Which blocs does AFH 1 identify in World War II, and what central airpower lesson does it state?
- **Answer:** The Allies began with France, Poland, the United Kingdom, British India, Australia, Canada, New Zealand, and South Africa; soon the Balkans, Netherlands, Belgium, Greece, and Yugoslavia joined. By 1942, the United Nations brought in the United States, Russia, and China. Germany, Japan, and Italy formed the Axis. Air superiority proved prerequisite to successful land, sea, and air operations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2C; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-052
- **Prompt:** What 1940 production goal did President Franklin D. Roosevelt set, and what was the Air Corps starting strength?
- **Answer:** He called for 50,000 military aircraft per year. The Air Corps then had about 1,700 tactical and training aircraft, 1,600 officers, and 18,000 enlisted men; Congress also passed the first U.S. peacetime conscription law that year.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2C; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-053
- **Prompt:** What happened at Pearl Harbor and in the Philippines on 7 December 1941?
- **Answer:** About 350 Japanese aircraft attacked Pearl Harbor in two waves, sinking or heavily damaging battleships; Japanese aviators also destroyed a U.S. aircraft fleet refueling on the ground in the Philippines.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2C; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-054
- **Prompt:** When did the United States declare war after Pearl Harbor, and which theater received priority?
- **Answer:** It declared war on Japan on 8 December 1941 and was at war with Germany and Italy three days later; defeating Germany in Europe took precedence.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2C; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-055
- **Prompt:** What production, manpower, and support figures show the U.S. air buildup in World War II?
- **Answer:** Factories produced 33,304 aircraft in 1942, and inventory rose to 72,726 by 1944; manpower exceeded two million by March 1944, with about 27 Airmen supporting each aircraft.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2C; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-056
- **Prompt:** What did Executive Order 8802 require, and what did it not end?
- **Answer:** Signed by President Roosevelt in 1941, it required vocational and training programs without discrimination by race, creed, color, or national origin. The source characterizes it as prohibiting racial discrimination within the services but explicitly says it did not end segregation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2C; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-057
- **Prompt:** Which two actions moved the Air Force from segregation toward integration in 1948 and 1949?
- **Answer:** President Truman signed Executive Order 9981 in 1948 to abolish racial discrimination; Air Force Letter 35.3 on 11 May 1949 ordered black Airmen reassigned to formerly all-white units according to qualifications.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2C; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-058
- **Prompt:** Which Tuskegee units and aircraft does AFH 1 identify for 1944?
- **Answer:** Colonel Benjamin O. Davis, Jr.’s 99th Fighter Squadron fought over Anzio in P-40s; the 332d Fighter Group then joined them with P-39s; later replacements were P-47s and P-51s, respectively.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2C; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-059
- **Prompt:** What World War II record does AFH 1 give the Tuskegee Airmen?
- **Answer:** Nearly 1,000 earned wings; they destroyed 112 enemy aircraft in air-to-air combat, lost 66 aircraft, and flew 312 escort missions, helping prepare the USAF to be the first U.S. armed service to integrate racially.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2C; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-060
- **Prompt:** What major firsts are associated with Benjamin O. Davis, Jr.?
- **Answer:** He commanded the Tuskegee Airmen, became the first African American general in the U.S. Air Force, and was promoted to four-star general on 8 December 1998.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2C; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-061
- **Prompt:** Who organized and led the first American Volunteer Group, and whose air force did it formally join?
- **Answer:** It was organized in early 1941 under U.S. presidential authority and led by Claire Lee Chennault; the “Flying Tigers” were formally members of the Chinese Air Force.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2C; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-062
- **Prompt:** What were the Flying Tigers’ three squadrons, combat credit, and successor?
- **Answer:** The Adam & Eves, Panda Bears, and Hell’s Angels; the group was credited with almost 300 enemy aircraft destroyed and lost 14 pilots in combat. On 4 July 1942, the 23rd Fighter Group replaced it.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2C; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-063
- **Prompt:** What did Public Law 99 authorize, and when did the first “flying sergeants” graduate?
- **Answer:** It authorized enlisted-pilot training in June 1941 for qualified enlisted men ages 18–25. Class 42-C graduated on 7 March 1942 from Kelly and Ellington Fields, and all went on to fly P-38s.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2C; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-064
- **Prompt:** How did the Women’s Army Auxiliary Corps change during World War II?
- **Answer:** Created in May 1942, it became the Women’s Army Corps within a year; participation grew from 800 women in training to 150,000 serving in warning, clerical, technical, medical, scientific, and aircraft-maintenance roles.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2C; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-065
- **Prompt:** What World War II aviation milestones are associated with Jacqueline Cochran?
- **Answer:** She organized 25 women to fly for Great Britain, became the first woman to fly a bomber across the Atlantic, and in 1943 became Director of the Women’s Air Force Service Pilots Training Program.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2C; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-066
- **Prompt:** What record made Lillian K. Keil the source’s most decorated woman in U.S. military history?
- **Answer:** She flew 250 World War II evacuation missions, including 23 transatlantic, then 175 Korean War evacuations; she attended to an estimated 10,000-plus wounded and received 19 medals.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2C; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-067
- **Prompt:** What was Operation Argument, and what result does AFH 1 report?
- **Answer:** “Big Week,” 20–25 February 1944, sought air superiority and destruction of Germany’s aircraft industry. At a cost of 226 American bombers, 114 British heavies, and 28 U.S. fighters, it damaged or destroyed about 500 Luftwaffe fighters and killed 400 pilots.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2C; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-068
- **Prompt:** What sortie totals demonstrate Allied air dominance on D-Day?
- **Answer:** On 6 June 1944, the U.S. Army Air Forces flew 8,722 sorties and the Royal Air Force 5,676; the Luftwaffe flew fewer than 100, with only two German aircraft damaging the invasion beaches.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2C; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-069
- **Prompt:** What scale does AFH 1 assign to the Combined Bomber Offensive?
- **Answer:** From 1942 to 1945, it was the longest, bloodiest air campaign in history; the Allies flew 1.44 million combat sorties and dropped 2.7 million tons of bombs.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2C; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-070
- **Prompt:** What humanitarian airlift did Operation Chowhound conduct in May 1945?
- **Answer:** Alongside the British mission the source calls Operation Mana, ten B-17 groups flew 2,268 sorties and delivered 4,000 tons of food to three million Dutch people in the occupied Netherlands.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2C; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-071
- **Prompt:** What wartime experience and later first are associated with Paul W. Airey?
- **Answer:** Shot down on his 28th mission in 1944, he endured 10 months as a prisoner of war and a 90-day, 400-mile march; in 1967 he became the first Chief Master Sergeant of the Air Force.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.16

### C02-FC-072

- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.17

## Section 2D — The Edge of Space

### C02-FC-079
- **Prompt:** Which people and technology linked early U.S. rocketry to postwar space development?
- **Answer:** Robert H. Goddard’s experiments began the U.S. foundation; Germany launched the first intermediate-range ballistic missile, the V-2, in 1944. After the war, nearly 1,600 specialists led by Wernher von Braun moved to the United States and advanced missiles, satellites, and crewed spaceflight.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2D; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-080
- **Prompt:** What space-related study did Curtis E. LeMay initiate in 1946?
- **Answer:** He tasked the Research and Development Corporation to propose a preliminary design for an experimental world-circling spaceship—what would later be called a satellite.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2D; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-081
- **Prompt:** What organizations accelerated U.S. intercontinental ballistic-missile development in 1953–1954?
- **Answer:** The Strategic Missiles Evaluation Group, code-named the Teapot Committee, recommended acceleration; Air Research and Development Command created the Western Development Division, which Brigadier General Bernard Schriever took over on 2 August 1954.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2D; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-082
- **Prompt:** What events opened the space race and accelerated U.S. missile development?
- **Answer:** Eisenhower approved a scientific satellite for the 1 July 1957–31 December 1958 International Geophysical Year; Sputnik I entered orbit on 4 October 1957. He then accelerated civil and military space work and prioritized the ICBM, leading to Air Force acceptance of Atlas and later Titan.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2D; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-083
- **Prompt:** What became of the Air Force’s 1958 plans for a military human presence in space?
- **Answer:** Eisenhower reserved crewed missions for NASA, but the Air Force plan became a basis for Mercury, Gemini, and Apollo; Air Force and NASA needs also helped produce the Space Shuttle.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2D; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-084
- **Prompt:** What requirement launched Air Force electro-optical space reconnaissance?
- **Answer:** Following the Research and Development Corporation’s Project Feedback report, Weapon System Requirement No. 5 directed an electro-optical reconnaissance satellite; later missions included missile warning, space awareness, and battlespace characterization.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2D; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-085
- **Prompt:** Which ground and launch systems supported early Air Force space capabilities?
- **Answer:** The ballistic-missile early-warning system, space-object surveillance, Air Force Satellite Control Network, and launch ranges at Cape Canaveral, Florida, and Vandenberg Air Force Base, California. The source places the launch bases under Space Force direction for DoD, NASA, other-government, and commercial support.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2D; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-086
- **Prompt:** What were Corona and Discoverer XIII?
- **Answer:** Corona was Eisenhower’s classified Satellite Photo Reconnaissance Program, publicly called Discoverer. Discoverer XIII’s successful 10 August 1960 launch acquired more than 3,000 feet of reconnaissance film; the National Reconnaissance Office was formed that year.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.23
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2D; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-087
- **Prompt:** What national-security effects did space systems provide from the Cold War through the Gulf War?
- **Answer:** Reconnaissance supported deterrence and treaty verification, while space systems supplied missile warning and strategic communications. In the early 1990s Gulf War, wider access to space information gave coalition planners an information advantage over Iraqi forces.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.23
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2D; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-088
- **Prompt:** Which civilian sectors does AFH 1 identify as reliant on space-based technology?
- **Answer:** Transportation, health, environment, telecommunications, education, commerce, agriculture, and energy; space services have become infrastructure comparable to highways, utilities, and electric grids.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.24
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2D; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-089
- **Prompt:** What GPS policy changed in 2000?
- **Answer:** The United States ended Selective Availability—the deliberate degradation of nonmilitary GPS accuracy—expanding civil and commercial uses of a system now identified as critical infrastructure.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.24
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2D; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-090
- **Prompt:** What warning and conclusion came from the 2001 Space Commission?
- **Answer:** The Donald Rumsfeld-led commission warned of a surprise “space Pearl Harbor” against U.S. military space assets and concluded that U.S. security and economic well-being depend on the ability to operate successfully in space—and, if deterrence fails, to prevail in conflict.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.25
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2D; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-091
- **Prompt:** What ¶2.26 points does AFH 1 make about contested space and China’s 11 January 2007 anti-satellite test?
- **Answer:** Space had become competitive, congested, and contested, and the United States would continue to be tested by competing programs or attempts to restrict U.S. space activities. China’s first successful direct-ascent ASAT test destroyed one of its own satellites, creating debris that increased risks to low-Earth-orbit satellites and crewed missions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.26
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2D; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-092
- **Prompt:** What comparison from LeMay’s 1946 study illustrates the “space imperative”?
- **Answer:** The study said people could no more foresee all uses of spaceships than the Wright brothers could have foreseen B-29 fleets bombing Japan and air transports circling the globe.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.27
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2D; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-093
- **Prompt:** How did Heather Wilson in 2017 and David Goldfein in 2018 characterize space?
- **Answer:** Wilson called it a warfighting domain, not merely an enabler; Goldfein urged the Air Force to embrace space superiority like air superiority and lead joint warfighting in the contested domain.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.27
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2D; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-094
- **Prompt:** What organizational change created the U.S. Space Force, and what are its members called?
- **Answer:** The source says the act signed on 20 December 2019 reorganized Air Force Space Command into an independent U.S. Space Force within the Department of the Air Force; members are Guardians. Its printed “Title 10 U.S.C., Ch. 908, U.S. Space Force Act, 1 January 2021” string is unclear, and its 8,400 end-strength figure is volatile.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.28
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2D; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-095
- **Prompt:** Who was Dr. Harry Armstrong, and what Air Force space-medicine vision did he pursue?
- **Answer:** A cabin-pressurization pioneer and the Air Force’s second Surgeon General, he used command of USAFSAM to build on aviation-medicine work dating to 1918 and promote space medicine.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.28
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2D; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-096
- **Prompt:** What space-medicine organization did Harry Armstrong establish in 1949?
- **Answer:** The world’s first Department of Space Medicine at USAFSAM, Randolph Air Force Base, centered on former German specialists recruited through the program the source spells “Operation Paper Clip.”
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.28
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2D; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-097
- **Prompt:** What two research advances did Heinz and Fritz Haber pioneer at USAFSAM?
- **Answer:** They designed the first space-cabin simulator, built as a low-pressure chamber in 1952, and in 1955 studied weightlessness through parabolic F-100 Super Sabre flights.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.28
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2D; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-098
- **Prompt:** Who completed the simulated lunar voyage in “Terrella I,” and what did it involve?
- **Answer:** A1C Donald Farrell spent seven days in February 1958 inside a two-and-a-half-ton, 2-by-3-foot low-pressure chamber at Randolph AFB using 100-percent recyclable air; future President Lyndon Johnson greeted him afterward.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.28
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2D; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-099
- **Prompt:** How did USAFSAM support NASA’s human-spaceflight programs?
- **Answer:** NASA contracted with the Air Force for 30 years through the six moon landings of 1969–1972. USAFSAM research at Randolph and Brooks supported Mercury, Gemini, and Apollo and studied acceleration, microgravity, and radiation before astronaut flight.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.28
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2D; A—Remembering

### C02-FC-100
- **Prompt:** Which milestones trace USAFSAM’s later space-medicine lineage?
- **Answer:** Enlisted technicians trained rhesus monkeys Sam Space and Miss Sam Space; Dr. Hans-Georg Clamman’s biopack monitored and confined a primate for a 1959 Little Joe flight. The work moved to Brooks AFB in 1959, received President Kennedy’s recognition on 21 November 1963, and was revisited by first CMSSF Roger Towberman in November 2020.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶2.28
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 2D; A—Remembering

## Source ambiguities and volatility

- **¶2.4 personnel limits:** The paragraph describes 60 officers and 260 enlisted men, then limits total personnel to 40 with no more than 12 enlisted. Card 021 preserves both rather than reconciling them.
- **¶2.5 place name:** The source prints “the Casa Grandes” in Chihuahua; do not silently normalize to “Casas Grandes.” Card 023 retains the expedition result without rewriting the place name.
- **¶¶2.6–2.7 early-war claims:** The source attributes Franz Ferdinand’s assassination to “Yugoslav forces,” states that more than 70 million lives were lost by November 1918, and credits Garros’s deflector system with the first aerial victory. These are source formulations, not independently adjudicated claims.
- **¶2.9 names and war-end framing:** The source varies between “Martin-Curtiss” and “Martin-Curtis” NBS-1 and distinguishes the 1918 Armistice from memorials using the 1919 Versailles Treaty. Cards retain the substantive source answer without normalizing those details.
- **¶2.10 / ¶2.15 singular or clipped forms:** The source prints “Army Air Force School of Applied Tactics” and “Reserve Officer Training Corp” (singular “Corp”); preserve those printed forms rather than silently expanding them.
- **¶2.15 discrimination and inclusion chronology:** The source broadly describes Executive Order 8802 as prohibiting discrimination “within the services” while also saying it did not end segregation. Its Tuskegee chronology, unit labels, aircraft sequence, and totals are preserved as printed.
- **¶2.16 organization form, operation spelling, and totals:** The source twice prints singular “U.S. Army Air Force” in count statements and spells the British food mission “Operation Mana”; other references may use different forms. Operation Argument, Normandy, Combined Bomber Offensive, and Chowhound figures remain source-bound estimates.
- **¶2.18 atomic-era framing:** The 1896 uranium/radiation wording, casualty figures, surrender framing, and account of the 1946 UN plan are retained from the assigned text.
- **¶2.26 contested-space wording:** The source includes the phrase “nearspace peers”; treat it as a flagged source/extraction form and do not invent a definition. Card 091 covers the contested framing, restriction attempts, and ASAT facts without defining that phrase.
- **¶¶2.22, 2.28 current facts:** Launch-base names, organizational control, force size, and statutory codification can change. In ¶2.28, the 20 December 2019 signing date appears beside the unclear phrase “Title 10 U.S.C., Ch. 908, U.S. Space Force Act, 1 January 2021”; Card 094 preserves and flags it.
- **¶2.28 USAFSAM wording:** The source spells “Operation Paper Clip” as three words and says a 1959 monkey flight occurred “less than a year” after Farrell’s February 1958 simulation, an interval that is not reconcilable as printed. Both belong to the exam-source track and should not be silently rewritten.
