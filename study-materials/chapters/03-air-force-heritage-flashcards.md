## Section 3A — The Air Force and the Mid-1900s

### C03-FC-001
- **Prompt:** What law created a separate Department of the Air Force, and when was the USAF established as an independent branch?
- **Answer:** President Truman signed the National Security Act of 1947 on 26 July 1947; the USAF was officially established on 18 September 1947 after about 40 years under the U.S. Army.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-002
- **Prompt:** Who was the first Chief of Staff of the Air Force, and what early Cold War task does AFH 1 assign him?
- **Answer:** General Carl A. Spaatz; under his leadership the Air Force clarified roles and missions for the growing Cold War.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-003
- **Prompt:** Who was the first woman in the USAF, and what law enabled permanent female service?
- **Answer:** Esther McGowin Blake enlisted on 8 July 1948 in the first minute duty was authorized. Public Law 80-625, the Women’s Armed Services Integration Act (12 June 1948), gave women permanent Regular and Reserve status. A source caption also prints “Ester.”
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-004
- **Prompt:** When did the Berlin Blockade begin, and whom did it isolate?
- **Answer:** On 24 June 1948 the Soviet Union blocked railroad and road corridors to about 2.5 million residents of West Berlin, deep inside Communist East Germany.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-005
- **Prompt:** What was Operation Vittles, and what delivery totals does AFH 1 give?
- **Answer:** The Berlin Airlift sustained West Berlin for 15 months with food, medicine, coal, and other supplies on C-47 and C-54 aircraft—more than 2.3 million tons on over 277,000 flights (about one every three minutes).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-006
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 connect the Berlin Airlift to the OODA loop?
- **Answer:** Allied forces iteratively observed, oriented, decided, and acted to sustain Berlin—illustrating OODA agility (also tied in the source to the DAF 8-step Practical Problem Solving Model/Method).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-007
- **Prompt:** What strategic Cold War judgment does AFH 1 make about the Berlin Airlift?
- **Answer:** It was arguably airpower’s single most decisive Cold War contribution: a nonviolent use of airpower that achieved profound strategic effect and defused a potentially disastrous confrontation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-008
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 describe Korea’s post–World War II division and the war’s start?
- **Answer:** The peninsula was divided at the 38th parallel—Soviet-aligned North Korea and U.S.-aligned South Korea. North Korea invaded South Korea on 25 June 1950.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-009
- **Prompt:** Why did President Truman favor intervention in Korea?
- **Answer:** Korea’s proximity to Japan and concern over rising Communist parties made intervention compelling; he emphasized global containment of communism.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-010
- **Prompt:** What happened at the Battle of Osan?
- **Answer:** In July 1950 the U.S. Army’s 24th Infantry Division took significant losses and retreated; with inadequate weapons and armor, South’s defense held only about 10 percent of the peninsula.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-011
- **Prompt:** What was the result of the Battle of Pusan Perimeter?
- **Answer:** In August–September 1950, six weeks of air, land, and sea attacks halted North Korean advances and pushed them back at all points along the perimeter while South Korean forces were reinforced.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-012
- **Prompt:** What airpower outcomes and limits does AFH 1 note for the Battle of Inchon phase?
- **Answer:** Air superiority and close air support succeeded, but a lengthy air effort to disrupt Communist supply lines failed; systematic campaigning then sought prolonged economic cost to North Korea and Chinese forces.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-013
- **Prompt:** What aircraft and locale does AFH 1 associate with restoring air superiority in Korea?
- **Answer:** F-86 Sabre jet fighters replaced older aircraft and fought over “MiG Alley,” where superior training and experience prevailed.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-014
- **Prompt:** What rescue and medical-evacuation totals does AFH 1 give for the Korean War Air Rescue Service?
- **Answer:** More than 9,600 wounded soldiers medically evacuated and nearly 1,000 personnel rescued after being shot down over enemy territory.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-015
- **Prompt:** When did Korean War fighting end, and what lasting status does AFH 1 describe?
- **Answer:** An armistice on 27 July 1953 created a demilitarized zone about four kilometers (2.48 miles) wide. With no peace treaty, the two Koreas are technically still at war.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-016
- **Prompt:** What did Operations Safe Haven I and II accomplish?
- **Answer:** After the 1956 Hungarian Revolution, the USAF airlifted over 10,000 Hungarian refugees to asylum in Safe Haven I (1956) and II (1957).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-017
- **Prompt:** What spurred Operation Amigos Airlift, and what did it deliver?
- **Answer:** May 1960 Chilean disasters left nearly 8,000 dead or missing, 5,500 injured, and more than 240,000 homeless; the month-long airlift delivered over 1,000 tons of material.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-018
- **Prompt:** What was the Bay of Pigs outcome, and how did the Soviet Union respond?
- **Answer:** Mid-April 1961 Cuban-exile landing at the Bay of Pigs was a crushing defeat; the Soviet Union almost immediately increased economic and military aid to Cuba.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-019
- **Prompt:** How were Soviet missiles in Cuba discovered, and what U.S. force preparations followed?
- **Answer:** August 1962 U-2 photos from Strategic Air Command confirmed intermediate- and medium-range ballistic missile complexes. Forces prepared for invasion, flew low-level reconnaissance, deployed aircraft to Florida, dispersed nuclear-capable B-47s, and kept B-52s airborne ready to strike.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-020
- **Prompt:** What course did President Kennedy choose in the Cuban Missile Crisis instead of invasion?
- **Answer:** A naval blockade to keep more materiel from reaching Cuba—still technically an act of war, but less escalatory than invasion, which many Joint Chiefs favored.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-021
- **Prompt:** How was the Cuban Missile Crisis resolved, and what deterrence lesson does AFH 1 draw?
- **Answer:** The United States agreed not to invade Cuba (and secretly to remove American missiles from Turkey) for Soviet missile removal. Strategic and tactical power, plus the will to use it, deterred nuclear war. The source also notes early-1960s mutually assured destruction doctrine.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-022
- **Prompt:** What ABM Treaty facts and U.S. withdrawal date does AFH 1 state?
- **Answer:** Signed May 1972, in force October 1972; originally two fixed ground-based sites (capital and an ICBM field), later halved by a July 1975 protocol. The United States withdrew in June 2002.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-023
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 frame the Vietnam Conflict’s sides and early U.S. involvement?
- **Answer:** North Vietnam (Viet Minh under Ho Chi Minh) with China and Russia versus South Vietnam aided by the United States and Great Britain. 1950s involvement began as a Cold War containment effort under Truman without a declared war.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-024
- **Prompt:** What engineer teams and enlisted aircrew roles does AFH 1 highlight in Vietnam?
- **Answer:** Prime BEEF built revetments, barracks, and facilities; REDHORSE provided longer-range civil engineer services; enlisted Airmen also served as aerial gunners and loadmasters on gunships.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-025
- **Prompt:** What was Operation Farmgate?
- **Answer:** In 1961 Kennedy ordered covert deployment of the 4400th Combat Crew Training Squadron (Jungle Jim) to train the South Vietnamese Air Force; American pilots flew T-28, A-26, and A-1E attack missions under a combat-training umbrella.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-026
- **Prompt:** What Gulf of Tonkin events led to Operation Pierce Arrow and broader combat authority?
- **Answer:** North Vietnam fired on USS *Maddox* and USS *Turner Joy* in 1964; the Gulf of Tonkin Resolution let President Johnson use conventional force without a formal war declaration, and advisory work rapidly became combat operations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-027
- **Prompt:** What triggered Operations Flaming Dart I and II?
- **Answer:** 1965 Viet Cong attacks near Pleiku and Camp Holloway killed eight Americans and wounded 104. Flaming Dart I struck North Vietnamese military targets; after a hotel housing Americans was hit, Flaming Dart II added more air attacks reinforced by three Navy carriers.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-028
- **Prompt:** What spectrum of airpower does AFH 1 associate with Operation Arc Light (1965–1973)?
- **Answer:** Close air support, interdiction, reconnaissance, airlift, tanker, and search-and-rescue—from O-1 FAC aircraft marking targets to B-52Ds dropping large bomb loads, plus AC-47 gunships and F-111s.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-029
- **Prompt:** What were Operation Rolling Thunder’s objectives and North Vietnamese defensive response?
- **Answer:** A measured 1965 campaign south of the 19th parallel to break North Vietnam’s will, destroy industry and air defenses, cut the Ho Chi Minh Trail, and force peace talks. North Vietnam multiplied air defenses, SA-2s, AAA, and MiG-17/MiG-21 interceptors under an advanced radar-controlled system.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-030
- **Prompt:** What was the Tet Offensive, and what strategic effect does AFH 1 assign it?
- **Answer:** In 1968 roughly 70,000 North Vietnamese and Viet Cong forces attacked more than 100 South Vietnamese cities. Though held off militarily, it was a strategic North Vietnamese victory that eroded U.S. public support and marked the start of methodical American withdrawal.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-031
- **Prompt:** What USAF aircrew loss figures over North Vietnam does AFH 1 give by fall 1968?
- **Answer:** Of 745 USAF crew members shot down, 145 were rescued, 255 confirmed killed, 222 captured, and 123 missing; 526 aircraft were downed despite virtually complete air superiority.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-032
- **Prompt:** What close-air-support feat does AFH 1 credit to Operation Niagara at Khe Sanh?
- **Answer:** In 1968, when more than 20,000 North Vietnamese surrounded 6,000 Marines, B-52s hit about every 90 minutes for most of a 77-day siege; aircraft dropped 100,000 tons of bombs (two-thirds from B-52s).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-033
- **Prompt:** What was Vietnamization under President Nixon?
- **Answer:** Ending the U.S. combat role by equipping and training South Vietnam; phased withdrawal from nearly 536,000 troops in 1968 to fewer than 100,000 by 1972 as their Air Force became the world’s fourth largest.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-034
- **Prompt:** What distinguished Operation Linebacker in 1972?
- **Answer:** It blunted a Communist offensive after reduced U.S. ground presence, using freer strategy/tactics and precision-guided/laser-guided bombs—e.g., 14 F-4s destroyed the Thanh Hóa Bridge on 13 May 1972 with 24 smart bombs.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-035
- **Prompt:** What was Operation Linebacker II, and what settlement followed?
- **Answer:** An intense 11-day December 1972 campaign (729 B-52s dropping 15,000 tons; fighters adding 5,000) that broke the peace-talk deadlock. A comprehensive ceasefire was signed 28 January 1973; the source says the last U.S. troops left 29 March 1974.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-036
- **Prompt:** Despite massive airpower in Vietnam, what political outcome does AFH 1 record?
- **Answer:** The United States did not win decisively; though the USAF flew more than five million sorties and dropped six million tons of bombs, Vietnam was unified under a Communist regime on 2 July 1976.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-037
- **Prompt:** For what actions is William H. Pitsenbarger remembered?
- **Answer:** On 11 April 1966 he left a helicopter into a firefight, refused evacuation, treated and protected wounded, and fought until killed. Posthumously promoted to SSgt, he received the Air Force Cross, later upgraded to the Medal of Honor (8 December 2000)—first enlisted Airman to receive both posthumously (source: “Airmen”).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-038
- **Prompt:** Why does AFH 1 call Duane Hackney among the most decorated enlisted Airmen?
- **Answer:** On 6 February 1967 he put his parachute on a rescued pilot when their HH-3E was hit, then survived being blown out with another chute. He earned the Air Force Cross, many other decorations (70+), and the Cheney Award; he retired as a CMSgt.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-039
- **Prompt:** What heroism at Lima Site 85 made Richard L. Etchberger notable?
- **Answer:** On 11 March 1968 at the Laos radar site he defended comrades, called air strikes, and loaded three Airmen into rescue slings under fire before being fatally wounded aboard the helicopter. His Air Force Cross was upgraded to the Medal of Honor on 21 September 2010—first DoD E-9 so honored.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-040
- **Prompt:** What did John L. Levitow do to earn the Medal of Honor?
- **Answer:** On 24 February 1969 aboard a damaged AC-47, despite ~40 shrapnel wounds, he saved a crewmember and dragged a burning magnesium flare out the cargo door before it ignited. Lowest-ranking Airman to receive the MoH for wartime heroism; namesake of the top enlisted PME award.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-041
- **Prompt:** What later Southeast Asia and heritage roles does AFH 1 give Wayne Fisk?
- **Answer:** He took part in the Son Tay raid and the USS *Mayaquez* recovery; recognized as the last American in ground combat with Communist forces in Southeast Asia; first USAF enlisted U.S. Jaycees Ten Outstanding Young Men recipient (1979); first director of the Air Force Enlisted Heritage Hall (1986).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-042
- **Prompt:** What training change does AFH 1 credit after Vietnam, and when/where did it begin?
- **Answer:** Red Flag at the USAF Weapons and Tactics Center, Nellis AFB, by 1975—realistic combat training after Nixon ended the draft in 1973 for an all-volunteer force; credited with revolutionizing USAF training.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-043
- **Prompt:** Which post-Vietnam aircraft does AFH 1 link to dogfighting, CAS/interdiction, and stealth?
- **Answer:** F-15 Eagle and F-16 Fighting Falcon for maneuverable dogfighting; A-10 Thunderbolt for deep attack, interdiction, and close air support; F-117 Nighthawk stealth fighter operationalized in 1980.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-044
- **Prompt:** How did precision technologies change USAF doctrinal focus after Vietnam?
- **Answer:** Laser-guided bombs, electro-optically guided missiles, and related precision systems shifted doctrine from strategic bombing toward pinpoint bombing through economy of force.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-045
- **Prompt:** What was Operation Eagle Claw meant to do, and why was it aborted?
- **Answer:** April 1980 attempt to rescue 52 American hostages from the Tehran embassy (held 4 November 1979–20 January 1981). Field commanders aborted after too many obstacles and helicopter mechanical problems.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-046
- **Prompt:** How were the Iran hostages freed, and what duration record does AFH 1 state?
- **Answer:** Political negotiations secured release and return home; the crisis is called the longest hostage crisis in recorded history (444 days).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-047
- **Prompt:** Why did the United States launch Operation Urgent Fury in Grenada?
- **Answer:** After an October 1983 coup assassinated Prime Minister Maurice Bishop and closed the airport, President Reagan intervened to rescue about 600 American medical students and avoid another Iran-style hostage crisis.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-048
- **Prompt:** What USAF contributions and political outcomes does AFH 1 note for Urgent Fury?
- **Answer:** Mainly airlift and special operations; AC-130s showed versatility and accuracy. Despite UN General Assembly criticism, U.S. public support was strong; an interim government and 1984 elections followed, and Grenada remained democratic.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-049
- **Prompt:** What Libyan leadership change and mid-1980s threat does ¶3.11 open with?
- **Answer:** In 1969 junior officers led by Muammar al-Qaddafi (source also notes Gaddafi/uses Qadhafi) overthrew the pro-Western monarchy; by the mid-1980s Libya sponsored worldwide terrorism, training camps, and related violence.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-050
- **Prompt:** What 1980s U.S. responses to Libyan terrorism does AFH 1 describe?
- **Answer:** NSDD 138 (3 April 1984) set preemptive/retaliatory policy; sanctions and public denunciation followed earlier attacks; after the La Belle discotheque bombing in West Berlin, Reagan authorized USAF, Navy, and Marine Corps air strikes within a week.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-051
- **Prompt:** What 2011 Libya operation does ¶3.11 place in Section 3A, and what followed NATO’s assumption of control?
- **Answer:** Operation Odyssey Dawn implemented a UN SCR 1973 no-fly zone in March 2011; NATO’s Operation Unified Protector then took the no-fly zone and strikes. Qadhafi was killed 20 October 2011; conflict continued into a second civil war in 2014.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-052
- **Prompt:** What post-2015 Libyan political status does AFH 1 state?
- **Answer:** A UN-assisted December 2015 cease-fire led to a new “unity government,” but with little public confidence; the country remains under that unity government.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-053
- **Prompt:** What canal-control background and Noriega threat set up Operation Just Cause?
- **Answer:** The United States controlled the Panama Canal until joint ownership under the 1979 Torrijos–Carter Treaties. By the late 1980s dictator Manuel Noriega’s drug trafficking and hostility endangered ~35,000 Americans and treaty integrity.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-054
- **Prompt:** What scale and USAF role does AFH 1 give Operation Just Cause (1989)?
- **Answer:** Largest, most complex U.S. air operation since Vietnam—four services, more than 250 aircraft. The USAF mainly airlifted troops/supplies and debuted the F-117; on night one, 84 aircraft dropped nearly 5,000 troops from 500 feet—largest nighttime airborne drop since World War II.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-055
- **Prompt:** How did Operation Just Cause end for Noriega and the canal?
- **Answer:** Organized resistance ended in six days; Noriega surrendered 3 January 1990 and was flown to Miami for federal drug and money-laundering charges. The canal fully transferred to Panama on 31 December 1999.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3A; A—Remembering

## Section 3B — The Air Force and the Post-Cold War

### C03-FC-056
- **Prompt:** What defensive concept did President Reagan propose on 23 March 1983 to replace mutually assured destruction?
- **Answer:** Assured survival through the Strategic Defense Initiative—space-based lasers, particle beams, railguns, and fast ground-launched missiles to intercept ICBMs in the outer atmosphere and space.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3B; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-057
- **Prompt:** What did the INF Treaty eliminate, and what 2019 status change does AFH 1 record?
- **Answer:** Signed December 1987 (in force June 1988), it eliminated ground-launched cruise and ballistic missiles of 500–5,500 km range. Citing Russian noncompliance, the United States suspended obligations in February 2019 and withdrew in August 2019 after Russia’s March suspension.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3B; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-058
- **Prompt:** What START I limits and USAF alert/command changes does AFH 1 state?
- **Answer:** Signed July 1991 (in force December 1994; expired December 2009) with agreement to reduce to 6,000 total warheads on deployed ICBMs, SLBMs, and heavy bombers. In September 1991 heavy bombers left alert; SAC inactivated June 1992 and U.S. Strategic Command assumed remaining USAF and Navy strategic nuclear forces.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3B; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-059
- **Prompt:** What became of START II and START III in AFH 1’s account?
- **Answer:** START II (January 1993) would have cut deployed warheads to a stated range of 3,500 nautical miles but never officially took effect; START III talks (1997) were never signed after negotiations broke down.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3B; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-060
- **Prompt:** What is New START, and what expiration timeline does AFH 1 give?
- **Answer:** The April 2010 U.S.–Russia strategic offensive arms treaty entered into force February 2011, was extended once for five years in February 2021, and (per the source) expires February 2026.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3B; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-061
- **Prompt:** How did Gorbachev’s leadership relate to late-Cold War arms control in AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Beginning March 1985, Soviet Communist Party General Secretary Mikhail Gorbachev initiated major changes in Soviet–American relations that framed treaties such as INF and later START agreements.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3B; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-062
- **Prompt:** Why did Iraq’s 2 August 1990 invasion of Kuwait alarm the United States?
- **Answer:** Saddam Hussein’s roughly 100,000 troops seized oil-rich Kuwait as a claimed 19th province; with a huge army and nuclear ambitions, Iraq threatened Saudi Arabia and potentially 50 percent of the world’s oil.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3B; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-063
- **Prompt:** What early Desert Shield deployments does AFH 1 highlight?
- **Answer:** After a UN sanction and a 35-nation coalition, on 8 August 1990 twenty-four F-15Cs arrived in Saudi Arabia from Langley AFB after a 15-hour flight; within a week C-141/C-5 airlift delivered the 82d Airborne Division and elements of the 1st Tactical Fighter Wing.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3B; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-064
- **Prompt:** What air-deployment claim and MAC totals does AFH 1 give for Desert Shield?
- **Answer:** It eclipsed the Berlin Airlift as history’s greatest air deployment: by late first month 1,220 Allied aircraft were combat-ready; August 1990–January 1991 MAC flew 20,500 strategic airlift missions, delivering 534,000 personnel and 542,000 tons of cargo.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3B; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-065
- **Prompt:** When and how did Operation Desert Storm’s air war open?
- **Answer:** At 0100 on 17 January 1991, three MH-53J Pave Lows led nine Army Apaches on the first strike; under Lt Gen Charles A. Horner (CENTAF), aircraft from 14 countries executed the master attack plan.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶3.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 3B; A—Remembering

### C03-FC-066
- **Prompt:** What platforms does AFH 1 associate with the “great scud hunt” and precision stealth strikes?
