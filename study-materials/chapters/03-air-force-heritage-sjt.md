# Chapter 3 Supplemental Situational Judgment Practice

> **Status:** Draft and provisional for 27E5/27E6 preparation; the official 2027 format is unconfirmed.
>
> **Reserve-chapter status:** Chapter 3 was outside the historical 26E5 and 26E6 catalog scopes and remains reserve pending the 2027 WAPS catalogs. Prior exclusion does not prove future inclusion, weighting, or scenario testability.
>
> **Two-track source rule:** For exam-source study, preserve the facts, dates, names, quantities, terminology, and ambiguities in the AFH edition assigned by the controlling catalog. For duty use, separately verify current organizations, missions, policies, terminology, force counts, locations, and authorities; duty-current information does not overwrite a catalog-controlled answer.
>
> These ten supplemental scenarios are wholly original. They are not actual, recalled, reconstructed, or predicted PFE items. They are supplemental SJT practice only, not a claim that Air Force heritage is scenario-tested.
>
> AFH 1 assigns Chapter 3 sections **3A and 3B as A—Remembering for both SSgt and TSgt**, and section **3C as A—Remembering for SSgt and B—Understanding for TSgt**. Those designations apply to recognizing, recalling, or—where rated B—explaining Chapter 3 heritage facts; they do not make these scenarios A-level or B-level behavioral items. Chapter 3's heritage facts provide context only and **do not establish behavioral scoring keys**. The most-effective and least-effective selections below are MyPromotion educational judgments based on Chapter 1 professionalism, core values, and ethical reasoning.
>
> The official Air Force uses subject-matter experts and behavioral scientists to establish operational SJT scoring keys. These supplemental judgments are not official keys or substitutes for current supervisory, safety, technical, legal, ethics, public-affairs, reporting, or command procedures. Any process or authority facts needed to rank an option are supplied within the scenario as invented instructional constraints.

## How to use this set

Each scenario produces two selections:

1. Select the **most effective** response.
2. Select the **least effective** response.

Read every option before choosing. Judge the learner's present-day conduct, not the wisdom of a historical actor. Consider whether the response:

- represents sources, performance, and uncertainty honestly;
- accepts responsibility within the authority stated in the scenario;
- protects mission requirements without treating caution or speed as automatic answers;
- recognizes contributors with accuracy, dignity, and impartiality;
- supports disciplined readiness, stewardship, and coordinated response; and
- strengthens teamwork across components, specialties, and partner audiences.

Ten scenarios produce 20 response selections, mirroring only the historical public structure announced for the [22E5/22E6 cycles in December 2021](https://www.afpc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/2874484/waps-adjusts-for-22e6-22e5-testing-cycles/). The separate [public SJT informational guide](https://www.studyguides.af.mil/Portals/15/documents/Notices/Situational-Judgment-Test-Informational-Guide.pdf) explains the four-option most/least format. Neither source confirms the 2027 structure, and the historical 22E5/22E6 format does not show that Chapter 3 heritage was or will be tested through scenarios.

## C03-SJT-001 — Closing a war that did not end that way

You are the reviewer for an unclassified public heritage briefing on the Korean War. The draft closing slide states that the fighting "ended with a lasting peace treaty that closed the war." The cited AFH 1 passage instead describes a 27 July 1953 armistice, creation of the demilitarized zone, and that the two Koreas remain technically still at war with no peace treaty signed. You may replace one conclusion slide before delivery; the speaker must otherwise use the existing, source-cleared material.

### Response options

A. Combine the armistice facts with a peace-treaty claim so the ending sounds conclusive.  
B. Keep the peace-treaty line and add only a soft oral caveat if someone asks.  
C. Replace the ending with AFH 1's armistice wording and the still-at-war note.  
D. Delete every statement about the war's end and close only on aircraft types.

### Most effective: C

Paragraph 3.3 supplies the historical context for the armistice and the absence of a peace treaty; it does not prescribe a behavioral scoring key for a briefing editor. C is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it uses the learner's stated revision authority to keep a clear closing that remains within the source. Chapter 1 honesty, accountability, and disciplined communication provide the behavioral basis. Chapter 3 heritage facts do not establish the behavioral scoring key.

### Least effective: A

A manufactures a settled peace-treaty ending the cited source does not support and presents disagreement as resolved fact. The Chapter 3 passage explains why the ending matters, but Chapter 1 accurate representation and transparency provide the behavioral basis for the least-effective judgment.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It blends unsupported treaty language into the conclusion instead of correcting the known conflict with the source.
- **B — Ineffective:** An oral caveat leaves the displayed closing still communicating an unsupported end-state.
- **C — Most effective:** It preserves a conclusive ending that matches the designated source without inventing a treaty.
- **D — Partially effective:** It avoids the false claim but withholds a source-supported closing the reviewer is authorized to fix.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶3.3 (historical context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Accurate heritage briefing; honesty; accountability; disciplined communication  
**ADTC context:** 3A, A—Remembering  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of public-briefing realism; approved source and public-affairs processes control real releases

## C03-SJT-002 — Heroism without invented supremacy

You edit a unit heritage exhibit caption on Vietnam-era enlisted valor. The draft calls Chief Master Sergeant Duane Hackney "the undisputed most decorated enlisted Airman in history." The approved AFH 1 extract documents extensive awards and states that at one point—and often even now—he is referred to as the "most decorated enlisted Airman," without presenting that phrase as an independently verified, permanent ranking. You may revise the caption; a side panel can carry award details.

### Response options

A. Drop personal recognition and describe only aircraft types used on rescue missions.  
B. Keep the absolute superlative and bury the source's cautious wording in a footnote.  
C. List every decoration in the caption without any interpretive phrase about standing.  
D. Credit documented awards and retain the source's cautious "referred to as" wording.

### Most effective: D

Paragraph 3.7 supplies historical context for Hackney's service and the source's cautious recognition language; it does not create a rule for scoring exhibit editors. D is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it honors documented valor without converting a referential phrase into an absolute claim. Chapter 1 honesty, respect, and accountable recognition provide the behavioral basis. Chapter 3 heritage facts do not establish the behavioral scoring key.

### Least effective: B

B knowingly preserves an absolute ranking the designated extract does not establish as settled fact. Audience impact does not justify presenting a stronger claim than the source supports. Chapter 1 honesty and respect provide the behavioral basis for the least-effective judgment.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It avoids exaggeration but withholds the documented human contribution central to the exhibit.
- **B — Least effective:** It retains an unsupported absolute ranking while treating the source caveat as optional fine print.
- **C — Partially effective:** Award counts stay factual but omit the carefully limited recognition language the source already provides.
- **D — Most effective:** It keeps recognition precise, dignified, and aligned with the approved extract.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶3.7 (historical context); ¶¶1.1, 1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Crediting enlisted heroes without exaggeration; honesty; respect; accountability  
**ADTC context:** 3A, A—Remembering  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human historical-attribution review; use source-supported language rather than inferred permanent rankings

B. Keep the compressed 1991 ending and add a vague note that timelines sometimes vary.  
C. Merge both operations under one end date so readers see a single tidy conclusion.

### Most effective: A

Paragraph 3.14 supplies the historical context distinguishing Provide Comfort and Provide Comfort II; it does not prescribe an editorial response. Chapter 1 honesty, accountability, and disciplined treatment of uncertainty provide the behavioral basis. Chapter 3 heritage facts do not establish the behavioral scoring key.

### Least effective: C

C creates a composite ending that neither the draft conflict nor the designated baseline supports as a single fact. Chapter 3 explains why the phases differ, but Chapter 1 accurate representation and transparency provide the behavioral basis for the least-effective judgment.

### Option analysis

- **A — Most effective:** It uses the assigned source consistently while documenting the precise conflict for follow-up.
- **B — Ineffective:** It retains non-baseline compression and offers only a vague caveat instead of identifying the conflict.
- **C — Least effective:** It manufactures apparent agreement by erasing the source's operation and date distinctions.

## C03-SJT-004 — Designing for the unpreparedness lesson

You lead a classroom readiness exercise built around the Korean War narrative that early operations illustrated the dangers of being unprepared as Airmen relearned close air support and interdiction skills. The stated objective is to show how neglected perishable skills reduce later mission effectiveness. After the first run, teams maximized opening launches but never had to refresh or demonstrate those skills before later taskings. You are authorized to choose one design change for the next run; every option fits the available class time and budget.

### Response options

A. Add more opening aircraft while leaving skill-refresh requirements outside the scoring model.  
B. Require each team to complete a limited skills-refresh gate whose neglect reduces later effectiveness.  
C. Increase weather disruptions and let facilitators mention perishable skills only in the debrief.  
D. Reset every team's skill status between rounds so neglected proficiency never carries forward.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 3.3 supplies historical context about unpreparedness and relearning combat-support skills; it does not establish a behavioral answer key for exercise design. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it directly aligns the next iteration with the stated learning objective and observed design gap. Chapter 1 accountability, mission stewardship, discipline, and continuous improvement provide the behavioral basis. Chapter 3 heritage facts do not establish the behavioral scoring key.

### Least effective: D

D removes the accumulated proficiency consequences the stated objective requires learners to experience. The historical campaign is context, not a verdict on this design choice. Chapter 1 mission focus and accountable stewardship provide the behavioral basis for finding D least effective.

### Option analysis

- **A — Partially effective:** It adds resources but still leaves perishable-skill readiness outside learner decisions.
- **B — Most effective:** It introduces the missing relationship through one decision whose later effects learners can observe.
- **C — Ineffective:** It increases difficulty, but skill atrophy remains commentary rather than part of the exercise's choices.
- **D — Least effective:** It creates equal rounds by erasing the sustained-skill consequences the revision should make meaningful.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶3.3 (historical context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Readiness lessons from unpreparedness narratives; mission stewardship; discipline; continuous improvement  
**ADTC context:** 3A, A—Remembering  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human instructional-design review; this classroom model does not represent current readiness procedures

## C03-SJT-005 — Two tasked mission types, one recommendation

You are the NCO asked for a next-quarter training-emphasis recommendation after a heritage seminar on concurrent Gulf War-era combat operations and worldwide humanitarian airlift. Your unit's approved annual plan tasks both combat-support readiness and humanitarian airlift support. The commander wants one written recommendation today; you may propose allocation percentages within existing flying-hour and simulator limits; you may not cancel either tasked mission type; and the operations officer owns final approval.

### Response options

A. Recommend dropping humanitarian blocks this quarter so combat tasks consume every training hour.  
B. Split hours evenly by default without stating effects on either tasked mission's shortfalls.  
C. Recommend a balanced allocation for both tasked mission types and state the capacity tradeoffs.  
D. Propose only humanitarian airlift drills and treat combat-support currency as next quarter's problem.

### Most effective: C

Paragraph 3.15 supplies historical context about sustaining military support and humanitarian relief while other operations continued; it does not rank a modern training recommendation. C is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it keeps both tasked mission types visible and gives the approval authority the tradeoff information needed to decide. Chapter 1 mission focus, accountability, and teamwork provide the behavioral basis. Chapter 3 heritage facts do not establish the behavioral scoring key.

### Least effective: A

A cancels a tasked mission type the stem forbids canceling and treats combat emphasis as an automatic trump rather than a planning balance the commander must weigh. Historical combat urgency does not authorize ignoring the stated dual tasking. Chapter 1 duty, accountability, and mission stewardship provide the behavioral basis for the least-effective judgment.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It violates the stated dual-tasking boundary and withholds a balanced recommendation the commander requested.
- **B — Ineffective:** Equal splitting is executable but omits the shortfall effects the approval decision needs.
- **C — Most effective:** It preserves both tasked requirements and makes the resource tradeoffs explicit for accountable approval.
- **D — Partially effective:** It protects humanitarian readiness but postpones the other tasked mission without authority to do so.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶3.15 (historical context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Humanitarian versus combat mission balance; mission focus; accountability; stewardship  
**ADTC context:** 3B, A—Remembering

## C03-SJT-006 — Shared homeland board, shared problem

You lead the Regular Air Force cell for an unclassified homeland air-response exercise with Air National Guard and Air Force Reserve partners. You decide how to use your status NCO today.

### Response options

A. Assign the status NCO to the joint cell's reconciliation work and accept thinner webpage polish.  
B. Keep the NCO on webpage polish and email the Guard cell your supporting extracts only.  
C. Load Regular Air Force-written Guard status lines onto the shared board without the joint cell.  
D. Ask the joint cell to blank Guard entries so the shared board shows no visible conflict.

### Most effective: A

Paragraph 3.16 supplies historical context about Guard, Reserve, and Regular Air Force participation in homeland air response; it does not prescribe this exercise workflow. A is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it uses the stated loan path to place expertise with the official owner of the shared board, trading optional polish for resolution of a shared required product. Chapter 1 Total Force professionalism, mission focus, and teamwork provide the behavioral basis. Chapter 3 heritage facts do not establish the behavioral scoring key.

### Least effective: C

C places revised partner-component text onto the shared board despite the stem's rule that the joint coordination cell alone owns that board. Under that stated authority, C is uniquely least effective: it does not merely recommend a poor content choice through the proper owner, but substitutes the Regular Air Force lead's draft for the cell's control. Chapter 1 respect for process, mission responsibility, and interdependent teamwork provide the behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Most effective:** It places relevant expertise on the shared problem through the official who owns the board.

- **C — Least effective:** It applies expertise while taking final board control away from the joint cell that alone owns it.
- **D — Ineffective:** It uses the proper channel but seeks to drop a partner contribution instead of resolving the conflict.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶3.16 (historical context); ¶¶1.1, 1.2, 1.3.2–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)
