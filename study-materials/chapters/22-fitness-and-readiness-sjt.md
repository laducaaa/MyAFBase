# Chapter 22 Situational Judgment Practice

> **Status:** Draft and provisional for 27E5/27E6. Chapter 22 is shared core in the historical 26E5/26E6 catalog scopes. All sections are treated as B—Understanding for both SSgt and TSgt (B/B).
>
> These scenarios are wholly original. They are not actual, recalled, reconstructed, or predicted PFE items. The official Air Force uses subject-matter experts and behavioral scientists to establish operational SJT scoring keys. Chapter 22 describes fitness, nutrition, substance-use, and readiness-of-mind facts, but those facts alone do not establish an official behavioral scoring key. All most-effective and least-effective keys below are MyPromotion educational judgments grounded in Chapter 1 professionalism, core values, and ethical reasoning, with Chapter 22 material supplying context only. They are not official scoring keys or substitutes for current command, FAC/UFPM, medical, ADAPT, mental-health, ACE, or emergency procedures.
>
> **Not medical or crisis authority:** Scenarios use AFH 1 fitness and readiness facts as context only. They are not clinical advice, FA-administration scripts, ADAPT diagnoses, or suicide-intervention certifications.
>
> **Sensitive-topic posture:** Substance misuse and suicide-risk scenarios use plain, dignified language. They do not sensationalize harm and are not operational scripts.
>
> **Two-track source rule:** For exam-source study, preserve the facts in the AFH edition assigned by the controlling catalog—including volatile FA/BCA snapshots. For duty use, separately verify current fitness, ADAPT, and mental-health rules; duty-current information does not overwrite a catalog-controlled answer.

## How to use this set

Each scenario produces two items:

1. Select the **most effective** response.
2. Select the **least effective** response.

Read every option before choosing. Evaluate whether the response:

- represents fitness, nutrition, substance, and mental-readiness facts honestly;
- protects people, mission, and help-seeking without stigma or concealment;
- refuses falsified scores, unsafe shortcuts, or pressure to hide risk;
- stays within stated role and routes care to UFPM/FAC, medical, ADAPT, command, or emergency helpers as appropriate;
- uses the two-track caution when FA/BCA snapshots may differ from current rules; and
- reflects integrity, accountability, loyalty, respect, and mission stewardship.

Chapter 22 supplies fitness-and-readiness context; it does not prescribe the behavioral sequence in every response. When a rationale depends on general professional judgment—such as honesty, courage, accountability, loyalty, respect, mission focus, or teamwork—it is identified as an application of the cited foundational Chapter 1 paragraphs.

Ten scenarios produce 20 response selections, mirroring the historical public structure announced for the [22E5/22E6 cycles in December 2021](https://www.afpc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/2874484/waps-adjusts-for-22e6-22e5-testing-cycles/). The separate [public SJT informational guide](https://www.studyguides.af.mil/Portals/15/documents/Notices/Situational-Judgment-Test-Informational-Guide.pdf) explains the four-option most/least format. The 2027 structure is unconfirmed. This set covers only selected Chapter 22 concepts and is not a full-spectrum SJT simulation.

## C22-SJT-001 — Honest fitness assessment scoring

You help administer unit fitness assessments as a trained PTL/FAC augmentee. After a run, a peer asks you to “round up” a failing cardio time on the DAF Form 4446 so a friend “doesn’t get in trouble,” then enter the inflated score in myFitness. AFH 1 requires documenting and signing accurate results on DAF Form 4446 and obtaining the Airman’s signature.

### Response options

A. Inflate the cardio time on the scorecard and in myFitness to protect the friend.  
B. Record the actual performance on DAF Form 4446, obtain required signatures, and enter accurate scores—refusing to falsify results.  
C. Destroy the scorecard so no score exists.  
D. Tell the Airman to videotape a make-up run secretly for an appeal.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 22.2 and 22.6–22.8 supply accurate documentation and no-videotape context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it preserves integrity of the official FA. Chapter 1 integrity and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A falsifies an official assessment and defeats the PFP’s readiness purpose. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** Falsifies official FA documentation.
- **B — Most effective:** Accurate DAF Form 4446/myFitness documentation.
- **C — Ineffective:** Conceals required results.
- **D — Ineffective:** Videotaping is not allowed and should not be used in appeals.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶22.2, 22.6–22.8 (FA documentation context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** FA integrity; DAF Form 4446; accountability  
**ADTC:** 22B, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of FAC process realism; current DAFMAN 36-2905 controls real testing

## C22-SJT-002 — Scheduling and FSQ honesty

An Airman is approaching the end of their FA currency window. They tell you their FSQ answers changed since they first scheduled in myFitness because a new medical issue started, but they want to “just test anyway” without telling the PFA Administrator. AFH 1 says members must inform the PFA Administrator if FSQ responses change between submission and execution, and Airmen should seek medical evaluation when a condition may impact the assessment.

### Response options

A. Advise the Airman to hide the change and test silently to avoid delay.  
B. Urge the Airman to inform the PFA Administrator of the FSQ change and seek appropriate medical evaluation/coordination through UFPM/medical channels before unsafe testing.  
C. Order them to skip the FA permanently without commander/medical process.  
D. Post their medical details on the unit social media page for “transparency.”

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 22.2 and 22.6 supply FSQ-change and medical-evaluation context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it protects safety and follows the notification duty. Chapter 1 integrity and respect for people provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A conceals a safety-relevant change and risks unsafe testing. Chapter 1 integrity and loyalty to Airmen provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** Hides FSQ changes contrary to AFH 1’s notify-administrator rule.
- **B — Most effective:** Honest FSQ update plus medical/UFPM coordination.
- **C — Ineffective:** Invents a permanent skip outside process.
- **D — Ineffective:** Violates privacy and is not a fitness process.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶22.2, 22.6 (FSQ/medical context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** FSQ; safety; honesty  
**ADTC:** 22A / 22B, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of medical-privacy realism

## C22-SJT-003 — Body composition improvement without stigma

A peer confides their WHtR was recorded above 0.55 and they were enrolled in the informal self-directed BCIP. Another Airman mocks them as a “failure” and suggests the commander should punish them immediately during the adaptation-period rules AFH 1 describes. AFH 1 states informal BCIP enrollment is not considered a failure to meet the BCP standard and commanders will not take administrative actions during informal BCIP; adaptation-period admin actions based solely on BCA results are not authorized.

### Response options

A. Join the mockery and demand immediate separation based only on the BCA.  
B. Correct the misconception: informal BCIP is not a failure; support the Airman’s improvement plan and avoid unauthorized admin-action pressure based solely on BCA results.  
C. Tell them to ignore HeRO guidance and skip the medical evaluation step.  
D. Alter myBodyComp entries to hide the WHtR.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 22.3–22.5 supply informal BCIP and adaptation-period context (2025 snapshots). B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it is accurate, supportive, and rejects improper punishment pressure. Chapter 1 respect and loyalty provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A stigmatizes a teammate and pushes unauthorized punishment contrary to printed BCP limits. Chapter 1 respect and integrity provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** Mockery plus improper sole-BCA punishment pressure.
- **B — Most effective:** Accurate informal-BCIP understanding and constructive support.
- **C — Ineffective:** Undercuts printed Airman BCIP actions.
- **D — Ineffective:** Falsifies official body-composition records.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶22.3–22.5 (BCP/BCIP context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** WHtR; informal BCIP; professionalism  
**ADTC:** 22A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Volatile BCP adaptation/WHtR snapshot—revalidate for duty use

## C22-SJT-004 — Nutrition wingman support

Your shift worker asks how to stop “crashing” mid-shift. They mainly drink energy drinks and skip meals, then binge late. AFH 1 emphasizes energy balance, SMART goals, hydration with water, and notes that caffeine and energy drinks do not hydrate; sports drinks may help in prolonged exertion, while balanced carbs/protein support fueling.

### Response options

A. Tell them to rely only on high-dose energy drinks and skip water.  
B. Help them set a SMART fueling/hydration plan emphasizing balanced meals, water, and sensible peri-workout nutrition—and encourage healthcare guidance before supplements.  
C. Shame them publicly at roll call about weight.  
D. Recommend alcohol after every shift as a recovery beverage.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 22.11–22.17 supply nutrition/hydration context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it builds sustainable habits without unsafe shortcuts. Chapter 1 loyalty and mission stewardship provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A doubles down on non-hydrating stimulants and skips foundational fueling. Chapter 1 loyalty to Airmen and mission readiness provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** Energy drinks are not meant to hydrate; ignores balanced fueling.
- **B — Most effective:** SMART, food-first, water-forward support with supplement caution.
- **C — Ineffective:** Stigma without help.
- **D — Ineffective:** Alcohol does not hydrate and adds empty calories/risk.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶22.11–22.17 (nutrition/hydration context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** SMART goals; hydration; wingman support  
**ADTC:** 22C, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Not individualized medical/dietetic advice

## C22-SJT-005 — Tobacco-free example

During a break, a trainee in technical training (in uniform) asks where the nearest designated tobacco area is so they can vape before class. AFH 1 states the USAF goal is a tobacco-free force and prohibits students in technical training from using tobacco products while in uniform.

### Response options

A. Escort them to vape in uniform behind the building “where no one will see.”  
B. Explain the tobacco-free goal and the in-uniform technical-training prohibition; redirect them to authorized cessation resources/command guidance rather than facilitating uniformed use.  
C. Confiscate their pay card as punishment without authority.  
D. Dare them to use tobacco on the playground to “save time.”

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 22.18–22.21 supply tobacco-free and training-environment restriction context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it upholds the standard and offers constructive redirection. Chapter 1 integrity and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A facilitates prohibited uniformed tobacco use. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** Assists a prohibited action.
- **B — Most effective:** Upholds restriction and points to cessation/command-consistent help.
- **C — Ineffective:** Unauthorized punishment sidestep.
- **D — Ineffective:** Playgrounds have larger standoff rules; still improper.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶22.18–22.21 (tobacco context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Tobacco-free force; training restrictions  
**ADTC:** 22D, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt

## C22-SJT-006 — Suspected substance problem and ADAPT lane

You supervise an Airman whose duty performance is slipping. They acknowledge drinking heavily after work and ask you to “just handle it in-house” with no documentation and no helping-agency involvement. AFH 1 says if signs exist, talk with the individual, document and discuss with supervisor/first sergeant/commander to expedite care, and that ADAPT personnel diagnose substance use disorders; RegAF alcohol-related misconduct triggers assessment referral.

### Response options

A. Agree to keep it entirely secret, create no documentation, and block any ADAPT/command visibility forever.  
B. Document concerning observations, involve the first sergeant/commander as appropriate, and help the Airman access ADAPT/assessment pathways while staying in your supervisory lane.  
C. Announce a public diagnosis at roll call.  
D. Order them to buy hemp/CBD products to “self-medicate legally.”

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 22.22–22.25 supply ADAPT/leadership context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it expedites care without pretending to diagnose. Chapter 1 loyalty, accountability, and respect provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A isolates the Airman from required help and conceals risk. Chapter 1 loyalty and integrity provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** Secrecy that blocks care and accountability.
- **B — Most effective:** Document, elevate appropriately, connect to ADAPT—without diagnosing.
- **C — Ineffective:** Stigmatizing pseudo-diagnosis.
- **D — Ineffective:** Hemp/CBD ingestion is prohibited; Article 92 context.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶22.22–22.25 (ADAPT/leadership context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** ADAPT referral; supervisor lane; no stigma theater  
**ADTC:** 22D, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of ADAPT process realism; not clinical advice

## C22-SJT-007 — Stress recognition without minimization

A coworker shows cognitive and behavioral stress signs—racing thoughts, withdrawal, and neglected self-care—after weeks of long hours. They say they are “fine” and ask you not to mention it. AFH 1 identifies cognitive/emotional/behavioral/physical stress reactions and encourages early recognition plus coping supports, including helping agencies.

### Response options

A. Ignore all warning signs because they said they are fine.  
B. Privately express concern, listen without judgment, encourage healthy coping and appropriate support resources, and involve the chain when risk or duty impact warrants.  
C. Mock them for being “weak” in front of the team.  
D. Demand they work longer hours with no breaks to “prove toughness.”

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 22.27–22.30 supply psychological first aid and stress-management context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it recognizes stress early and connects help. Chapter 1 respect and loyalty provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: C

C adds stigma and destroys trust when support is needed. Chapter 1 respect provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** Misses early intervention opportunity.
- **B — Most effective:** Concern + listening + resources + appropriate elevation.
- **C — Least effective:** Stigma and ridicule.
- **D — Ineffective:** Increases overload rather than managing it.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶22.27–22.30 (stress/PFA context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Stress categories; wingman support  
**ADTC:** 22E, B—Understanding

## C22-SJT-008 — ACE when warning signs appear

An Airman says life feels hopeless after legal and relationship problems and hints they “might not be around.” You are unsure about their safety. AFH 1’s ACE model says to ask directly about killing/hurting oneself, care by listening without judgment, not promise secrecy, and escort to command or professional support without leaving them alone; use emergency resources/Veterans Crisis Line as needed.

### Response options

A. Promise total secrecy and leave them alone overnight to “think it over.”  
B. Ask directly about suicidal thoughts, listen with care, refuse secrecy promises, and escort them to command/professional/emergency help without leaving them alone.  
C. Argue philosophy until they “snap out of it,” then go home.  
D. Post their crisis details on a group chat for entertainment.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 22.36 supplies ACE context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it follows Ask–Care–Escort and protects life. Chapter 1 loyalty, courage, and respect provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A promises secrecy and abandons a person in distress. Chapter 1 loyalty and integrity provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** Secrecy + isolation increases risk.
- **B — Most effective:** ACE-consistent direct ask, care, and escort.
- **C — Ineffective:** Talks them out instead of getting help.
- **D — Ineffective:** Cruel privacy violation.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶22.36 (ACE context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** ACE; suicide prevention; escort  
**ADTC:** 22E, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Educational only—real crises require emergency services / Veterans Crisis Line / command

- **B — Most effective:** CAF-balanced Wingman leadership under pressure.
- **C — Ineffective:** Misstates spiritual domain (beliefs/principles/values sustaining purpose—not coerced uniformity).
- **D — Ineffective:** Abandons standards rather than integrating wellness with mission.
