## Section 9A — Promotion Systems and Programs

### C09-FC-001
- **Prompt:** What three DoD Directive 1304.20 goals does the enlisted promotion system support?
- **Answer:** Providing visible, relatively stable career progression opportunities; attracting, retaining, and motivating the people the military needs; and ensuring a uniform application of the principle of equal pay for equal work among the services.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9A; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-002
- **Prompt:** Which instruction provides detailed information regarding enlisted promotion systems, and to which component does Chapter 9 apply?
- **Answer:** AFI 36-2502, *Enlisted Airman Promotion and Demotion Programs*. Chapter 9 applies to RegAF enlisted promotions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9A; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-003
- **Prompt:** What limits the number of RegAF Airmen in the top five enlisted grades, and what factors affect SSgt–CMSgt promotion quotas?
- **Answer:** DoD limits the number of Airmen the RegAF may have in the top five enlisted grades. Quotas for Staff Sergeant through Chief Master Sergeant are tied to fiscal year-end strength and are affected by funding limits, regulatory limits, and the number of projected vacancies in specific grades.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9A; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-004
- **Prompt:** What public-law percentage limits apply to RegAF E-8 and E-9 averages in a fiscal year?
- **Answer:** The authorized average of enlisted members on RegAF status (other than for training) in pay grades E-8 and E-9 in a fiscal year may not be more than 2.5 percent and 1.25 percent, respectively (Title 10, U.S.C., Chapter 36).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9A; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-005
- **Prompt:** Under the Senior Airman Below-the-Zone Program, who may be considered, how often, and how early is the promotion effective date?
- **Answer:** Airmen in the grade of Airman First Class may be considered one time for early advancement to Senior Airman if they meet minimum eligibility criteria. If promoted BTZ, the promotion effective date is six months before their fully qualified date.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9A; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-006
- **Prompt:** In which months are Airmen considered for Senior Airman below-the-zone, and how do those months relate to eligibility quarters?
- **Answer:** Airmen are considered in the month (December, March, June, or September) before the quarter (January–March, April–June, July–September, or October–December) that they are eligible.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9A; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-007
- **Prompt:** What is the purpose of the Stripes for Exceptional Performers (STEP) Program, and what should not be the sole basis for a STEP promotion?
- **Answer:** STEP is designed to meet unique circumstances that, in a commander’s judgment, clearly warrant promotion for compelling, although perhaps not quantifiable, reasons. Isolated acts or specific achievements should not be the sole basis for promotion under this program.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9A; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-008
- **Prompt:** To which ranks may commanders promote under STEP, and what 12-month promotion-combination limit applies?
- **Answer:** Commanders at various organizational levels may promote a limited number of exemplary performing Airmen with exceptional potential to Staff Sergeant and Technical Sergeant. An individual may not receive more than one promotion under any combination of promotion programs within a 12-month period (with the exception that Senior Airmen must serve six months of time in grade before being promoted to Staff Sergeant).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9A; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-009
- **Prompt:** In which CAFSC do NCOs and SNCOs compete and test under WAPS, and how are contributing factors treated?
- **Answer:** They compete and test under WAPS in the control Air Force Specialty Code (CAFSC) held on the promotion eligibility cutoff date. Contributing factors are “weighted” or assigned points based on importance relative to promotion.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9A; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-010
- **Prompt:** What do the Promotion Fitness Exam (PFE) and Specialty Knowledge Test (SKT) each cover under WAPS?
- **Answer:** The PFE contains a wide range of USAF knowledge; the SKT covers AFSC broad technical knowledge.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9A; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-011
- **Prompt:** How does the USAF make WAPS promotion selections within AFSCs, and what happens when more than one individual has the same total score at the cutoff?
- **Answer:** Selections are made within each AFSC, not across all AFSCs—eligibles compete with those currently working in their AFSC. Selectees are those with the highest scores in each AFSC within quota limitations. If more than one individual has the same total score at the cutoff point, the USAF promotes everyone with that score.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9A; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-012
- **Prompt:** In what specialty framework do Airmen compete for promotion to MSgt, SMSgt, and CMSgt as of the PECD?
- **Answer:** To MSgt in their control AF specialty code; to SMSgt in the superintendent level of their control AF specialty code; and to CMSgt in the chief enlisted manager code of their control AF specialty code as of the PECD. Individuals with a reporting indicator or special duty indicator as CAFSC on the PECD compete within that indicator. SNCO promotions are accomplished via a central board.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9A; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-013
- **Prompt:** How often is the in-system supplemental promotion process typically run, and who competes?
- **Answer:** Typically monthly. Eligible Staff Sergeants through Senior Master Sergeants whose weighable data changes in their promotion file compete monthly. It also applies to Airmen who test after initial selects have been made (for example, deployed Airmen or anyone unable to test during their normal testing window).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9A; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-014
- **Prompt:** How often are SNCO supplemental evaluation boards conducted, and for which grades?
- **Answer:** Semiannually, for members promotion-eligible to Master Sergeant, Senior Master Sergeant, or Chief Master Sergeant.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9A; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-015
- **Prompt:** When may supplemental promotion consideration not be granted for an error or omission on the DVR or in the SNCO selection record?
- **Answer:** When the error or omission appeared on the data verification record or in the SNCO selection record in PRDA and the individual did not take the necessary steps to correct the error prior to promotion selection or prior to the evaluation board. (Exception noted: missing static closeout date evaluation has automatic-approval language; otherwise there are no automatic approvals when a record did not meet a previous board for which the member was eligible.)
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9A; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-016
- **Prompt:** How are fully documented SNCO supplemental consideration requests submitted?
- **Answer:** Submitted to the military personnel flight in writing with the recommendation of the individual’s unit commander, including proof of corrective or follow-up actions taken by the individual; the MPF forwards the request to Air Force Personnel Center for final approval.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9A; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-017
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 characterize the USAF promotion system’s baseline design for eligible Airmen recommended by their commander?
- **Answer:** It is designed to promote eligible Airmen recommended by their commander on a noncompetitive basis, while additional opportunities and factors also apply (BTZ, STEP, WAPS, SNCO boards, and supplemental processes).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9A; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-018
- **Prompt:** What STEP-related duty do commanders retain regarding eligibility?
- **Answer:** Commanders must ensure personnel who are promoted meet eligibility requirements.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9A; B—Understanding

## Section 9B — Promotion Cycles

### C09-FC-019
- **Prompt:** Why does the USAF establish promotion cycles?
- **Answer:** To ensure timely periodic promotions, permit accurate forecasting of vacancies, balance the promotion administrative workload, and provide promotion eligibility cutoff dates (PECD).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9B; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-020
- **Prompt:** What factors may promotion eligibility include according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Proper skill level, sufficient time in grade, sufficient time in service, commander recommendation, completion of enlisted PME, completion of a college degree, cumulative years of enlisted service, and high year of tenure.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9B; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-021
- **Prompt:** On what combination are Air Reserve Components promotions based?
- **Answer:** A combination of position vacancy, time-in-grade, time-in-service, fitness, and completion of appropriate professional military education.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9B; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-022
- **Prompt:** When may Airman Basic be promotion eligible to Airman?
- **Answer:** At six months of time in grade.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9B; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-023
- **Prompt:** When may Airman be promotion eligible to Airman First Class under the basic timeline?
- **Answer:** At 10 months of time in grade.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9B; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-024
- **Prompt:** How does a six-year initial enlistment affect promotion from Airman Basic to Airman First Class, and how is the A1C date of rank adjusted?
- **Answer:** Airmen initially enlisting for six years are promoted from Airman Basic to Airman First Class upon completion of either technical training or 20 weeks of technical training after BMT graduation (whichever occurs first). The A1C date of rank is adjusted to the signatory date on the BMT certificate, without back pay and allowances.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9B; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-025
- **Prompt:** What time-in-service / time-in-grade and skill-level requirements apply for promotion eligibility to Senior Airman?
- **Answer:** 36 months TIS and 20 months TIG, or 28 months TIG (whichever occurs first); required skill level in primary AFSC is 3-level. SrA BTZ is a one-time consideration to advance six months earlier than basic SrA timelines.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9B; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-026
- **Prompt:** What TIS/TIG, PECD, test cycle, and skill level apply for promotion eligibility to Staff Sergeant?
- **Answer:** Three years TIS and six months TIG; PECD 31 March; test cycle May–June; required skill level in primary AFSC is 5-level.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9B; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-027
- **Prompt:** What TIS/TIG, PECD, test cycle, and skill level apply for promotion eligibility to Technical Sergeant?
- **Answer:** Five years TIS and 23 months TIG; PECD 31 January; test cycle February–March; required skill level in primary AFSC is 7-level.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9B; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-028
- **Prompt:** What TIS/TIG, PECD, and skill level apply for promotion eligibility to Master Sergeant?
- **Answer:** Eight years TIS and 24 months TIG; PECD 30 November; required skill level in primary AFSC is 7-level.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9B; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-029
- **Prompt:** What TIS/TIG, PECD, and skill level apply for promotion eligibility to Senior Master Sergeant?
- **Answer:** Eleven years TIS and 20 months TIG; PECD 30 September; required skill level in primary AFSC is 7-level.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9B; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-030
- **Prompt:** What TIS/TIG, PECD, and skill level apply for promotion eligibility to Chief Master Sergeant?
- **Answer:** Fourteen years TIS and 21 months TIG; PECD 31 July; required skill level in primary AFSC is 9-level.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9B; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-031
- **Prompt:** Who must sign a Promotion Statement of Understanding after selection, and within what timeline?
- **Answer:** Selects to MSgt and SMSgt with more than 18 years TAFMS by effective date of promotion, and all CMSgt-selects regardless of TAFMS, must sign within 10 duty days after selections are confirmed.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9B; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-032
- **Prompt:** What retainability and ADSC do MSgt-/SMSgt-selects and CMSgt-selects acknowledge on the Promotion Statement of Understanding?
- **Answer:** MSgt- and SMSgt-selects must obtain two years of service retainability and incur a two-year ADSC from the effective date of promotion. CMSgt-selects must obtain three years of service retainability and incur a three-year ADSC from the effective date of promotion.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9B; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-033
- **Prompt:** What happens if a select fails to withdraw an existing retirement application within 10 duty days from presentation of the Promotion Statement of Understanding?
- **Answer:** Failure to withdraw an existing retirement application (approved or pending) within 10 duty days from presentation of the Promotion Statement of Understanding will result in removal from the selection list.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9B; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-034
- **Prompt:** On what factors does AFPC assign promotion sequence numbers for SSgt through CMSgt selectees?
- **Answer:** Date of rank, TAFMS, and date of birth.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9B; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-035
- **Prompt:** What promotion sequence number suffixes may supplemental selectees receive?
- **Answer:** .9 (increment previously announced) or .5 (unannounced future increment).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9B; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-036
- **Prompt:** How may Airmen decline a promotion, and until when?
- **Answer:** In writing by submitting a letter to the MPF any time prior to the promotion effective date. The letter must include name, social security number, promotion cycle, promotion sequence number if already selected, and a statement of understanding that reinstatement will not be authorized.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9B; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-037
- **Prompt:** What does the MPF do with a promotion declination letter?
- **Answer:** Ensures MilPDS is updated and forwards a copy of the declination letter to AFPC to be filed in the member’s ARMS.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9B; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-038
- **Prompt:** What are examples of reasons an Airman may be ineligible for promotion, and what are the effects of ineligibility?
- **Answer:** Examples include approved retirement, declination for extension or reenlistment, court-martial conviction, control roster action, no commander recommendation, failure to appear for scheduled testing without a valid reason, and AWOL. Ineligible individuals cannot test, cannot be considered if already tested, and a projected promotion, if already selected, will be canceled.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9B; B—Understanding

## Section 9C — Preparation and Responsibilities

### C09-FC-039
- **Prompt:** Whose responsibility is preparing for promotion testing according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** An individual responsibility. The importance of individual responsibility cannot be overemphasized.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9C; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-040
- **Prompt:** What must all promotion-eligible individuals know and do regarding testing preparation?
- **Answer:** Know their eligibility status; maintain specialty and military qualifications; initiate a self-study plan; obtain all applicable study references as outlined in the EPRRC; ensure their selection folder is accurate if it will be reviewed by the central evaluation board; and be prepared to test on the beginning of the testing window.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9C; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-041
- **Prompt:** What must members do if they will be unavailable during the entire testing cycle?
- **Answer:** Be prepared to test prior to their departure, even if that is before the first day of the testing cycle. Airmen may opt to test early provided the correct test booklets are available.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9C; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-042
- **Prompt:** What kind of study program must Airmen use under WAPS, and what is prohibited regarding group study?
- **Answer:** A self-initiated program of individual study and effort. Group study (two or more people) for the purposes of enlisted promotion testing is strictly prohibited.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9C; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-043
- **Prompt:** Why does AFH 1 prohibit group study for enlisted promotion testing?
- **Answer:** To protect the integrity of the promotion testing program by ensuring promotion test scores are a reflection of each member’s individual knowledge.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9C; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-044
- **Prompt:** What UCMJ exposure does AFH 1 cite for enlisted personnel who violate enlisted promotion test-compromise prohibitions?
- **Answer:** Subject to prosecution under Article 92, UCMJ, for violating a lawful general regulation. Conviction can result in a dishonorable discharge, forfeiture of all pay and allowances, and confinement for up to two years. Information is contained in DAFMAN 36-2664.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9C; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-045
- **Prompt:** What training does AFH 1 say does not constitute prohibited group study for promotion tests?
- **Answer:** Training designed to improve general military knowledge (if the intent is not to study for promotion tests), and training to improve general study habits or test-taking skills (if the training does not focus on preparing for promotion tests).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9C; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-046
- **Prompt:** With whom may an Airman discuss promotion test content, and how are written inquiries or complaints about a test handled?
- **Answer:** Discussing promotion test content with anyone other than the test control officer or test examiner is prohibited. Written inquiries or complaints about a test are handled by the test control officer.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9C; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-047
- **Prompt:** What sharing of study or recalled test materials does AFH 1 prohibit?
- **Answer:** Sharing pretests or lists of test questions recalled from a current or previous promotion test; personal study materials; underlined or highlighted study reference material; and commercial study guides with other individuals.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9C; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-048
- **Prompt:** What commercial study-guide and government-computer restrictions apply to promotion testing preparation?
- **Answer:** Placing commercial study guide software on government computers is prohibited. While Airmen may use commercial study materials, the USAF does not recommend, endorse, or support commercial study guides. Creating, storing, or transferring personal study notes on government computers is prohibited; government computers may only be used to view electronic versions of official study references.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9C; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-049
- **Prompt:** Who provides access to WAPS Career Development Courses, and where are study guides available?
- **Answer:** The Barnes Center for Enlisted Education, Air Force Career Development Academy, provides access to WAPS CDCs online at the USAF Enlisted Promotion Study Guides website (https://www.studyguides.af.mil/). The site is updated to coincide with release of the EPRRC.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9C; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-050
- **Prompt:** Where are instructions, manuals, or technical orders used as study materials made available?
- **Answer:** Online at the Air Force Departmental Publishing Office website (https://www.e-publishing.af.mil/) or other approved repositories. Individuals may ask unit WAPS monitors to order study reference material listed in the catalog that is not locally available.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9C; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-051
- **Prompt:** How many days before the scheduled test date must promotion-eligible Airmen have access to reference materials, and what may they request if not?
- **Answer:** At least 60 days before the scheduled test date (per DAFMAN 36-2664). If not, the Airman may request a delay in testing.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9C; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-052
- **Prompt:** What two roles does the promotion eligibility cutoff date (PECD) serve?
- **Answer:** It determines Airman promotion eligibility and is the cutoff date for when contents of the selection folder and information on promotion evaluation briefs must be updated and complete. It is the date promotion criteria are considered for promotion, such as data on the Data Verification Brief used for board evaluation consideration.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9C; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-053
- **Prompt:** How does CAFSC on the PECD affect competition for SMSgt and CMSgt?
- **Answer:** Individuals with a reporting identifier or special duty identifier as CAFSC on the PECD compete within that identifier. Otherwise, MSgts competing for SMSgt compete in the superintendent level of the control AFSC held as of the PECD; SMSgts competing for CMSgt compete in the chief enlisted manager code of the control AFSC held as of the PECD.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9C; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-054
- **Prompt:** What is the Data Verification Record (DVR), and why must eligible Airmen review it?
- **Answer:** The DVR is the document utilized for promotion consideration and the most important tool to review to ensure information on a promotion record is complete. All eligible Airmen must review their DVR in ARMS/PRDA; if an error or omission is noted, they must immediately contact their MPF for assistance.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9C; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-055
- **Prompt:** What should Airmen do after the MPF updates incorrect DVR data?
- **Answer:** Review the updated record to verify changes have been completed accurately. The MPF updates MilPDS with the correct data and updates the DVR on virtual MPF.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9C; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-056
- **Prompt:** What should data on the DVR not be confused with, and what do the PRDA Selection Folder and Board categories represent?
- **Answer:** Data on the DVR should not be confused with the Data Verification Brief or a Single Unit Retrieval Format (SURF). In PRDA, the Selection Folder category is the “As Is” record; the Board category is the “As Met” record. Access path: USAF Portal → PRDA → My Sections → PRDA.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9C; B—Understanding

## Section 9D — Promotion Testing

### C09-FC-057
- **Prompt:** Who produces all USAF enlisted promotion tests, and how are they characterized?
- **Answer:** Air Education and Training Command, Studies and Analysis Squadron, Airman Advancement Section, Joint Base San Antonio-Randolph, Texas. Tests are written by Airmen for Airmen.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9D; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-058
- **Prompt:** How do SNCO test-development teams begin a project, and what expertise do specialists provide?
- **Answer:** At the beginning of a project, the most current tests are administered to the test development teams to give writers the test-taker point of view and help evaluate how content relates to specialty performance. Teams carefully check references for currency and accuracy before developing new questions. Test development specialists provide psychometric and developmental expertise to ensure tests are credible, valid, and fair.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9D; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-059
- **Prompt:** What does the Promotion Fitness Examination measure, and who develops and validates PFE questions?
- **Answer:** The PFE measures military and supervisory knowledge required of Staff Sergeants and Technical Sergeants. Test development specialists select Chief Master Sergeants based on extensive experience and major command representation to develop and validate questions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9D; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-060
- **Prompt:** What do Specialty Knowledge Tests measure, and what resources support SKT development?
- **Answer:** SKTs measure important job knowledge required in a particular specialty. Specialists work with USAF career field managers; SNCOs develop and validate SKT questions using Career Field Education and Training Plans, occupational analysis data, and experiences to ensure content relates to important specialty tasks.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9D; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-061
- **Prompt:** When is the Enlisted Promotions References and Requirements Catalog (EPRRC) published, and what does it list?
- **Answer:** Published annually on 1 October. It lists all enlisted promotion tests authorized for administration and the study references associated with those tests.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9D; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-062
- **Prompt:** Where does every promotion-test question come from according to AFH 1, and which publications appear in the EPRRC?
- **Answer:** Every question on a promotion test comes from one of the publications listed in the EPRRC, and only publications used to support questions on a given promotion test are listed.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9D; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-063
- **Prompt:** What are the study references for the PFE and for SKTs?
- **Answer:** PFE study references are the grade-specific Enlisted Promotion Study Guides derived from AFH 1. SKT study references are often a combination of Career Development Courses and technical references. CDCs used as study references may be different from those issued for upgrade training.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9D; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-064
- **Prompt:** Where is the EPRRC available, and what else does the catalog contain?
- **Answer:** At the USAF Enlisted Promotion Study Guides website (https://www.studyguides.af.mil/). It also contains administrative and special instructions for test control officers.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9D; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-065
- **Prompt:** How are promotion tests administered to Airmen competing for SSgt through TSgt, and why are procedures standardized?
- **Answer:** Administered electronically. Procedures are standardized to ensure fairness for all members competing for promotion.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9D; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-066
- **Prompt:** Where are all promotion tests electronically scored, and when may paper copies be used?
- **Answer:** Electronically scored at HQ AETC SAS after thorough quality-control steps. Paper administration is the exception, must be approved by AFPC, and uses strict handling, storing, and transmitting procedures; scanning/scoring safeguards for those tests are conducted at AFPC.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9D; B—Understanding

## Section 9E — Evaluation Boards

### C09-FC-073
- **Prompt:** Why does AFH 1 say the promotion evaluation board is very important?
- **Answer:** Because it accounts for over half the total promotion score points.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9E; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-074
- **Prompt:** What determines the career-field backgrounds of board members?
- **Answer:** The number of promotion-eligible personnel, identified by major commands and AFSCs.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9E; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-075
- **Prompt:** How are board members organized into panels, and who is the board president?
- **Answer:** Panels each consist of one Colonel and two Chief Master Sergeants. The board president is always a General Officer.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9E; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-076
- **Prompt:** What do board members do before evaluating “live” records?
- **Answer:** They are briefed and sworn to complete the board’s task impartially and participate in an extensive trial-run process to ensure scoring consistency before evaluating any “live” records.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9E; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-077
- **Prompt:** What areas may the evaluation board look at regarding an Airman’s career?
- **Answer:** Performance, education, breadth of experience, job responsibility, professional competence, specific achievements, and leadership.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9E; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-078
- **Prompt:** Why do board scores vary from year to year and between panels, and what remains consistent within a CEM Code or AFSC?
- **Answer:** Factors include new panel members with different thought processes, changed or improved records, and a pool of new eligibles—so scores vary (often significantly) from one board to the next and between panels. All records within a CEM Code or AFSC are evaluated under the same standard. The important aspect of a final board score is how one eligible compares to peers in the final order of merit.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9E; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-079
- **Prompt:** Do board members have access to weighted scores, and do they know who was selected when they disband?
- **Answer:** No. Board members do not have access to the weighted scores of individuals competing for promotion. When board members disband, they do not know who was selected.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9E; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-080
- **Prompt:** What is the trial-run process before actual board scoring?
- **Answer:** Board members are given two selected sets of records to score as a practice exercise using secret ballots before actual scoring begins. The trial run helps establish a scoring standard applied consistently across the board. Each board arrives at its own scoring standard; everyone competing in a CEM Code or AFSC is looked at under the same standard.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9E; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-081
- **Prompt:** Which panel evaluates all eligibles in a CEM Code or AFSC, and what scale do members use?
- **Answer:** The same panel evaluates all eligibles competing in a CEM Code or AFSC. Each panel member scores each record using a 6- to 10-point scale with half-point increments.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9E; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-082
- **Prompt:** What are the minimum and maximum panel composite scores, and how is the total board score calculated?
- **Answer:** Panel composite score (three members) ranges from a minimum of 18 (6-6-6) to a maximum of 30 (10-10-10). The composite score is later multiplied by a factor of 15, resulting in a total board score of 270 to 450.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9E; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-083
- **Prompt:** How do panel members score records to ensure independence?
- **Answer:** Using a secret ballot, panel members score the record individually with no discussion. Records are given to each panel member; after scoring, ballots go directly to a recorder so each member has scored independently.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9E; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-084
- **Prompt:** What is a split vote, and how is it resolved?
- **Answer:** A record scored with a difference of more than one point between any panel members (for example, 8.5, 8.0, and 7.0) is a split vote and is returned to the panel for resolution. Panel members may discuss the record openly; only those directly involved in the split may change their scores. If they cannot agree, the record goes to the board president for resolution.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9E; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-085
- **Prompt:** Why does split-vote resolution matter for fairness?
- **Answer:** It ensures consistency of scoring and eliminates the possibility that one panel member will have a major impact (positive or negative) on an individual’s board score.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9E; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-086
- **Prompt:** What happens in post-board processing after the board finishes?
- **Answer:** Weighted factor scores are combined with board scores in a completely electronic operation that builds an order of merit listing by total score within each CEM Code or AFSC; the overall promotion quota is then applied to each list. After selection results are approved, data is transmitted to the military personnel section.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9E; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-087
- **Prompt:** What is the purpose of promotion score notices, and who may receive or use them?
- **Answer:** They give Airmen a report of their relative standing in the promotion consideration process and should never be provided to or used by anyone other than the individual and his or her commander. An Airman’s scores cannot be disclosed without the Airman’s written consent.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9E; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-088
- **Prompt:** Who is not authorized access to promotion scores, and what may the commander use scores for?
- **Answer:** Commander’s support staffs, first sergeants, supervisors, etc., are not authorized access to promotion scores. The commander has the specific duty to notify Airmen of selection or non-selection and may review score notices to determine status, but must restrict use to notification and advisory counseling on behalf of the Airmen and must not allow further dissemination.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9E; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-089
- **Prompt:** Where may individuals retrieve their score notice and compare scores with promotion statistics?
- **Answer:** Individuals may retrieve a copy of their score notice on virtual MPF after initial promotion selection for the current cycle, and can compare scores with promotion statistics available in the MPF or posted on virtual MPF through AFPC (https://www.afpc.af.mil/Promotion/Enlisted-Promotions/).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶9.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9E; B—Understanding

### C09-FC-090
- **Prompt:** What insight can understanding board member selection, process, and considered areas provide?
- **Answer:** Valuable insight into what is required to get promoted—because the board accounts for over half the total promotion score points and evaluates performance, education, breadth of experience, job responsibility, professional competence, specific achievements, and leadership under a common CEM Code/AFSC standard.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶¶9.19–9.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 9E; B—Understanding
