## Section 16B — Cognitive Processes

### C16-FC-016
- **Prompt:** What are mental frames, and when is their effect particularly noticeable?
- **Answer:** Frameworks/shortcuts of assumptions about how things are related and work that help process information quickly. Particularly noticeable when framing a challenge as a risk or an opportunity.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-017
- **Prompt:** What caution does AFH 1 give leaders about mental frames?
- **Answer:** Leaders of any organization or decision-making team must be careful about imposing mental frames on themselves and their teams where critical thinking is needed.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-018
- **Prompt:** What is prospect theory as AFH 1 presents it?
- **Answer:** Framing a situation as a potential gain causes decision-makers to act differently than when framing the same situation as a potential loss; it helps explain escalating commitment based on sunk costs instead of rational evaluation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-019
- **Prompt:** Under sunk-cost arguments, what risk behavior do leaders often show?
- **Answer:** They take on more risk, committing additional resources to avoid losses even when chances of success are low.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-020
- **Prompt:** How do organizations often react to change framed as threat versus opportunity?
- **Answer:** Threats to comfortable frameworks meet rigid resistance; changes seen as opportunities meet flexibility and adaptability.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-021
- **Prompt:** What should Airmen consciously avoid regarding change, and what intentional framing may help?
- **Answer:** Avoid the inherent tendency to view change as threatening; intentionally frame change as an opportunity so others can exercise habits of mind for well-informed decisions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-022
- **Prompt:** What is a key danger of reasoning by analogy without deliberate consideration?
- **Answer:** Focusing on similarities between events and downplaying important differences.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-023
- **Prompt:** When allowing analogy in decision-making, what separation should be made?
- **Answer:** Clearly separate fact from assumption; questioning assumptions is at the heart of habits of mind for good critical thought.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-024
- **Prompt:** What is a strength of using intuition in decision-making?
- **Answer:** It is based on matching patterns from previous experiences to cues picked up in the current environment.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-025
- **Prompt:** How can intuition and formal analysis work together?
- **Answer:** Formal analysis can check intuition, while intuition is useful in validating and testing assumptions that underlie analysis.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-026
- **Prompt:** What four process questions should Airmen consider when using groups for decision-making?
- **Answer:** Who should be involved? In what sort of environment should the decision take place? How will participants communicate? How will the leader control the decision process?
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-027
- **Prompt:** What conditions support the “wisdom of groups” as AFH 1 describes them?
- **Answer:** Diverse disciplines/expertise; decentralized discussion location; effective aggregation of individual judgments; independent members not subordinate to one another.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-028
- **Prompt:** Name hindrances to groups that should not be tolerated.
- **Answer:** Withholding information for personal reasons, filtering information, and selectively presenting information up the chain to affect the group’s efforts.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-029
- **Prompt:** What balance of participation characterizes the most successful decision-making groups?
- **Answer:** A good balance of individuals who do not dominate discussions but also do not constantly remain in the background.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-030
- **Prompt:** What is groupthink?
- **Answer:** A decision trap in which tremendous pressures demand conformity and a strong desire for unanimity at the expense of true critical thinking.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-031
- **Prompt:** List common symptoms of groupthink.
- **Answer:** Feeling invulnerable/egocentric; rationalizing away disconfirming data and warning signs; feeling of unanimity; pressuring dissenters; members regressing in desire/ability to contribute.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-032
- **Prompt:** Name two ways AFH 1 says Airmen can reduce groupthink.
- **Answer:** Define roles by giving responsibility for aspects of analysis and holding members accountable for those perspectives; invite healthy disagreement during analysis to encourage candid dialogue.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-033
- **Prompt:** Why should you speak up if concerned the group will make a bad decision?
- **Answer:** Groups tend to make better decisions when exposed to an opposing view because members become more likely to see the situation from different angles.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-034
- **Prompt:** What does AFH 1 say about majority first-ballot jury positions and final decisions?
- **Answer:** About 85% of the time the majority position on a jury’s first ballot ends up being the group’s final decision.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-035
- **Prompt:** How can even a single opposing view improve multi-alternative decisions?
- **Answer:** It can foster divergent thinking—generation of a broader range of possible alternatives—even if the group does not adopt that specific position.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-036
- **Prompt:** According to DuBrin’s point in AFH 1, what does a high-performance team demand?
- **Answer:** Sincere and tactful criticism among members.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-037
- **Prompt:** What must be the collective purpose of productive feedback or criticism?
- **Answer:** All parties share the same expected outcome—to improve a process or procedure that positively contributes to the mission.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-038
- **Prompt:** What distinction does AFH 1 draw about honesty in criticism?
- **Answer:** Honesty is important, but brutal honesty can be offensive; constructive criticism focuses on fixing/improving a problem, not personal or oppositional gain.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-039
- **Prompt:** Distinguish cognitive conflict from affective conflict.
- **Answer:** Cognitive conflict is debate focused on issues and ideas (constructive). Affective conflict is emotional and personal outbursts (not constructive).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-040
- **Prompt:** What is the Culture of No?
- **Answer:** A decision culture where lone dissenters issue non-concurs that block organizational goals for sub-organizational interests; dissenters tear down/block proposals rather than strengthen them.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-041
- **Prompt:** What is the Culture of Yes, and what must Airmen understand about silence?
- **Answer:** Dissenters stay silent, creating tacit endorsement without analysis/debate; later they may distance themselves or undermine implementation. Silence does not mean assent—watch for non-contributors.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-042
- **Prompt:** What is the Culture of Maybe / analysis paralysis?
- **Answer:** Decision-makers gather as much information as possible and constantly delay action hoping more information will clarify the choice; balance more information against diminishing returns; intuitive judgment can cut off unnecessary delay.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-043
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define consensus in procedural justice?
- **Answer:** Consensus does not mean unanimity; it is a commitment to, and shared understanding of, the desired outcome.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-044
- **Prompt:** Why does procedural fairness matter even when people agree with the chosen course of action?
- **Answer:** If they do not see the process as legitimate, they are often disenchanted with the outcome; fair processes aid implementation because perspectives feel considered.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-045
- **Prompt:** What adage does AFH 1 use about consensus, and what do great leaders do?
- **Answer:** “A camel is a horse built by consensus” is not so farfetched; great leaders do not seek consensus—they build it.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-046
- **Prompt:** List actions that create procedural legitimacy culture.
- **Answer:** Provide a process roadmap; reinforce/demonstrate open mindset; active listening; separate advocacy from analysis; explain rationale; appreciate participation; show how alternative inputs contributed.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-047
- **Prompt:** What assumption underlies normal accident theory?
- **Answer:** In any highly complex high-risk organizational structure, decision failures are unavoidable; interactively complex and tightly coupled systems are especially vulnerable to catastrophic failure from small unrecognized mistakes.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-048
- **Prompt:** What is normalized deviance theory?
- **Answer:** Gradual acceptance of unexpected events and risks as normal behavior, including lower standards; shortcuts become normalized until deviance is no longer noticed.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-049
- **Prompt:** What classic case does AFH 1 use for normalized deviance?
- **Answer:** The Challenger space shuttle disaster—O-ring erosion outside tolerances occurred several times without catastrophe and became accepted as normal despite deviation from engineering standards.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-050
- **Prompt:** What is practical drift, and how can leaders minimize its problems?
- **Answer:** Localized efficiency procedures become accepted practice and organizational norms drift. Minimize by transparency, fixing root causes (not Band-Aids), monitoring handoff seams, and careful process-focused after-action reviews; also empower front-line workers and flatten hierarchies.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16B; B—Understanding

## Section 16C — Informed Decision-Making

### C16-FC-051
- **Prompt:** Why is the process of decision-making as important as the information analyzed?
- **Answer:** Habitual critical thinking in gathering/analyzing information helps reduce the unconscious tendency to accept an available option as satisfactory without exploring all feasible options.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16C; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-052
- **Prompt:** What does CRAAP stand for in information literacy?
- **Answer:** Currency, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, and Purpose.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16C; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-053
- **Prompt:** What does Currency ask you to evaluate?
- **Answer:** Timeliness—when published or last updated; whether newer articles exist; whether the topic requires recent information.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16C; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-054
- **Prompt:** What does Relevance ask you to evaluate?
- **Answer:** Importance for your needs—whether it answers your question, who the audience is, and whether the level is appropriate (not too simple or overly technical).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16C; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-055
- **Prompt:** What does Authority ask you to evaluate?
- **Answer:** Source of the information—publisher/sponsor, author credentials or affiliations, and whether the author is qualified to write on the topic.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16C; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-056
- **Prompt:** What does Accuracy ask you to evaluate?
- **Answer:** Reliability, truthfulness, and correctness—evidence support, editorial/expert review, and verifiability in other sources.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16C; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-057
- **Prompt:** What does Purpose ask you to evaluate?
- **Answer:** Why the information exists—inform/teach versus sell/entertain/persuade; objectivity; political, ideological, cultural, religious, institutional, or personal biases.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16C; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-058
- **Prompt:** Why are newspaper articles or scientific papers normally more reliable than blogs, per AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Errors in those forums can seriously damage the author’s reputation, while blog/message-board writers may face little consequence for wrong facts.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16C; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-059
- **Prompt:** What is the small-sample-size principle in analytical thinking?
- **Answer:** Judgments based on a large number of observations are more likely accurate than judgments based on a small number of observations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16C; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-060
- **Prompt:** What does “correlation doesn’t imply causality” require decision-makers to do?
- **Answer:** Even when two factors are related, do not immediately assume one causes the other; make fair comparisons and apply the idea of a control group—ask whether groups are otherwise mostly the same.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16C; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-061
- **Prompt:** Define Divergent Thinking and Convergent Thinking.
- **Answer:** Divergent Thinking: ability to generate multiple varied solutions (brainstorming). Convergent Thinking: process seeking the correct or best possible solution from identified possibilities.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.18
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16C; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-062
- **Prompt:** What is the principle of deferred judgment in the Osborn-Parnes model?
- **Answer:** Allow a period for ideas to flow freely (divergent) without internal or external evaluation; only after identifying many possible ideas should you evaluate them (convergent).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.18
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16C; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-063
- **Prompt:** List the six Osborn-Parnes stages in order.
- **Answer:** Mess Finding; Data Finding; Problem Finding; Idea Finding; Solution Finding; Acceptance Finding.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.18
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16C; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-064
- **Prompt:** How should Mess Finding summarize the problem?
- **Answer:** As a “How to…” statement (e.g., “How to reduce the number of Airmen calling in sick”).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.18
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16C; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-065
- **Prompt:** What five W questions support Data Finding?
- **Answer:** Who is involved? What is involved / examples? When does/will it happen? Where does/will it happen? Why does it happen?
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.18
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16C; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-066
- **Prompt:** What prompt does Problem Finding use, and what should you select afterward?
- **Answer:** “In What Ways Might We…” (IWWMW); after reflection, select the problem statement(s) capturing the most important or underlying problem.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.18
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16C; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-067
- **Prompt:** What numeric idea goals and narrowing targets does AFH 1 suggest for Idea Finding?
- **Answer:** Set a numeric goal such as 50–75 ideas before reviewing; only later narrow (e.g., to 6–8 ideas with greatest potential). Anonymity in groups can help.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.18
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16C; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-068
- **Prompt:** How does Solution Finding use Table 16.1’s decision matrix?
- **Answer:** Create and weight criteria; rate each idea on each criterion; multiply ratings by weights; prefer ideas evaluated most favorably on the highest-weighted criteria.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.18; Table 16.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16C; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-069
- **Prompt:** What is Acceptance Finding?
- **Answer:** Attempt to gain acceptance for the solution, develop a plan of action, and implement the solution (follow through).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.18
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16C; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-070
- **Prompt:** What is the Vroom-Yetton-Jago model used for?
- **Answer:** Deciding how to decide—five primary levels of decision-making with different involvement/complexity.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16C; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-071
- **Prompt:** Describe Level I: Decide and Announce—pros and key cons.
- **Answer:** Leader makes and announces the decision (quick control); should explain unilateral rationale. Cons: may miss necessary information; may alienate members.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16C; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-072
- **Prompt:** Describe Level II: Gather Input from Individuals and Decide.
- **Answer:** Leaders gather input from selected individuals then decide—expert input without a full-group meeting; others may feel excluded or see favoritism.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16C; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-073
- **Prompt:** Describe Level III: Gather Input from the Group and Decide—key risk if the leader differs.
- **Answer:** Group input then leader decides (synergy). If the leader chooses differently, the group may feel unvalued or that the process was a façade, undermining implementation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16C; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-074
- **Prompt:** Describe Level IV: Facilitate Consensus.
- **Answer:** Leaders present issues and facilitate the group’s decision process; if no consensus, the leader may decide. Enhances buy-in/ownership/trust; takes more time and may yield adequate-not-optimal compromise.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16C; B—Understanding

### C16-FC-075
- **Prompt:** Describe Level V: Delegate with Constraints.
- **Answer:** Leaders delegate the problem within specified boundaries and facilitate support/resources—builds team leadership and ownership; takes more time; may be non-optimal; skill gaps may cause disharmony.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶16.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 16C; B—Understanding
