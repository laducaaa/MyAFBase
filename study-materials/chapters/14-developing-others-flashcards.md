## Section 14A — Teamwork

### Core B—Understanding cards (SSgt and TSgt)

### C14-FC-001
- **Prompt:** What does AFH 1 mean by ‘dynamic’ in the team-building discussion?
- **Answer:** A way of describing elements of a process or system—constant change, activity, or progress—and also a force that stimulates change or progress.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14A; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-002
- **Prompt:** What foundation do healthy, high-performing teams most often have, and what is at the core?
- **Answer:** A foundation of trust, communication, and cooperation; trust is at the core of all healthy team interaction.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14A; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-003
- **Prompt:** What must team members share for effective teamwork trust, and what is the hallmark of a trust-based team?
- **Answer:** Mutual confidence in the integrity and ability of teammates, plus comfort to take risks, think outside the box, and share ideas without fear of being shut down. Freedom to communicate openly, honestly, and directly within the group is the hallmark.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14A; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-004
- **Prompt:** According to the Suzanne Zoglio quotation in AFH 1, what reduces trust fastest in a group?
- **Answer:** Members saying one thing within the group and something else outside the group. Trust is enhanced when members say what they need to say directly to the appropriate people and refrain from talking behind each other’s backs.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14A; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-005
- **Prompt:** How do leaders promote a trusting team atmosphere under AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Encourage open and honest communication; empower teams to solve problems innovatively through collaboration free of selfish goals; set the right tone for trusting relationships; know and establish good rapport; discourage cliques or divisions; set the example and lead by example.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14A; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-006
- **Prompt:** What happens when teams do not allow honest, open sharing, and how can leaders increase team success through communication?
- **Answer:** Teams quickly lose effectiveness; members may withhold vital information or disengage, causing confusion, frustration, and inability to complete tasks. Leaders increase success by giving complete access to necessary data, discouraging discounting of ideas and feelings, and encouraging active listening.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14A; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-007
- **Prompt:** Why is cooperation critical for teams, and what signs indicate a team is not performing cohesively?
- **Answer:** Cooperation combines skills/approaches for challenges, customer requirements, and mission changes; yields synergistic results and shared ownership. Unhelpful competition—such as ‘all-starring’ to outshine others—signals poor cohesion and may reflect a power struggle.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14A; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-008
- **Prompt:** How should leaders reduce power-play or ‘all-starring’ behavior on a team?
- **Answer:** Reemphasize each team member’s specific roles and responsibilities, which eliminates potential barriers to cooperation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14A; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-009
- **Prompt:** What are the four stages of team building most often experienced according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Forming, storming, norming, and performing.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14A; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-010
- **Prompt:** What characterizes the forming stage of team building?
- **Answer:** Initial period of uncertainty in which individuals try to determine their place and establish or accept procedures and rules; members cautiously explore boundaries of acceptable behavior; transition from individual to member status; leader’s guidance may be tested formally and informally.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14A; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-011
- **Prompt:** What characterizes the storming stage, and why is it often most difficult?
- **Answer:** Conflicts arise as members resist team influence and rebel against the task; some realize the task is different/more difficult than expected; impatience with lack of progress; arguing about actions; reliance on personal experience avoiding collaboration. Despite tensions, members begin to understand one another.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14A; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-012
- **Prompt:** What happens during the norming stage of team building?
- **Answer:** Members establish cohesiveness and commitment, discover new ways of working together, and set norms for appropriate behavior; reconcile competing loyalties; accept the team, ground rules, roles, and others’ individuality; emotional conflict reduces as competitive relationships become cooperative; more focus on the team objective.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14A; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-013
- **Prompt:** What characterizes the performing stage of team building?
- **Answer:** The team develops proficiency in achieving goals and becomes more flexible in working patterns; settles relationships and expectations; diagnoses and solves problems and implements changes; accepts strengths/weaknesses and roles; becomes an effective, cohesive, productive unit.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14A; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-014
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 distinguish constructive conflict from destructive conflict?
- **Answer:** Constructive/positive conflict addresses problems for a solution, greater understanding, and enhanced communication when managed effectively. Destructive conflict creates barriers to cooperation and communication, degrades morale, and diverts attention from tasks.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14A; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-015
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define conflict, and what are the three source categories it develops?
- **Answer:** Extended struggle or disagreement that can frustrate an important concern (real or perceived). Sources: communication factors, structural factors, and personal behavior factors.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14A; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-016
- **Prompt:** Why does AFH 1 say communication often gets blamed for workplace problems, and what do personnel need?
- **Answer:** The real crux is more likely miscommunication—misinterpreted, inaccurate, or incomplete information (even with over-communication) leading to frustration and stress. Personnel need constructive, understandable, and accurate information.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14A; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-017
- **Prompt:** What structural factors can increase conflict according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Larger organizations (more people), scarce or high-demand resources, increased interaction making differences noticeable, line-staff distinctions where contributions may not be heard/valued/accepted, and rewards perceived as unfair, unjust, or biased.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14A; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-018
- **Prompt:** What personal behavior factors can cause conflict, and what alignment can reduce it?
- **Answer:** Individual differences such as goals/objectives, perceptions, values, and personalities. Aligning personal needs and values with the overall USAF mission increases willingness to change, set aside self-interests, listen to others, and reduce conflict.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14A; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-019
- **Prompt:** In Dr. Kenneth Thomas’s model as presented in AFH 1, what do cooperation and assertiveness mean for conflict management?
- **Answer:** Cooperation: how willing a person or group is to satisfy the other’s needs. Assertiveness: how active a person is in addressing the conflict. Styles are defined by levels of both.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14A; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-020
- **Prompt:** What are the five major conflict management styles and their assertiveness/cooperativeness levels?
- **Answer:** Competing/forcing (high assertiveness, low cooperativeness); collaborating (high/high); accommodating (low assertiveness, high cooperativeness); avoiding (low/low); compromising (some/some).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14A; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-021
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 describe collaborating versus competing as conflict styles?
- **Answer:** Collaborating seeks to satisfy both sides through honest discussion, may share resources so both are better off, requires trust/openness, and seeks a ‘win’ for both. Competing attempts to overwhelm an opponent with formal authority, threats, or power.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14A; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-022
- **Prompt:** What three considerations does AFH 1 say to weigh when choosing a conflict management style?
- **Answer:** The who, the stakes, and the situation—because all situations are unique depending on individuals involved, criticality of issues, and urgency.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14A; B—Understanding

## Section 14B — Develops People

### Core B—Understanding cards (SSgt and TSgt)

### C14-FC-023
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 describe leading in the USAF as an art and science?
- **Answer:** The art and science of accomplishing the USAF mission by motivating, influencing, and directing Airmen—highlighting the mission/objective/task and the Airmen who accomplish it. Science: methods and understanding of what leadership is. Art: demonstration and application of leading.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14B; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-024
- **Prompt:** What Merriam-Webster and military-leader definitions of lead/leader does AFH 1 cite?
- **Answer:** Lead: to guide on a way especially by going in advance, or to direct on a course or in a direction. Military leader: a person who directs a military force or unit, or one who has commanding authority or influence.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14B; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-025
- **Prompt:** What distinction does Warren G. Bennis draw between management and leadership in AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Management is getting people to do what needs to be done. Leadership is getting people to want to do what needs to be done.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14B; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-026
- **Prompt:** What three primary behavioral leader characteristics did Bennis label, and what needs do they help satisfy?
- **Answer:** Motivate, develop, and inspire. Leaders help satisfy belonging (belongingness and love), recognition/self-esteem (esteem), control over their lives (safety and security), leading toward achievement (self-actualization).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14B; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-027
- **Prompt:** What is Maslow’s Hierarchy of Needs key rule, and what workplace example does AFH 1 give?
- **Answer:** To attempt success at one level, you must be successful in the previous. Example: hunger/thirst (physiological) blocks focusing on relationships (belongingness). At work, concern about job security blocks striving for achievement and reaching full potential (self-actualization).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14B; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-028
- **Prompt:** What three primary behavioral characteristics did Bennis label for managers?
- **Answer:** Administrators, maintainers, and controllers—focusing on tasks and a level of control to guide people; ensuring needed resources are available and effectively used.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14B; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-029
- **Prompt:** How do managers and leaders support mission success differently under AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Managers organize projects, staff positions, communicate plans, delegate, and monitor implementation; bring order/consistency to readiness, availability, and sustainment. Leaders launch/steer toward goals and strategies and align personnel needs, wants, emotions, and aspirations with the task; lead people to accomplish the mission.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14B; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-030
- **Prompt:** Why do organizations need both leadership and management according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** They go hand in hand in producing elements that promote mission success; organizations need a strong balance of both. Best managers tend to become good leaders through practicing good management; effective leaders are often good managers.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14B; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-031
- **Prompt:** What development opportunities can professional associations provide?
- **Answer:** Additional opportunities for leadership roles, public speaking, and mentoring, as well as broadening technical expertise.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14B; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-032
- **Prompt:** What unofficial unit organizations and rank-tailored organizations does AFH 1 name?
- **Answer:** Unofficial: squadron booster club and event planning committees. Rank-tailored: Junior Enlisted Airmen Council, 5/6 Council, Top III, and the Chiefs’ Group. Active roles are highly encouraged for personal and professional development.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14B; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-033
- **Prompt:** What terms does AFH 1 associate with leadership roles versus managerial roles?
- **Answer:** Leadership: flight leader, team leader, squad leader. Managerial: career field managers, major command functional managers, program managers, and project managers.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14B; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-034
- **Prompt:** What two central elements does USAF leading highlight according to AFH 1 ¶14.6?
- **Answer:** The mission, objective, or task to be accomplished; and the Airmen who will accomplish it.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14B; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-035
- **Prompt:** Is leadership only for those with a title or position of authority under AFH 1?
- **Answer:** No. Leadership is often indicated by title or position, but it is an ability we can all develop, cultivate, and expand upon.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14B; B—Understanding

## Section 14C — Service Mindset

### Core B—Understanding cards (SSgt and TSgt)

### C14-FC-036
- **Prompt:** What is mentoring according to AFH 1, and what obligation do USAF leaders have?
- **Answer:** A process of helping each individual reach his or her maximum potential. USAF leaders have an inherent obligation and responsibility to mentor future leaders, passing on experience, wisdom, philosophy, traditions, shared values, quality, and lessons learned.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14C; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-037
- **Prompt:** At a minimum, what does a supervisor’s mentoring consist of during performance feedback?
- **Answer:** A discussion of performance, potential, and professional development plans. Conversations should address promotions, PME, advanced degree work, physical fitness, personal goals and expectations, professional qualities, future assignments, and long-range plans.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14C; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-038
- **Prompt:** What areas does the Air Force mentoring program cover, and what must individuals focus on first?
- **Answer:** Career guidance, professional development, leadership, USAF history and heritage, airpower doctrine, strategic vision, and contributions to joint warfighting. Individuals must first focus on USAF institutional needs—developing people skilled in employment and support of airpower meeting national security needs.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14C; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-039
- **Prompt:** Who is the primary mentor, and what publication governs USAF mentoring?
- **Answer:** The immediate supervisor or rater is the primary mentor, coach, counselor, guide, or role model (Airmen may also seek other mentors). USAF mentoring is governed by DAFMAN 36-2643, Air Force Mentoring Program.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14C; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-040
- **Prompt:** What must mentors distinguish among regarding goals and expectations?
- **Answer:** Individual goals, career aspirations, and realistic expectations—because each individual defines a successful career, goal, or life accomplishment differently and there are numerous paths to meet goals.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14C; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-041
- **Prompt:** What does the MENTORING model’s letter M (Model) emphasize?
- **Answer:** An effective mentor serving as a role model understands that actions speak louder than words; the protégé constantly observes and learns from how the mentor deals with a variety of situations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14C; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-042
- **Prompt:** What do Empathize and Nurture require of mentors in the MENTORING model?
- **Answer:** Empathize: genuine compassion; remember being new/inexperienced; cultivates bonds and mutual commitment. Nurture: caring attitude; invest time, patience, and effort; provide attention, training, feedback, and time to apply, internalize, and value learning.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14C; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-043
- **Prompt:** What five teaching steps does AFH 1 list under Teach in the MENTORING model?
- **Answer:** (1) Organize material into logical, systematic units of manageable size; (2) correct errors immediately; (3) frequently review previously covered material and relate it to the current lesson; (4) include practical exercises; (5) evaluate protégé progress and provide detailed feedback.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14C; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-044
- **Prompt:** What do Organize and Respond require in the MENTORING model?
- **Answer:** Organize: mentors must themselves be organized; know from the beginning what they want to achieve; use a logical sequential plan aimed at a precisely defined target. Respond: two-way communication; actively listen; useful timely responses; alert to nonverbal cues; anticipate needs/problems/concerns and address them immediately.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14C; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-045
- **Prompt:** What do Inspire, Network, and Goal-Set mean in the MENTORING model?
- **Answer:** Inspire: profound impact encouraging transformation; distinguishes leaders from managers. Network: introduce/connect protégé with others for guidance, support, resources, and opportunities. Goal-Set: help understand why goals matter; establish short- and long-term SMART goals (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Realistic, Time-bound); assist achievement.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14C; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-046
- **Prompt:** Why should leaders develop counseling abilities according to AFH 1 ¶14.12?
- **Answer:** Being involved in an Airman’s development and growth is essential to a leader’s influence and credibility; leaders should improve counseling abilities to ensure effective counseling is provided.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14C; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-047
- **Prompt:** What is the key to successful counseling timing under AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Conduct the counseling as close to the event as possible. Good leaders take advantage of naturally occurring events as opportunities for providing feedback.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14C; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-048
- **Prompt:** How do crisis counseling and referral counseling differ in AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Crisis counseling helps an Airman through the initial shock after receiving negative news. Referral counseling may follow crisis counseling, helps work through a personal situation, may serve as preventive counseling, and often involves agencies such as legal services, religious affairs, or an alcohol and drug counselor.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14C; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-049
- **Prompt:** When is professional growth counseling often conducted?
- **Answer:** While reviewing an Airman’s duty performance during a certain period and setting standards for the next period—typically, but not only, during performance feedback assessments. Leaders may also counsel for superior or substandard duty performance or behavior.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14C; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-050
- **Prompt:** What are the three counseling approaches, and what is the major difference among them?
- **Answer:** Nondirective, directive, and combined. The major difference is the degree to which the Airman participates and interacts during the counseling session.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14C; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-051
- **Prompt:** Why is the nondirective counseling approach preferred for most sessions?
- **Answer:** The leader listens before helping the individual make decisions or giving advice; encourages exploring/clarifying important points; refrains from providing solutions or rendering opinions; keeps focus on individual and organizational goals and ensures the Airman’s plan aligns with them.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14C; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-052
- **Prompt:** When does the directive counseling approach work best?
- **Answer:** To correct simple problems, make on-the-spot corrections, and correct specific aspects of duty performance—especially when time is short, the solution is clear, or an Airman has limited problem-solving skills and needs guidance. The leader directs a course of action.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14C; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-053
- **Prompt:** What is the combined counseling approach?
- **Answer:** A blend of directive and nondirective approaches adjusted to the situation. The leader emphasizes the Airman’s planning and decision-making by listening, offering options, helping analyze solutions, encouraging the Airman to decide which is best, and assisting with a plan of action.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14C; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-054
- **Prompt:** What advantages and disadvantages does AFH 1 list for nondirective counseling?
- **Answer:** Advantages: encourages maturity, open communication, and personal responsibility. Disadvantages: more time consuming; requires greatest counselor skills.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14C; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-055
- **Prompt:** What advantages and disadvantages does AFH 1 list for directive counseling?
- **Answer:** Advantages: quickest method; good for people needing clear, concise direction; allows counselors to use experience. Disadvantages: does not encourage Airmen to be part of the solution; may treat symptoms not problems; may discourage talking freely; counselor provides the solution.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14C; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-056
- **Prompt:** What four basic components should a counseling session follow, and what session-length guidance does AFH 1 give?
- **Answer:** State the purpose, discuss the issues, develop a plan of action, and record the plan. When possible, conduct during the duty day, aim for less than one hour, and be prepared to schedule a second session if necessary.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14C; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-057
- **Prompt:** What environmental and preparation expectations apply to counseling sessions?
- **Answer:** Establish intent and an appropriate environment with minimal interruptions and free from distractions. Both leader and Airman should understand why, where, and when; be prepared to discuss main points, pertinent information, and plausible obtainable goals; schedule future meetings tentatively before closing.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14C; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-058
- **Prompt:** What follow-on measures may be appropriate after counseling?
- **Answer:** A follow-up session, making referrals, informing the chain of command, and taking corrective measures.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14C; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-059
- **Prompt:** How do effective leaders coach Airmen according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** By thoroughly understanding strengths, weaknesses, and professional goals, then setting goals, developing and implementing plans of action, and providing oversight and motivation throughout the process—similar to athletic coaches improving teams.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14C; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-060
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define vision for USAF leaders?
- **Answer:** A collective vision that empowers, inspires, challenges, and motivates followers to the highest levels of commitment and a continuously improving environment. Vision is helping people believe they can accomplish goals in anticipation of a better future as a result of their efforts; inspiration is one way to convey vision.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14C; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-061
- **Prompt:** What example does AFH 1 use to illustrate inspirational vision?
- **Answer:** President John F. Kennedy’s 1961 announcement of the United States intention to put a man on the moon within the decade—an ambitious goal achieved with dramatic announcement and infectious inspiration.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14C; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-062
- **Prompt:** What must lower-level leaders do to implement vision, and how should vision evolve?
- **Answer:** Communicate the vision, bolster Airmen’s courage and understanding, and solicit ideas and suggestions. Vision-forming should be continual because re-vision may be required as technology/environment evolve, but vision should not be arbitrarily modified—if it works and aligns with developments, affirm and support it. Vision must be logical, deductive, plausible; specific enough for guidance yet unbounded enough to encourage initiative.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.18
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14C; B—Understanding

### TSgt C—Applying overlay cards (14C; no SSgt tag)

### C14-FC-063
- **Prompt:** A TSgt supervisor’s Airman asks only for promotion tips during feedback and wants to skip performance and development planning. Applying AFH 1 mentoring minimums, what must the mentoring discussion still include?
- **Answer:** At minimum, discuss performance, potential, and professional development plans during performance feedback. Conversations should also address promotions, PME, advanced degree work, physical fitness, personal goals/expectations, professional qualities, future assignments, and long-range plans—not promotions alone.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.9
- **Tags:** TSgt; 14C; C—Applying

### C14-FC-064
- **Prompt:** Your protégé sets vague career hopes with no timeline. Applying the MENTORING Goal-Set element, what do you help establish?
- **Answer:** Help the protégé understand why goals are important and establish short- and long-term goals that are Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Realistic, and Time-bound, then remain available to assist in achieving them.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.11
- **Tags:** TSgt; 14C; C—Applying

### C14-FC-065
- **Prompt:** An Airman is struggling after receiving negative personal news and seems in initial shock. Applying AFH 1 counseling types, what counseling is appropriate first, and what may follow?
- **Answer:** Conduct crisis counseling to help through the initial shock. Referral counseling may follow to help work through the personal situation and may involve agencies such as legal services, religious affairs, or an alcohol and drug counselor.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.13
- **Tags:** TSgt; 14C; C—Applying

### C14-FC-066
- **Prompt:** You need an on-the-spot correction for a simple, clear duty-performance error with little time. Applying AFH 1, which counseling approach fits best and why?
- **Answer:** Directive counseling: the leader directs a course of action. It works best for simple problems, on-the-spot corrections, and specific duty-performance aspects when time is short, the solution is clear, or problem-solving skill is limited.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.14
- **Tags:** TSgt; 14C; C—Applying

### C14-FC-067
- **Prompt:** An Airman needs to own a complex developmental decision and you want to offer options without deciding for them. Applying AFH 1, which counseling approach should you use?
- **Answer:** Combined counseling: blend directive and nondirective methods; listen, offer options, help analyze solutions, encourage the Airman to decide which is best, and assist with a plan of action—emphasizing the Airman’s planning and decision-making responsibilities.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.14
- **Tags:** TSgt; 14C; C—Applying

### C14-FC-068
- **Prompt:** You are preparing a developmental counseling session but have not built a long formal script. Applying AFH 1’s four basic components, what sequence do you follow?
- **Answer:** State the purpose, discuss the issues, develop a plan of action, and record the plan—as simple or elaborate as the situation requires—then schedule future meetings at least tentatively before closing.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.15
- **Tags:** TSgt; 14C; C—Applying

### C14-FC-069
- **Prompt:** A peer suggests counseling in a noisy shared workspace during a rushed break. Applying AFH 1 counseling-process guidance, what environment and timing standards do you apply?
- **Answer:** Ensure intent is established and the environment is appropriate—minimal interruptions and free from distractions. When possible, counsel during the duty day, aim for less than one hour, and prepare to schedule a second session if needed so both parties understand why, where, and when.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.15
- **Tags:** TSgt; 14C; C—Applying

### C14-FC-070
- **Prompt:** Your team member has clear strengths and gaps but no written development path. Applying AFH 1 coaching, what three coaching actions do you take?
- **Answer:** Set goals, develop and implement plans of action, and provide oversight and motivation throughout the process, based on a thorough understanding of the member’s strengths, weaknesses, and professional goals.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.16
- **Tags:** TSgt; 14C; C—Applying

### C14-FC-071
- **Prompt:** Your section’s improvement goals exist only in your head and Airmen say they do not know what success looks like. Applying AFH 1 vision implementation, what must you do as a lower-level leader?
- **Answer:** Communicate the vision, bolster Airmen’s courage and understanding, and solicit ideas and suggestions. Keep the vision logical, deductive, and plausible—specific enough for guidance but unbounded enough to encourage initiative—and do not arbitrarily modify a working, aligned vision.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶¶14.17–14.18
- **Tags:** TSgt; 14C; C—Applying

### C14-FC-072
- **Prompt:** A mentor candidate focuses only on the Airman’s personal wish list and ignores institutional airpower needs. Applying AFH 1 mentoring-program priorities, how do you correct the approach?
- **Answer:** Individuals must first focus on USAF institutional needs—developing people skilled in employment and support of airpower meeting national security needs—while still distinguishing individual goals, career aspirations, and realistic expectations under DAFMAN 36-2643 mentoring governance.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.10
- **Tags:** TSgt; 14C; C—Applying

## Section 14D — Leadership

### Core B—Understanding cards (SSgt and TSgt)

### C14-FC-073
- **Prompt:** What does AFH 1 say about self-awareness before counseling Airmen?
- **Answer:** Leaders must be fully aware of their own values and needs before counseling Airmen. Self-aware leaders are more likely to act consistently with their values and actions and less likely to project onto Airmen.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14D; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-074
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define empathy for leaders?
- **Answer:** Being understanding of and sensitive to another person’s feelings, thoughts, and experiences to the point that you can almost feel or experience them yourself; putting yourself in another’s shoes to see from their perspective and help develop a plan of action that works.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14D; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-075
- **Prompt:** What dual roles must effective leaders accept under leadership milestones?
- **Answer:** Being both a master student and a master teacher by embracing the role of both follower and leader, with high attainable standards for continual growth, guidance, and feedback.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14D; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-076
- **Prompt:** How do leaders value experience and foster growth according to AFH 1 ¶14.21?
- **Answer:** Valuing experience: insist Airmen focus on aspects they control and set challenging, enlightening experiences that build confidence. Fostering growth: identify knowledge/improvement opportunities so advancements are permanent and pervasive; formally and publicly recognize individual and unit successes.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14D; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-077
- **Prompt:** What do facing challenges and professional/personal development require of leaders?
- **Answer:** Facing challenges: consistent exposure with gradual responsibility increases; identify leadership potential early; recognize skills/talents/experiences. Professional development: diagnose needs (off-duty education, PME, skills training, seminars, communication). Personal development: may include relationships, interpersonal skills, off-duty education.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14D; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-078
- **Prompt:** How should leaders deal with setbacks and change under AFH 1 ¶14.21?
- **Answer:** Setbacks: encourage trying new things; provide experience/understanding; keep followers optimistic in adversity. Change: learn as much as possible about the change and its effects; deal with associated emotions; motivate support for implementation; maintain clear understanding of the present and clear focus on the future.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14D; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-079
- **Prompt:** Who provided the 1976 leadership advice AFH 1 summarizes in ¶14.22, and what is the first point?
- **Answer:** Pacific Air Forces Commander, General Louis L. Wilson, Jr. First point: Be Tough—set standards high, insist people measure up, and have courage to correct those who fail.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14D; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-080
- **Prompt:** What do ‘Get Out from Behind Your Desk’ and ‘Search Out the Problems’ mean in General Wilson’s advice?
- **Answer:** Get out: see for yourself what is going on; people see interest in problems, conditions, and welfare. Search out problems: if you think there are none, you may be wrong; foster an environment that encourages people to bring problems they cannot solve themselves.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14D; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-081
- **Prompt:** What does ‘Find the Critical Path to Success’ warn leaders to avoid?
- **Answer:** The activity trap—do not spend valuable time on inconsequential or trivial matters; get personally involved in make-or-break issues on a priority basis and weigh in where it counts.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14D; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-082
- **Prompt:** What do ‘Do Not Alibi,’ ‘Do Not Procrastinate,’ and ‘Do Not Tolerate Incompetence’ require?
- **Answer:** Do not be defensive about mistakes or use constant alibis/half-truths. Do not put off hard decisions (without making precipitous unreasonable decisions just to be prompt). When people show laziness, disinterest, or inability, have courage to terminate their assignments; recognize and encourage good work.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14D; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-083
- **Prompt:** What does ‘Be Honest’ require of leaders in General Wilson’s advice?
- **Answer:** Create an atmosphere of trust and confidence; be candid and insist others do likewise; people set behavior patterns based on your example; nothing is more disastrous than half-truths; be honest with yourself—do not gimmick reports and figures to look good on paper.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14D; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-084
- **Prompt:** What positive framing of power does AFH 1 adopt from Ken Blanchard’s Points of Power?
- **Answer:** Developing one’s own sources of power enables leaders to be less dependent on others, take initiative, and make greater contributions. “The sole advantage of power is the ability to do better.” Apply various aspects of power where most appropriate rather than relying only on one strongest aspect.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.23
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14D; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-085
- **Prompt:** What are position power and task power?
- **Answer:** Position power: inherent in the authority of the position (often indicated by title showing power to manage people or command resources). Task power: stems from being good at a particular task and able to help others with a process or procedure they may be responsible for.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.23
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14D; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-086
- **Prompt:** What are personal power, relationship power, and knowledge power?
- **Answer:** Personal: character attributes such as strength of character, passion, inspiration, or personal vision, enhanced by interpersonal/persuasion skills. Relationship: association through friendship, familiarity, cultivation, preferential treatment, or reciprocity—perceived favoritism can have negative effects. Knowledge: expertise often shown by degrees/certifications; generally transferable.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.23
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14D; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-087
- **Prompt:** What three core elements make up the FRLD system, and what engagement actions does AFH 1 list?
- **Answer:** Leader, follower, and situation. Engage by developing relationships; taking advantage of opportunities; efficiently using resources; properly evaluating situations and follower performance; rewarding appropriately (and disciplining accordingly); identifying improvement areas in oneself, followers, and the workplace.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.24
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14D; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-088
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 describe laissez-faire leadership?
- **Answer:** Leaders view subordinate development/needs as someone else’s concern; abandon responsibilities; remain indifferent to important issues; hesitate to make decisions; may be absent from work—negatively affecting peer and subordinate relationships.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.25
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14D; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-089
- **Prompt:** How do Management by Exception-Passive and Management by Exception-Active differ?
- **Answer:** MBE-Passive: “if it’s not broke, don’t fix it”—sit back and intervene only when rules are broken or goals unmet. MBE-Active: monitor and govern through forced compliance with rules/standards in a structured system with detailed instructions, careful observation, and very active supervision—without going so far as to be perceived as a dictator.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.25
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14D; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-090
- **Prompt:** What is Contingent Rewards leadership in the FRLD model?
- **Answer:** A transactional style with constructive contracts/agreements: leader sets goals, identifies ways to reach them, and supports the follower; follower performs to a specified level; leader reinforces with a reward contingent on meeting expectations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.25
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14D; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-091
- **Prompt:** What defines transformational leadership, and what are its four components?
- **Answer:** Offers followers a vision and inspires their mission; inspires exceeding goals through intrinsic motivation so others act because they want to. Components: Individualized Consideration (Nurturing), Intellectual Stimulation (Thinking), Inspirational Motivation (Charming), Idealized Influence (Influencing).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.25
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14D; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-092
- **Prompt:** What does Individualized Consideration (Nurturing) require of transformational leaders?
- **Answer:** Treat followers as individuals with different needs, abilities, and aspirations—not just as a group; empathize and support while maintaining healthy communication; nurture by acting as mentor or coach.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.25
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14D; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-093
- **Prompt:** What does Intellectual Stimulation (Thinking) emphasize?
- **Answer:** Value subordinates’ rationality and intellect by seeking different perspectives and considering opposing points of view; stimulate creativity; encourage independent thinking; take risks and solicit ideas.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.25
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14D; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-094
- **Prompt:** How do Inspirational Motivation and Idealized Influence differ?
- **Answer:** Inspirational Motivation: articulate optimistic, enthusiastic visions that elevate expectations and inspire extra effort. Idealized Influence: charismatic positive role models who “walk the walk,” exhibit high moral behavior/virtues/character/work ethic, represent organizational values, and set aside personal interests for the group if necessary.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.25
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14D; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-095
- **Prompt:** What do Leading by Example and Involvement require as leadership attributes?
- **Answer:** Leading by Example: model in word and action; lead rather than drive; fair/firm decisions; cannot demand the best if unwilling/unable to do the same; live values without dwelling on personal effort. Involvement: success reflected in followers’ efficiency, productivity, morale, and enthusiasm; active involvement in Airmen’s careers and lives maximizes performance.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.26
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14D; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-096
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 describe Learning from Failure, Transparency, and Flexibility?
- **Answer:** Learning from Failure: failure is a great learning tool; leaders never fear failure; learning empowers change. Transparency: intentions clear through communication, shared decision-making, consensus, and healthy debate so Airmen know why decisions are made. Flexibility: listen to other views, bend when necessary, change course if needed—especially valued during change.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.26
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14D; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-097
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 describe Resilience, Accountability, and Positive Attitude?
- **Answer:** Resilience: compassion and grit to persevere in uncertainty, deviation, turmoil, and conflict. Accountability: clear roles, pride/ownership, regular feedback, integrity, positive example—focused on continuous learning/improvement, not punishment for being unaccountable. Positive Attitude: demonstrate the attitude to emulate; enthusiasm is contagious; encourage Airmen.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.26
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14D; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-098
- **Prompt:** How do Values, Competence, and Character relate as leadership attributes?
- **Answer:** Values (trust, loyalty, integrity) in leaders relate to organizational effectiveness; leadership sustains values and includes willingness to remove people who do not align. Competence develops via training, education, and experience. Character is who the leader is as a person—developed over time—and must be coupled with competence for great leadership.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.26
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14D; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-099
- **Prompt:** What cautions apply to Charisma, and what do Compassion and Courage contribute?
- **Answer:** Charisma inspires when tasks are not intrinsically motivating but should not be contrary to authority or undermine commander intent. Compassion promotes healthy open communication about personal issues. Courage includes moral and physical courage to address substandard performance, welcome ideas, do what is ethically right, and be honest.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.26
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14D; B—Understanding

### C14-FC-100
- **Prompt:** How is Credibility earned and lost according to AFH 1, and what publication pointer closes ¶14.26?
- **Answer:** Credibility is being trusted and believed in; earned through humility, commitment, using team strengths, sometimes working alongside followers, leading by example, and taking responsibility for self and Airmen; may take years and can be lost with one thoughtless action. AFH 36-2647, Competency Modeling, includes attachments on developing self, others, ideas, and organizations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶14.26
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 14D; B—Understanding
