## Section 5A — United States Armed Forces

### C05-FC-001
- **Prompt:** How does command authority connect responsibility and accountability across a chain of command?
- **Answer:** It establishes responsibility for command, control, and communication at each level; every level is responsible for those below it and accountable to those above it.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5A; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-002
- **Prompt:** How does civilian control qualify the President's role as Commander in Chief?
- **Answer:** The Constitution gives the President final military command authority, while the legislative and judicial branches retain checks and balances on executive power.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5A; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-003
- **Prompt:** How do the operational and administrative chains differ for forces assigned to a combatant command?
- **Answer:** The operational chain runs President–Secretary of Defense–combatant commander; administrative control normally continues through the force's military service. For forces not assigned to a combatant commander, command runs through the military department Secretary.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5A; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-004
- **Prompt:** Why can the CJCS appear in command communications without becoming a commander in the operational chain?
- **Answer:** The President may authorize communications through the CJCS, but that communications role does not confer command over the Joint Chiefs or Armed Forces.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.1, 5.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5A; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-005
- **Prompt:** How does the Department of Defense's organization support its mission?
- **Answer:** To deter war and protect national security, DoD joins the Secretary's office, Joint Chiefs, Joint Staff, military departments, unified commands, combined-command forces, defense agencies, and field activities.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5A; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-006
- **Prompt:** What links the Secretary of Defense's policy role to operational command?
- **Answer:** As DoD's civilian head and the President's principal defense-policy advisor, the Secretary formulates general defense policy, executes approved policy, and occupies the operational chain between the President and combatant commanders.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5A; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-007
- **Prompt:** What is the purpose of the Secretary of Defense's written guidance to defense leaders?
- **Answer:** It translates national-security objectives into policy, mission priorities, resource assumptions, contingency-planning direction, and guidance for detecting and monitoring aerial and maritime threats.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5A; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-008
- **Prompt:** How do the Armed Forces Policy Council and DoD's functional staff support the Secretary of Defense differently?
- **Answer:** The Council provides requested long-range, broad-policy advice; Under Secretaries and specialized agencies provide continuing functional expertise and staff assistance.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5A; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-009
- **Prompt:** What is the Joint Staff's relationship to the Joint Chiefs and the CJCS?
- **Answer:** It helps the Joint Chiefs with strategic direction, unified-command operations, and service integration, but legal direction of the staff belongs exclusively to the CJCS, normally exercised through the Director.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5A; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-010
- **Prompt:** Why does the CJCS's senior rank not make the office the military's operational commander?
- **Answer:** The CJCS outranks other officers but is the principal military advisor to the President, National Security Council, and Secretary of Defense and may not command the Joint Chiefs or Armed Forces. The Secretary of Defense may assign oversight of combatant-command activities, illustrating that oversight does not itself confer command.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5A; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-011
- **Prompt:** How do the CJCS, VCJCS, and SEAC roles complement one another?
- **Answer:** The CJCS presides and provides military advice; the VCJCS performs approved duties and acts as Chairman when required during the Chairman's absence. The source designates the SEAC as the Armed Forces' highest senior enlisted position; the SEAC advises the CJCS and Secretary on enlisted Total Force integration, utilization, health, and joint development and serves as a spokesperson on applicable enlisted issues.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5A; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-012
- **Prompt:** What collective purposes distinguish the Joint Chiefs of Staff from an operational headquarters?
- **Answer:** They advise national leaders, provide strategic direction, review major requirements, establish joint doctrine, assign service logistics responsibilities, shape joint training policy, and coordinate military education.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5A; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-013
- **Prompt:** Who belongs to the Joint Chiefs of Staff, and how do Service Chiefs balance joint and service duties?
- **Answer:** The members are the CJCS, VCJCS, Army Chief of Staff, Chief of Naval Operations, Air Force Chief of Staff, Chief of Space Operations, Marine Corps Commandant, and Chief of the National Guard Bureau. Service Chiefs give JCS duties priority, delegate many service tasks to Vice Chiefs, and retain overall responsibility.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5A; B—Understanding

## Section 5B — Military Departments

### C05-FC-014
- **Prompt:** How are the Armed Forces grouped into military departments?
- **Answer:** The departments are Army; Navy, including the Marine Corps and the Coast Guard in wartime; and Air Force, including the Space Force.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-015
- **Prompt:** What common national purpose unites the military departments despite their different domains?
- **Answer:** They protect national survival, lives, property, and vital interests; jointly, senior leaders support homeland defense, reassure allies, and dissuade, deter, or defeat enemies.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-016
- **Prompt:** What did the Key West Agreement establish, and what general obligations apply to every service?
- **Answer:** It allocated general and specific service roles and missions. All services must defend the Constitution, secure the United States and vital areas through timely military action, and advance national policies and interests. **AFH source note:** The handbook dates the agreement to 1948 and identifies 1958 as its latest revision.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-017
- **Prompt:** Why do distinct service roles not justify operating in isolation?
- **Answer:** The Key West framework directs each service to use other services' people, equipment, and facilities whenever doing so improves economy and effectiveness.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-018
- **Prompt:** What is the Army's distinctive role in preparing for conflict?
- **Answer:** It prepares land forces for prompt, sustained land dominance, expands peacetime components for war, develops land-combat capabilities, and coordinates matters of joint concern with the Navy and Air Force.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-019
- **Prompt:** Which land outcomes organize the Army's specific functions?
- **Answer:** Conducting land and joint operations, seizing or defending land—including airborne and joint amphibious operations—and occupying land areas.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-020
- **Prompt:** How does the Navy's force design support its central mission?
- **Answer:** Naval combat and service forces, naval aviation, and the Marine Corps are prepared primarily for sustained combat at sea, freedom of the seas, deterrence, wartime expansion, and victory.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-021
- **Prompt:** What operational effects distinguish the Navy's specific functions?
- **Answer:** Sea and joint operations; control of vital seas and sea lanes; suppression of enemy sea commerce; support to occupation forces; limited shore seizure; reconnaissance; antisubmarine warfare; and protection of shipping.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-022
- **Prompt:** How does the Marine Corps connect naval operations, land operations, and national policy?
- **Answer:** It provides forces for advanced naval bases and related limited land operations, develops landing-force aspects of amphibious warfare, supplies Navy detachments and security, performs directed foreign missions, and assists the Army and Air Force.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-023
- **Prompt:** What makes the Coast Guard's departmental relationship different from that of the other Armed Forces?
- **Answer:** It is always a military service but normally belongs to Homeland Security; it operates as part of the Navy during a declared war or when directed by the President.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-024
- **Prompt:** How do the Coast Guard's peacetime functions prepare it for national defense?
- **Answer:** Maritime law enforcement and interdiction, safety regulation, navigation aids, icebreaking, rescue, and oceanographic research sustain maritime capability while the service remains ready for specialized Navy and Maritime Defense Zone duties in war.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-025
- **Prompt:** What defines the Air Force's broad role under the Key West framework?
- **Answer:** It prepares military aviation not otherwise assigned for prompt, sustained offensive and defensive air operations, expands for wartime needs, coordinates air defense across services, and supports Army and Navy missions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-026
- **Prompt:** How do the Air Force's specific functions combine independent air effects with joint support?
- **Answer:** They include air and joint operations, air supremacy or local superiority, strategic forces and reconnaissance, airlift for airborne operations, support to land and naval forces, military air transport, and coordinated capability development.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-027
- **Prompt:** How do the Air Force and Space Force contribute through the Department of the Air Force?
- **Answer:** The department organizes, trains, equips, and provides air, space, and cyberspace forces for combat, engagement, security cooperation, and joint support; together the services provide global vigilance, reach, and power.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-028
- **Prompt:** What outcomes organize the Space Force's specific functions?
- **Answer:** Freedom of U.S. operation in, from, and to space; prompt and sustained space operations; protection of U.S. interests; deterrence of space-related aggression; and conduct of space operations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

## Section 5C — Military Command Structure

### C05-FC-029
- **Prompt:** What makes a command “combined,” and what governs its command responsibilities?
- **Answer:** It joins forces or agencies from at least two allied nations, and its missions and command arrangements must conform to a treaty, alliance, bilateral agreement, or other relevant multinational agreement. Source examples include NORAD, Combined Forces Command Korea, and Allied Command Operations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5C; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-030
- **Prompt:** What is the central difference between combined and unified commands?
- **Answer:** A combined command is multinational and agreement-based; a unified combatant command is a U.S. command with a broad, continuing mission and forces from at least two U.S. military departments.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.6–5.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5C; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-031
- **Prompt:** How do command and service authority coexist inside a unified combatant command?
- **Answer:** The President establishes the command with CJCS assistance through the Secretary of Defense; assigned or attached units remain under the combatant command authority of the CCDR and the administrative control of their service component commander.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5C; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-032
- **Prompt:** Why can a force assigned or attached to a unified command not simply be moved by that command?
- **Answer:** The source says transfer requires Secretary of Defense authority or use of special procedures approved by the President.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5C; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-033
- **Prompt:** How does the Unified Command Plan shape combatant commands?
- **Answer:** The classified executive-branch plan, prepared by the CJCS, assigns missions, planning, training, operational responsibilities, and geographic areas; those choices affect command organization, training, and resources. **AFH source note:** The handbook says the UCP is reviewed and updated every two years.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5C; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-034
- **Prompt:** How do geographic and functional combatant commands differ?
- **Answer:** Geographic commands operate within UCP-defined physical boundaries and have regional focus; functional commands operate globally and supply specialized capabilities. **AFH source ambiguity:** The handbook lists six geographic and four functional commands but separately profiles USSPACECOM as the eleventh without adding it to the functional enumeration.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5C; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-035
- **Prompt:** How does a COMAFFOR connect Air Force administrative authority with joint operational authority?
- **Answer:** The Secretary of the Air Force assigns forces to an Air Force Service Component Command led by the COMAFFOR; the joint force commander typically delegates OPCON, while the Secretary's administrative chain supplies ADCON.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5C; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-036
- **Prompt:** What function distinguishes U.S. Africa Command (USAFRICOM)?
- **Answer:** It manages military relations with African nations, the African Union, and regional security organizations while building partner defense capacity and conducting cooperative missions against transnational threats.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5C; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-037
- **Prompt:** What function distinguishes U.S. Central Command (USCENTCOM)?
- **Answer:** It uses national and international partnerships in the central region to build cooperation, respond to crises, deter and defeat threats, and promote regional stability.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5C; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-038
- **Prompt:** What function distinguishes U.S. European Command (USEUCOM)?
- **Answer:** It works with NATO and other partners on European and neighboring security needs through cooperative planning, training, humanitarian assistance, and strategies for peace and stability.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5C; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-039
- **Prompt:** What function distinguishes U.S. Northern Command (USNORTHCOM)?
- **Answer:** It concentrates on homeland security and civil support in its North American area. The Commander, USNORTHCOM, is designated Commander of U.S. Element, North American Aerospace Defense Command and, when a U.S. officer fulfills the role, Commander of North American Aerospace Defense Command.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5C; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-040
- **Prompt:** What function distinguishes U.S. Indo-Pacific Command (USINDOPACOM)?
- **Answer:** It works with regional partners to improve security, deter aggression, respond with force when required, provide humanitarian assistance, and build collective capability through multinational exercises.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5C; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-041
- **Prompt:** What function distinguishes U.S. Southern Command (USSOUTHCOM)?
- **Answer:** It engages Latin American and Caribbean partners to strengthen peacekeeping, promote human rights, deter illicit trafficking, and develop shared capability through multinational exercises.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5C; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-042
- **Prompt:** What function distinguishes U.S. Special Operations Command (USSOCOM)?
- **Answer:** It plans and conducts special operations, including direct action, special reconnaissance, unconventional warfare, foreign internal defense, civil affairs, counterterrorism, information activities, and other directed missions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5C; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-043
- **Prompt:** What function distinguishes U.S. Strategic Command (USSTRATCOM)?
- **Answer:** It partners globally to deter and detect strategic attacks and is responsible for nuclear capabilities, global strike, joint electromagnetic-spectrum analysis and targeting, and missile-threat assessment.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5C; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-044
- **Prompt:** What function distinguishes U.S. Cyber Command (USCYBERCOM)?
- **Answer:** It seeks and maintains cyberspace superiority in support of national security and defense strategy and the advancement of U.S. interests.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5C; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-045
- **Prompt:** What function distinguishes U.S. Transportation Command (USTRANSCOM)?
- **Answer:** It conducts globally integrated mobility, leads joint deployment and distribution, and provides capabilities that project and sustain the Joint Force.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5C; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-046
- **Prompt:** What function distinguishes U.S. Space Command (USSPACECOM)?
- **Answer:** It operates in, from, and to space to deter conflict, defeat aggression when necessary, deliver space combat power to joint and combined forces, and defend vital interests with allies and partners.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5C; B—Understanding

## Section 5D — Air Force Total Force

### C05-FC-047
- **Prompt:** What does the “one team” concept mean for the USAF Total Force?
- **Answer:** The three components named by the source are the Regular Air Force, Air Force Reserve, and Air National Guard. Military members, civilian Airmen, and contractors contribute to the Total Force team, but civilians and contractors are contributors rather than additional military components; integrating the three components' capabilities is necessary for efficient and effective airpower.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5D; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-048
- **Prompt:** How has recurring use changed the role of the Guard and Reserve?
- **Answer:** They evolved from a mainly strategic reserve into forces that also provide routine operational capability, long-term strategic depth, and surge capacity.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5D; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-049
- **Prompt:** Why are civilian Airmen central rather than peripheral to Total Force effectiveness?
- **Answer:** They supply experience, continuity, leadership, and perspectives that broaden problem-solving, improve adaptation, and support mission success alongside military Airmen.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5D; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-050
- **Prompt:** How do classic and active Total Force associations reverse the host-tenant relationship?
- **Answer:** In a classic association, the Regular Component hosts and retains weapon-system responsibility while a Guard or Reserve unit is the tenant; in an active association, the Guard or Reserve hosts the Regular tenant. Both share resources for a common mission.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5D; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-051
- **Prompt:** What relationship makes the Air National Guard and Air Force Reserve one Air Reserve Component?
- **Answer:** Both augment rapidly deploying Regular forces, train to the same standards and readiness levels, and receive equipment at equal priority; diverting their equipment requires written Secretary of Defense approval.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5D; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-052
- **Prompt:** Why can geographic dispersion benefit Air Reserve Component units?
- **Answer:** Dispersed units can take advantage of state or regional demographics, strengthen ties with civilian communities, and build public support without requiring concentration at large multi-squadron bases.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5D; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-053
- **Prompt:** How does command of a non-mobilized Reserve unit differ from command of a non-mobilized IMA?
- **Answer:** Reserve units are commanded through the Air Force Reserve Command commander, who answers to the CSAF; Individual Mobilization Augmentees are commanded concurrently through Reserve Command and their attached unit.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5D; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-054
- **Prompt:** What changes when Air Reserve Component forces are mobilized?
- **Answer:** After presidential authorization under Title 10, the Secretary of Defense may delegate call-up authority to the service Secretary; activated forces pass to the gaining commander's operational command, and that commander provides training resources.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5D; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-055
- **Prompt:** How do dual-status military technicians connect the Air National Guard's daily and mobilized missions?
- **Answer:** They provide daily organization, administration, recruiting, instruction, training, and maintenance as federal civil servants who must belong to their employing unit; they train and mobilize with that unit.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5D; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-056
- **Prompt:** How do the Air National Guard's federal and state missions differ?
- **Answer:** Federally, units stay trained and equipped for mobilization and national emergencies; outside federal control, governors direct missions protecting life, property, peace, and public safety. For the District of Columbia, the President holds jurisdiction and delegates it to the Secretary of the Army.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5D; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-057
- **Prompt:** How does the Air Force Reserve provide both reinforcement and specialized capability?
- **Answer:** It supplies people and units when Regular capacity is insufficient while maintaining ready, standby, and retired categories and combat-ready capability across Air Force functions and combatant commands. Its Chief advises the SecAF and CSAF on reserve matters.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5D; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-058
- **Prompt:** When does Civil Air Patrol act as the Air Force Auxiliary rather than only as a nonprofit corporation?
- **Answer:** CAP is the volunteer civilian auxiliary when a federal department or agency uses its services. The SecAF employment authority described in ¶5.12 permits CAP use for noncombat Air Force programs and missions instead of or alongside Air Force resources. When approved and assigned by the Air Force, CAP conducts missions as the Air Force Auxiliary; the source aligns CAP under Air Combat Command for fiscal and operational oversight and utilization.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5D; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-059
- **Prompt:** How do CAP's three primary programs support public service and development?
- **Answer:** Emergency services and civil support aid routine and crisis response; aerospace education serves members and the public; cadet programs develop responsible citizens through aviation. CAP uses geographic regions, state-level wings, and lower units to deliver them.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5D; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-060
- **Prompt:** Why is AFJROTC a citizenship program rather than a military accession program?
- **Answer:** Its Title 10 mission develops high-school students as citizens of character through leadership, aerospace science, discipline, and hands-on activities; enrollment creates no military service obligation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5D; B—Understanding

## Section 5E — Air Force Structure

### C05-FC-061
- **Prompt:** How is the Department of the Air Force structured to prepare forces?
- **Answer:** Its Secretariat, Air Staff, Space Staff, and Air Force and Space Force field units prepare air, space, and cyber forces for war, other military operations, and wartime expansion.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5E; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-062
- **Prompt:** How do the Air Force's primary functions turn airpower into joint effects?
- **Answer:** They combine air and missile defense, air superiority, nuclear operations, precision attack, ISR, global mobility, agile combat support, personnel recovery, and integrated command and control to enable U.S. and allied forces.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5E; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-063
- **Prompt:** What is the Secretary of the Air Force's relationship to readiness and administration?
- **Answer:** As the civilian DAF head under the Secretary of Defense, the SecAF recruits, organizes, supplies, equips, trains, services, mobilizes, and administers personnel while providing equipment, facilities, utilities, and real property.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5E; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-064
- **Prompt:** How does the CSAF serve both the SecAF and the Joint Chiefs without compromising either role?
- **Answer:** The CSAF works under the SecAF, presides over the Air Staff, and carries out approved plans and supervision, while independently performing JCS duties and informing the SecAF of joint advice affecting the department.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5E; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-065
- **Prompt:** What is the Air Staff's purpose, and who provides its senior functional leadership?
- **Answer:** It supports the CSAF through the Chief, Vice Chief, Deputy and Assistant Chiefs, Surgeon General, Judge Advocate General, Chief of Air Force Reserve, and assigned military and civilian personnel.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5E; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-066
- **Prompt:** What functions do the Air Staff's two-letter office codes represent?
- **Answer:** A1—Manpower, Personnel, and Services; A2/6—Intelligence, Surveillance, Reconnaissance, and Cyber Effects Operations; A3—Operations; A4—Logistics, Engineering, and Force Protection; A5/7—Strategy, Integration, and Requirements; A6—Communications; A8—Strategic Plans and Programs; A10—Strategic Deterrence and Nuclear Integration. **AFH source ambiguity:** The handbook prints both the merged A2/6 cyber-effects portfolio and a separate A6 communications entry.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5E; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-067
- **Prompt:** How does a Field Operating Agency differ from a Direct Reporting Unit?
- **Answer:** A FOA reports to a HAF functional manager and performs specialized, enterprise-wide field work beyond a MAJCOM's scope; examples are AFPC and AFOSI. A DRU reports directly to the CSAF for a mission outside the MAJCOMs; examples are AFDW and the Air Force Academy.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5E; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-068
- **Prompt:** What makes a MAJCOM a major subdivision, and how do operational and support MAJCOMs differ?
- **Answer:** Each reports directly to HAF and represents a major Air Force subdivision with a specific portion of the Air Force mission. Operational commands contain combat or directly supporting flying forces; support commands provide systems, materiel, maintenance, transportation, education, training, and other support.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5E; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-069
- **Prompt:** How do Lead MAJCOM and Component MAJCOM responsibilities differ?
- **Answer:** A Lead MAJCOM consolidates Air Force-wide responsibility for a function; a C-MAJCOM is the Air Force component to a unified command, led by a COMAFFOR and presenting forces through Component NAFs. One MAJCOM may be both.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5E; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-070
- **Prompt:** How do Air Combat Command and Air Mobility Command provide different joint-force capabilities?
- **Answer:** ACC prepares combat, command-and-control, intelligence, and information forces for air and information superiority; AMC provides global air mobility through airlift, refueling, aeromedical evacuation, and related support as the Air Force component to USTRANSCOM.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5E; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-071
- **Prompt:** How do Pacific Air Forces (PACAF) and United States Air Forces in Europe & Air Forces Africa (USAFE-AFAFRICA) organize Air Force support to regional commands?
- **Answer:** PACAF supplies integrated air and cyberspace capability to USINDOPACOM; the blended USAFE-AFAFRICA staff supports USEUCOM and USAFRICOM through two distinct component roles held by one commander.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5E; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-072
- **Prompt:** How do AETC and AFMC support capability at different points in the Air Force life cycle?
- **Answer:** Air Education and Training Command recruits, trains, and educates Airmen; Air Force Materiel Command develops technology, manages acquisition, tests and evaluates systems, and sustains weapon systems throughout their life cycles.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5E; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-073
- **Prompt:** How do AFSOC and AFGSC provide specialized combat power?
- **Answer:** Air Force Special Operations Command provides specialized, rapidly deployable airpower as the component to USSOCOM; its special tactics squadrons combine combat controllers, special reconnaissance personnel, pararescuemen, and tactical air control party specialists with other services in joint special operations teams. Air Force Global Strike Command provides strategic deterrence, global strike, ICBM, and bomber capability as an Air Force component to USSTRATCOM.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5E; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-074
- **Prompt:** What distinguishes Air Force Reserve Command's MAJCOM role?
- **Answer:** AFRC organizes, trains, and equips Reserve units to provide combat-ready capability across deterrence, air and cyber superiority, command and control, ISR, precision attack, special operations, mobility, recovery, and support missions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5E; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-075
- **Prompt:** How does a Component Numbered Air Force differ from a conventional Numbered Air Force?
- **Answer:** A conventional NAF is an administrative echelon under a MAJCOM that provides intermediate operational leadership with an incomplete functional staff. A C-NAF is a warfighting headquarters with an Air Force Forces staff and operations center; when its commander is the COMAFFOR to a geographic CCMD, that commander is normally also the theater JFACC. C-NAF status alone does not grant control; designation, delegated authority, and governing orders determine control.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5E; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-076
- **Prompt:** What organizational purpose distinguishes a wing from the echelons above and below it?
- **Answer:** Below a NAF, a wing has a distinct mission of significant scope, may contain several squadrons within dependent groups, and normally carries installation responsibilities; wings are operational, air base, or specialized mission.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5E; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-077
- **Prompt:** How do operational wings and air base wings differ in mission and support?
- **Answer:** An operational wing has an operations group and operational activity. When it performs the base's primary mission, it usually maintains and operates the base and is capable of self-support in needed functional areas, such as maintenance and munitions; as a tenant, it normally receives base and logistics support from the host. An air base wing centers on operating and supporting a base rather than an operational mission.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5E; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-078
- **Prompt:** What makes a specialized mission wing distinct, and what determines whether it is host or tenant?
- **Answer:** It performs a focused mission, such as intelligence or training, and usually has no assigned aircraft or missiles; it is the host if it maintains and operates the base and otherwise is a tenant.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5E; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-079
- **Prompt:** How do groups and squadrons differ in scope, and how do dependent and independent groups compare?
- **Answer:** A group is a minimally staffed tactical echelon below a wing, usually with multiple units; a dependent group serves a wing function, while an independent group performs wing-like duties at smaller scope. A squadron is the Air Force's basic mission or functional building block.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5E; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-080
- **Prompt:** How do numbered or named, alpha, and functional flights organize squadron work, and where do elements fit?
- **Answer:** Numbered or named flights combine smaller elements with squadron-like administrative reporting; alpha flights group elements doing identical missions; functional flights group elements doing specific missions. Elements are subdivisions of flights.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5E; B—Understanding
