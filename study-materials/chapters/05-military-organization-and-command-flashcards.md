## Section 5A — United States Armed Forces

### C05-FC-001
- **Prompt:** How does command authority connect responsibility and accountability across a chain of command?
- **Answer:** It establishes responsibility for command, control, and communication at each level; every level is responsible for those below it and accountable to those above it.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5A; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-002
- **Prompt:** How does civilian control qualify the President's role as Commander in Chief?
- **Answer:** The Constitution gives the President final military command authority, while the legislative and judicial branches retain checks and balances on executive power.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5A; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-003
- **Prompt:** How do the operational and administrative chains differ for forces assigned to a combatant command?
- **Answer:** The operational chain runs President–Secretary of Defense–combatant commander; administrative control normally continues through the force's military service. For forces not assigned to a combatant commander, command runs through the military department Secretary. Version caveat: this reflects the 15 February 2025 AFH 1 structure.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5A; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-004
- **Prompt:** Why can the CJCS appear in command communications without becoming a commander in the operational chain?
- **Answer:** The President may authorize communications through the CJCS, but that communications role does not confer command over the Joint Chiefs or Armed Forces. Version caveat: this reflects the authorities described in the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.1, 5.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5A; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-005
- **Prompt:** How does the Department of Defense's organization support its mission?
- **Answer:** To deter war and protect national security, DoD joins the Secretary's office, Joint Chiefs, Joint Staff, military departments, unified commands, combined-command forces, defense agencies, and field activities. Version caveat: organization reflects the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5A; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-006
- **Prompt:** What links the Secretary of Defense's policy role to operational command?
- **Answer:** As DoD's civilian head and the President's principal defense-policy advisor, the Secretary formulates general defense policy, executes approved policy, and occupies the operational chain between the President and combatant commanders. Version caveat: command structure reflects the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5A; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-007
- **Prompt:** What is the purpose of the Secretary of Defense's written guidance to defense leaders?
- **Answer:** It translates national-security objectives into policy, mission priorities, resource assumptions, contingency-planning direction, and guidance for detecting and monitoring aerial and maritime threats.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5A; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-008
- **Prompt:** How do the Armed Forces Policy Council and DoD's functional staff support the Secretary of Defense differently?
- **Answer:** The Council provides requested long-range, broad-policy advice; Under Secretaries and specialized agencies provide continuing functional expertise and staff assistance. Version caveat: organizational relationships reflect the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5A; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-009
- **Prompt:** What is the Joint Staff's relationship to the Joint Chiefs and the CJCS?
- **Answer:** It helps the Joint Chiefs with strategic direction, unified-command operations, and service integration, but legal direction of the staff belongs exclusively to the CJCS, normally exercised through the Director. Version caveat: organization reflects the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5A; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-010
- **Prompt:** Why does the CJCS's senior rank not make the office the military's operational commander?
- **Answer:** The CJCS outranks other officers but is the principal military advisor to the President, National Security Council, and Secretary of Defense and may not command the Joint Chiefs or Armed Forces. Version caveat: authority reflects the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5A; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-011
- **Prompt:** How do the CJCS, VCJCS, and SEAC roles complement one another?
- **Answer:** The CJCS presides and provides military advice; the VCJCS performs approved duties and acts for an absent Chair; the SEAC advises the CJCS and Secretary on enlisted Total Force integration, use, health, and joint development. Version caveat: roles reflect the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5A; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-012
- **Prompt:** What collective purposes distinguish the Joint Chiefs of Staff from an operational headquarters?
- **Answer:** They advise national leaders, provide strategic direction, review major requirements, establish joint doctrine, assign service logistics responsibilities, shape joint training policy, and coordinate military education.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5A; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-013
- **Prompt:** Who belongs to the Joint Chiefs of Staff, and how do Service Chiefs balance joint and service duties?
- **Answer:** The members are the CJCS, VCJCS, Army Chief of Staff, Chief of Naval Operations, Air Force Chief of Staff, Chief of Space Operations, Marine Corps Commandant, and Chief of the National Guard Bureau. Service Chiefs give JCS duties priority, delegate many service tasks to Vice Chiefs, and retain overall responsibility. Version caveat: membership reflects the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5A; B—Understanding

## Section 5B — Military Departments

### C05-FC-014
- **Prompt:** How are the Armed Forces grouped into military departments?
- **Answer:** The departments are Army; Navy, including the Marine Corps and the Coast Guard in wartime; and Air Force, including the Space Force. Version caveat: departmental organization reflects the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-015
- **Prompt:** What common national purpose unites the military departments despite their different domains?
- **Answer:** They protect national survival, lives, property, and vital interests; jointly, senior leaders support homeland defense, reassure allies, and dissuade, deter, or defeat enemies.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-016
- **Prompt:** What did the Key West Agreement establish, and what general obligations apply to every service?
- **Answer:** It allocated general and specific service roles and missions. All services must defend the Constitution, secure the United States and vital areas through timely military action, and advance national policies and interests. Version caveat: the source dates the agreement to 1948 and its latest revision to 1958.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-017
- **Prompt:** Why do distinct service roles not justify operating in isolation?
- **Answer:** The Key West framework directs each service to use other services' people, equipment, and facilities whenever doing so improves economy and effectiveness.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-018
- **Prompt:** What is the Army's distinctive role in preparing for conflict?
- **Answer:** It prepares land forces for prompt, sustained land dominance, expands peacetime components for war, develops land-combat capabilities, and coordinates matters of joint concern with the Navy and Air Force.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-019
- **Prompt:** Which land outcomes organize the Army's specific functions?
- **Answer:** Conducting land and joint operations, seizing or defending land—including airborne and joint amphibious operations—and occupying land areas.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-020
- **Prompt:** How does the Navy's force design support its central mission?
- **Answer:** Naval combat and service forces, naval aviation, and the Marine Corps are prepared primarily for sustained combat at sea, freedom of the seas, deterrence, wartime expansion, and victory.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-021
- **Prompt:** What operational effects distinguish the Navy's specific functions?
- **Answer:** Sea and joint operations; control of vital seas and sea lanes; suppression of enemy sea commerce; support to occupation forces; limited shore seizure; reconnaissance; antisubmarine warfare; and protection of shipping.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-022
- **Prompt:** How does the Marine Corps connect naval operations, land operations, and national policy?
- **Answer:** It provides forces for advanced naval bases and related limited land operations, develops landing-force aspects of amphibious warfare, supplies Navy detachments and security, performs directed foreign missions, and assists the Army and Air Force.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-023
- **Prompt:** What makes the Coast Guard's departmental relationship different from that of the other Armed Forces?
- **Answer:** It is always a military service but normally belongs to Homeland Security; it operates as part of the Navy during a declared war or when directed by the President. Version caveat: departmental assignment reflects the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-024
- **Prompt:** How do the Coast Guard's peacetime functions prepare it for national defense?
- **Answer:** Maritime law enforcement and interdiction, safety regulation, navigation aids, icebreaking, rescue, and oceanographic research sustain maritime capability while the service remains ready for specialized Navy and Maritime Defense Zone duties in war.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-025
- **Prompt:** What defines the Air Force's broad role under the Key West framework?
- **Answer:** It prepares military aviation not otherwise assigned for prompt, sustained offensive and defensive air operations, expands for wartime needs, coordinates air defense across services, and supports Army and Navy missions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-026
- **Prompt:** How do the Air Force's specific functions combine independent air effects with joint support?
- **Answer:** They include air and joint operations, air supremacy or local superiority, strategic forces and reconnaissance, airlift for airborne operations, support to land and naval forces, military air transport, and coordinated capability development.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-027
- **Prompt:** How do the Air Force and Space Force contribute through the Department of the Air Force?
- **Answer:** The department organizes, trains, equips, and provides air, space, and cyberspace forces for combat, engagement, security cooperation, and joint support; together the services provide global vigilance, reach, and power. Version caveat: organizational scope reflects the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-028
- **Prompt:** What outcomes organize the Space Force's specific functions?
- **Answer:** Freedom of U.S. operation in, from, and to space; prompt and sustained space operations; protection of U.S. interests; deterrence of space-related aggression; and conduct of space operations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5B; B—Understanding

## Section 5C — Military Command Structure

### C05-FC-029
- **Prompt:** What makes a command “combined,” and what governs its command responsibilities?
- **Answer:** It joins forces or agencies from at least two allied nations, and its missions and command arrangements must conform to a treaty, alliance, bilateral agreement, or other relevant multinational agreement. Source examples include NORAD, Combined Forces Command Korea, and Allied Command Operations. Version caveat: examples reflect the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5C; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-030
- **Prompt:** What is the central difference between combined and unified commands?
- **Answer:** A combined command is multinational and agreement-based; a unified combatant command is a U.S. command with a broad, continuing mission and forces from at least two U.S. military departments.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶¶5.6–5.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5C; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-031
- **Prompt:** How do command and service authority coexist inside a unified combatant command?
- **Answer:** The President establishes the command with CJCS assistance through the Secretary of Defense; assigned or attached units remain under the combatant command authority of the CCDR and the administrative control of their service component commander. Version caveat: relationships reflect the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5C; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-032
- **Prompt:** Why can a force assigned or attached to a unified command not simply be moved by that command?
- **Answer:** Transfer requires Secretary of Defense authority or special procedures approved by the President, preserving national control over force assignment.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5C; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-033
- **Prompt:** How does the Unified Command Plan shape combatant commands?
- **Answer:** The classified executive-branch plan, prepared by the CJCS, assigns missions, planning, training, operational responsibilities, and geographic areas; those choices affect command organization, training, and resources. Version caveat: the source says the UCP is reviewed and updated every two years.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5C; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-034
- **Prompt:** How do geographic and functional combatant commands differ?
- **Answer:** Geographic commands operate within UCP-defined physical boundaries and have regional focus; functional commands operate globally and supply specialized capabilities. The source lists six geographic and four functional commands but separately profiles USSPACECOM as the eleventh command; verify current UCP classification. Version caveat: classification reflects the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5C; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-035
- **Prompt:** How does a COMAFFOR connect Air Force administrative authority with joint operational authority?
- **Answer:** The Secretary of the Air Force assigns forces to an Air Force Service Component Command led by the COMAFFOR; the joint force commander typically delegates OPCON, while the Secretary's administrative chain supplies ADCON. Version caveat: assignment relationships reflect the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5C; B—Understanding

## Section 5D — USAF Total Force

### C05-FC-047
- **Prompt:** What does the “one team” concept mean for the USAF Total Force?
- **Answer:** Military members, civilians, and contractors contribute through the Regular Air Force, Air Force Reserve, and Air National Guard; integrating each component's capabilities is necessary for efficient and effective airpower. Version caveat: component structure reflects the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5D; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-048
- **Prompt:** How has recurring use changed the role of the Guard and Reserve?
- **Answer:** They evolved from a mainly strategic reserve into forces that also provide routine operational capability, long-term strategic depth, and surge capacity.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5D; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-049
- **Prompt:** Why are civilian Airmen central rather than peripheral to Total Force effectiveness?
- **Answer:** They supply experience, continuity, leadership, and perspectives that broaden problem-solving, improve adaptation, and support mission success alongside military Airmen.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5D; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-050
- **Prompt:** How do classic and active Total Force associations reverse the host-tenant relationship?
- **Answer:** In a classic association, the Regular Component hosts and retains weapon-system responsibility while a Guard or Reserve unit is the tenant; in an active association, the Guard or Reserve hosts the Regular tenant. Both share resources for a common mission. Version caveat: association structure reflects the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5D; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-051
- **Prompt:** What relationship makes the Air National Guard and Air Force Reserve one Air Reserve Component?
- **Answer:** Both augment rapidly deploying Regular forces, train to the same standards and readiness levels, and receive equipment at equal priority; diverting their equipment requires written Secretary of Defense approval. Version caveat: component policy reflects the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5D; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-052
- **Prompt:** Why can geographic dispersion benefit Air Reserve Component units?
- **Answer:** Locating units around state or regional populations can improve access to people, strengthen ties with civilian communities, and build public support without requiring concentration at large multi-squadron bases. Version caveat: basing structure reflects the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5D; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-053
- **Prompt:** How does command of a non-mobilized Reserve unit differ from command of a non-mobilized IMA?
- **Answer:** Reserve units are commanded through the Air Force Reserve Command commander, who answers to the CSAF; Individual Mobilization Augmentees are commanded concurrently through Reserve Command and their attached unit. Version caveat: command relationships reflect the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5D; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-054
- **Prompt:** What changes when Air Reserve Component forces are mobilized?
- **Answer:** After presidential authorization under Title 10, the Secretary of Defense may delegate call-up authority to the service Secretary; activated forces pass to the gaining commander's operational command, and that commander provides training resources. Version caveat: authorities reflect the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5D; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-055
- **Prompt:** How do dual-status military technicians connect the Air National Guard's daily and mobilized missions?
- **Answer:** They provide daily organization, administration, recruiting, instruction, training, and maintenance as federal civil servants who must belong to their employing unit; they train and mobilize with that unit. Version caveat: personnel structure reflects the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5D; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-056
- **Prompt:** How do the Air National Guard's federal and state missions differ?
- **Answer:** Federally, units stay trained and equipped for mobilization and national emergencies; outside federal control, governors direct missions protecting life, property, peace, and public safety. For the District of Columbia, the President holds jurisdiction and delegates it to the Secretary of the Army. Version caveat: jurisdiction reflects the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5D; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-057
- **Prompt:** How does the Air Force Reserve provide both reinforcement and specialized capability?
- **Answer:** It supplies people and units when Regular capacity is insufficient while maintaining ready, standby, and retired categories and combat-ready capability across Air Force functions and combatant commands. Its Chief advises the SecAF and CSAF on reserve matters. Version caveat: organization reflects the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5D; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-058
- **Prompt:** When does Civil Air Patrol act as the Air Force Auxiliary rather than only as a nonprofit corporation?
- **Answer:** CAP is the volunteer civilian auxiliary when a federal department or agency uses its services; the SecAF may employ it for noncombat missions instead of or alongside Air Force resources. Air Force-approved missions receive Air Combat Command oversight. Version caveat: assignment reflects the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5D; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-059
- **Prompt:** How do CAP's three primary programs support public service and development?
- **Answer:** Emergency services and civil support aid routine and crisis response; aerospace education serves members and the public; cadet programs develop responsible citizens through aviation. CAP uses geographic regions, state-level wings, and lower units to deliver them. Version caveat: organization reflects the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5D; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-060
- **Prompt:** Why is AFJROTC a citizenship program rather than a military accession program?
- **Answer:** Its Title 10 mission develops high-school students as citizens of character through leadership, aerospace science, discipline, and hands-on activities; enrollment creates no military service obligation. Version caveat: program scope reflects the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5D; B—Understanding

Version caveat: functions reflect the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.

### C05-FC-067
- **Prompt:** How does a Field Operating Agency differ from a Direct Reporting Unit?
- **Answer:** A FOA reports to a HAF functional manager and performs specialized, enterprise-wide field work beyond a MAJCOM's scope; examples are AFPC and AFOSI. A DRU reports directly to the CSAF for a mission outside the MAJCOMs; examples are AFDW and the Air Force Academy. Version caveat: organization reflects the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5E; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-068
- **Prompt:** What makes a MAJCOM a major subdivision, and how do operational and support MAJCOMs differ?
- **Answer:** Each reports directly to HAF and owns a major portion of the Air Force mission. Operational commands contain combat or directly supporting flying forces; support commands provide systems, materiel, maintenance, transportation, education, training, and other support. Version caveat: structure reflects the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.

### C05-FC-069
- **Prompt:** How do Lead MAJCOM and Component MAJCOM responsibilities differ?
- **Answer:** A Lead MAJCOM consolidates Air Force-wide responsibility for a function; a C-MAJCOM is the Air Force component to a unified command, led by a COMAFFOR and presenting forces through Component NAFs. One MAJCOM may be both. Version caveat: roles reflect the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.

- **Answer:** ACC prepares combat, command-and-control, intelligence, and information forces for air and information superiority; AMC provides global air mobility through airlift, refueling, aeromedical evacuation, and related support as the Air Force component to USTRANSCOM. ### C05-FC-075
- **Prompt:** How does a Component Numbered Air Force differ from a conventional Numbered Air Force?
- **Answer:** A conventional NAF is an administrative echelon under a MAJCOM that provides intermediate operational leadership with an incomplete functional staff. A C-NAF is a warfighting headquarters with an Air Force Forces staff and operations center; when its commander is the COMAFFOR to a geographic CCMD, that commander is normally also the theater JFACC. Version caveat: structure reflects the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.

### C05-FC-076
- **Prompt:** What organizational purpose distinguishes a wing from the echelons above and below it?
- **Answer:** Below a NAF, a wing owns a distinct mission of significant scope, may contain several squadrons within dependent groups, and normally carries installation responsibilities; wings are operational, air base, or specialized mission. Version caveat: structure reflects the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5E; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-077
- **Prompt:** How do operational wings and air base wings differ in mission and support?
- **Answer:** An operational wing has an operations group and operational activity; as host it usually operates the base and supports itself, while as tenant it normally receives host support. An air base wing centers on operating and supporting a base rather than an operational mission. Version caveat: structure reflects the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5E; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-078
- **Prompt:** What makes a specialized mission wing distinct, and what determines whether it is host or tenant?
- **Answer:** It performs a focused mission, such as intelligence or training, and usually has no assigned aircraft or missiles; it is the host if it maintains and operates the base and otherwise is a tenant. Version caveat: structure reflects the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5E; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-079
- **Prompt:** How do groups and squadrons differ in scope, and how do dependent and independent groups compare?
- **Answer:** A group is a minimally staffed tactical echelon below a wing, usually with multiple units; a dependent group serves a wing function, while an independent group performs wing-like duties at smaller scope. A squadron is the Air Force's basic mission or functional building block. Version caveat: structure reflects the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 5E; B—Understanding

### C05-FC-080
- **Prompt:** How do numbered or named, alpha, and functional flights organize squadron work, and where do elements fit?
- **Answer:** Numbered or named flights combine smaller elements with squadron-like administrative reporting; alpha flights group elements doing identical missions; functional flights group elements doing specific missions. Elements are subdivisions of flights. Version caveat: structure reflects the 15 February 2025 AFH 1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶5.16
