# Chapter 14 Situational Judgment Practice

> **Status:** Draft and provisional for 27E5/27E6. Chapter 14 is shared core in the historical 26E5/26E6 catalog scopes.
>
> These scenarios are wholly original. They are not actual, recalled, reconstructed, or predicted PFE items. The official Air Force uses subject-matter experts and behavioral scientists to establish operational SJT scoring keys. Chapter 14 describes teamwork, people development, mentoring/counseling/vision, and leadership facts, but those facts alone do not establish an official behavioral scoring key. All most-effective and least-effective keys below are MyPromotion educational judgments grounded in Chapter 1 professionalism, core values, and ethical reasoning, with Chapter 14 material supplying context only. They are not official scoring keys or substitutes for current command, supervisory, mentoring, counseling, referral, safety, legal, ethics, or reporting procedures.
>
> **Rank / ADTC use:** Metadata reflects consolidated ADTC levels (14A/14B/14D B for both; 14C B for SSgt and C for TSgt). Scenarios that emphasize TSgt application of service-mindset principles are tagged accordingly; SSgt study still uses the shared behavioral practice set. Section 14E is deleted and excluded.
>
> **Two-track source rule:** For exam-source study, preserve the facts in the AFH edition assigned by the controlling catalog. For duty use, separately verify current mentoring, counseling, and leadership-development rules; duty-current information does not overwrite a catalog-controlled answer.

## How to use this set

Each scenario produces two items:

1. Select the **most effective** response.
2. Select the **least effective** response.

Read every option before choosing. Evaluate whether the response:

- represents team, mentoring, counseling, and leadership facts honestly;
- protects people, mission, and developmental integrity;
- uses supervisory influence without coercion, favoritism, or retaliation;
- stays within stated role and delegated authority;
- treats Airmen fairly in conflict, feedback, and recognition of contribution;
- respects people, support agencies, and the chain of command; and
- reflects integrity, accountability, loyalty, respect, and mission stewardship.

Chapter 14 supplies developing-others context; it does not prescribe the behavioral sequence in every response. When a rationale depends on general professional judgment—such as honesty, courage, accountability, loyalty, respect, mission focus, or teamwork—it is identified as an application of the cited foundational Chapter 1 paragraphs.

Ten scenarios produce 20 response selections, mirroring the historical public structure announced for the [22E5/22E6 cycles in December 2021](https://www.afpc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/2874484/waps-adjusts-for-22e6-22e5-testing-cycles/). The separate [public SJT informational guide](https://www.studyguides.af.mil/Portals/15/documents/Notices/Situational-Judgment-Test-Informational-Guide.pdf) explains the four-option most/least format. The 2027 structure is unconfirmed. This set covers only selected Chapter 14 concepts and is not a full-spectrum SJT simulation.

## C14-SJT-001 — Trust and open team communication

You lead a project team preparing a time-sensitive mission package. In the meeting, members agree on tasking. Afterward, two members tell other flights a different story that undercuts the team’s plan and implies the team agreed to something it did not. AFH 1 ties trust to open, honest, direct communication within the group and notes that saying one thing inside the group and another outside reduces trust fastest.

### Response options

A. Join the hallway version of the story so you appear loyal to the louder members.  
B. Address the inconsistency directly with the appropriate people, restore accurate shared understanding of the plan, and reaffirm open, honest communication without talking behind backs.  
C. Withhold remaining mission data from the whole team as punishment.  
D. Publicly ridicule the two members in a mass email to shame them.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 14.1 supplies trust and communication context; it does not create an official behavioral scoring key. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it restores direct, honest communication and protects team trust. Chapter 1 honesty, respect, and loyalty to the mission provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A continues the outside-the-group mismatch that AFH 1 identifies as rapidly destroying trust. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It amplifies the trust-breaking pattern.
- **B — Most effective:** It confronts the issue with the right people and restores accurate collaboration.
- **C — Ineffective:** Withholding needed data creates an inaccurate picture and harms team success.
- **D — Ineffective:** Public shaming is disrespectful and corrosive, not professional correction.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶14.1 (team trust/communication context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Team trust; honesty; respect; mission focus  
**ADTC:** 14A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of team-conflict realism; command climate and local procedures control real interventions

## C14-SJT-002 — Storming-stage conflict

Your newly formed work center is in conflict: members argue about methods, resist collaboration, and insist on personal experience alone. Progress is slow, and frustration is rising. AFH 1 describes storming as a normal, often difficult stage where conflict appears even as members begin to understand one another.

### Response options

A. Declare the team permanently failed and stop all meetings.  
B. Acknowledge the conflict as a normal stage, keep expectations clear, discourage unhelpful all-starring, reemphasize roles/responsibilities, and guide the team toward cooperative norms and the shared objective.  
C. Reward the loudest member with exclusive credit to “motivate” everyone else.  
D. Tell members never to disagree again, even constructively.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 14.1–14.3 supply team-stage and conflict context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it treats storming as developmental while restoring role clarity and cooperation. Chapter 1 respect, accountability, and teamwork provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: C

C fuels unhelpful competition and power-play dynamics AFH 1 warns against. Chapter 1 fairness/respect and loyalty to the team provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** Normal storming does not justify abandoning the mission team.
- **B — Most effective:** It manages conflict toward cooperation without denying the stage.
- **C — Least effective:** It rewards all-starring and damages shared ownership.
- **D — Ineffective:** Constructive conflict can improve understanding; total suppression is not the goal.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶14.1–14.3 (team stages/conflict context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Storming; constructive conflict; cooperation  
**ADTC:** 14A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of team-facilitation realism

## C14-SJT-003 — Conflict style selection

Two shops dispute ownership of a shared process. Both need a workable outcome, trust is still possible, and time allows honest discussion. A peer urges you to “just force it with rank” immediately. AFH 1 maps collaborating as high assertiveness and high cooperativeness seeking a win for both sides when trust and openness are present, and reminds leaders to consider who, stakes, and situation.

### Response options

A. Force a one-sided decision with threats solely to dominate, ignoring workable joint options.  
B. Facilitate honest discussion aimed at satisfying both shops’ critical concerns where feasible, using shared resources or process redesign if it leaves both better off.  
C. Avoid any discussion and hope the dispute disappears.  
D. Promise each shop exclusive ownership of the same resource knowing both promises cannot be kept.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 14.5 supplies collaborating-style context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it matches a high-assertiveness/high-cooperativeness approach to a situation where mutual gain is possible. Chapter 1 respect, honesty, and mission stewardship provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D uses dishonest dual promises and destroys trust. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective extremity:** Competing may fit some urgent one-way cases, but here mutual gain and trust make collaboration more fitting; threats-only forcing is corrosive.
- **B — Most effective:** It seeks a win for both through honest discussion.
- **C — Ineffective:** Avoiding can allow escalation.
- **D — Least effective:** It knowingly misleads both parties.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶14.5 (conflict styles context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Collaborating; honesty; respect  
**ADTC:** 14A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of conflict-resolution realism; command authorities control real process ownership

## C14-SJT-004 — Leadership and management balance

Your section is missing suspense tracking and resource coordination (management gaps) while also showing low willingness to pursue the mission purpose (leadership gap). A coworker says you should pick only “hard management” and ignore motivating people. AFH 1 says organizations need a strong balance of both and quotes Bennis on management getting people to do what needs to be done versus leadership getting people to want to do it.

### Response options

A. Ignore broken tracking systems and only give motivational speeches.  
B. Restore order and consistency in readiness/sustainment systems while also aligning people’s needs and purpose so they want to accomplish the mission.  
C. Tell Airmen motivation is irrelevant because title alone leads.  
D. Falsify tracking metrics so the section looks healthy on paper.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 14.7 supplies leadership/management balance context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it pairs managerial order with leadership motivation. Chapter 1 accountability, honesty, and mission focus provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D gimmicks reports—the opposite of honest leadership. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** Speeches without systems leave readiness gaps.
- **B — Most effective:** Balances management consistency and leadership motivation.
- **C — Ineffective:** Leadership is more than title; willingness matters.
- **D — Least effective:** Metric falsification destroys trust and accountability.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶14.7 (leadership/management context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Leadership–management balance; honesty; accountability  
**ADTC:** 14B, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of supervisory realism

## C14-SJT-005 — Mentoring during feedback (TSgt C)

You are a Technical Sergeant conducting a performance feedback session. The Airman wants to talk only about a dream assignment and asks you to skip performance, potential, and development planning “because mentoring is just networking.” AFH 1 makes mentoring an inherent leader obligation and sets a minimum discussion of performance, potential, and professional development plans during feedback.

### Response options

A. Skip performance and potential entirely and only swap assignment rumors.  
B. Cover performance, potential, and professional development plans, and include related topics such as promotions, PME, fitness, goals, professional qualities, future assignments, and long-range plans.  
C. Tell the Airman supervisors are forbidden from mentoring.  
D. Promise a top assignment recommendation in exchange for the Airman covering your additional duties off the books.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 14.9–14.10 supply mentoring minimum and program context for TSgt application. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it applies the required mentoring content in the feedback situation. Chapter 1 accountability, respect, and loyalty to Airmen’s development provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D converts mentoring into a personal duty bargain. Chapter 1 integrity and misuse-of-authority concerns provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It omits the mentoring minimum.
- **B — Most effective:** It applies AFH 1 mentoring content in the feedback setting.
- **C — Ineffective:** Mentoring is an inherent leader obligation.
- **D — Least effective:** It corrupts mentoring with a personal trade.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶14.9–14.10 (mentoring application context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Applying mentoring minimums; integrity; accountability  
**ADTC:** 14C, C—Applying  
**Ranks:** TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of TSgt mentoring realism; DAFMAN 36-2643 and command procedures control real mentoring

## C14-SJT-006 — Counseling approach selection (TSgt C)

You supervise an Airman who repeatedly misses a simple, clearly defined checklist step. The error is happening now on the floor, time is short, and the correct action is unambiguous. The Airman asks to “talk it out for an hour” before correcting anything. AFH 1 prefers nondirective counseling for most sessions but identifies directive counseling as best for simple problems, on-the-spot corrections, and clear solutions when time is short.

### Response options

A. Ignore the live error and schedule a nondirective session next month.  
B. Make the on-the-spot directive correction for the clear checklist requirement, then follow with appropriate documentation/plan as needed.  
C. Threaten the Airman with personal humiliation in front of the shift to “motivate” compliance.  
D. Tell the Airman no counseling is allowed for duty performance.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 14.13–14.14 supply timing and directive-approach context for TSgt application. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it applies the directive approach to a simple, time-critical, clear-solution correction. Chapter 1 accountability, courage, and mission/safety stewardship provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: C

C substitutes humiliation for professional correction. Chapter 1 respect and dignity concerns provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** Counseling should be close to the event; the live simple error needs immediate correction.
- **B — Most effective:** Applies directive counseling to the situation AFH 1 describes.
- **C — Least effective:** Abusive humiliation is not professional counseling.
- **D — Ineffective:** Leaders may counsel for duty performance issues.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶14.13–14.14 (counseling application context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Applying directive counseling; courage; respect; accountability  
**ADTC:** 14C, C—Applying  
**Ranks:** TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of on-the-spot correction realism; safety/technical orders control real floor corrections

## C14-SJT-007 — Referral after crisis counseling (TSgt C)

An Airman receives sudden negative personal news at work and is in visible initial shock. After you help the Airman through the immediate moment, the Airman discloses a personal situation that appears beyond what you can resolve alone and may involve legal or support-agency needs. AFH 1 distinguishes crisis counseling from referral counseling that may follow and may involve agencies such as legal services, religious affairs, or an alcohol and drug counselor.

### Response options

A. Mock the Airman for being upset and order an immediate return to full unsupervised duties with no further help.  
B. Complete appropriate crisis support for the initial shock, then use referral counseling pathways through proper agencies/chain as appropriate for the personal situation.  
C. Guarantee a legal outcome yourself even though you are not the responsible agency.  
D. Post the Airman’s private situation to a group chat so “everyone can help.”

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 14.13 supplies crisis and referral counseling context for TSgt application. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it sequences crisis help and proper referral rather than overreaching or exposing private matters. Chapter 1 compassion/respect, loyalty, and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D violates privacy and respect by broadcasting a personal crisis. Chapter 1 respect and loyalty provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** Cruelty and abandonment ignore crisis counseling duties.
- **B — Most effective:** Applies crisis-then-referral logic through proper channels.
- **C — Ineffective:** Leaders must not invent agency authority.
- **D — Least effective:** Public exposure of private distress is harmful and unprofessional.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶14.13 (crisis/referral context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Applying referral counseling; respect; compassion; accountability  
**ADTC:** 14C, C—Applying  
**Ranks:** TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of sensitive-support realism; legal/medical/chaplain/command procedures control real referrals

## C14-SJT-008 — Vision communication

Your flight has a workable improvement vision aligned with current mission needs, but day-to-day execution looks like “the way we have always done it,” and Airmen say they do not know what success looks like. AFH 1 expects lower-level leaders to communicate the vision, bolster courage and understanding, and solicit ideas—without arbitrarily modifying a vision that still works and aligns.

### Response options

A. Keep the vision in your notebook and criticize people for not reading your mind.  
B. Communicate the vision clearly, invite ideas and suggestions, reinforce understanding and initiative within the vision’s guidance, and affirm the vision while it remains aligned and effective.  
C. Change the vision every morning to keep people guessing.  
D. Announce that vision work is only for senior leaders and NCO supervisors should stay silent.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 14.17–14.18 supply vision implementation context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it communicates, engages, and sustains an aligned vision. Chapter 1 loyalty to mission, respect, and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A withholds guidance and then blames followers. Chapter 1 accountability and respect provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** Secret vision cannot guide initiative.
- **B — Most effective:** Matches communicate / bolster / solicit guidance.
- **C — Ineffective:** Arbitrary modification is cautioned against.
- **D — Ineffective:** Lower-level leaders still implement vision with their people.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶14.17–14.18 (vision context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Vision implementation; communication; mission focus  
**ADTC:** 14C, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of change-communication realism

## C14-SJT-009 — Full-range leadership, not laissez-faire

A section you oversee has recurring standard breaches. You have been told “leave them alone unless something explodes.” Standards keep slipping, and Airmen say nobody evaluates or develops them. AFH 1 describes laissez-faire leaders as abandoning responsibilities and remaining indifferent, and presents FRLD as requiring engagement with leader, follower, and situation—including evaluating performance and identifying improvement areas.

### Response options

A. Continue total non-engagement and call it empowerment.  
B. Re-engage: evaluate the situation and performance, clarify standards, use appropriate leadership behaviors, reward or correct accordingly, and identify improvement areas in people and processes.  
C. Wait until a major failure occurs, then only punish without prior guidance.  
D. Fabricate perfect compliance records so outsiders stop asking questions.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 14.24–14.25 supply FRLD engagement and laissez-faire contrast. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it restores responsible engagement across leader–follower–situation. Chapter 1 accountability, courage, and loyalty to standards provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D falsifies compliance. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** Laissez-faire abandonment is the problem, not the cure.
- **B — Most effective:** Applies FRLD engagement expectations.
- **C — Ineffective:** Purely passive waiting mirrors MBE-Passive/laissez-faire harm.
- **D — Least effective:** Record falsification destroys credibility and accountability.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶14.24–14.25 (FRLD context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** FRLD engagement; accountability; integrity  
**ADTC:** 14D, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of supervisory engagement realism

## C14-SJT-010 — Credibility and honest reporting

Your flight’s metrics are below standard. A peer suggests altering figures “just for the briefing” so leadership will stop asking questions, arguing that credibility comes from looking good. AFH 1 ties credibility to being trusted, leading by example, and taking responsibility, and General Wilson’s advice warns against gimmicking reports and figures to look good on paper.

### Response options

A. Alter the figures for the briefing and hope no one notices.  
B. Present accurate data, take responsibility for gaps, and brief a corrective plan with honest status.  
C. Blame an absent Airman for metrics you know were a leadership/process failure.  
D. Refuse to brief at all and ignore tasking without explanation.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 14.22 and 14.26 supply honesty/credibility context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it preserves trust through accurate reporting and ownership. Chapter 1 honesty, courage, and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A gimmicks figures and burns credibility that AFH 1 says can be lost quickly. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** False metrics destroy credibility.
- **B — Most effective:** Honest status plus responsibility and corrective action.
- **C — Ineffective:** Scapegoating violates fairness and honesty.
- **D — Ineffective:** Silent refusal abandons accountability without solving the gap.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶14.22, 14.26 (honesty/credibility context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Credibility; honesty; accountability; courage  
**ADTC:** 14D, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of reporting-integrity realism; command reporting rules control real metrics briefings
