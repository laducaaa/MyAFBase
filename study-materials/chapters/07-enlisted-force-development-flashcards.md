## Section 7A — Leadership Levels

### C07-FC-001
- **Prompt:** Why does AFH 1 say officers, civilians, and enlisted Airmen need a deliberate and common approach to force development?
- **Answer:** Many enlisted Airmen have officers or civilians as direct supervisors, so all three groups need a shared approach to force development, career progression, and assuming increased supervisory and leadership responsibilities, tied to force structure, core values, and foundational and occupational competencies.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7A; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-002
- **Prompt:** What are the three officer tiers, and how do higher tiers relate to lower-tier mastery?
- **Answer:** Company Grade Officer (O-1 thru O-3), Field Grade Officer (O-4 thru O-6), and General Officer (O-7 thru O-10). Progression builds increased leadership and managerial responsibilities and tactical, operational, and strategic competencies. General Officers are expected to have mastered Field Grade responsibilities; Field Grade Officers are expected to have mastered Company Grade responsibilities. Each tier focuses on leadership, professionalism, and mission success.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7A; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-003
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 distinguish functional enlisted development from institutional enlisted development?
- **Answer:** Functionally, the USAF develops technical experts through career field progression and succession planning. Institutionally, it strives to develop experienced leaders. Airmen and supervisors must understand the progression path for technical and professional development, and opportunities should expand as expertise and potential are shown.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7A; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-004
- **Prompt:** What developmental opportunities does AFH 1 cite as offering unique perspectives on how the USAF functions?
- **Answer:** Deployments, exercises, joint assignments, special duty assignments, and headquarters staff assignments.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7A; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-005
- **Prompt:** What does AFH 1 say effective career progression and development depend upon?
- **Answer:** Deliberate conversations between supervisors and subordinates regarding career field and USAF requirements, personal qualifications, and goals.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7A; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-006
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 characterize the scale and variety of USAF Civil Service, and why do civilians need deliberate force development?
- **Answer:** More than 180,000 professional civilians in over 35 countries; within 11 personnel systems there are 22 career fields, 472 occupational series, and 27 different pay plans. Civilians provide corporate knowledge and stability and, like enlisted and officer corps, need a deliberate common approach to force development, career progression, and increased supervisory and leadership responsibilities. **AFH source note:** Civilian counts and structure details are volatile snapshots from the 15 February 2025 edition.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7A; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-007
- **Prompt:** What is the continuum of learning, and what three leadership levels does it produce?
- **Answer:** It is a career-long process combining challenging experiences with education and training through a common taxonomy to produce Airmen with tactical expertise, operational competence, and strategic vision to lead and execute the full spectrum of USAF missions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7A; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-008
- **Prompt:** How do force development and occupational competency development generally differ in outcomes?
- **Answer:** Force development generally results in leadership, management, and warrior ethos proficiency. Occupational competency development generally results in technical skill proficiency. Both align through the continuum of learning and defined competencies so Airmen are qualified and ready for current and future operating environments.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7A; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-009
- **Prompt:** What characterizes development at the tactical expertise leadership level?
- **Answer:** A general understanding of team leadership and appreciation for organization leadership; mastering core duty skills; gaining experience; beginning ethical leadership knowledge; becoming primary technicians and specialists; assimilating into USAF culture and core values; honing followership; motivating subordinates; influencing peers; developing warrior ethos; and exercising communication skills.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7A; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-010
- **Prompt:** What characterizes development at the operational competence leadership level?
- **Answer:** Broader USAF perspective and integrating diverse capabilities in operational execution; transitioning from specialists to leaders who understand themselves as leaders and followers; applying organizational and team dynamics; leading teams by developing and inspiring others and taking care of people; fostering collaborative relationships, building teams and coalitions (especially in large organizations), and negotiating with others, often external to the organization.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7A; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-011
- **Prompt:** At which leadership levels does AFH 1 say the majority of enlisted Airmen operate?
- **Answer:** The tactical expertise and operational competence levels.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7A; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-012
- **Prompt:** What characterizes development at the strategic vision leadership level?
- **Answer:** Combining highly developed personal and people/team institutional competencies with broad organizational competencies; leading exceptionally complex, multi-tiered organizations; understanding synergistic results and effects; employing military capabilities and operational/strategic arts; understanding unit, USAF, joint, and coalition capabilities; using an enterprise perspective for strategic objectives; and focusing on effects across the USAF and DoD.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7A; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-013
- **Prompt:** How do occupational competencies and Airman’s Foundational Competencies differ, and how are foundational competencies grouped?
- **Answer:** Occupational competencies are required within a specific workforce category or specialty and describe technical/functional knowledge, skills, and abilities for that function’s mission. Foundational competencies prepare Airmen across the widest array of USAF tasks, apply across enlisted/officer/civilian members and occupations, and form the force-development framework; they are grouped into Developing Self, Developing Others, Developing Ideas, and Developing Organizations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7A; B—Understanding

## Section 7B — Enlisted Force Structure

### C07-FC-014
- **Prompt:** What need does the enlisted force structure fulfill, and of what three tiers is it comprised?
- **Answer:** It fulfills a compelling need for a deliberate and common approach to force development, career progression, and increased supervisory and leadership responsibilities, providing a framework to meet mission requirements while developing foundational and occupational competencies. It comprises three distinct tiers, each correlating to increased education, training, and experience that build proficiency, leadership, and managerial responsibilities.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7B; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-015
- **Prompt:** Which ranks make up the junior enlisted tier, and what is that tier’s developmental focus?
- **Answer:** Airman Basic, Airman, Airman First Class, and Senior Airman. Initial accessions enter here and focus on adapting to the military, the profession of arms, occupational proficiency, and becoming productive USAF members; they train, qualify, and prepare to operate at home station and in an expeditionary environment and for increased responsibilities.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7B; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-016
- **Prompt:** Through what Airmanship Continuum stages do junior enlisted Airmen progress early in their careers?
- **Answer:** 100 (BMT), 200 (Technical Training), and 300 (First-Term Airmen Center).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7B; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-017
- **Prompt:** What are the written abbreviation and official terms of address for Airman Basic, and what is that grade’s primary focus?
- **Answer:** Abbreviation “AB”; official term of address “Airman Basic” or “Airman.” Airmen Basic (and those who initially enlist as Airman or Airman First Class) primarily adapt to the military profession, acquire knowledge of standards, customs, and courtesies, and strive for occupational proficiency under close supervision.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7B; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-018
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 describe the Airman grade’s expectations and supervision needs?
- **Answer:** Airmen continue learning and adapting, are expected to understand and conform to military standards, customs, and courtesies, begin showing occupational proficiency at basic tasks, and still require significant supervision and support. Abbreviation “Amn”; term of address “Airman.”
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7B; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-019
- **Prompt:** What does AFH 1 expect of an Airman First Class, and which skill level is typically earned at that grade?
- **Answer:** Fully comply with USAF standards; devote time to increasing career-field and military-profession skills while becoming effective team members; continued supervision remains essential. Typically the 5-skill level is earned at this grade. Abbreviation “A1C”; term of address “Airman First Class” or “Airman.”
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7B; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-020
- **Prompt:** What roles do Senior Airmen commonly fill, and what PME must they complete before assuming Staff Sergeant?
- **Answer:** They commonly perform as skilled technicians and trainers, begin developing supervisory and leadership skills through progressive responsibility, individual study, and mentoring, and strive to become effective trainers with guidance from officer and enlisted leaders. They must complete Airman Leadership School before assuming Staff Sergeant. Abbreviation “SrA”; term of address “Senior Airman” or “Airman.”
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7B; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-021
- **Prompt:** Which ranks form the NCO tier, and what dual growth does AFH 1 assign that tier?
- **Answer:** Staff Sergeant and Technical Sergeant. NCOs continue occupational growth and become expert technicians while developing as leaders, supervisors, managers, and mentors; they keep themselves and subordinates trained, qualified, and ready to deploy and operate at home station and in an expeditionary environment, and they internalize foundational competencies while pursuing professional development, including developmental special duty selection and PME.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7B; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-022
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 characterize Staff Sergeants, including typical skill level and resource responsibility?
- **Answer:** Skilled technicians with supervisory and training responsibilities; typically the 7-skill level is earned at this rank; they ensure proper use of resources under their control for effective, efficient mission accomplishment. Abbreviation “SSgt”; term of address “Staff Sergeant” or “Sergeant.”
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7B; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-023
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 characterize Technical Sergeants, and what PME must they complete before Master Sergeant?
- **Answer:** Often a unit’s technical experts who continuously develop as technicians, supervisors, leaders, and mentors through professional development opportunities, including PME. They must complete the NCO Academy before assuming Master Sergeant. Abbreviation “TSgt”; term of address “Technical Sergeant,” “Tech Sergeant,” or “Sergeant.”
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7B; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-024
- **Prompt:** Which ranks form the SNCO tier, and what leadership roles does AFH 1 assign SNCOs?
- **Answer:** Master Sergeant, Senior Master Sergeant, and Chief Master Sergeant. SNCOs serve as leaders in the profession of arms; advise, supervise, mentor, and develop junior enlisted Airmen and NCOs; participate in decision-making on technical, operational, and organizational issues; leverage resources and personnel against mission requirements; and continue professional development through functional opportunities, PME, and possibly sister-service or international SNCO courses.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7B; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-025
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 describe the Master Sergeant transition, and what PME is required before Senior Master Sergeant?
- **Answer:** Master Sergeants are technical experts transitioning from first-line supervisors to leaders of operational competence, with increased responsibilities for leadership, management, and team building; they should consider broadening through developmental special duty selection. They must complete the SNCO Academy before assuming Senior Master Sergeant. Abbreviation “MSgt”; term of address “Master Sergeant” or “Sergeant.”
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7B; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-026
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 describe Senior Master Sergeants?
- **Answer:** Experienced operational leaders skilled at merging team talents, skills, and resources with other organizations. Abbreviation “SMSgt”; term of address “Senior Master Sergeant,” “Senior,” or “Sergeant.”
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7B; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-027
- **Prompt:** What experience, influence, and mentoring charge does AFH 1 assign Chief Master Sergeants?
- **Answer:** They possess substantial operational and occupational experience, hold strategic leadership positions with tremendous influence, and are charged with mentoring and developing junior enlisted personnel and influencing the professional development of company grade officers. Abbreviation “CMSgt”; term of address “Chief Master Sergeant” or “Chief.”
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7B; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-028
- **Prompt:** What Chief Leadership Course attendance rule does AFH 1 state for newly selected chiefs?
- **Answer:** All newly selected RegAF chiefs will attend the Chief Leadership Course. Newly selected Reserve Component chiefs will attend either the Chief Leadership Course or their Chief Orientation Course.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7B; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-029
- **Prompt:** How do the three enlisted tiers relate increased education, training, and experience to responsibility?
- **Answer:** Each tier correlates to increased levels of education, training, and experience that build increasing levels of proficiency, leadership, and managerial responsibilities, within a consistent framework of expectations, standards, and growth opportunities for all Airmen regardless of rank or specialty.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶¶7.4–7.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7B; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-030
- **Prompt:** Where does AFH 1 place the NCO tier’s dual technician-and-leader identity relative to junior enlisted and SNCO roles?
- **Answer:** Junior enlisted focus on adapting, occupational proficiency, and preparing for increased responsibility; NCOs continue occupational growth as expert technicians while developing as leaders, supervisors, managers, and mentors and keeping forces ready; SNCOs serve as leaders who advise, supervise, mentor, and participate in broader decision-making.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶¶7.5–7.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7B; B—Understanding


## Section 7C — Duty Titles and Special Positions

### C07-FC-031
- **Prompt:** What purpose do properly applied enlisted duty titles serve, and on what basis are they assigned?
- **Answer:** They facilitate a quick understanding of a person’s role and level of responsibility. Enlisted duty titles are assigned based upon the scope of responsibility and the duties being performed; AFH 1 lists the official, authorized duty titles for the enlisted force.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7C; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-032
- **Prompt:** Who may hold the duty title Supervisor, and what restriction applies to junior enlisted Airmen?
- **Answer:** The title is used for NCOs and some Senior Airmen who are first-line supervisors (such as heavy equipment supervisors and shift supervisors). Junior enlisted Airmen will not have the duty title of supervisor unless they are at least an Airman Leadership School graduate and supervise the work of others.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7C; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-033
- **Prompt:** When is the title Noncommissioned Officer in Charge (NCOIC) used?
- **Answer:** Only for NCOs and SNCOs in charge of a work center or element (typically with subordinate supervisors), and also for those whose primary duty is a unit-wide program or management function even if they do not directly supervise personnel.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7C; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-034
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define Section Chief and Flight Chief titles?
- **Answer:** Section Chief is used for NCOs and SNCOs in charge of a section with at least two subordinate work centers or elements; section chiefs are typically SNCOs, with rank varying by section size. Flight Chief is used for NCOs and SNCOs who are the enlisted leaders of a flight; flight chiefs are typically SNCOs, with rank varying by flight size.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7C; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-035
- **Prompt:** How do the duty titles Manager and Chief differ in AFH 1’s enlisted duty-title framework?
- **Answer:** Manager is used for NCOs and SNCOs who are program, project, and policy managers at designated USAF levels (in addition to special SNCO positions of USAF career field managers and major command functional managers); they may or may not have personnel working for them and may be enlisted leaders of a branch, division, or directorate. Chief is used for Chief Master Sergeants who are program, project, or policy managers at designated USAF levels, with the same personnel and enlisted-leader possibilities.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7C; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-036
- **Prompt:** What precedence and advisory roles does AFH 1 assign the Chief Master Sergeant of the Air Force?
- **Answer:** The CMSAF is the senior enlisted leader of the USAF and takes precedence over all USAF enlisted members; provides leadership to the enlisted force; and advises the CSAF, SECAF, CJCS, and Secretary of Defense on enlisted matters for Airmen and their families.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7C; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-037
- **Prompt:** Beyond advising senior leaders, what additional CMSAF responsibilities does AFH 1 list?
- **Answer:** Communicates with the force; serves on boards and committees for organizations affecting Airmen; testifies before Congress; is the USAF career field manager for command chief master sergeants and group superintendents; consults with sister-service senior enlisted advisors; engages foreign military leaders on theater security cooperation and partner-nation development; represents the USAF to the American public, professional organizations, and media; and manages the Air Force Order of the Sword Program.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7C; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-038
- **Prompt:** What creation timeline does AFH 1 give for the CMSAF position, and who was the first CMSAF?
- **Answer:** Proposed by the Air Force Association’s Airman Advisory Council in 1964; Congressman Mendel Rivers introduced a 1966 bill that never passed; in October 1966 CSAF General John P. McConnell announced the newly created CMSAF position; in April 1967 Chief Paul W. Airey became the first to wear the unique CMSAF insignia with the wreath around the star.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7C; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-039
- **Prompt:** Where does the title Command Chief Master Sergeant traditionally apply, and what general advisory responsibilities does the CCM hold?
- **Answer:** Senior enlisted leader at a designated USAF level—traditionally at wings, centers, numbered air forces, and major commands. The CCM provides general supervision to the command’s enlisted force and advises commanders and staff on mission effectiveness, professional development, recognition, key enlisted nominations and hires, accelerated promotions, performance evaluations, military readiness, training, utilization, health, morale, and welfare of the organization’s enlisted force, and acts on shortfalls or challenges.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7C; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-040
- **Prompt:** What RegAF enlisted force distribution panel and functional-manager roles does AFH 1 assign command chiefs?
- **Answer:** RegAF CCMs serve on the enlisted force distribution panel by advising their senior rater and panel membership on enlisted Airmen’s potential to serve in the next higher grade; assist and advise in selection and nomination for positions of greater responsibility, including developmental special duties; and are the functional manager for subordinate units’ senior enlisted leaders and first sergeants.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7C; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-041
- **Prompt:** When may the titles SEA, SEL, and CEM be used, and how do joint SEA/SEL titles relate to command chief roles?
- **Answer:** SEA, SEL, and CEM are only used when holding a designated and approved position. The COCOM or JTF equivalent to a command chief is a CCMD or joint task force senior enlisted leader. Approved SEA and SEL titles are often used in joint and coalition structures and are not always synonymous with a command chief master sergeant’s role.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7C; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-042
- **Prompt:** At what organizational levels is the title Senior Enlisted Leader used, and what mentorship and readiness roles does AFH 1 assign SELs?
- **Answer:** For SNCOs in charge of squadron, group, and headquarters levels. They provide mentorship, guidance, experience, and expertise in organizing, equipping, training, and mobilizing the organization’s enlisted force for home-station and expeditionary mission requirements, and work with other enlisted leaders on readiness, training, health, morale, welfare, and quality of life.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7C; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-043
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 characterize the First Sergeant position?
- **Answer:** A key leader serving in a time-honored career-broadening position rich in custom and tradition; First Sergeants epitomize the highest qualities of Air Force SNCOs and must remain perceptive and credible and exemplify USAF core values.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7C; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-044
- **Prompt:** What organizing and career-path responsibilities does AFH 1 assign Enlisted Air Force Career Field Managers?
- **Answer:** Typically Chief Master Sergeants at HAF who organize and manage one or more enlisted career fields; establish entry requirements; manage trained personnel requirements; develop and manage career-long training plan requirements and programs; construct viable career paths; evaluate training effectiveness; monitor health and manning; provide input on manning and personnel policies; visit Airmen including GSUs; and, through enlisted development teams, execute progression and succession planning.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7C; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-045
- **Prompt:** How do Major Command Functional Managers relate to AFCFMs?
- **Answer:** Enlisted MFMs are SNCOs who manage designated enlisted career fields and serve as major command liaisons for their respective AFCFMs; they visit organizations, monitor career-field health and manning within the command, elevate concerns to AFCFMs, manage command training, coordinate training and personnel issues across MAJCOM staff and with AFCFMs, disseminate policies, and coordinate with AFPC (RegAF only) through their MAJCOM on prioritization of allocated and assigned personnel resources.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7C; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-046
- **Prompt:** Why must supervisors apply official enlisted duty titles carefully rather than invent informal labels?
- **Answer:** When properly applied, official titles facilitate quick understanding of role and responsibility level; AFH 1 identifies specific authorized titles (Supervisor, NCOIC, Section Chief, Flight Chief, Manager, Chief) assigned by scope of responsibility and duties performed.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7C; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-047
- **Prompt:** How do CMSAF, CCM, and SEL roles differ in organizational span according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** CMSAF is the USAF senior enlisted leader with Service-wide precedence and senior-leader advisory duties; CCM is the senior enlisted leader at designated levels traditionally including wing, center, NAF, and MAJCOM; SEL is used for SNCOs in charge at squadron, group, and headquarters levels as key advisors in every command team and unit.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7C; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-048
- **Prompt:** Where does AFH 1 direct readers for additional special duty positions beyond those described in Section 7C?
- **Answer:** The Air Force Enlisted Classification Directory.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7C; B—Understanding

## Section 7D — Force Development

### C07-FC-049
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define enlisted force development’s life-cycle approach?
- **Answer:** It combines training, education, and experience to develop innovative Airmen prepared to accomplish the USAF mission and lead in a rapidly evolving global environment. Deliberate development uses education on foundational competencies, training on occupational competencies, varied experiential opportunities, and additional force-development tools for continued growth meeting personal and professional needs.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7D; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-050
- **Prompt:** What purpose do enlisted development teams serve for career field managers?
- **Answer:** They leverage development of qualified Airmen with leadership capacity and acculturation to support current and projected mission requirements. Career field managers use them for progression and succession planning to place Airmen in key leadership and developmental positions based on defined education, training, experience, and performance requirements—“the right people in the right place at the right time.”
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7D; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-051
- **Prompt:** What charge and membership does AFH 1 assign the Enlisted Force Development Panel?
- **Answer:** Responsible for guiding enlisted force development initiatives; reviews, evaluates, and recommends to senior USAF leaders on education, training, and experiences impacting enlisted development. Members include command chiefs, career field managers, and representatives from AETC, AFPC, and HAF.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7D; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-052
- **Prompt:** What responsibilities does AFH 1 assign the Air Force Senior Enlisted Leadership Council?
- **Answer:** Addresses deliberate development initiatives for enhanced performance; comprised of CMSgts in strategic/joint/combined commands, MAJCOM command chiefs, other senior enlisted leaders, and selected advisors; aims to elevate enlisted caliber and performance, develop solutions to high-priority Airmen and family issues, and counsel and advise SECAF, CSAF, and senior USAF commanders and leaders.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7D; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-053
- **Prompt:** Who comprises the Air Force Enlisted Council, and what ambassador roles does it serve?
- **Answer:** The current 12 Outstanding Airmen of the Year. With AFSELC mentorship and oversight, it serves as enlisted ambassadors for USAF enlisted recruiting and retention and engages with the Air Force Association, enlisted PME, and various enlisted USAF organizations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7D; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-054
- **Prompt:** What is MyVECTOR, and what mentoring capabilities does AFH 1 highlight?
- **Answer:** A Total Force, experience-tracking enterprise solution supporting USAF development priorities; a web-based career planning and force-development tool giving individuals greater transparency into development and career management. It supports CFMs and development teams and offers mentoring via traditional by-name mentor requests and mentor-matching on weighted characteristics, plus a real-time mentoring plan, discussion forums, a bullet tracker, and online mentor dialogue.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7D; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-055
- **Prompt:** What is the Developmental Special Experience Catalog in MyVECTOR, and how does it differ from assignment systems?
- **Answer:** DSEs provide hands-on experiences typically outside AFSC or career-series formal training to gain or enhance competencies. Unlike assignment systems, the Catalog lists all DSEs regardless of vacancies or application windows so users know all requirements or prerequisites.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7D; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-056
- **Prompt:** What self-assessment and 360-degree feedback features does MyVECTOR offer for foundational competencies?
- **Answer:** A developmental self-assessment identifying personal strengths and improvement areas among USAF Foundational Competencies, with immediate feedback recommending online resources; members may also request confidential feedback from supervisors and/or subordinates, peers, or higher-ranking members (360-degree feedback), and can access a searchable library of competency-linked resources at any time.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7D; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-057
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 connect talent management to warfighting effectiveness?

- **Prompt:** What three enlisted off-duty education funding programs does AFH 1 identify?
- **Answer:** Military Tuition Assistance, Montgomery GI Bill, and Post-9/11 GI Bill, offered under the Voluntary Education Program supporting high-quality force, professional and personal development, recruitment, retention, and readiness goals.

### C07-FC-114
- **Prompt:** What does military tuition assistance pay for, and what costs does AFH 1 say it cannot cover?
- **Answer:** Pays tuition cost at regionally and nationally accredited institutions not to exceed hourly and annual caps in DoDI 1322.25 and DAFI 36-2670. Cannot be used to purchase textbooks, e-books, reference/instructional materials, electronic equipment/supplies, certificate/license examinations, or fees, regardless of institutional reimbursement policy. Students whose tuition exceeds the semester-hour cap may use MGIB or Post-9/11 top-up for the remaining portion.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.26
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7H; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-115
- **Prompt:** What key Montgomery GI Bill participation and use facts does AFH 1 state?
- **Answer:** Eligible individuals who entered service for the first time on or after 1 July 1985 are enrolled; pay is reduced by $100 a month for 12 months following the election window; participants may elect an additional $600 contribution adding a maximum of $5,400 to the benefit; in-service use permitted after two years continuous RegAF status; benefits expire 10 years after separation or retirement; total benefit adjusts yearly with the cost-of-living index. **AFH source note:** Benefit amounts and rules are volatile; verify current VA/DoD guidance for duty use.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.26
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7H; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-116
- **Prompt:** What distinctive Post-9/11 GI Bill features and caution does AFH 1 emphasize?
- **Answer:** Eligible individuals on RegAF status on or after 11 September 2001 may choose to enroll; allows transfer of GI Bill benefits to dependents with sufficient retainability; details differ from the Montgomery GI Bill; the decision to move to this program is irrevocable, so Airmen are recommended to get full details from the VA before converting.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.26
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7H; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-117
- **Prompt:** How do CLEP and DSST credit-by-examination options differ in AFH 1’s description?
- **Answer:** Both are no-cost DANTES-managed options helping members earn college credit for prior knowledge, with scores documented on JST and CCAF transcripts. DSST: 38 subject exams across disciplines such as Business, Humanities, Mathematics, and Physical Science. CLEP: 34 college exams across Composition and Literature, World Languages, History and Social Sciences, Science and Mathematics, and Business; passing typically earns 3–12 recommended lower-level credits depending on the exam. Credits applied depend on accepting-institution policies. **AFH source note:** Exam counts are source snapshots.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.27
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7H; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-118
- **Prompt:** What degree requirement applies to Officer Training School commissioning eligibility for military members in AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Possess a baccalaureate or higher degree from an accredited college or university; DAFMAN 36-2032 contains specific guidance, and base education services can assist.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.28
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7H; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-119
- **Prompt:** How do LEAD and SOAR commissioning paths differ according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** LEAD delegates authority to unit and wing commanders to nominate highly qualified Airmen for USAF Academy attendance (possibly direct Academy entry, Preparatory School, or referral to other programs). SOAR allocates ROTC scholarship quotas; if selected, Airmen separate from RegAF, join a ROTC detachment as full-time students, receive up to $18,000/year tuition and fees plus textbook allowance and monthly nontaxable stipend for two to four years, then commission as Second Lieutenants and typically return to RegAF within 60 days for at least four years. **AFH source note:** Scholarship amounts are volatile snapshots.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.28
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7H; B—Understanding

### C07-FC-120
- **Prompt:** What enlisted commissioning/medical pathways does AFH 1 summarize beyond OTS, LEAD, SOAR, and ASCP?
- **Answer:** Nurse Enlisted Commissioning Program (up to 24 months upper-division nursing baccalaureate study via ROTC detachment assignment; must pass NCLEX-RN before OTS); Enlisted to Medical Degree Preparatory Program (24-month USUHS preparatory coursework while remaining RegAF); and Interservice Physician Assistant Program (29-month master’s pathway with University of Nebraska Medical Center while remaining RegAF; must pass the PA National Certifying Exam). **AFH source note:** Durations and program details are volatile snapshots.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶7.28
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 7H; B—Understanding
