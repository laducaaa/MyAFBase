# Chapter 15 Situational Judgment Practice

> **Status:** Draft and provisional for 27E5/27E6. Chapter 15 is shared core in the historical 26E5/26E6 catalog scopes.
>
> These scenarios are wholly original. They are not actual, recalled, reconstructed, or predicted PFE items. The official Air Force uses subject-matter experts and behavioral scientists to establish operational SJT scoring keys. Chapter 15 describes accountability and communication facts—self-management, military/written/spoken communication, preparation, and electronic messaging—but those facts alone do not establish an official behavioral scoring key. All most-effective and least-effective keys below are MyPromotion educational judgments grounded in Chapter 1 professionalism, core values, and ethical reasoning, with Chapter 15 material supplying context only. They are not official scoring keys or substitutes for current command, supervisory, PA, OPSEC, Privacy Act, legal, ethics, or reporting procedures.
>
> **ADTC note:** Sections 15A–15E are B—Understanding for SSgt and TSgt. Section 15F is B for SSgt and C—Applying for TSgt. Scenario C15-SJT-010 carries the TSgt C messaging overlay; other scenarios use shared B context for both ranks.
>
> **Two-track source rule:** For exam-source study, preserve the facts in the AFH edition assigned by the controlling catalog. For duty use, separately verify current messaging, PA, Privacy Act, OPSEC, and acceptable-use rules; duty-current information does not overwrite a catalog-controlled answer.

## How to use this set

Each scenario produces two items:

1. Select the **most effective** response.
2. Select the **least effective** response.

Read every option before choosing. Evaluate whether the response:

- represents accountability and communication facts honestly;
- uses authority without coercion or status misuse;
- protects OPSEC, Privacy Act, and messaging integrity;
- stays within stated role and delegated authority;
- balances candor with courtesy and mission needs;
- respects people, process owners, and the chain of command; and
- reflects integrity, accountability, loyalty, respect, and mission stewardship.

Chapter 15 supplies communication and self-development context; it does not prescribe the behavioral sequence in every response. When a rationale depends on general professional judgment—such as honesty, courage, accountability, loyalty, respect, mission focus, or teamwork—it is identified as an application of the cited foundational Chapter 1 paragraphs.

Ten scenarios produce 20 response selections, mirroring the historical public structure announced for the [22E5/22E6 cycles in December 2021](https://www.afpc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/2874484/waps-adjusts-for-22e6-22e5-testing-cycles/). The separate [public SJT informational guide](https://www.studyguides.af.mil/Portals/15/documents/Notices/Situational-Judgment-Test-Informational-Guide.pdf) explains the four-option most/least format. The 2027 structure is unconfirmed. This set covers only selected Chapter 15 concepts and is not a full-spectrum SJT simulation.

## C15-SJT-001 — Accountability when peers cheat

You discover that a friend in a nuclear weapons specialty has been cheating on exams needed to maintain weapons system certification. Your friend asks you to stay quiet. A co-worker says, “Reporting will ruin a career—just look the other way.” AFH 1 frames accountability as reliability, honesty, and responsibility for self and team, and uses similar scenarios to highlight significance, self-efficacy, and autonomy.

### Response options

A. Ignore the cheating because friendship outranks certification integrity.  
B. Report through proper channels and, where appropriate, use your skills to help address the underlying competence problem without enabling the cheat.  
C. Join the cheating scheme so you are not left out.  
D. Threaten to expose the friend publicly on social media for leverage.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 15.1 supplies accountability context (significance, self-efficacy, autonomy). B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it takes responsibility for safety/competence risk rather than enabling misconduct. Chapter 1 integrity, courage, and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: C

C participates in the misconduct. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It abandons accountability for peer loyalty.
- **B — Most effective:** It reports properly and uses competence constructively without enabling cheating.
- **C — Least effective:** It joins the violation.
- **D — Partially effective impulse, ineffective execution:** Exposure desire without proper channels becomes coercion/service-discrediting risk.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶15.1 (accountability context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Accountability; honesty; courage; autonomy  
**ADTC:** 15A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of reporting realism; current security, IG, and command channels control real cases

## C15-SJT-002 — Growth mindset under setback

After a failed certification attempt, a subordinate says, “I’m just not a smart person—effort is pointless.” AFH 1 contrasts fixed and growth mindsets and lists growth behaviors: persist, embrace challenges, value effort, seek feedback, and learn from others’ success.

### Response options

A. Agree that ability is permanently fixed and tell them to stop trying.  
B. Help them reframe the setback as a learning challenge, build a practice plan, and seek feedback on weak areas.  
C. Mock their failure in front of peers to “motivate” them.  
D. Promise to take the retest for them so they avoid effort.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 15.2 supplies growth-mindset context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it converts a fixed-mindset claim into persistence, effort, and feedback. Chapter 1 respect and developing others’ related accountability support mentoring without humiliation.

### Least effective: D

D substitutes fraud/avoidance for growth. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It reinforces fixed mindset.
- **B — Most effective:** It applies growth-mindset behaviors to a concrete plan.
- **C — Ineffective:** Humiliation contradicts respect and useful feedback.
- **D — Least effective:** It replaces honest effort with deceit.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶15.2 (perseverance/mindset context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Growth mindset; perseverance; respect; honesty  
**ADTC:** 15A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of mentoring realism

## C15-SJT-003 — Social media and OPSEC

A peer drafts a public social post that includes controlled unclassified details from an unofficial work chat and jokes that degrade a unit’s discipline. They ask you to “just hit share—it’s not classified.” AFH 1 holds individuals responsible for posts, prohibits service-discrediting/morale-degrading commentary, and bars posting CUI/official DoD information on social networking without proper authority.

### Response options

A. Share the post immediately to show loyalty.  
B. Stop the post, explain the OPSEC and service-discrediting risks, and help remove official/CUI content before any release; escalate if needed.  
C. Copy the CUI into a non-DoD e-mail chain for “safer” distribution.  
D. Tell them social media has no rules for Airmen.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 15.9 supplies social-media/OPSEC context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it prevents unauthorized disclosure and discrediting content. Chapter 1 integrity, loyalty to the institution, and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: C

C moves official/CUI information into a non-DoD e-mail channel without proper authority. Chapter 1 integrity/accountability and ¶15.9 restrictions provide the least-effective basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** Sharing amplifies the violation.
- **B — Most effective:** It stops harm and corrects content through responsible action.
- **C — Least effective:** It relocates the OPSEC problem into another prohibited channel.
- **D — Ineffective:** It invents a no-rules claim AFH 1 contradicts.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶15.9 (social media/OPSEC context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** OPSEC; social media responsibility; honesty; loyalty  
**ADTC:** 15B, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of OPSEC realism; current PA/OPSEC directives control real cases

## C15-SJT-004 — Preparing a contested message with FOCUS

Your flight needs a short update on a contentious schedule change. A co-worker wants to send a long, unsupported rant that strays into unrelated grievances. AFH 1’s FOCUS principles require focused, organized, clear, audience-understanding, and supported communication, and the seven steps include analyzing purpose/audience and fighting for feedback.

### Response options

A. Send the rant as-is to “get everything off your chest.”  
B. Define purpose and audience, organize a clear supported message on the schedule issue only, and seek quick feedback before release.  
C. Fabricate statistics to make the rant look supported.  
D. Refuse to communicate anything because contention is uncomfortable.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 15.14–15.15 supply FOCUS and seven-step context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it keeps the issue focused and supported. Chapter 1 disciplined communication and honesty provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: C

C uses distorted/false support. Chapter 1 honesty provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It violates focus, clarity, and support.
- **B — Most effective:** It applies FOCUS and feedback discipline.
- **C — Least effective:** It corrupts support with false data.
- **D — Partially effective caution, ineffective extremity:** Avoiding needed communication abandons leadership intent.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶15.14–15.15 (preparation context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** FOCUS; preparation; honesty; leadership communication  
**ADTC:** 15C, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of staffing realism

## C15-SJT-005 — Choosing the right written product

A commander needs a quick single-issue cue for an unexpected hallway question. A peer insists on a multi-page staff study with full recommendations before anyone speaks. AFH 1 distinguishes point papers (memory jogger/quick extemporaneous informing) from staff studies (problem analysis with conclusions/recommendations).

### Response options

A. Force a full staff study before any hallway answer, even when only a memory jogger is needed.  
B. Prepare a point paper for the single-issue quick cue, and start a staff study only if deeper analysis/recommendations are later required.  
C. Invent answers on the spot with no product and no follow-up when facts are unknown.  
D. Send a personal condolence letter format as the staff answer.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 15.21–15.22 supply paper-type context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it matches format to task. Chapter 1 accountability and mission focus support right-sized communication.

### Least effective: D

D uses an inappropriate personal-letter format for staff issue support. Chapter 15 format purpose and Chapter 1 professionalism provide the least-effective basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Partially effective thoroughness, ineffective mismatch:** Staff studies matter for analysis, not every extemporaneous cue.
- **B — Most effective:** Matches point-paper function to the need.
- **C — Ineffective:** Guessing without follow-up contradicts briefing honesty norms as well.
- **D — Least effective:** Wrong written platform entirely.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶15.21–15.22 (written products context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Written format selection; mission focus; honesty  
**ADTC:** 15D, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of staff-product realism; DAFH 33-337 controls real formats

## C15-SJT-006 — Briefing integrity when you do not know

During a mission briefing, a senior asks a detailed question you cannot answer. A peer whispers, “Just wing it—sound confident.” AFH 1 says briefers should be accurate and brief, anticipate questions, and if unable to answer, request time to research and follow up rather than answer off-the-cuff.

### Response options

A. Invent a confident answer to protect the flight’s image.  
B. State that you do not have the answer, commit to research, and follow up with accurate information.  
C. Blame a junior Airman publicly for your knowledge gap.  
D. End the briefing abruptly without addressing the question or follow-up.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 15.26 supplies briefing context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it preserves accuracy over false confidence. Chapter 1 honesty and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A knowingly fabricates. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** Off-the-cuff fabrication contradicts briefing accuracy.
- **B — Most effective:** Honest gap acknowledgment with follow-up.
- **C — Ineffective:** Deflection harms respect and accountability.
- **D — Ineffective:** Avoidance leaves decision-makers without a path to the answer.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶15.26 (briefing context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Briefing accuracy; honesty; accountability  
**ADTC:** 15E, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of briefing realism

## C15-SJT-007 — Empathic listening in counseling

An Airman comes to you visibly upset about a family emergency affecting duty focus. A co-worker says to “just grade their feelings and move on to taskers.” AFH 1 describes empathic listening as useful when communication is emotional or the relationship is as important as the message—often appropriate in mentoring/counseling—before purely informational or critical listening.

### Response options

A. Immediately criticize their emotions as weakness and assign extra duties.  
B. Use empathic listening first to understand and stabilize the conversation, then address duty impacts and available support channels.  
C. Record the conversation secretly to use later in a performance attack.  
D. Tell them to post personal medical details on the unit social page for sympathy.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 15.31 supplies listening-type context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it sequences empathic listening before task problem-solving. Chapter 1 respect and loyalty support care without abandoning mission stewardship.

### Least effective: C

C weaponizes a vulnerable disclosure. Chapter 1 respect and integrity provide the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** Pure criticism skips empathic listening and harms trust.
- **B — Most effective:** Matches empathic-then-problem-solving posture.
- **C — Least effective:** Exploitative and trust-destroying.
- **D — Ineffective:** Pushes Privacy Act/personal information into an inappropriate public channel.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶15.31 (listening context); ¶¶1.3.2–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Empathic listening; respect; privacy; loyalty  
**ADTC:** 15E, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of counseling realism; current helping-agency and privacy rules control real cases

## C15-SJT-008 — Self-control when angry

After a disrespectful exchange, you feel the urge to yell and curse in the open bay. AFH 1 teaches that self-control is limited like a muscle and that changing the situation (removing yourself from triggers) is often more effective than relying only on internal willpower in the moment.

### Response options

A. Stay in the bay and escalate with yelling and curses to “win.”  
B. Remove yourself from the trigger, regain composure, then return to address the issue courteously and directly.  
C. Send an ALL-CAPS abusive e-mail to the entire unit distribution list.  
D. Pretend nothing happened and never set expectations afterward.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 15.3 and 15.27 supply self-control and courtesy context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it uses situational control and then professional communication. Chapter 1 self-control, respect, and courage provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: C

C converts anger into abusive broadcast messaging. Chapter 1 respect/integrity and ¶15.32 tone rules provide the least-effective basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** Escalation abandons self-control.
- **B — Most effective:** Changes the situation, then communicates professionally.
- **C — Least effective:** Abusive wide e-mail compounds the failure.
- **D — Partially effective de-escalation, ineffective follow-through:** Avoiding needed correction leaves the standard unaddressed.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶15.3, 15.27, 15.32 (self-control/courtesy/tone context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Self-control; courtesy; respect  
**ADTC:** 15A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of workplace-conflict realism

## C15-SJT-009 — Meeting vs e-mail for a decision

You need cross-talk among three sections to solve a recurring scheduling conflict. A peer wants only a one-way e-mail announcement with no agenda or minutes. AFH 1 says meetings fit information/advice/concerns/problem-solving/decision-making that needs dialogue, and successful meetings use purpose, invitees, agenda, and well-formatted minutes.

### Response options

A. Skip dialogue; blast a vague e-mail with no agenda and no follow-up record.  
B. Schedule a short meeting with a clear purpose, right attendees, agenda in advance, and minutes afterward.  
C. Invite the entire base to an untimed meeting with no purpose.  
D. Make the decision alone while falsely claiming all sections concurred.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 15.17 supplies meeting context. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it matches channel to dialogue needs and uses basic meeting discipline. Chapter 1 teamwork, honesty, and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D fabricates concurrence. Chapter 1 honesty provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** One-way e-mail loses needed cross-talk and record quality.
- **B — Most effective:** Right channel plus agenda/minutes discipline.
- **C — Ineffective:** Oversized, purposeless meeting wastes time.
- **D — Least effective:** False concurrence is dishonest.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶15.17 (meetings context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Meeting discipline; honesty; teamwork  
**ADTC:** 15C, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of meeting realism

## C15-SJT-010 — Applying electronic-messaging rules (TSgt C)

You are a Technical Sergeant. A Staff Sergeant drafts an e-mail that (1) reply-alls a heated argument in ALL CAPS, (2) attaches nothing though the body says “see attached Privacy Act roster,” and (3) proposes auto-forwarding the thread from .mil to a commercial ISP “so people on leave can see it.” AFH 1’s e-mail rules require professional tone, sparing reply-all, checking attachments, careful handling of personal/Privacy Act information, and prohibit .mil-to-commercial auto-forward.

### Response options

A. Approve the draft as written to avoid conflict.  
B. Stop the send; require a professional directed reply (not ALL-CAPS reply-all), verify official need/authorized recipients and proper markings before any personal information is transmitted, ensure the attachment is correct, and prohibit .mil-to-commercial auto-forward.  
C. Tell them to post the Privacy Act roster on a public commercial social site instead.  
D. Order them to leave the workstation unlocked so others can “finish the e-mail later.”

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 15.32–15.33 supply electronic-messaging application context for TSgt-level use. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it applies tone, distribution, Privacy Act, attachment, and auto-forward rules together. Chapter 1 integrity, accountability, and respect provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: C

C moves Privacy Act/personal information onto a prohibited public commercial channel. Chapter 1 integrity and ¶¶15.33–15.34 restrictions provide the least-effective basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** Approving compounds multiple rule violations.
- **B — Most effective:** Applies 15F controls to the concrete situation.
- **C — Least effective:** Escalates Privacy Act exposure publicly.
- **D — Ineffective:** Violates keeping e-mail/workstations under control.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶15.32–15.34 (electronic messaging application context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Applying e-mail etiquette; Privacy Act caution; auto-forward prohibition; accountability  
**ADTC:** 15F, C—Applying  
**Ranks:** TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of TSgt supervisory realism; current acceptable-use, Privacy Act, and OPSEC directives control real messaging practice
