# Chapter 18 Situational Judgment Practice

> **Status:** Draft and provisional for 27E5/27E6. Chapter 18 is shared core in the historical 26E5/26E6 catalog scopes.
>
> These scenarios are wholly original. They are not actual, recalled, reconstructed, or predicted PFE items. The official Air Force uses subject-matter experts and behavioral scientists to establish operational SJT scoring keys. Chapter 18 describes security, OPSEC, information protection, cyber/access, and antiterrorism facts, but those facts alone do not establish an official behavioral scoring key. All most-effective and least-effective keys below are MyPromotion educational judgments grounded in Chapter 1 professionalism, core values, and ethical reasoning, with Chapter 18 material supplying context only. They are not official scoring keys or substitutes for current command, security, cyber, AFOSI, legal, ethics, or reporting procedures.
>
> **Not security advice / classified-content boundary:** Scenarios use unclassified AFH 1 security facts as context only. They do not invent classified content, classification markings for real documents, or operational threat details beyond instructional constraints stated in the scenario.
>
> **Two-track source rule:** For exam-source study, preserve the facts in the AFH edition assigned by the controlling catalog. For duty use, separately verify current security, cyber, Privacy Act/FOIA, and antiterrorism rules; duty-current information does not overwrite a catalog-controlled answer.

## How to use this set

Each scenario produces two items:

1. Select the **most effective** response.
2. Select the **least effective** response.

Read every option before choosing. Evaluate whether the response:

- represents security and source facts honestly;
- protects classified information, CUI, PII, and credentials;
- reports incidents and contacts through proper channels without delay or concealment;
- stays within stated role and delegated authority;
- uses the two-track caution when source snapshots may differ from current rules; and
- reflects integrity, accountability, loyalty, respect, and mission stewardship.

Chapter 18 supplies security context; it does not prescribe the behavioral sequence in every response. When a rationale depends on general professional judgment—such as honesty, courage, accountability, loyalty, respect, mission focus, or teamwork—it is identified as an application of the cited foundational Chapter 1 paragraphs.

Ten scenarios produce 20 response selections, mirroring the historical public structure announced for the [22E5/22E6 cycles in December 2021](https://www.afpc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/2874484/waps-adjusts-for-22e6-22e5-testing-cycles/). The separate [public SJT informational guide](https://www.studyguides.af.mil/Portals/15/documents/Notices/Situational-Judgment-Test-Informational-Guide.pdf) explains the four-option most/least format. The 2027 structure is unconfirmed. This set covers only selected Chapter 18 concepts and is not a full-spectrum SJT simulation.

## C18-SJT-001 — Integrated defense honesty in a briefing

You are reviewing an unclassified newcomer briefing. A draft slide states the base fence line “is always identical to the base boundary and base security zone, so standoff risk outside the fence is never the installation commander’s concern.” AFH 1 distinguishes perimeter, boundary, and security zone and assigns the commander mitigation-or-acceptance responsibility when the boundary does not include all security-zone terrain. You may correct one slide before the briefing.

### Response options

A. Keep the slide because newcomers find a single “fence equals everything” line easier to remember.  
B. Replace the slide with AFH 1’s distinctions: perimeter as physical boundary; boundary as a coordination line that may not match the fence; security zone as the USAF area of concern outside the perimeter; and commander mitigate-or-accept responsibility when the boundary omits security-zone terrain.  
C. Delete every boundary concept and tell newcomers security is “someone else’s job.”  
D. Add invented classified standoff ranges to “make the slide more operational.”

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 18.2 supplies boundary/security-zone context; it does not score the briefing-editor behavior. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it uses stated authority to keep the briefing accurate to the source. Chapter 1 honesty and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D invents classified operational detail the scenario does not authorize and violates the unclassified-content boundary. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It preserves a known false simplification.
- **B — Most effective:** It corrects to source distinctions and commander responsibility.
- **C — Ineffective:** It abandons required understanding and All Airmen ownership.
- **D — Least effective:** It invents classified content.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶18.2 (perimeter/boundary/security zone context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Source-faithful teaching; no invented classified content; integrity  
**ADTC:** 18A, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Human review of briefing realism; local defense plans control real training products

## C18-SJT-002 — OPSEC indicator silence

During an unclassified unit social-media planning huddle, a coworker proposes posting exact deployment bank times, gate patterns, and “we always leave Friday at 0300” routines because “transparency builds morale.” AFH 1 defines OPSEC indicators as friendly detectable actions and open-source information that adversaries can piece together, and describes exposure and profiles as valuable to adversaries. You are asked to approve the post wording.

### Response options

A. Approve the post as written to avoid conflict.  
B. Strip the routine/pattern details, explain OPSEC indicator risk in unclassified terms, and route a sanitized morale message through the proper public-affairs/OPSEC review path stated in your unit process.  
C. Secretly message the adversary-facing details to a personal blog “for historians.”  
D. Demand the coworker be publicly shamed on the unit page without using any review channel.

### Most effective: B

Paragraphs 18.7–18.8 supply OPSEC context; they do not score the approval behavior. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it protects critical-information indicators while still supporting legitimate communication through proper channels. Chapter 1 integrity and loyalty to the mission provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: C

C knowingly publishes pattern data outside official channels. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It approves predictable exposure.
- **B — Most effective:** It sanitizes and uses proper review.
- **C — Least effective:** It deliberately leaks indicator-rich details.
- **D — Ineffective:** It substitutes humiliation for process.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶¶18.7–18.8 (OPSEC/indicators context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** OPSEC discipline; honesty; mission loyalty  
**ADTC:** 18B, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Public affairs/OPSEC offices control real release decisions

## C18-SJT-003 — Classified material found out of control

You find an unmarked folder in a common area that clearly contains classified cover-sheet material (SF 704 visible on a document partially sticking out). AFH 1 says anyone finding classified material out of proper control must take custody, safeguard it, and immediately notify the commander, supervisor, or security manager. A peer jokes, “Just leave it—someone will come back,” and walks away.

### Response options

A. Leave the material where it is to avoid “getting involved.”  
B. Take custody, safeguard it from further exposure, and immediately notify your commander, supervisor, or security manager.  
C. Photograph every page and text the photos to a group chat “so leadership can see.”  
D. Confirm the document’s contents to a news reporter who happens to be visiting the base.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 18.12 supplies the finder’s duties; it does not score courage as a formal key. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it matches the required custody-and-notify actions. Chapter 1 integrity and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D risks confirming or further disclosing protected information to the public media, which AFH 1 specifically cautions against. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It abandons custody and notification.
- **B — Most effective:** It safeguards and notifies immediately.
- **C — Ineffective:** It creates additional uncontrolled copies/exposure.
- **D — Least effective:** It engages media confirmation/disclosure risk.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶18.12 (out-of-control material / public-media caution context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Classified safeguarding; reporting; media caution  
**ADTC:** 18C, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Security manager/commander channels control real incidents; scenario uses cover-sheet cue only—no invented classified text

## C18-SJT-004 — Access triad refusal

A visiting coworker without a need-to-know asks you to “just flash” a classified working paper because “we’re the same rank and I’m cleared for something else somewhere.” AFH 1 requires security clearance eligibility, a signed SF 312, and need-to-know before access, and requires the holder to verify appropriate access.

### Response options

A. Show the paper because rank equality feels like enough.  
B. Refuse access, explain the clearance + SF 312 + need-to-know requirements without disclosing the paper’s content, and offer to help them request access through the proper owner/security channel if mission requires it.  
C. Leave the paper on the table and walk away so they can read it “without you showing them.”  
D. Read the paper aloud in the hallway so several people can hear.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 18.11 supplies the access triad; it does not score the refusal behavior as an official key. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it protects information while remaining professional and process-oriented. Chapter 1 integrity and respect provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D expands disclosure without need-to-know. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** Rank is not a substitute for need-to-know.
- **B — Most effective:** It enforces access rules and offers a proper path.
- **C — Ineffective:** It enables unauthorized access by neglect.
- **D — Least effective:** It creates broad unauthorized disclosure.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶18.11 (access prerequisites context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Need-to-know; safeguarding; respectful refusal  
**ADTC:** 18C, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Security manager controls real access decisions

## C18-SJT-005 — Public computing credentials

On temporary duty, a teammate suggests using the hotel business-center computer to log into web-based government e-mail “just for a minute.” AFH 1 prohibits using public computing facilities or services to process government-owned unclassified, sensitive, or classified information and states that using e-mail/messaging/web apps there to access web-based government services constitutes a compromise of login credentials that must be reported as a security incident.

### Response options

A. Agree and share your login so the teammate can “finish faster.”  
B. Refuse, explain the public-computing ban and credential-compromise rule, use an approved government device/path instead, and if credentials were already entered, report the security incident per local procedures.  
C. Use the hotel computer only for unclassified traffic and assume that makes it authorized.  
D. Post a photo of the hotel login screen to social media to “warn others later.”

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 18.21 supplies the public-computing rule; it does not score the refusal as an official key. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it prevents or reports credential compromise and uses an approved path. Chapter 1 integrity and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A knowingly shares credentials on a banned public system. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It causes credential compromise and sharing.
- **B — Most effective:** It refuses, uses an approved path, and reports if needed.
- **C — Ineffective:** Unclassified government-owned information is still covered by the ban.
- **D — Ineffective:** It creates further exposure.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶18.21 (public computing context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Public computing ban; incident reporting; honesty  
**ADTC:** 18D, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** ISSO/security incident procedures control real reporting

## C18-SJT-006 — Privacy Act / PII restraint

B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it prevents unauthorized PII access/potential access while still supporting the mission task properly. Chapter 1 integrity and respect provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A creates unauthorized potential access to PII on a personal account. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It moves PII to an unauthorized personal location.
- **B — Most effective:** It keeps PII inside authorized channels.
- **C — Ineffective:** Uncontrolled sharing expands breach risk.
- **D — Ineffective:** It bargains with protected information.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶18.15 (Privacy Act/PII context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** PII safeguarding; official need-to-know; integrity

## C18-SJT-007 — Suspicious package discipline

An unexpected package arrives with no return address, excessive postage, oily stains, and a “personal—rush—do not delay” marking. A coworker reaches for scissors. AFH 1 lists those indicators, says never cut tape/strings/wrappings on a suspect package, and directs immediate reporting and listing personnel present.

### Response options

A. Cut the wrapping open to “see if it’s safe.”  
B. Stop handling/cutting, treat it as suspicious, report immediately per installation procedures, and help list personnel who were in the room when it was identified.  
C. Shake and smell the package thoroughly to investigate.  
D. Carry it around the building asking random people if they ordered something.

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 18.27 supplies suspicious-mail context; it does not score the response as an official key. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it follows the no-cut, report, and accountability cues. Chapter 1 accountability and courage provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A does the one action AFH 1 says never to do. Chapter 1 accountability provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It cuts wrappings on a suspect package.
- **B — Most effective:** It stops, reports, and documents who was present.
- **C — Ineffective:** It increases handling/exposure.
- **D — Ineffective:** It moves a suspect package and expands the exposed population.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶18.27 (suspicious mail context); ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Suspicious package response; reporting; restraint  
**ADTC:** 18E, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** Installation antiterrorism/security procedures control real package response

## C18-SJT-008 — Reportable contact to AFOSI

At an overseas social event, a person you do not know presses you for details about unclassified controlled work and suggests “friends overseas would pay for deeper access.” AFH 1 requires individuals with reportable contacts to report immediately (within 30 days) to AFOSI and defines contact to include face-to-face meetings and requests for illegal or unauthorized access.

### Response options

A. Keep talking and quietly agree to share documents later for cash.  
B. End the conversation without providing access, document what happened, and report the contact to AFOSI immediately (within the 30-day outer bound, without delay).  
C. Wait eleven months to see if anything else happens, then decide.  
D. Post the stranger’s name and your unit roster online to “crowd-source who they are.”

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 18.31 supplies reporting context; it does not score the courage of reporting as an official key. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it refuses unauthorized access and reports promptly through AFOSI. Chapter 1 integrity, courage, and loyalty provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: A

A agrees to unauthorized access for personal gain. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Least effective:** It accepts illegal/unauthorized access for payment.
- **B — Most effective:** It refuses and reports to AFOSI promptly.
- **C — Ineffective:** It exceeds the 30-day reporting window and delays without cause.
- **D — Ineffective:** It creates a separate OPSEC/PII/exposure problem.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶18.31 (reportable contact context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** AFOSI reporting; refuse unauthorized access; courage  
**ADTC:** 18E, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** AFOSI controls real CI reporting; do not delay for convenience

## C18-SJT-009 — Two-track security policy caution

A junior Airman shows you a study note claiming a local 2026 cyber base policy “overwrites AFH 1’s FOIA 20-workday rule for the promotion exam.” You know MyPromotion’s two-track rule: exam study preserves the catalog-controlled AFH facts; duty use separately verifies current procedures.

### Response options

A. Tell the Airman to memorize only the local 2026 e-mail and ignore AFH 1 for exam study.  
B. Explain the two-track rule: preserve AFH 1’s FOIA 20-workday / 10-workday extension facts for exam-source study, and separately verify current FOIA procedures with the responsible office for duty use—without letting duty-current practice silently overwrite the controlling exam source.  
C. Invent a third FOIA timeline that averages both numbers.  
D. Order the Airman to stop studying FOIA entirely because “security never changes.”

### Most effective: B

Paragraph 18.16 supplies the FOIA timeline context; the two-track rule is MyPromotion source governance. B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it teaches honest source discipline without inventing a blended rule. Chapter 1 honesty and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: C

C invents a false averaged rule that matches neither track. Chapter 1 honesty provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It overwrites the exam source with a duty artifact.
- **B — Most effective:** It separates exam-source preservation from duty verification.
- **C — Least effective:** It fabricates a hybrid “fact.”
- **D — Ineffective:** It denies both study and the reality of policy change.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶18.16 (FOIA timeline context); MyPromotion two-track rule; ¶¶1.3.1, 1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Two-track source discipline; honesty in teaching  
**ADTC:** 18D, B—Understanding  
**Ranks:** SSgt; TSgt  
**Review flag:** FOIA office controls real processing timelines

C. Accuse the Airman publicly of espionage on social media.  
D. Offer to hide the foreign contacts if the Airman shares award bullets.

B is the MyPromotion most-effective judgment because it meets the immediate-reporting duty with factual discipline. Chapter 1 integrity, courage, and accountability provide the behavioral basis.

### Least effective: D

D conceals potential security-relevant information for personal benefit. Chapter 1 integrity provides the least-effective behavioral basis.

### Option analysis

- **A — Ineffective:** It abandons the supervisor reporting duty.
- **B — Most effective:** It reports facts promptly through proper channels.
- **C — Ineffective:** Public accusation without process harms people and the investigation.
- **D — Least effective:** It bargains to conceal reportable concerns.

**Source basis:** AFH 1, ¶18.14 (personnel security / adjudicative guidelines context); ¶¶1.3.1–1.3.3 (behavioral basis)  
**Primary concepts:** Continuous evaluation reporting; integrity; no concealment  
**ADTC:** 18C, B—Understanding
