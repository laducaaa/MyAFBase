## Section 24A — Honored Traditions

### Core B—Understanding cards (SSgt and TSgt)

### C24-FC-001
- **Prompt:** What are military customs and courtesies, and what do they help build according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Proven traditions deep-rooted in culture reflecting pride in military service. They play an extremely important role in building morale, esprit de corps, discipline, and mission effectiveness; they reflect the unique nature of the profession (AFI 1-1) and emphasize bonds and mutual respect for one another and civilian leadership.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24A; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-002
- **Prompt:** What is protocol in military/government practice according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** An internationally recognized system of courtesy and respect with rules for official life and ceremonies involving governments/nations/representatives; for military/government, a code of traditional precedence, courtesy, and etiquette. Modern military protocol combines established service customs with contemporary etiquette and courtesy. DAFI 90-1202 is cited for detailed guidance.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24A; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-003
- **Prompt:** What is the Base Honor Guard Program, and what is its primary mission today?
- **Answer:** A mandatory USAF program under installation commanders emphasizing customs and courtesies, dress and appearance, and drill and ceremonies. Primary mission: employ, equip, and train USAF members to provide professional military funeral honors for active members, retirees, and veterans of the U.S. Air Force.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24A; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-004
- **Prompt:** From which grades are Base Honor Guard members generally selected, and what minimum funeral honors did January 2000 public law provide for veterans?
- **Answer:** Generally Airman Basic to Technical Sergeant (usually volunteers from host/tenant units). Minimum: folding of a United States flag, presentation of the flag to the veteran’s family, and playing of Taps.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24A; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-005
- **Prompt:** Where was the first base honor guard activated, and what was its early responsibility?
- **Answer:** Within the 1100th Air Police Squadron, Bolling Field, Washington, D.C.; responsible for maintaining USAF ceremonial capability in the National Capital Region.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24A; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-006
- **Prompt:** What respects are shown outdoors during the national anthem for members in uniform vs civilian attire?
- **Answer:** In uniform: render the military salute from first note to last note. In civilian attire: stand at attention facing the flag with right hand over the heart; remove headgear with right hand and hold at left shoulder with hand over the heart.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24A; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-007
- **Prompt:** What should vehicles and occupants do on USAF installations during The Star-Spangled Banner or To the Colors?
- **Answer:** Vehicles in motion pull to the side and stop if consistent with safety and mission requirements; individuals in vehicles sit quietly until the last note.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24A; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-008
- **Prompt:** How do indoor national-anthem respects differ for military members with vs without headgear?
- **Answer:** In uniform in formation with appropriate headgear: render the military salute. In uniform without headgear: stand at attention without saluting. If the flag is not displayed, face the music and act as if the flag were displayed.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24A; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-009
- **Prompt:** What respect is shown to anthems of friendly foreign nations, and is order mandated when multiple anthems are played?
- **Answer:** The same respect as for The Star-Spangled Banner. Typically foreign anthems are played before the host nation’s anthem, but no regulation or law mandates when or in what order when more than one is played.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24A; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-010
- **Prompt:** Is the Pledge of Allegiance traditionally recited at military events, and what do members in uniform do outdoors when it is rendered?
- **Answer:** Not traditionally recited at military events. Outdoors in uniform: stand at attention, remain silent, face the flag, and salute.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24A; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-011
- **Prompt:** What do military personnel in uniform do during the Pledge indoors, and when is reciting optional?
- **Answer:** Stand at attention, remain silent, face the flag, but do not salute. At indoor events where participants are primarily civilians or in civilian attire, reciting is optional for those in uniform.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24A; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-012
- **Prompt:** What do the 13 white stars and the yellow numerals under the shield represent on the DAF Seal?
- **Answer:** 13 white stars = original 13 colonies. Yellow numerals = 1947, the year the DAF was established.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24A; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-013
- **Prompt:** What are the official Air Force colors reflected in the DAF Seal, and what do crest elements (eagle, wreath, clouds) symbolize?
- **Answer:** Ultramarine blue and Air Force yellow. Eagle = United States airpower; wreath of six alternate white (silver) and light blue folds repeats shield metal/color; white clouds = start of a new sky.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24A; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-014
- **Prompt:** What does the shield’s thunderbolt show, and how is the shield background colored?
- **Answer:** Striking power through aerospace (yellow vertical twist with three flames on each end crossing wings with eight lightning bolts). Top background light blue (sky); lower white (metal silver); nebular line represents clouds.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24A; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-015
- **Prompt:** When was the Air Force Symbol designated official, and what do the stylized wings represent?
- **Answer:** 5 May 2004. Stylized wings represent the stripes of strength—enlisted men and women; six sections = distinctive capabilities (air and space superiority, global attack, rapid global mobility, precision engagement, information superiority, agile combat support).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24A; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-016
- **Prompt:** What do the sphere, five-pointed star, and three diamonds of the Air Force Symbol represent?
- **Answer:** Sphere = globe / global vigilance, reach, and power and expeditionary challenge. Star’s five points = RegAF, civilians, Guard, Reserve, retirees (also space high ground and officer corps). Three diamonds = Integrity First, Service Before Self, Excellence In All We Do.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24A; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-017
- **Prompt:** Why must the Air Force Symbol be protected, and where does AFH 1 point for use/display instructions?
- **Answer:** It is a registered trademark and must be protected against unauthorized use or alterations. DoDI 5535.12_DAFI 35-114; DoD seals/logos guide; and the Air Force and Space Force Intellectual Property Management website (approved versions also on USAF Portal).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24A; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-018
- **Prompt:** Who wrote the Air Force Song, and what etiquette applies when it is played?
- **Answer:** Robert Crawford (unanimous winner after 700+ compositions; first U.S. Army band recordings 1939 titled The U.S. Air Force). Airmen stand at attention but may sing; during official events the official party may depart after the first verse (DAFI 90-1202).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24A; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-019
- **Prompt:** What bugle call receives the same respects as the national anthem?
- **Answer:** To the Colors.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24A; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-020
- **Prompt:** What legal protection cue does AFH 1 give for unauthorized use of the DAF Seal?
- **Answer:** Falsely making, forging, counterfeiting, mutilating, altering, or knowingly using or possessing the Seal with fraudulent intent is punishable by law; AFMAN 33-326 Attachment 2 outlines authorized users/uses.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24A; B—Understanding

## Section 24B — Respect for the Flag

### Core B—Understanding cards (SSgt and TSgt)

### C24-FC-021
- **Prompt:** Why shall Airmen treat the United States flag with high respect, and which U.S. Code titles does AFH 1 cite?
- **Answer:** It represents principles/ideals Airmen pledged to defend and for which many made the ultimate sacrifice; treat with respect similar to highest military and public officials. Titles 4, 10, and 36 U.S.C. pertain to proper display and respect.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24B; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-022
- **Prompt:** Is there an official meaning for the folds of the flag or an official flag folding script?
- **Answer:** No official meaning for the folds; according to DAFI 90-1202 there is no official flag folding script. Interest-group ceremonies/narratives are not official; Figure 24.3 narrative is not for official ceremonial use.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24B; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-023
- **Prompt:** What design did the Second Continental Congress determine on 14 June 1777, and what do today’s stripes and stars represent?
- **Answer:** 13 stripes alternating seven red and six white; union of 13 stars white in a blue field representing a new constellation. Today: 13 stripes = original colonies; stars = 50 states.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24B; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-024
- **Prompt:** What do the colors of the flag symbolize according to the AFH 1 narrative?
- **Answer:** Red = hardiness and valor; white = purity and innocence; blue = vigilance, perseverance, and justice.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24B; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-025
- **Prompt:** What is the installation flag’s size/material and when is it displayed?
- **Answer:** Lightweight nylon bunting, 8 feet 11 3/8 inches by 17 feet; only displayed in fair weather from an installation flagstaff; typical USAF installation flag.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24B; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-026
- **Prompt:** When is the all-purpose (all-weather) storm flag used, and what is its size?
- **Answer:** As an alternate for the installation flag in inclement weather; lightweight nylon bunting 5 feet by 9 feet 6 inches.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24B; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-027
- **Prompt:** What sizes may a retirement flag be, and who is entitled to presentation of a United States flag upon retirement?
- **Answer:** 3×4 feet or 3×5 feet. Members retiring from the USAF are entitled to presentation of a United States flag (O&M funds guidance in DAFMAN 65-605 V1).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24B; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-028
- **Prompt:** What is the garrison flag size and occasion, and what may substitute for it?
- **Answer:** 20 by 38 feet; flown on holidays and special occasions; can be substituted with the installation flag.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24B; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-029
- **Prompt:** What is the interment flag, and how is it placed on a closed casket?
- **Answer:** 5 feet by 9 feet 6 inches cotton; authorized for deceased military and veterans; size used to drape a closed casket. Place lengthwise with the union at the head and over the left shoulder of the deceased; do not lower into the grave or allow to touch the ground.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶¶24.12, 24.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24B; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-030
- **Prompt:** What is the universal custom for displaying the flag from sunrise to sunset, and when may it fly 24 hours?
- **Answer:** Display only from sunrise to sunset on buildings and stationary outdoor flagstaffs. May display 24 hours a day if properly illuminated during darkness; other flags with it should also be illuminated.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24B; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-031
- **Prompt:** How many installation flags may USAF installations fly from reveille to retreat, and where are additional flags authorized?
- **Answer:** One installation flag, normally on a flagstaff in front of installation headquarters; additional flagstaffs/flags authorized adjacent to each dependent school on the installation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24B; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-032
- **Prompt:** How is the flag flown on Memorial Day according to the holiday list cue?
- **Answer:** Half-staff until noon.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24B; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-033
- **Prompt:** Where is the United States flag displayed on a stage relative to the audience, and where is the union placed?
- **Answer:** On its own right—for an audience looking at a stage, on the audience’s left. Union at the uppermost right side of the flag itself.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24B; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-034
- **Prompt:** When carried in procession with other flags, where should the United States flag be?
- **Answer:** Either on the right of all others, or in front of and centered ahead of other flags if there is a line of other flags in the same procession.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24B; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-035
- **Prompt:** When displayed with another flag against a wall from crossed staffs, where is the U.S. flag and its staff?
- **Answer:** On its own right (observer’s left), with its staff in front of the staff of the other flag.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24B; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-036
- **Prompt:** When flown with flags radiating from a central point and no foreign flags, where is the U.S. flag?
- **Answer:** In the center and at the highest point of the group.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24B; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-037
- **Prompt:** When displayed with one or more nations, what staff/size rules apply and where is the U.S. flag usually placed?
- **Answer:** Separate staffs of the same height; flags of equal size; usually in a line alphabetically with the U.S. flag at its own right (observer’s left).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24B; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-038
- **Prompt:** On a speaker’s platform, where should the U.S. flag hold the position of honor?
- **Answer:** At the clergyman’s or speaker’s right as he or she faces the audience; any other flag on the left of the speaker or to the right of the audience.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24B; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-039
- **Prompt:** What does half-staff mean, and what is the hoist/lower procedure?
- **Answer:** One-half the distance between top and bottom of the staff. First hoist to the peak for an instant, then lower to half-staff; raise to the peak before lowering at day’s end. Other flags with it also half-staff except foreign national flags unless that country grants permission.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24B; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-040
- **Prompt:** When in uniform, when do you salute the U.S. flag in a procession?
- **Answer:** Salute six paces before it passes and hold the salute until it has passed six paces.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24B; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-041
- **Prompt:** Which flags are dipped to the U.S. flag, and is the U.S. flag dipped?
- **Answer:** Regimental colors, state flags, and organizational/institutional flags are always dipped; USAF and organizational flags may be dipped as appropriate. The U.S. flag will not be dipped to any person or thing; foreign national flags are never dipped.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24B; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-042
- **Prompt:** When may the U.S. flag be displayed with the union down?
- **Answer:** Only as a signal of dire distress in instances of extreme danger to life or property.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24B; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-043
- **Prompt:** How should a flag no longer a fitting emblem for display be retired, and does a folded flag require a salute?
- **Answer:** Destroyed in a dignified way, preferably by burning (historically significant flags may be preserved with proper disposition guidance). A folded flag is considered cased; a salute is not necessary.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24B; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-044
- **Prompt:** List the service-flag precedence order from their own right / observer’s left as printed in AFH 1.
- **Answer:** National Guard (13 December 1635), U.S. Army (14 June 1775), U.S. Navy (13 October 1775), U.S. Marine Corps (10 November 1775), U.S. Coast Guard (4 August 1790), U.S. Air Force (18 September 1947), U.S. Space Force (20 December 2019).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24B; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-045
- **Prompt:** After service flags, what is the continuing order of precedence of flags summarized in AFH 1?
- **Answer:** NORAD; U.S. Space Command; major commands (alphabetical); field operating agencies; Air National Guard; Air Force Reserve; direct reporting units; Numbered Air Forces; wings (descending); personal/position (branch-appropriate flags).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24B; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-046
- **Prompt:** What is the ceremonial flag’s size and fringe cue?
- **Answer:** Rayon or synthetic substitute, 4 feet 4 inches by 5 feet 6 inches, trimmed on three edges with yellow rayon fringe 2 inches wide.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24B; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-047
- **Prompt:** What is the all-purpose flag size and example uses?
- **Answer:** Rayon bunting 3 by 4 feet; outdoor display with flags of friendly foreign nations, arrival ceremonies for international dignitaries, or to indicate joint occupancy of a building by two or more countries.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24B; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-048
- **Prompt:** Should the U.S. flag ever touch the ground, be carried flat, or be used for advertising?
- **Answer:** No—should never touch ground/floor/water/merchandise; never carried flat or horizontally but always aloft and free; never used for advertising purposes.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24B; B—Understanding

## Section 24C — Respect for Individuals

### Core B—Understanding cards (SSgt and TSgt)

### C24-FC-049
- **Prompt:** What simple workplace courtesies does AFH 1 cite as contributing to a positive professional environment?
- **Answer:** Saying please and thank you; respecting other people’s time; being helpful; taking and delivering messages; offering assistance when possible. Demonstrate common courtesy on- and off-duty.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24C; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-050
- **Prompt:** What four categories may define a distinguished visitor (DV)?
- **Answer:** (1) General or Flag Officer; (2) government official with rank equivalent to Brigadier General or higher; (3) foreign military officer or civilian designated a DV by the Under Secretary of the Air Force for International Affairs; (4) visitor or group designated by the commander.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24C; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-051
- **Prompt:** What is the position of honor when walking, riding, or sitting with a senior?
- **Answer:** Senior (enlisted or commissioned) on the right; junior to the senior’s left.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.18
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24C; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-052
- **Prompt:** When should a room be called to attention for a senior official, and what is the exception?
- **Answer:** Unless told otherwise or impractical, rise and stand at attention when a senior official enters or departs; the first who sees the officer calls the area to attention. Exception: do not call to attention if an officer already present equals or outranks the entering officer.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.18
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24C; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-053
- **Prompt:** In what order do military personnel enter and exit automobiles and small boats?
- **Answer:** Enter in reverse order of rank—juniors enter first and sit on the senior’s left; senior officer last to enter and first to exit.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.18
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24C; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-054
- **Prompt:** How should officers and enlisted members be addressed, and what tier terms are acceptable for enlisted?
- **Answer:** By grade/title (e.g., Lieutenant Colonel; Master Sergeant). Enlisted may also be addressed by tier: Airman, Sergeant, Senior, Chief. Sir/Ma’am acceptable. Chaplains as Chaplain or ecclesiastical title.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24C; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-055
- **Prompt:** How should civilians and retirees be addressed according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Civilians: Mr./Mrs./Miss/Ms. and last name, or Sir/Ma’am. Retirees: same respect/courtesies as active; addressed by retired grade on official records/correspondence except matters relating to civilian employment.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24C; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-056
- **Prompt:** To whom are salutes appropriate according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** U.S. President, Vice President, Secretary of Defense, Service Secretaries, all superior commissioned and warrant officers, all Medal of Honor recipients, and superior officers of friendly foreign nations; also to the U.S. flag and during official ceremonies as covered.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24C; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-057
- **Prompt:** Who salutes first when a salute is exchanged, and are salutes exchanged with NOAA and USPHS as appropriate?
- **Answer:** The junior member always salutes the senior first (initiate in time for return). Yes—salutes exchanged with Uniformed Services of NOAA and U.S. Public Health Service as appropriate.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24C; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-058
- **Prompt:** When are salutes rendered indoors?
- **Answer:** Not rendered indoors except for formal reporting (knock once; enter when told; two paces; halt; salute; report; salute again when finished until acknowledged).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24C; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-059
- **Prompt:** What outdoor locations require exchange of salutes on and off installations?
- **Answer:** Outside a building—including porch, covered sidewalk, entryway, reviewing stand, or bus stop.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24C; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-060
- **Prompt:** Who salutes for a formation or work detail?
- **Answer:** Members in formation or individual workers do not salute unless commanded (formation); the person in charge salutes and acknowledges for those in the formation/detail.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24C; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-061
- **Prompt:** What happens when a senior officer approaches a group outdoors?
- **Answer:** The first individual noticing the officer calls the group to attention; all face the officer and salute. If addressed, remain at attention (unless ordered otherwise) until the conversation ends, then salute.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24C; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-062
- **Prompt:** Is exchange of salutes mandatory between members in moving military vehicles?
- **Answer:** No—not mandatory. Pedestrians must salute when officer passengers are readily identifiable (e.g., marked staff vehicles), including President, Vice President, SecDef, Service Secretaries, and seniors distinguished by plates/flags.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24C; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-063
- **Prompt:** May persons in uniform salute civilians or seniors in civilian clothes?
- **Answer:** Yes—upon recognition.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24C; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-064
- **Prompt:** Is saluting required in designated “no salute” areas?
- **Answer:** No.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24C; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-065
- **Prompt:** What saluting rules apply when wearing physical training gear?
- **Answer:** Saluting individuals due to rank recognition is not required in PT gear but may be expected in specified academic training environments. Outdoors in PT gear, Airmen are required to salute during reveille and retreat.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24C; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-066
- **Prompt:** What should you salute at a military funeral or memorial in uniform?
- **Answer:** The caisson or hearse as it passes; the casket as it is carried past; during firing of volleys; and during Taps. Note: salute not required for installation Taps signifying lights out.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24C; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-067
- **Prompt:** If carrying articles in both hands unable to transfer to the left hand, must you salute?
- **Answer:** Need not initiate or return the salute when impractical, but should nod or offer a verbal greeting acknowledging the appropriateness of a salute.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24C; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-068
- **Prompt:** What respect should be shown to national anthems and flags of friendly foreign nations?
- **Answer:** The same respect rendered to the United States national anthem and flag; learn ranks/grades/insignia of frequently contacted nations especially overseas.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24C; B—Understanding

### TSgt C—Applying overlay cards (24C; no SSgt tag)

### C24-FC-069
- **Prompt:** A colonel enters your workcenter while a major is already present. Applying AFH 1 position-of-honor/room rules, should the room be called to attention?
- **Answer:** No—do not call the room to attention when an officer already present equals or has higher rank than the officer entering.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.18
- **Tags:** TSgt; 24C; C—Applying

### C24-FC-070
- **Prompt:** You are walking outdoors with your flight chief (TSgt) and a senior master sergeant joins. Applying AFH 1, who takes the position of honor?
- **Answer:** The senior person—here the senior master sergeant—takes the position of honor on the right; juniors take positions to the senior’s left.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.18
- **Tags:** TSgt; 24C; C—Applying

### C24-FC-071
- **Prompt:** Loading a government sedan with a captain and two junior enlisted: applying AFH 1 vehicle courtesy, who enters first and who exits first?
- **Answer:** Juniors enter first and sit on the senior’s left; the senior officer is last to enter and first to exit.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.18
- **Tags:** TSgt; 24C; C—Applying

### C24-FC-072
- **Prompt:** You are outdoors in PT gear when reveille sounds, and later the same day you pass a captain while still in PT gear (not a specified academic environment). Applying AFH 1, what saluting actions fit?
- **Answer:** Salute during reveille/retreat outdoors in PT gear. Rank-recognition saluting is not required in PT gear (unless a specified academic training environment expects it).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.20
- **Tags:** TSgt; 24C; C—Applying

### C24-FC-073
- **Prompt:** At end-of-day installation Taps for lights out you are in uniform outdoors; later you attend a military funeral where Taps is played. Applying AFH 1, when is the salute required?
- **Answer:** Salute not required for lights-out Taps. At a military funeral/memorial in uniform, salute during Taps (and caisson/hearse, casket, volleys).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.20
- **Tags:** TSgt; 24C; C—Applying

### C24-FC-074
- **Prompt:** You must report to an officer indoors. Applying AFH 1 formal reporting procedures, what sequence do you follow?
- **Answer:** Knock once; enter when told; march to approximately two paces; halt; salute; report “Sir/Ma’am, (rank and last name) reports as ordered” or “…reports.” When finished, sharp salute until acknowledged; face and depart.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.20
- **Tags:** TSgt; 24C; C—Applying

## Section 24D — Ceremonies and Events

### Core B—Understanding cards (SSgt and TSgt)

### C24-FC-075
- **Prompt:** Name several types of official military ceremonies listed in AFH 1.
- **Answer:** Promotions; changes/assumptions of command; activations/inactivations; redesignations; enlistments/reenlistments; awards; decorations; arrivals/departures; reveille; retreat; building dedications; ribbon cuttings; retirements; funerals.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24D; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-076
- **Prompt:** What planning lead-time cue does AFH 1 give for event details, and how early might committees begin?
- **Answer:** Aim to have details planned at least three weeks in advance or more; depending on size/scope, planning may begin several months to a year before invitations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24D; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-077
- **Prompt:** For whom are full honors reserved, and who determines which honors are rendered?
- **Answer:** Statutory appointees and General or Flag Officers, foreign dignitaries, and occasions promoting international good will. Installation commander determines which types of honors are rendered.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.23
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24D; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-078
- **Prompt:** What must commanders/supervisors ensure about award presentation methods?
- **Answer:** Because there are no specific guidelines for an award presentation method, ensure the presentation method reflects the significance of the award (formal or informal; many units use commander’s call).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.23
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24D; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-079
- **Prompt:** What do decoration ceremonies formally recognize, and when should they be conducted?
- **Answer:** Meritorious service, outstanding achievement, or heroism. Conduct at the earliest possible date after approval; commanders should personally present when possible; wear host-specified uniform.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.23
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24D; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-080
- **Prompt:** When are promotion ceremonies customarily conducted relative to the effective date?
- **Answer:** Because most promotions are effective the first day of the month, customarily on the last duty day before the promotion effective date.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.23
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24D; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-081
- **Prompt:** What reenlistment ceremony courtesies does AFH 1 emphasize regarding officer choice, family, flag, and uniform?
- **Answer:** Any commissioned officer may perform; invite guests/immediate family; U.S. flag as traditional backdrop when available; reenlistee and administering officer wear authorized uniform unless the officer is retired (then that officer’s uniform optional). Must be physically collocated for the oath.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶¶24.23–24.24
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24D; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-082
- **Prompt:** May “so help me God” be omitted from oaths?
- **Answer:** Yes—if desired, the words may be omitted.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.24
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24D; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-083
- **Prompt:** How does the enlisted oath’s obedience clause differ in emphasis from the officer oath’s office-discharge clause?
- **Answer:** Enlisted: obey orders of the President and officers appointed over them according to regulations and the UCMJ. Officer: well and faithfully discharge the duties of the office upon which about to enter (plus freely take obligation without mental reservation/purpose of evasion).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.24
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24D; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-084
- **Prompt:** What additional constitutional loyalty does the ANG enlisted oath include beyond the U.S. Constitution?
- **Answer:** Support and defend the constitution of the State; obey orders of the President and the Governor and officers appointed over them according to law and regulations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.24
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24D; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-085
- **Prompt:** What must commanders offer retiring members regarding a ceremony, and what items are typically presented?
- **Answer:** Offer the courtesy of a formal ceremony unless the member prefers otherwise. Typical presentations include certificate of retirement, United States flag, USAF retired lapel button, certificates/letters of appreciation, and appropriate awards/decorations/honors; spouses also receive special recognition.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.25
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24D; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-086
- **Prompt:** What twofold purpose does the retreat ceremony serve?
- **Answer:** Signals the end of the official duty day and serves as a ceremony for paying respect to the United States flag.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.27
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24D; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-087
- **Prompt:** What does reveille signal, and who designates the time?
- **Answer:** The start of the official duty day; the commander designates the specified time because start times vary by location.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.26
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24D; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-088
- **Prompt:** During reveille, on what signal does the flag security detail begin to raise the flag, and what care is required?
- **Answer:** On the first note of the national anthem or To the Colors, raise briskly; the senior member holds the flag to keep it from touching the ground.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.26
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24D; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-089
- **Prompt:** For uniformed members not in formation at retreat, what positions do they assume and when?
- **Answer:** Face the flag (if visible) or the music; parade rest on the first note of retreat; then attention and salute on the first note of the national anthem or To the Colors.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.27
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24D; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-090
- **Prompt:** What detail sizes does AFH 1 recommend when practical for lowering the all-purpose vs installation flag?
- **Answer:** All-purpose: one senior and three junior members. Installation: one senior and five junior members.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.27
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24D; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-091
- **Prompt:** If the flag is at half-staff at retreat, what hoist/lower timing cue applies?
- **Answer:** Briskly hoist to the staff head while retreat is sounded, then lower on the first note of the national anthem or To the Colors.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.27
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24D; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-092
- **Prompt:** What is the significant difference between a dining-in and a dining-out?
- **Answer:** Dining-out: nonmilitary spouses, friends, and civilians may attend. Dining-in: formal dinner for military members only.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.28
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24D; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-093
- **Prompt:** How does combat dining-in differ from dining-in primarily?
- **Answer:** Maintains traditional form, but differs primarily in dress and atmosphere—typically utility uniform in a more relaxed environment deliberately prepared to encourage camaraderie.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.28
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24D; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-094
- **Prompt:** What is the Order of the Sword, and what are the only approved award levels?
- **Answer:** Highest honor/tribute an enlisted member can bestow—for outstanding leadership and support to enlisted members as “Leader among Leaders and an Airman among Airmen.” Only USAF level and major command level are approved.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.29
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24D; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-095
- **Prompt:** Who are the “keepers of the sword,” and whom does the Order recognize (grade cue)?
- **Answer:** CMSAF and MAJCOM command chiefs maintain official lists. USAF adopted/updated in 1967 to recognize military senior officers Colonel or above and civilian equivalents for conspicuous contributions to enlisted force welfare/prestige/mission effectiveness and the overall military establishment.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.29
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24D; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-096
- **Prompt:** What dress is required for military members at dining-in/dining-out?
- **Answer:** Mess dress or the semiformal uniform; civilians wear dress specified in the invitations. Order of the Sword: mess dress, semiformal, or equivalent.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶¶24.28–24.29
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24D; B—Understanding

## Section 24E — Drill and Formation

### Core B—Understanding cards (SSgt and TSgt)

### C24-FC-097
- **Prompt:** Why are drill and formations used, and what is the most practical drill group size cue?
- **Answer:** Honor distinguished persons, recognize special events, and demonstrate proficiency/training; precision/promptness/teamwork support ceremonial appearance. Flight formation is most practical: at least two but no more than four elements.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.30
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24E; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-098
- **Prompt:** What two parts does a drill command usually have, illustrated by “Flight, HALT”?
- **Answer:** Preparatory command (Flight—designates unit/explains movement) and command of execution (HALT—movement to perform).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.31
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24E; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-099
- **Prompt:** What cadence and step standards does AFH 1 cite for orderly drill movement?
- **Answer:** 24-inch step; cadence of 100 to 120 steps per minute; plus distance and interval standards.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.31
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24E; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-100
- **Prompt:** What is a supplementary command, with examples?
- **Answer:** Given when one unit must execute a different movement or the same movement at a different time. Examples: CONTINUE THE MARCH; STAND FAST.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.31
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24E; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-101
- **Prompt:** What is an informational command, with examples?
- **Answer:** Has no preparatory command or command of execution and is not supplementary; directs others to give commands. Examples: PREPARE FOR INSPECTION; DISMISS THE SQUADRON.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.31
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24E; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-102
- **Prompt:** What do mass commands help develop, and how are they usually confined?
- **Answer:** Confidence, self-reliance, assertiveness, and enthusiasm by making individuals recall, give, and execute proper commands. Usually simple movements with short preparatory/execution commands carried out simultaneously by all elements.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.31
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24E; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-103
- **Prompt:** What happens on the command “FALL IN”?
- **Answer:** Individuals form a flight in line formation (long line shoulder to shoulder), automatically establishing dress, cover, interval, and distance, executing automatic ready front, and remaining at attention.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.32
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24E; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-104
- **Prompt:** What foot position is required at Attention?
- **Answer:** Heels together smartly and on line; feet turned out equally to form a 45-degree angle; weight equally on heels and balls of both feet; silence and immobility required.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.32
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24E; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-105
- **Prompt:** On “Open Ranks, MARCH,” what does each rank do?
- **Answer:** Fourth rank stands fast and automatically executes dress right dress; third takes one pace, second two, first three paces forward. Flight commander aligns, then Ready, FRONT.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.32
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24E; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-106
- **Prompt:** On “Close Ranks, MARCH,” what does each rank do?
- **Answer:** First rank stands fast; second takes one pace forward and halts at attention; third and fourth take two and three paces forward respectively and halt at attention.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.32
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24E; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-107
- **Prompt:** What are the four positions of rest, and what must precede them?
- **Answer:** Parade rest, at ease, rest, and fall out. Commander and members must be at attention before going to any rest position.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.33
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24E; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-108
- **Prompt:** How does parade rest position the feet and hands?
- **Answer:** Heels 12 inches apart (inside of heels); arms fully extended to the back; right hand in palm of left, right thumb over left forming an “X”; head/eyes straight; silent and immobile.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.33
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24E; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-109
- **Prompt:** How do at ease and rest differ?
- **Answer:** Both allow relaxing in a standing position with the right foot in place and unchanged formation position. At ease: silence maintained. Rest: moderate speech permitted.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.33
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24E; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-110
- **Prompt:** What may individuals do on “FALL OUT,” and how do they return to the formation?
- **Answer:** Relax standing or break ranks; remain in the immediate area; moderate speech permitted. Resume with “FALL IN” (not “Flight, ATTENTION,” which resumes attention from other rests).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.33
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24E; B—Understanding

## Section 24F — Preserving Our Heritage

### Core B—Understanding cards (SSgt and TSgt)

### C24-FC-111
- **Prompt:** What is the Air Force Enlisted Heritage Research Institute, and where is it located?
- **Answer:** A repository for Air Force enlisted history and heritage with over 200,000 original documents/reference materials from balloon aviation to current operations; at Maxwell AFB–Gunter Annex, Alabama, housed within the Enlisted Heritage Hall Museum.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.34
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24F; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-112
- **Prompt:** Why is the Enlisted Heritage Hall Museum distinctive, and what size/exhibit cues does AFH 1 give?
- **Answer:** Only enlisted-centric museum in the Air Force; place of honor to remember/teach enlisted contributions to Air Power. 14,000 sq ft; developed by SNCOA in 1983 from a 1942 segregated dining facility; over 280 exhibits / 3,000+ artifacts.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.34
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24F; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-113
- **Prompt:** When and by whom was the Air Force Memorial dedicated, and whom does it commemorate?
- **Answer:** Dedicated 14 October 2006 by President George W. Bush (fifteen years after the 1991 Persian Gulf War). Commemorates Airmen—past, present, and future.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.35
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24F; B—Understanding

### C24-FC-114
- **Prompt:** What do the Memorial’s northern wall, southern wall, contemplation wall, and Honor Guard statue represent/themes?
- **Answer:** Northern wall theme Valor (MoH recipients/hometowns); southern wall theme Air Force Core Values; glass contemplation wall with 4-ship missing-man formation for fallen Airmen; 16-ft bronze Honor Guard statue testifies to courtesies, dress/appearance, drill/ceremonies, and values people most.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.35
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 24F; B—Understanding

### TSgt C—Applying overlay cards (24F; no SSgt tag)

### C24-FC-115
- **Prompt:** You are building a professional-development hour on enlisted contributions to airpower. Applying AFH 1 heritage resources, which institution’s purpose best anchors an enlisted-centric lesson?
- **Answer:** The Air Force Enlisted Heritage Hall Museum—the only enlisted-centric museum, designed to remember and teach the importance of enlisted contributions to Air Power—supported by the Research Institute’s enlisted history repository.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.34
- **Tags:** TSgt; 24F; C—Applying

### C24-FC-116
- **Prompt:** A mentee asks why enlisted heritage artifacts (MoH medals, CMSAF uniforms, aircrew guns) matter beyond “museum trivia.” Applying AFH 1, what teaching purpose do you emphasize?
- **Answer:** They help remember and teach the importance of enlisted contributions to Air and Space missions—connecting identity and mission effectiveness to real enlisted heritage, not souvenir facts.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.34
- **Tags:** TSgt; 24F; C—Applying

### C24-FC-117
- **Prompt:** Your unit wants a values-focused heritage discussion tied to a national Air Force commemorative site. Applying AFH 1 Memorial themes, which wall explicitly centers Core Values?
- **Answer:** The southern granite wall, whose inscription theme is Air Force Core “Values.”
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.35
- **Tags:** TSgt; 24F; C—Applying

### C24-FC-118
- **Prompt:** During a mentorship on sacrifice and fallen Airmen, which Memorial feature does AFH 1 describe as paying tribute via the missing-man formation?
- **Answer:** The glass contemplation wall engraved with the 4-ship missing-man formation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.35
- **Tags:** TSgt; 24F; C—Applying

### C24-FC-119
- **Prompt:** You are explaining why customs and courtesies still matter using the Memorial’s central statue. Applying AFH 1, what does the Honor Guard Statue represent?
- **Answer:** A testimony to military courtesies, dress and appearance, and drill and ceremonies—and most importantly, that people are valued most.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.35
- **Tags:** TSgt; 24F; C—Applying

### C24-FC-120
- **Prompt:** A peer treats heritage sites as optional tourism. Applying AFH 1’s Memorial dedication idea, how do you explain why a ground memorial uniquely matters for Airmen?
- **Answer:** Unlike Soldiers/Marines who can revisit battlefields/beaches, an Airman cannot visit the patch of sky raced across on a mission; the Memorial recognizes achievements and sacrifices in the skies.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶24.35
- **Tags:** TSgt; 24F; C—Applying
