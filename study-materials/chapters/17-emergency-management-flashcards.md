## Section 17A — Air Force Emergency Management

### C17-FC-001
- **Prompt:** Why was the DAF Emergency Management Program developed, and what three readiness outcomes does AFH 1 name?
- **Answer:** To ensure readiness of forces to save lives; minimize the loss or degradation of resources; and continue, sustain, and restore operational capability in all-hazards threat environments at DAF installations worldwide.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17A; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-002
- **Prompt:** What mission of Great Power Competition does AFH 1 link to the need for the EM Program?
- **Answer:** The ability of the DAF to carry out its mission of Great Power Competition depends on continuance of the mission in the face of adversity, including natural and man-made disasters, terrorist use of WMD, and hostile attacks.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17A; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-003
- **Prompt:** What is the primary mission of the DAF EM Program?
- **Answer:** Prevent, protect, prepare for, respond to, and recover from all hazards/threats that pose the greatest risk to lives, key resources, facilities, infrastructure, and operational missions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17A; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-004
- **Prompt:** What is the ancillary mission of the DAF EM Program?
- **Answer:** Support homeland defense and provide support to civil and host-nation authorities; antiterrorism programs; and the countering weapons of mass destruction enterprise.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17A; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-005
- **Prompt:** List the seven pillars of EM preparedness.
- **Answer:** Risk Management; Planning Framework; Common Core Capabilities; Capability Evaluation and Validation; Program Management Reviews; Capability Investment Strategy; and Adaptation via Information.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17A; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-006
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define Prevention as an EM mission area?
- **Answer:** Actions to avoid an incident or to intervene to stop an incident from occurring; Prevention involves actions to protect lives and property.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17A; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-007
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define Protection as an EM mission area?
- **Answer:** Protection provides the capabilities necessary to protect the installation against all threats, hazards, and manmade or natural disasters.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17A; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-008
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define Response as an EM mission area?
- **Answer:** Activities that address the short-term, direct effects of an incident, including immediate actions to save lives, protect property, and meet basic human needs, plus execution of emergency operations plans and incident mitigation activities.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17A; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-009
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define Mitigation as an EM mission area, and when may it be implemented?
- **Answer:** Activities designed to reduce or eliminate risks to persons or property or to lessen the actual or potential effects or consequences of an incident. Mitigation measures may be implemented prior to, during, or after an incident.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17A; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-010
- **Prompt:** What is the Disaster Response Force (DRF)?
- **Answer:** Specific organizational offices and responders in the DAF EM Program that respond to disasters/incidents and manage and conduct response operations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17A; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-011
- **Prompt:** What is the Emergency Communications Center (ECC), and what core functions does it incorporate?
- **Answer:** A 24/7 operation incorporating the core functions of fire and emergency services, security forces, and medical dispatch. It provides emergency communications, alarm/sensor/video monitoring, incident communications support, tactical channel assignments, emergency notification, responder reachback, and notification of emergencies to medical treatment facilities.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17A; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-012
- **Prompt:** What is the Incident Commander (IC) responsible for?
- **Answer:** All incident activities, including developing strategies and tactics and ordering and releasing resources. The IC has overall authority and responsibility for conducting and managing incident operations at the incident site.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17A; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-013
- **Prompt:** How do first responders differ from emergency responders in AFH 1?
- **Answer:** First responders (F&ES, medical, security forces, and others) immediately engage to save lives, stabilize the incident, and prevent further property damage. Emergency responders deploy after first responders to expand IC capabilities and perform support functions (e.g., EM follow-on, CBRN specialists, EOD, medical providers, mortuary affairs).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17A; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-014
- **Prompt:** What do Control Centers do when activated during EOC activation?
- **Answer:** Provide expertise and resource support to the EOC director and serve as the command and control node for all installation units tasked in the Installation Emergency Management Plan.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17A; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-015
- **Prompt:** What is the Emergency Operations Center (EOC)?
- **Answer:** The installation’s critical function to address imminent or realized threats/hazards and coordinate support to the incident command, on-scene personnel, and/or other EOCs.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17A; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-016
- **Prompt:** What is the Command Post’s role in EM structure?
- **Answer:** A full-time 24/7 C2 node, directly responsible to commander(s), serving as the focal point of operations including receipt and dissemination of orders, information, and requests necessary for C2 of assigned forces and operations.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17A; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-017
- **Prompt:** What is the Crisis Action Team (CAT), and what does AFH 1 say it is not intended to manage?
- **Answer:** The top echelon of installation mission continuation and sustainment, including senior officers and senior enlisted leaders across the A-Staff and supporting units (joint/civilian/host-nation partners may be present). It supports the installation commander by assessing the situation, determining mission priorities, and directing subordinate units. It is intended to focus on mission continuation and sustainment, not management of the incident.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17A; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-018
- **Prompt:** Through which four alignment areas does the DAF EM Program generate interoperability?
- **Answer:** Operational levels; planning and risk management; incident command and coordination; and command and control.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17A; B—Understanding


## Section 17B — Disasters, Attacks, and Hazards

### C17-FC-019
- **Prompt:** Why does AFH 1 say emergency plans matter for disasters and incidents?
- **Answer:** Disasters or incidents can occur at any time with little or no advance warning and can be local or widespread, predictable or unpredictable. Emergency plans help prepare for and mitigate the impact of these threats on an installation, personnel, and the mission.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.3
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17B; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-020
- **Prompt:** What must installations develop regarding hazards likely to affect their areas?
- **Answer:** Plans for the hazards likely to affect their areas based on appropriate response, mitigation, and recovery procedures. Installation emergency management plans provide comprehensive guidance for emergency responses to various incidents.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17B; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-021
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define natural disasters, and give representative examples?
- **Answer:** Non-routine events that occur and cause severe damage that may require assistance. Examples include hurricanes/typhoons, tornadoes, storms, high water, high winds, tidal waves, tsunamis, earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, landslides, mudslides, snowstorms, fires, floods, explosions, and droughts.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17B; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-022
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define technological hazards, and what are example causes/outcomes?
- **Answer:** Man-made events caused by a malfunction in a technological structure or human error in controlling the technology. They can cause loss of life or injury, property damage, social and economic disruptions, or environmental degradation and can happen with little to no warning. Examples include hazardous materials release and nuclear power plant failures.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17B; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-023
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define man-made hazards?
- **Answer:** A non-routine event that exceeds the capacity of the affected area to respond in a way that saves lives, preserves property, and maintains the affected region’s social, ecological, economic, and political stability. Examples include HAZMAT releases/spills, terrorism, active shooters, nuclear accidents, crime, arson, civil disorder, war, biological/chemical threat, cyber-attacks, pollution, fires, and transportation accidents.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17B; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-024
- **Prompt:** What are CBRN hazards, and what directive does AFH 1 point to for more information?
- **Answer:** Chemical, Biological, Radiological, and Nuclear hazards, including TIC/TIMs, that cause adverse effects through deliberate release and dissemination. More information is in DAFI 10-2503, CBRN Defense Program.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17B; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-025
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define a chemical agent, and how are chemical agents categorized?
- **Answer:** A chemical substance intended for use in military operations to kill, seriously injure, or incapacitate mainly through physiological effects. Categories include lung-damaging (choking) agents, blister agents, blood agents, incapacitating agents, and nerve agents.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17B; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-026
- **Prompt:** Which chemical agents pose a primary threat to air bases due to their persistent nature?
- **Answer:** Nerve and blister agents.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17B; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-027
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define a biological agent, and what forms may it take?
- **Answer:** A microorganism (or a toxin derived from it) that causes disease in personnel, plants, or animals or causes the deterioration of materiel. Biological agents include viruses, bacteria, fungi, and toxins and may be found as liquid droplets, aerosols, or dry powders.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17B; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-028
- **Prompt:** Why may symptoms of biological attacks not be experienced immediately?
- **Answer:** Each agent has a different exposure (incubation) period before infection.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17B; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-029
- **Prompt:** What is the difference between nonionizing and ionizing radiation as AFH 1 presents it?
- **Answer:** Nonionizing radiation (e.g., light infrared to ultraviolet, radiofrequency emissions, microwaves) generally produces heat when it interacts with the body. Ionizing radiation has sufficient energy to produce ions when it interacts with matter, including the human body, and can alter or destroy cell processes and structures.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17B; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-030
- **Prompt:** What are the primary concerns of a nuclear detonation listed in AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Blast/shock, thermal radiation (heat), ionizing radiation, and ballistic debris for surface and shallow subsurface bursts. Nuclear blasts may also pose an electromagnetic pulse hazard that can cause widespread communications or electrical problems.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17B; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-031
- **Prompt:** What is a Toxic Industrial Chemical (TIC)?
- **Answer:** A chemical developed or manufactured for use in industrial operations or research by industry, government, or academia that poses a hazard.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17B; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-032
- **Prompt:** What is a Toxic Industrial Material (TIM)?
- **Answer:** A generic term for toxic, chemical, biological, or radioactive substances in solid, liquid, aerosolized, or gaseous form that may be used, or stored for use, for industrial, commercial, medical, military, or domestic purposes.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17B; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-033
- **Prompt:** In a TIM incident, what is the most important action if feasible, and what protection limitation does AFH 1 emphasize?
- **Answer:** Immediately evacuate to an area outside the hazard’s path if feasible. The protective mask, ensemble, and military standard collective protection filters are not designed to provide protection from toxic industrial materials. If evacuation is impractical, implement shelter-in-place procedures.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17B; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-034
- **Prompt:** Must all DAF personnel plan, prepare, respond, and recover from CBRN incidents according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Yes. Defense against CBRN threats requires all DAF personnel to plan, prepare, respond, and recover from CBRN incidents.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.4
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17B; B—Understanding


## Section 17C — Preparedness and Protection

### C17-FC-035
- **Prompt:** Why does AFH 1 say preparedness actions should be considered mission critical when enemy attack threat is high?
- **Answer:** Establishing and conducting preparedness actions effectively and efficiently increases protection of mission-critical resources and force survivability, enabling prompt, sustained, and decisive operations in a contested environment.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.5
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17C; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-036
- **Prompt:** What must every DAF installation have regarding emergency notifications?
- **Answer:** A rapid and effective system to quickly disseminate emergency notifications and information such as watches, warnings, evacuation routes, and protective actions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17C; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-037
- **Prompt:** What does a three-to-five-minute steady tone indicate, and what are individual actions?
- **Answer:** A disaster or incident is imminent or in progress. Individual actions: be alert, take cover or evacuate to safety, follow instructions, and account for personnel.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17C; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-038
- **Prompt:** What does a three-to-five-minute wavering tone indicate, and what are individual actions?
- **Answer:** An attack or hostile act is imminent or in progress. Individual actions: be alert, execute security measures, follow instructions, and account for personnel.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17C; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-039
- **Prompt:** What does the announcement “Lockdown, Lockdown, Lockdown” indicate, and what should individuals do?
- **Answer:** An active shooter incident is in progress. Individuals should remain calm and implement lockdown procedures.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17C; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-040
- **Prompt:** What does a Shelter-in-Place announcement require for technological disasters?
- **Answer:** Immediately take shelter indoors and perform Emergency Action Plan actions: take refuge in a pre-designated room, shut off heating/ventilation/air conditioning, and cover vents and doors with plastic.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17C; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-041
- **Prompt:** What does Alarm Green mean, and what MOPP/actions accompany it?
- **Answer:** Attack is not probable. MOPP 0 (or as directed); Normal Wartime Conditions; Resume Operations; Continue Recovery Actions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17C; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-042
- **Prompt:** What does Alarm Yellow mean, and what MOPP/actions accompany it?
- **Answer:** Attack is probable in less than 30 minutes. MOPP 2 (or as directed); Protect and Cover Assets; Go to Protective Shelter or Seek Best Protection with Overhead Cover.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17C; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-043
- **Prompt:** What does Alarm Red/Blue mean, and what MOPP/actions accompany it?
- **Answer:** Air or ground attack is imminent or in progress (Blue variation may be used at some Pacific installations). MOPP 4 (or as directed); Seek Immediate Protection with Overhead Cover; Report Observed Activity or Attacks.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17C; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-044
- **Prompt:** What does Alarm Black mean, and what are the three release categories?
- **Answer:** Attack is over and CBRN contamination and/or UXO hazards are suspected or present. MOPP 4 (or as directed); perform Self-Aid/Buddy Care; remain under overhead cover or within shelter unless directed otherwise. Initial Release—CBRN and Specialized Teams (EOD, Damage Assessment, etc.); Limited Release—Post Attack Reconnaissance Teams; General Release—mission-essential personnel only.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17C; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-045
- **Prompt:** What does All Clear indicate, and what are individual actions?
- **Answer:** The immediate disaster or threat has ended. Remain alert, account for personnel, and report hazards, injuries, and damages.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.6
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17C; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-046
- **Prompt:** What are MOPP levels used for, and who determines the initial level?
- **Answer:** To quickly communicate required individual protective equipment and protection levels for in-place forces during CBRN operations or attack conditions. The installation commander determines the initial level based on MOPP condition analysis and adjusts as mission priorities and intelligence change.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17C; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-047
- **Prompt:** What is Split MOPP?
- **Answer:** A tactic used to divide an installation or operating location into two or more CBRN zones rather than declaring the entire installation contaminated. Zone transition points contain contamination while allowing movement of people, equipment, and supplies between zones.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.7
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17C; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-048
- **Prompt:** What does individual protection comprise according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Singular use or a combination of individual protective equipment, vaccinations and prophylaxes, protective shelters, evacuation, relocation, exposure control, contamination control, and warning and notification systems.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17C; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-049
- **Prompt:** What is the ground crew ensemble, and against what does it protect?
- **Answer:** A whole-body system that is the best immediate protective equipment against chemical agents regardless of type, concentration, or method of attack. It protects against chemical-biological warfare agents, toxins, and radiological particulates.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17C; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-050
- **Prompt:** What IPE components and detection/decon items does AFH 1 list with individual protection?
- **Answer:** Protective mask with filters, overgarment, protective gloves, and footwear covers or overboots; also M8 and M9 detector paper, reactive skin decontamination lotion, and M295 decontamination kits.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.8
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17C; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-051
- **Prompt:** At MOPP Level Ready, what is carried and what is worn?
- **Answer:** Carry: Field Gear. Wear: Field Gear (as directed).
- **Source:** AFH 1, Table 17.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17C; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-052
- **Prompt:** At MOPP Level Zero, what is carried and what is worn?
- **Answer:** Carry: Overgarment, Footwear, Mask/Hood, and Handwear. Wear: Field Gear.
- **Source:** AFH 1, Table 17.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17C; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-053
- **Prompt:** At MOPP Level One, what is carried and what is worn?
- **Answer:** Carry: Footwear, Mask/Hood, and Handwear. Wear: Field Gear and Overgarment.
- **Source:** AFH 1, Table 17.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17C; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-054
- **Prompt:** At MOPP Level Two, what is carried and what is worn?
- **Answer:** Carry: Mask/Hood and Handwear. Wear: Field Gear, Overgarment, and Footwear.
- **Source:** AFH 1, Table 17.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17C; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-055
- **Prompt:** At MOPP Level Three, what is carried and what is worn?
- **Answer:** Carry: Handwear. Wear: Field Gear, Overgarment, Footwear, and Mask/Hood.
- **Source:** AFH 1, Table 17.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17C; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-056
- **Prompt:** At MOPP Level Four, what is carried and what is worn?
- **Answer:** Carry: N/A. Wear: Field Gear, Overgarment, Footwear, Mask/Hood, and Handwear.
- **Source:** AFH 1, Table 17.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17C; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-057
- **Prompt:** What is COLPRO, and how does it allow occupants to operate?
- **Answer:** Collective Protection Systems—fixed-site or expeditionary locations that provide protection from CBRN agents by utilizing an overpressure system to keep threat agents outside certain facilities, allowing occupants to work or rest without wearing protective gear. Many have contamination control areas for entry after decontamination and outer-garment removal.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.9
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17C; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-058
- **Prompt:** How should critical resources be protected before a CBRN attack if they cannot be placed under overhead cover?
- **Answer:** Cover or wrap with at least one layer of barrier material to prevent contamination; when possible, use two layers of barrier material to cover the tops of vehicles and equipment so contamination can be removed, safely discarded, and replaced.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.10
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17C; B—Understanding


## Section 17D — Crisis Response and Recovery

### C17-FC-059
- **Prompt:** In threat areas, what does AFH 1 identify as the key to keeping assets and personnel from becoming contaminated?
- **Answer:** Contamination avoidance.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17D; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-060
- **Prompt:** Who typically disseminates attack warning signals, alarm signals, and MOPP levels, and on whose recommendation?
- **Answer:** Typically the command post, at the recommendation of the CBRN Control Center within the EOC.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17D; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-061
- **Prompt:** Upon witnessing an incident, what immediate actions does AFH 1 list?
- **Answer:** Alert others in the immediate area; report to security forces, fire and emergency services, or installation C2; stay uphill and upwind; avoid inhaling fumes, smoke, or vapors; attempt to rescue and care for casualties; avoid handling material/components involved; evacuate if rescue/containment is impractical or as directed.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17D; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-062
- **Prompt:** Should DAF personnel who recognize an attack in progress wait for mass notification before acting?
- **Answer:** No. They should take immediate action regardless of whether or not mass notification has been disseminated.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17D; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-063
- **Prompt:** When Alarm Red is declared or an attack begins without notification, what individual sequence does AFH 1 prescribe?
- **Answer:** Drop to the ground; don protective mask (if not already donned); crawl to closest available protection; don remaining individual protective equipment; immediately check every member in your immediate area to make sure they are protected and have donned proper equipment; assist injured and provide self-aid and buddy care with caution.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.11
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17D; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-064
- **Prompt:** What reporting method does AFH 1 prescribe for attack or enemy movement observations?
- **Answer:** The S-A-L-U-T-E reporting method, submitted to the control center or work center through the most secure means.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.12
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17D; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-065
- **Prompt:** In SALUTE reporting, what does Size include?
- **Answer:** The number of persons and vehicles seen or the size of an object.
- **Source:** AFH 1, Table 17.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17D; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-066
- **Prompt:** In SALUTE reporting, what does Activity include?
- **Answer:** Description of enemy activity (assaulting, fleeing, observing).
- **Source:** AFH 1, Table 17.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17D; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-067
- **Prompt:** In SALUTE reporting, what do Location and Time include?
- **Answer:** Location: where the enemy was sighted (grid coordinate or reference point). Time: time activity was observed.
- **Source:** AFH 1, Table 17.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17D; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-068
- **Prompt:** In SALUTE reporting, what do Unit and Equipment include?
- **Answer:** Unit: distinctive signs, symbols, identification on people, vehicles, aircraft, or weapons (numbers, patches, clothing type). Equipment: equipment and vehicles associated with the activity.
- **Source:** AFH 1, Table 17.2
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17D; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-069
- **Prompt:** What does recovery focus on according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Saving lives, detecting and mitigating hazards, mission restoration, and sustainment. Immediate actions treat casualties, assess damage, and control contamination. Reestablishing the mission is a top priority.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17D; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-070
- **Prompt:** Who determines when post-attack CBRN surveys begin, and what do PAR teams do?
- **Answer:** The installation commander determines when specialized and unit reconnaissance teams begin. PAR teams collect information about attack effects, report to their control center, and mark/report contamination and contaminated equipment.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17D; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-071
- **Prompt:** What is the primary C2 function for collecting and consolidating post-attack information?
- **Answer:** The CBRN Control Center within the EOC. The EOC Director up-channels information and makes recommendations regarding alarm signals and MOPP levels to the CAT.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17D; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-072
- **Prompt:** For personnel not on a PAR team, what recovery actions does AFH 1 emphasize?
- **Answer:** Perform immediate decontamination if contaminated and remain under cover in a safe location unless otherwise directed.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17D; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-073
- **Prompt:** What are the most effective methods AFH 1 names for removing chemical agents from skin or protective equipment?
- **Answer:** Reactive skin decontamination lotion and M295 individual decontamination kits. If an agent gets in the eyes (nerve or blister), irrigate with water.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17D; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-074
- **Prompt:** When may 5-percent and 0.5-percent chlorine solutions be used for decontamination per AFH 1?
- **Answer:** 5-percent chlorine solution for equipment and 0.5-percent chlorine solution for skin should only be used after consultation from CBRN Defense Specialists because some chemicals react negatively to chlorine.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.13
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17D; B—Understanding


## Section 17E — Department of the Air Force Mishap Prevention

### C17-FC-075
- **Prompt:** What is the DAF safety management system, and what directive mandates it?
- **Answer:** A mishap prevention framework mandated by DAFI 91-202, The U.S. Air Force Mishap Prevention Program, designed to minimize risk and reduce the occurrence and cost of injuries, illnesses, fatalities, and property damages.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17E; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-076
- **Prompt:** How do DAF leaders implement the safety management system according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** By providing guidance and goals, establishing safety responsibility and accountability, applying risk management to all activities, and promoting safety throughout the organization.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.14
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17E; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-077
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define a DAF mishap?
- **Answer:** An unplanned occurrence, or series of occurrences, that results in occupational illnesses, death, or injury to DAF personnel, or damage to Department of Defense (DoD) property.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17E; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-078
- **Prompt:** What is the purpose of DAF safety investigations according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** Mishap prevention purposes only—not for punitive actions or to establish financial liability.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17E; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-079
- **Prompt:** What constitutes a Class A mishap?
- **Answer:** Costs totaling $2,500,000, destruction of a Department of Defense aircraft, or a fatality or permanent total disability.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17E; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-080
- **Prompt:** What constitutes a Class B mishap?
- **Answer:** Costs totaling $600,000 or more but less than $2,500,000, a permanent partial disability, or inpatient hospitalization of three or more personnel.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17E; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-081
- **Prompt:** What constitutes a Class C mishap?
- **Answer:** Costs totaling $60,000 or more but less than $600,000, or a nonfatal injury or illness that results in one or more days away from work.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17E; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-082
- **Prompt:** What constitutes a Class D mishap?
- **Answer:** Costs totaling $25,000 or more but less than $60,000, or a recordable injury or illness not otherwise classified as a Class A, B, or C mishap.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.15
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17E; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-083
- **Prompt:** What form is used for hazard reports, and where must it be available?
- **Answer:** AF Form 457, USAF Hazard Report, must be readily available in all work centers.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17E; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-084
- **Prompt:** Within what time must individuals report personal injury, property damage, or suspected hazardous-material exposure?
- **Answer:** As soon as practical, but not to exceed 24 hours.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17E; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-085
- **Prompt:** How quickly must hazard-report investigators respond in writing?
- **Answer:** Within 10 duty days, addressing the corrective action or plans, and they conduct follow-up reviews until corrective action is completed.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17E; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-086
- **Prompt:** Can hazard reports be submitted anonymously, and what if a hazard is eliminated on the spot?
- **Answer:** Yes, reports can be submitted anonymously. If a hazard is eliminated on the spot, no further action is required, though sharing information with other units or agencies is recommended.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.16
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17E; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-087
- **Prompt:** What enlisted AFSC is associated with career safety professionals on the safety office staff?
- **Answer:** AFSC 1S0X1.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17E; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-088
- **Prompt:** What must commanders appoint to assist with unit safety programs?
- **Answer:** A primary and alternate unit safety representative to assist in implementing safety programs, including PPE procurement/proper use and worker/facility compliance with applicable standards.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17E; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-089
- **Prompt:** What does AFH 1 say about failing to intervene when a potential unsafe act is identified?
- **Answer:** A failure to intervene when a potential unsafe act is identified is a failure to protect.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17E; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-090
- **Prompt:** What system does the Analysis and Cyberspace Operations Division develop and sustain?
- **Answer:** The Air Force Safety Automated System (AFSAS), which collects and maintains safety-related data used to investigate mishaps and mitigate hazards for all safety disciplines.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.17
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17E; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-091
- **Prompt:** What does AFH 1 identify as the number one cause of Air and Space Forces mishaps, and which division addresses it?
- **Answer:** Human error, addressed by the Human Performance Division.

### C17-FC-092

- **Answer:** Approximately 2,000 students through 11 professional courses in 70 sessions, ranging from three days to seven weeks.

- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17E; B—Understanding


## Section 17F — Risk Management

### C17-FC-093
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define risk management?
- **Answer:** A decision-making process designed to systematically evaluate possible courses of action, identify risks and benefits, and determine the best course of action for any given situation.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.18
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17F; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-094
- **Prompt:** When is a job safety analysis used?
- **Answer:** To evaluate each work task not governed by a technical order or other definitive guidance and when a new work task or process is introduced into the workplace.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.18
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17F; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-095
- **Prompt:** List the four principles that govern all actions associated with risk management.
- **Answer:** Accept No Unnecessary Risk; Make Risk Decisions at the Appropriate Level; Integrate Risk Management into Operations and Planning at All Levels; Apply the Risk Management Process Cyclically and Continuously.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17F; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-096
- **Prompt:** What does “Accept No Unnecessary Risk” mean?
- **Answer:** Unnecessary risk comes without a commensurate return in real benefits or available opportunities. Choose actions that meet mission requirements while exposing personnel and resources to the lowest acceptable risk. Even high-risk endeavors may be undertaken when there is a well-founded basis that benefits exceed costs.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17F; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-097
- **Prompt:** Why make risk decisions at the appropriate level?
- **Answer:** Some risk acceptance decisions must be made by an appropriate decision-making authority to effectively allocate resources and implement controls. Making decisions at the appropriate level also establishes clear accountability.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17F; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-098
- **Prompt:** Why integrate RM into operations and planning at all levels as early as possible?
- **Answer:** Early integration provides the greatest opportunity to make informed risk decisions and implement effective risk controls. Assessments are most successful when incorporated deliberately in the normal sequence of events by those directly involved, not as a last-minute add-on.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.19
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17F; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-099
- **Prompt:** What are the two primary levels of risk management?
- **Answer:** Deliberate and real-time. They are interrelated and separated only at the point where planning transitions to execution of the mission or activity.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17F; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-100
- **Prompt:** What is in-depth risk management planning, and when is it used?
- **Answer:** An aspect of deliberate RM generally associated with strategic-level planning, reserved for complex operations and systems and high-priority/high-visibility situations where hazards are not well understood or easily predictable (e.g., large troop movements, airshow planning, tactics and training curricula development).
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.20
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17F; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-101
- **Prompt:** List the five steps of the Risk Management Process in order.
- **Answer:** Step 1 Identify the Hazards; Step 2 Assess the Hazards; Step 3 Develop Controls and Make Decisions; Step 4 Implement Controls; Step 5 Supervise and Evaluate.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.21; Figure 17.1
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17F; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-102
- **Prompt:** How does AFH 1 define a hazard in Step 1 of RM?
- **Answer:** Any real or potential condition that can cause mission degradation; injury, illness, or death to personnel; or damage to or loss of equipment or property.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17F; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-103
- **Prompt:** What does assessing hazards in Step 2 determine?
- **Answer:** The probability and severity of negative effects that may result from exposure to risks or hazards and that directly affect mission or activity success, including determining the level of risk related to severity and probability.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17F; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-104
- **Prompt:** What three risk components can effective mitigation measures reduce in Step 3?
- **Answer:** Probability, severity, or exposure.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17F; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-105
- **Prompt:** What must an implementation strategy identify in Step 4?
- **Answer:** Who, what, when, where, and how much cost is associated with the control measure, plus accountability, resources, sustainability, and a feedback mechanism.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17F; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-106
- **Prompt:** What does Step 5 Supervise and Evaluate require leaders and supervisors to do?
- **Answer:** Supervise and monitor the operation or activity, and review and evaluate to ensure risk and cost are in balance; reapply controls as necessary; use after-action reports, surveys, and in-progress reviews; establish feedback so corrective action is effective.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.21
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17F; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-107
- **Prompt:** What is Real-Time Risk Management?
- **Answer:** A less formal risk assessment using basic RM process steps to identify and mitigate hazards in a new or changing situation, streamlining the 5-Step process when decisions must be made quickly.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17F; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-108
- **Prompt:** What three stages of situational awareness does AFH 1 require for complete real-time assessment?
- **Answer:** (a) Perception of what is happening; (b) Integration of information and goals; and (c) Projection into the future.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17F; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-109
- **Prompt:** What do “Knock-it-off” and “Timeout” signal during an operation or activity?
- **Answer:** That those involved should stop, take a moment to reset, and reevaluate the current situation. All current actions are immediately halted and stabilized to a safe position—nonnegotiable and cannot be overridden by command authority.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17F; B—Understanding

### C17-FC-110
- **Prompt:** Who is empowered to use Knock-it-off or Timeout according to AFH 1?
- **Answer:** All Airmen and Guardians, regardless of rank or position, without any fear of repercussions.
- **Source:** AFH 1, ¶17.22
- **Tags:** SSgt; TSgt; 17F; B—Understanding
